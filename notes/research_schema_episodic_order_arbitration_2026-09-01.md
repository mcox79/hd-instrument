# Research drill (2x/operational): arbitrating script-schema order vs. the story's own episodic order on CONFLICT

**Date:** 2026-09-01 | **Type:** literature drill, level-2 operational (no experiments run, no sub-agents spawned per task instruction) | **Field:** cognitive neuroscience of schema memory (SLIMM / PE-gated consolidation), relational/transitive-inference memory, hippocampal pattern completion | **Builds directly on:** `notes/research_narrative_before_after_order_mechanism_2026-09-01.md` (same-day predecessor; that note established alignment + script/schema as the two dominant gates and flagged script-fallback as "when the text is silent" — this drill answers the harder case the predecessor didn't cover: **what happens when the text is NOT silent but actively contradicts the script**)

---

## HEADLINE

The brain does not blend schema and episode into a weighted average order judgment. It runs an **encoding-time triage that makes the conflict self-resolving by the time retrieval happens**: schema-*congruent* material is cheaply integrated by mPFC and never gets a dedicated high-fidelity trace (it just inherits the schema's order, correctly, because it agrees) — but schema-*incongruent* material triggers a hippocampal prediction-error (PE) response that lays down a disproportionately strong, detail-specific episodic trace **specifically for the deviating fact** (van Kesteren et al. 2012 SLIMM; Tse et al. 2007; Sinclair-type PE-disrupts-hippocampal-representations findings). The practical result: at answer time, an aligned, explicit episodic signal should **categorically outrank** the schema prior — not because episode is generically privileged, but because PE-driven encoding guarantees the episodic trace is the *strongest available signal* exactly in the cases where it conflicts with schema. Order between genuinely causally-independent/parallel events is not stored as a forced binary at all — hippocampal relational computation (transitive inference; Kumaran & McClelland's REMERGE) resolves order only across a *chain* of overlapping pairwise codes, so an unlinked pair has no path and, mechanistically, no answer — the same signal-detection null seen in temporal-order-judgment psychophysics at the point of subjective simultaneity. Event-mention alignment itself is two-staged: a schema/frame-level gist match narrows to the right situational "slot" (cortex), then CA3 attractor pattern-completion (Marr 1971; Rolls & Kesner 2006; Nakazawa et al. 2002) reconstructs the specific trace from the partial/reworded cue — meaning the dominant *failure* mode on paraphrase is not "no match" but **mis-binding to the wrong event** when the cue's role-structure overlaps a neighboring script step.

## Q1 — Script/schema vs. episodic order: the arbitration rule on CONFLICT

**Mechanism.** SLIMM (van Kesteren, Ruiter, Fernández & Henson 2012, *Trends Neurosci.*) is the load-bearing model: mPFC represents an active schema and generates an online prediction; when incoming information is *congruent*, mPFC integrates it rapidly into the existing knowledge structure (cheap, fast, doesn't require hippocampal binding); when incoming information is *incongruent*, the mismatch is a genuine **prediction error** that recruits the hippocampus/MTL to encode the deviating item with full episodic (contextual, detail-specific) binding. Tse et al. (2007, *Science*, "Schemas and Memory Consolidation") is the causal demonstration in rats: schema-congruent one-trial associations become hippocampus-independent within ~2 days (vs. weeks for novel material) — i.e., congruent content is handled by a fast, low-effort pathway that essentially *borrows* the schema's structure rather than laying down its own detailed trace. Ghosh & Gilboa (2014, *Neuropsychologia*) add that vmPFC actively **biases retrieval toward schema-consistent memories** as a function of current-context congruency — a retrieval-side confirmation that schema is a genuine competing prior, not just an encoding shortcut. Separately, a 2021-2022 line of work (Sinclair-type fMRI studies, "Prediction errors disrupt hippocampal representations and update episodic memories," *PNAS*) shows PE measurably **disrupts** the sustained hippocampal representation of an unfolding episode, and the *degree* of disruption predicts how much the memory subsequently updates — PE is not just a flag, it's the mechanism that *reallocates encoding resources* toward the deviating content. A very recent follow-up (2025, *PNAS*, "Hippocampal mismatch signals are based on episodic memories and not schematic knowledge") complicates the picture usefully: it suggests the hippocampus's own mismatch detector compares incoming input against **specific episodic predictions**, not generic schema — while schema-level violations recruit a partially separate semantic-control/subcortical PE circuit. Read together, this implies two coupled but distinguishable PE channels (episodic-specific and schema-generic), with the episodic one being the higher-precision, higher-priority signal once any trace exists (flag: this 2025 result is new and its exact division of labor is not yet settled — treat as directionally supportive, not confirmatory).

**Arbitration rule the brain appears to use (encoding-triage, not retrieval-blend):**
1. At encoding, compare the incoming fact against the active schema's prediction.
2. **Congruent → fast mPFC integration, no dedicated high-fidelity trace.** Order is available from schema only, and it happens to be correct, so no conflict ever surfaces later.
3. **Incongruent → PE fires → hippocampal high-fidelity encoding specifically of the deviating fact**, at increased "cost" but increased fidelity, precisely at the locus of conflict.
4. At retrieval, the *system doesn't need to arbitrate weighted confidence between two live competing signals* — it queries the episodic index first; where a PE-flagged trace exists (i.e., exactly the deviation cases), it is the strongest, most detailed signal available and wins by construction; where no episodic trace exists (the pair was never in conflict, or was never made explicit), schema is consulted as the only available signal, functioning correctly as a low-cost default precisely because it was never contradicted.

**Concrete computational prediction.** Implement this as a **precedence cascade, not a weighted blend**: (a) if the story provides an *explicit, alignment-confirmed* textual signal for a pair's order (a directly-stated sequence, a connective, or two mentions in an event chain — see the predecessor note's EST/event-chain mechanism), that signal wins outright regardless of what the canonical script says — do not let a script-prior confidence score ever outvote an aligned, explicit episodic signal; this is exactly the "confidently wrong" failure named in the task context, and per SLIMM it is a *category error* to treat schema as a peer-strength signal to an explicit episodic one. (b) Only when no explicit/aligned episodic signal exists for a pair does the script/schema canonical order get consulted, as a genuine fallback. HARD test: on the subset of pairs where script-order and story-order are known to conflict (deviation cases), arbitration-fixed accuracy should approach ceiling (≥0.85) if the fix is precedence-correct, because these are exactly the cases the brain's own PE machinery over-invests in making unambiguous.

## Q2 — Representing PARTIAL / absent order (no forced answer for causally-independent pairs)

**Mechanism.** Two convergent lines support a genuinely partial (not total) order representation. First, Howard & Kahana's Temporal Context Model (already established in the predecessor note) gives a *graded*, metric representation (drifting context vectors), not a symbolic total order — order-strength is a continuous quantity, and where two items' context overlap is symmetric/negligible, the directional signal is genuinely near-zero, not just "hard to compute." Second, and more directly on point: hippocampal **relational/transitive-inference memory** (Eichenbaum's flexible relational-memory account; Dusek & Eichenbaum 1997; the hippocampal-lesion transitive-inference literature reviewed via PNAS 1997 "The hippocampus and memory for orderly stimulus relations") shows the hippocampus supports inference across **overlapping pairwise experiences** (A>B, B>C ⟹ infer A>C), and Kumaran & McClelland's **REMERGE** model formalizes this as a *retrieval-time* recurrent computation over pattern-separated episodic codes — relations are not pre-stored as a global order, they are *derived on demand* by traversing a chain of overlapping pairwise codes. This has a direct, falsifiable structural consequence: **a relation between two items is only computable when a path exists connecting them through the relational graph of directly-experienced/directly-stated pairs.** Two causally-independent, never-co-ordered script steps have no such path — mechanistically, there is nothing to retrieve, not a suppressed or low-confidence answer. Third, temporal-order-judgment (TOJ) psychophysics independently gives the *signature* this produces when forced to answer anyway: as two events' onsets approach true simultaneity, the psychometric order-judgment curve flattens toward chance around a well-defined **point of subjective simultaneity (PSS)** — i.e., "no fixed order" is not a categorical third symbol, it is the region where the underlying decision variable's signed difference collapses toward zero relative to noise (Sternberg & Knoll's classic TOJ modeling; van Eijk et al.-style SJ/TOJ joint models).

**Concrete computational prediction.** Represent order confidence as a **graded margin** (e.g., relative strength/activation difference between the two candidate directions, or existence-and-length of a path in a relational graph built from directly-stated/directly-chained order facts), not as a forced `argmax(before, after)`. **Abstain** ("no fixed order can be determined") whenever (i) no path connects the two event nodes in the relational graph built from explicit textual statements + script-template edges, or (ii) the margin between the two directions falls below a calibrated noise floor. Calibrate the floor using genuinely-parallel script-slot pairs (which should cluster near zero margin) against genuinely-sequential pairs (which should show a large, clearly separated margin) — this is the engineering analog of fitting a PSS psychometric curve.

## Q3 — Event-mention alignment / re-instatement (matching a reworded question to the encoded event)

**Mechanism.** Two-stage matching, not a single similarity computation. Stage 1 is coarse and **gist/schema-level**: cortical (not hippocampal) representations during naturalistic narrative encoding/recall are comparatively general and schema-typical, functioning to narrow a query to the right situational "slot" or frame (consistent with "Flexible reuse of cortico-hippocampal representations during encoding and recall of naturalistic events," and with the SLIMM/Ghosh-Gilboa picture of vmPFC-biased, gist-level retrieval). Stage 2 is fine-grained and **hippocampal**: CA3's recurrent collateral network is a classic autoassociative attractor (Marr 1971; Rolls & Kesner 2006 review the "symmetric completion" property — the full stored pattern is recoverable from any sufficiently-overlapping partial cue), empirically confirmed by Nakazawa et al. (2002)'s CA3-NMDAR-knockout mice, which selectively lose pattern-completion-dependent retrieval from degraded cues while retaining full-cue retrieval. This is exactly the computational shape of "match a reworded question to a previously-encoded event": the question's paraphrase functions as a **partial, corrupted retrieval cue**, and success/failure is governed by attractor-basin geometry, not lexical overlap per se.

**What determines success vs. failure.** Attractor completion succeeds when the cue retains enough of the *originally-encoded feature set* to fall within the correct trace's basin — and critically, hippocampal CA3 representations are believed to be organized around **event/situational structure (who did what to what/whom)**, not surface wording. This predicts alignment success tracks **semantic-role overlap** (shared predicate-argument / participant-role structure between question and text mention) far more than string/lexical overlap, and predicts a *specific, diagnostic failure mode*: because CA3 completion is a pull toward the nearest attractor, a paraphrase that superficially resembles a **different, nearby** script step (e.g., confusing "decided what to eat" with "was given a menu" when both share participants/setting) should produce a confident **mis-binding to the wrong event**, not a null/no-match result. This is empirically distinguishable and should be checked directly in the error analysis (coverage-gap vs. mis-binding are different bugs with different fixes).

**Concrete computational prediction.** Build the alignment key as an **event-frame representation** (predicate + core semantic roles: agent, patient/theme, setting), not string or embedding cosine similarity alone. Predict: (a) situational paraphrases that preserve role structure but change surface wording succeed at near-ceiling; (b) failures cluster on cases where the paraphrase's role structure overlaps a *neighboring* script step more than the true target — i.e., look for mis-binding, not blank alignment failure, in the current pipeline's error cases.

## The single most brain-faithful arbitration rule to implement first

**A three-tier hard-precedence cascade, not a weighted score fusion:**

1. **Explicit, alignment-confirmed episodic signal (from the text itself) wins outright**, unconditionally, over the script/schema prior — this is the direct engineering translation of SLIMM's finding that PE-triggered hippocampal encoding makes the episodic trace the strongest available signal exactly where conflict occurs. Do not implement this as "high weight"; implement it as "schema is not even consulted when this tier fires." This is the fix for the "confidently wrong" failure mode named in the task context.
2. **When no explicit episodic signal exists for the pair, fall back to the canonical script/schema order** — this is the correct, low-cost default, justified because (per Tse 2007 / SLIMM) schema-congruent facts never generate a competing episodic trace in the first place, so there is no real conflict to lose.
3. **When neither an aligned episodic signal nor a script-template edge exists connecting the two events (no path in the relational graph; margin at floor), abstain** — output "no fixed order," implemented as a graded-margin threshold (analogous to the psychophysical point of subjective simultaneity), not a symbolic special case bolted onto a forced binary classifier.

This rule is cheap to implement (a precedence check + a graph-path/margin abstain gate over signals the substrate likely already computes — explicit order cues, script-template lookup, and some existing symmetric co-occurrence/order score) and is the most literature-convergent account across four independent sub-fields (schema-memory neuroscience, relational/transitive-inference memory, TOJ psychophysics, hippocampal pattern completion).

## Falsifiable predictions (HARD-PASS / HARD-FAIL)

- **HARD-PASS (confirms the precedence-cascade arbitration rule):** On the Schema-Conflicts + Explicit + Aligned slice, precedence-rule re-scoring lifts accuracy to ≥0.80 with non-overlapping CI over the current pipeline's accuracy on the same slice. This is the direct test of "episode should categorically beat schema when both are present and aligned."
- **HARD-PASS (confirms the abstain/partial-order rule as measurement-valid, not just permissive):** On a held-out set of manually-verified genuinely-parallel (no-path) pairs, an abstain-enabled scorer achieves ≥0.90 correct-abstain rate while abstain rate on genuinely-sequential (has-path, large-margin) pairs stays ≤0.10 (false-abstain). If abstain fires indiscriminately (high false-abstain on sequential pairs), the margin/path-graph threshold is miscalibrated, not the mechanism wrong.
- **HARD-PASS (confirms alignment as role-structure, not lexical):** Role-structure-based alignment scoring shows higher precision-at-1 than lexical/embedding-cosine alignment specifically on the paraphrased subset (≥0.15 absolute gap, non-overlapping CI).
- **HARD-FAIL (refutes the precedence-cascade specifically, as opposed to a general "give episode more weight" fix):** A simple weighted blend (e.g., raise episodic-signal weight without hard precedence) achieves statistically indistinguishable accuracy from the hard-precedence cascade on the Schema-Conflicts slice (gap < 0.03, CIs overlap). This would mean the brain-motivated "categorical override, not weighting" distinction doesn't cash out as a measurable engineering difference, and a simpler weighted-fusion implementation is justified instead.
- **HARD-FAIL (refutes the mis-binding prediction for alignment failures, Q3):** Manual inspection of paraphrase-alignment errors shows the dominant failure mode is null/no-match (cue matches nothing) rather than mis-binding to a neighboring script step (cue matches the wrong event). This would redirect the alignment fix from role-structure discrimination toward pure coverage/recall improvement instead.
- **HARD-FAIL (refutes schema-conflict as a real, separable phenomenon in this corpus at all):** The Schema-Conflicts slice, once explicit-alignment cases are isolated, turns out to be near-empty (very few pairs in the eval set actually have the story deviate from a well-defined canonical script order). If so, the "confidently wrong" failure named in the task context is a real but *low-frequency* phenomenon — worth fixing via the precedence rule (it's cheap and correct either way) but should not be expected to move the aggregate 0.59 baseline by much; in that case the predecessor note's alignment/script-coverage gates remain the dominant levers.

## TLDR
When a story does something different from the "normal" way an errand usually goes (decided on spaghetti *before* going to the restaurant, instead of after), the brain does not average the two possibilities. Reading naturally over-invests in remembering the surprising part exactly as it happened — the brain treats a deviation from the routine as a mini alarm bell that says "pay extra attention to this specific detail," so by the time you're asked about it, the specific memory of what actually happened is the strongest signal in the room and simply wins, no averaging needed. Ordinary, script-following parts of the story never trigger that alarm, so it's fine to just use "how the errand normally goes" as a cheap default there. And when two things in the errand genuinely have no fixed order (both are optional side-steps with no set sequence), the brain doesn't force a guess — it reports genuine "don't know," the same way people naturally say two things felt simultaneous rather than being pushed to force an order. Matching a rephrased question back to the right sentence in the story works in two steps: first a fuzzy "which part of the story is this about" step, then a sharper "reconstruct exactly what happened" step — and when that second step goes wrong, it usually locks onto the WRONG nearby moment in the story (a mix-up) rather than finding nothing at all.
