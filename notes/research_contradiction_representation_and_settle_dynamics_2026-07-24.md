## HEADLINE

**The all-positive-cosine failure is not an encoder bug -- it is the textbook "antonym problem" in distributional semantics, and the literature is unambiguous that raw continuous similarity structurally cannot separate synonymy from antonymy without an explicit supervised signal.** Antonyms are *paradigmatically* associated -- they occur in near-identical contexts precisely because they contrast along the same dimension ("the water is hot" / "the water is cold") -- so distributional models place them CLOSE, not far, exactly the geometry we measured (Yih, Zweig & Platt 2012 name this "the antonym problem" directly; multiple independent NLP fixes exist, all of them supervised: PILSA's sign-flipped matrix, Mrksic et al.'s counter-fitting/Attract-Repel margin loss, the POLAR framework's antonym-derived subspace). **This is convergently reinforced from the biology side, and the convergence argues AGAINST fixing this purely as embedding geometry**: three independent lit-scans agree that abstract propositional opposition is not brain-like the sensory bipolar/anti-correlated codes (color/motion opponency) that DO exist as a single reversible-sign channel -- those require a natural physical continuum (wavelength, direction) to split, and even they are implemented as paired mutually-suppressive populations, not a literal antipodal vector, once you leave single retinal/LGN cells. At the abstract/propositional level (amygdala valence clusters, antonym neuroimaging), the evidence instead favors **separate representations joined by an explicit relational/comparator link** -- i.e., contradiction is a labeled RELATION, computed and attached, not a property that falls out of a similarity metric. The N400/negation literature adds a third, temporal constraint: contradiction-detection in the brain is **staged, not unified** -- an early, negation-AGNOSTIC semantic-mismatch signal (Fischler et al. 1983: negated-true and affirmative-false sentences BOTH produce large N400s, tracking the "kernel" concept-pair mismatch regardless of the NOT) is followed by a later, distinct truth-evaluation stage, itself distinct again from response-level ACC conflict. Negation is not a clean algebraic flip even in the brain's own processing (Kaup/Ludtke/Zwaan two-step simulation: the negated/illusory state activates FIRST, gets suppressed only later).

**Net verdict for the substrate: design option (c) -- an explicit, rule/lookup-populated RELATION-POLARITY signal computed at fact/choice PAIR-construction time, feeding into the already-correct CI settle dynamics -- is the brain-faithful, lit-supported primary fix. Options (a) antonym-repulsion retrofitting and (b) negation-as-vector-transform are real but secondary: (a) helps the residual cosine-only edges generalize a little further (useful, bounded, per the field's own limited-generalization findings) but is not, on its own, how the brain does abstract opposition; (b) is actively contraindicated as a clean algebraic operation by the two-step-simulation/N400 evidence -- negation is a staged SUPPRESSION process, not an orthogonal-projection flip, so it should be implemented as a rule-detector feeding the relation-polarity slot, not as a vector-space negation operator.** P_deflated: **0.42** (see Calibration).

## (1) Antonym/opposition representation -- distinct from unrelatedness (biology + vector-space literature)

**The vector-space diagnosis, exact and citable.** Distributional models (word2vec/GloVe-style) infer meaning purely from co-occurrence context and thereby capture *paradigmatic substitutability* -- words that fill the same syntactic/semantic slot -- rather than "same meaning." Antonyms are prototypically paradigmatically associated (they contrast along one shared dimension, so they co-occur in nearly identical frames), which is exactly why they end up geometrically close under cosine, not far. Yih, Zweig & Platt (2012, EMNLP, "Polarity Inducing Latent Semantic Analysis"/PILSA) name this "the antonym problem" as their explicit motivation and fix it by constructing a signed term-document matrix (antonym-relation "documents" get their sign flipped relative to synonym-relation ones) BEFORE running LSA/SVD -- polarity is injected structurally into the matrix, not learned as a side effect, and the result is antonym pairs at cosine approx -1, synonym pairs at approx +1. Mrksic et al. (2016, NAACL, "Counter-fitting Word Vectors to Linguistic Constraints" and the 2017 TACL "Attract-Repel" extension) instead retrofit an EXISTING embedding post-hoc via a 3-term loss: antonym-REPEL (margin-based cosine-below-threshold for known antonym pairs), synonym-ATTRACT, and a vector-space-preservation regularizer so unrelated pairs aren't disturbed; both use WordNet/PPDB antonym pairs as the supervised constraint set and set SOTA on SimLex-999 (a benchmark specifically built to penalize synonym/antonym conflation). Ono, Miwa & Sasaki (2015, NAACL) build antonym-awareness INTO training instead of retrofitting. **All three fixes are supervised, thesaurus-driven repulsion -- none derives polarity from distributional statistics alone; this is the field's unanimous verdict, not one dissenting method among several.**

**Is there a single generalizing "polarity axis," or is each antonym pair its own fix?** Osgood's semantic-differential work (1950s-60s) found connotative meaning across many unrelated word pairs collapses onto ~3 universal factors dominated by EVALUATION (good-bad), explaining the largest variance share -- real evidence for a shared bipolar dimension underlying much of "oppositeness." The POLAR framework (Mathew et al. 2020, WWW) operationalizes this directly: given an antonym-pair list, it finds the subspace spanned by their difference vectors and projects arbitrary words onto it, producing interpretable dimensions whose polarity aligns with human judgment -- a shared, not pair-idiosyncratic, structure. Counter-fitting, by contrast, treats each pair as its own repulsion constraint (though many pairs share an underlying evaluative/intensity load in aggregate). **Read as complementary, not competing: an axis-style fix is more parsimonious/interpretable, pairwise repulsion is more empirically validated for raw similarity-benchmark performance; no primary source was found directly pitting the two head-to-head, flagged as a synthesis-level judgment, not a confirmed ranking.**

**The biology's answer to whether this generalizes to ABSTRACT (non-sensory) opposition is a clean NO, and this is the single most decisive finding of the whole drill.** Color opponency (L-M / red-green, S-vs-L+M / blue-yellow retinal ganglion and LGN cells) genuinely is a single reversible-sign channel on ONE neuron (De Valois et al. 1966; Wiesel & Hubel 1966; reviewed Solomon & Lennie 2007) -- excitation from one cone-input combination, active suppression below baseline from the opposing one, which is why reddish-green is perceptually impossible (Hurvich & Jameson hue-cancellation). But this exists ONLY because wavelength is a natural physical continuum to split around. Even one step away -- motion direction (MT/V5; Rust, Mante, Simoncelli & Movshon 2006) and auditory spatial location (Stecker, Harrington & Middlebrooks 2005, PLOS Biology) -- opposition is already relaxed to PAIRED, mutually-suppressive POPULATIONS (excitatory pool for leftward, a separate excitatory pool for rightward, subtracted downstream), not one bipolar cell; there's no single physical axis to split around anymore. At the motivational/valence level the evidence is genuinely split: some OFC/ACC work suggests a shared signed value-scale (a real, partial counter-datapoint, flagged not resolved), but the most directly relevant mechanistic study -- Bidirectional valence coding in amygdala intercalated clusters (2024, bioRxiv/PMC11621204) -- found TWO ANATOMICALLY SEPARATE clusters (ITCdm = aversive-only, ITCvm = reward-only, "no clear relationship between responses to appetitive and aversive stimuli"), opposed via mutual GABAergic cross-inhibition between distinct populations -- a comparator architecture, not a shared axis. At the fully abstract/semantic level (antonym CONCEPTS, not sensory percepts): antonym generation neuroimaging (Sass et al. 2009; Kalenine/Green) recruits essentially the SAME core semantic network as synonym/association judgments, differing only modestly in a couple of regions -- antonyms and synonyms together account for ~40% of free-association responses, i.e. antonyms are stored as a LABELED RELATIONAL LINK ("contrast," alongside "synonym," "co-hyponym" edges) in an associative semantic network, not as endpoints of a dedicated geometric signed dimension.

**Verdict for Q1:** opposition is representable as a genuine sign/anti-correlation ONLY where a natural physical continuum exists to split (rare, sensory, not our regime); for abstract propositional content the brain's own solution is an explicit, LABELED relational edge attached between two otherwise-ordinary representations -- structurally identical to what Kintsch's CI matrix already does (a supplied, not derived, negative weight for "these two propositions are marked mutually exclusive by the knowledge net") and to what the "bound RELATION-POLARITY slot" design option (c) proposes.

## (2) Competition/inhibition dynamics -- static representation vs dynamic settling (build-on, not re-derived)

This question is thoroughly answered by the prior drill and is NOT re-run here: `notes/research_bindsettle_ci_settle_dynamics_multifact_aggregation_2026-07-24.md` already established, from Kintsch's own 1988 primary text, that his signed matrix has three-way semantics (positive=coherent-support, zero=irrelevant, negative=actively-contradictory-per-the-knowledge-net) and that this is a genuinely different mechanism from plain spreading activation/ACT-R (which has no negative-edge term at all). Desimone-Duncan biased competition, Reynolds-Heeger normalization, and O'Reilly-Frank PBWM gating all explain HOW many co-activated candidates get funneled down via mutual suppression + top-down bias, but (per that note) none of them derives contradiction/opposition CONTENT -- they operate on whatever activation they're handed, agnostic to whether two candidates are "opposite" or merely "both plausible." **This drill's contribution to Q2 is narrow and specific: contradiction is BOTH a static (supplied, labeled-relation) property AND a dynamic (settling/mutual-suppression) property, and these are DIFFERENT PIPELINE STAGES, not alternatives** -- the label ("these two facts contradict") must be attached before settling starts (Q1's finding: it cannot be read off raw similarity), and the CONSEQUENCE of that label (how much it suppresses the contradicted candidate, how fast, whether it fully zeroes vs partially discounts) is exactly what the CI relaxation dynamics (already specified) compute. Neither stage substitutes for the other.

## (3) Mismatch/prediction-error detection -- N400, negation timing, ACC conflict monitoring

**N400 and Fischler et al. (1983), the decisive primary finding (verified via the PMC review "Validating the truth of propositions"):** for simple categorical sentences ("A robin is/is not a bird/truck"), a LARGE N400 appeared for BOTH the false affirmative ("A robin is a truck") AND the true negative ("A robin is not a truck") -- with no clean N400 inversion by the negation operator. N400 amplitude tracks the semantic MISMATCH between the subject and predicate concepts (the "kernel"/inner positive proposition -- robin vs. truck), essentially IGNORING whether the sentence is negated or what its final truth value is. A later component (~500-1000ms, distinct negativity/positivity, per the Nieuwland & Kuperberg lineage) tracks final truth-value evaluation -- layered ON TOP of, not instead of, the earlier semantic-match signal. **This is directly load-bearing for the substrate design**: it means "does this fact's content clash with this candidate's content" (an early, negation-agnostic semantic-mismatch check) and "given the negation, is this candidate actually TRUE or FALSE" (a later, explicit truth-evaluation step) are TWO SEPARATE COMPUTATIONS in the brain's own timeline, not one. A single cosine number trying to do both jobs at once (as our current FC/FF edges do) is asking one signal to carry two brain-separated computations.

**Negation as a process, not an operator (Kaup, Ludtke & Zwaan; two-step/non-incremental simulation account):** sentence-picture verification studies (Kaup, Yaxley, Madden, Zwaan & Ludtke 2007) show that IMMEDIATELY after reading a negated sentence ("the door is not open"), recognition is FASTER to pictures matching the NEGATED/illusory state (an open door) than the actual state -- the pattern reverses only after a delay. So negation does not cleanly "flip" a representation; it first ACTIVATES the literal/positive content, then SUPPRESSES it in favor of the real state, over a real time-course. A competing account (Nieuwland & Kuperberg 2008) found this two-step delay is mainly present for PRAGMATICALLY UNLICENSED negation (denying an implausible state); when negation is pragmatically expected (e.g., "in moderation, red wine isn't bad"), integration is immediate and N400 tracks final truth value on a normal timescale. **Reading for our design**: negation is not analytically clean even biologically -- it is a staged, plausibility-modulated suppression process, which argues against implementing our own "NOT X" as a single deterministic vector-transform (design option b) and toward implementing it as an explicit rule-detected relation (option c) that the settle DYNAMICS (already specified, Q2) then apply as a suppression over iterations -- which is, structurally, the brain's own staged solution, not an engineering shortcut around it.

**ACC conflict monitoring is NOT the same signal as propositional contradiction.** Botvinick, Carter, Cohen and colleagues' conflict-monitoring theory was built and tested primarily on RESPONSE/motor conflict (Stroop, flanker, Simon tasks) -- co-activation of two incompatible ACTIONS, not semantic content per se. Follow-up fMRI/EEG work found response-conflict and semantic-conflict recruit overlapping-but-SEPARABLE networks (a Stroop-task dissociation found left parietal more implicated in response conflict, right mediodorsal thalamus more in semantic conflict; an early posterior delta/theta EEG signal precedes ACC-linked control). **Conclusion: there is no single unified "contradiction" brain signal to imitate as ONE substrate mechanism -- the brain assembles contradiction-handling across (at least) three separable stages: early negation-agnostic semantic mismatch (N400-like) -> later truth-value evaluation -> separate response-selection conflict (ACC-like).** For the substrate, the CI settle's readout stage (choice-vs-choice mutual inhibition, already specified in the prior note) is the closest analog of the ACC/response-conflict stage; the NEW piece this drill supplies (relation-polarity edge construction) is the analog of the earlier semantic-mismatch/truth-evaluation stages, which were previously entirely missing (nothing fed the sign) rather than merely underweighted.

**Predictive coding, signed vs unsigned:** the literature distinguishes signed prediction error (directional, this-specific-alternative-was-contradicted) from unsigned prediction error (magnitude-of-surprise/novelty only, valence-and-identity-agnostic, used for learning-rate/precision-weighting, associated with dopaminergic modulation). Kuperberg's 2024 predictive-coding model recasts the N400 itself as a lexico-semantic prediction-error magnitude signal -- closer to UNSIGNED (how much mismatch) than to a "this-specific-alternative" signed representation; the sub-agent flagged this as not independently confirmed against the primary PDF (fetch failed on binary content) and it should be treated as an open point, not a settled claim. This is a genuine, honestly-flagged gap: it weakly suggests even the brain's own early mismatch signal may not distinguish "surprising" from "specifically contradicts alternative X" at the earliest stage -- consistent with, not contradicting, this drill's overall recommendation that CONTENT-SPECIFIC contradiction (which alternative, specifically) needs an explicit relational tag rather than a magnitude-only signal.

## (4) The vector-rep design -- evaluated against the biology, with exact code targets

Current state (verified by direct read, 2026-07-24): `experiments/exp_arc_aggregation_retriever_bindsettle_v1.py::_ci_two_phase` computes `FC = fact_hd @ choice_hd.T` and `FF = fact_hd @ fact_hd.T` where `fact_hd`/`choice_hd` come from `SemanticHDEncoder.encode()` (`experiments/exp_semantic_hd_encoder_meaning_match_v1.py`), whose `fused(w) = L2(glove(w) + ALPHA*mean(glove(wn_synonyms(w))) + BETA*mean(glove(wn_hypernyms(w))))` -- an ALL-ADDITIVE, ALL-POSITIVE-PULL construction (synonym + hypernym pulls only; `_wn_neighbors()` already iterates each WordNet lemma object but never calls `.antonyms()`). This is the precise, minimal-diff location of the bug: there is currently NO polarity source anywhere in the pipeline, at either the word-fusion stage or the edge-construction stage.

**(a) Antonym/polarity axis (Yih/PILSA, Mrksic/counter-fitting, POLAR)** -- REAL and directly buildable (WordNet's `lemma.antonyms()` is already loaded and already iterated in `_wn_neighbors`, zero new data dependency), but per Q1's biology finding, this is a SECONDARY generalization layer, not the primary mechanism: it only repels pairs an antonym list actually covers, and the biology explicitly does not support a clean geometric axis for abstract opposition. Recommend as a bolt-on counter-fitting-style pass on the underlying 300d GloVe table feeding `SemanticHDEncoder.fused()` (a GAMMA*antonym term SUBTRACTED, using the same lemma objects already in the loop) -- cheap, small vocab (ARC/WorldTree content words only), public reference recipe (Mrksic et al. 2016/2017) to check the loss-function details against if built as a full retrofit rather than a simple subtractive term. Treat this as improving the FALLBACK case (no explicit rule fires) rather than the primary fix.

**(b) NEGATION as a binding/transform operation** -- theoretically available (Widdows & Peters 2003's orthogonal-negation/quantum-logic operator: NOT = projection onto the orthogonal complement subspace of the negated concept, reduced synonym/neighbor retrieval by up to 76% in their IR experiments) but CONTRAINDICATED as the primary mechanism by the two-step-simulation/N400 evidence in Q3: the brain does not cleanly algebraically flip negated content, it stages activation-then-suppression, plausibility-modulated. Recommend NOT building a vector-space negation operator; instead implement negation as a CUE-DETECTOR (regex/dependency check for "not"/"n't"/"no"/"never"/"lack of"/"without" plus polarity-flipping verb pairs like increase/decrease) that SETS the relation-polarity slot in (c), letting the already-specified CI settle dynamics do the actual staged suppression -- this is the brain-faithful division of labor (detection is a rule, suppression is a dynamic), not one overloaded vector operation.

**(c) A bound RELATION-POLARITY slot in the fact (supports/contradicts) -- PRIMARY RECOMMENDATION, most brain-faithful per all 3 lit-scans converging.** Concretely: before `_ci_two_phase` builds `FC`/`FF` from raw cosine, compute an OVERRIDE matrix `POL[i,j] in {-1, 0, +1}` (or a small signed magnitude) from three already-available, zero-new-dependency sources, in priority order: (i) **WorldTree tablestore relation type** -- per `notes/ingestion_learn_sleep_loop_2026-07-24.md`'s own already-computed Easy-vs-Challenge fact-type distribution, IFTHEN and COUPLEDRELATIONSHIP rows are already identified as more prevalent in Challenge (IFTHEN 8.2 vs 6.6, COUPLEDRELATIONSHIP 5.5 vs ~0) and are EXPLICITLY DIRECTIONAL relations ("if X then Y," "as X up Y down") -- these should set edge sign directly from the relation's own semantics (a rule fact IFTHEN(X,Y) creates a POSITIVE edge to any choice matching Y when the question asserts X, and this is naturally extendable to a NEGATIVE edge to any choice matching NOT-Y or an antonym of Y); (ii) **WordNet antonym lookup** between fact content-words and choice content-words (same lemma objects, `.antonyms()`, already in the loop -- zero new dependency); (iii) **negation-cue detection** per (b) on fact/choice text. Where no rule fires, FALL BACK to raw cosine (honestly reported as the known-limited default case, per Q1). This is a direct implementation of Kintsch's own documented mechanism -- his negative weights come from "the knowledge net" MARKING specific propositions mutually exclusive, i.e. SUPPLIED, not derived from a generic similarity metric -- so this recommendation is not a deviation from the already-adopted CI framework, it is literally what CI always required and what the encoder never supplied.

**(d) Competition/inhibition at the settle layer (dynamic) rather than static encoding** -- ALREADY SPECIFIED in the prior 07-24 drill (Kintsch relaxation, choice-choice mutual inhibition, positive-only ablation as the fair must-fail control) and does not need re-derivation; this drill's finding is that (c) is the missing INPUT to that already-correct dynamic, not a replacement for it.

**(e) Directional CAUSE/IFTHEN rules (WorldTree)** -- folded into (c)(i) above as the highest-priority, cheapest, already-typed polarity source; this is real, unused, structured data sitting in the existing ingestion pipeline, and per the `ingestion_learn_sleep_loop` note's own prior diagnosis ("a rule fact should create a SUPPORT edge to the answer it implies, not just static word-support... VET criterion: on Challenge misses, is it retrieving+applying conditional/coupled rules, or falling for the lure?") this is independently identified, from a different angle, as the single highest-value fix for exactly the Challenge/surface-lure problem this drill was triggered by.
