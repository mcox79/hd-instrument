## HEADLINE

The brain does not defeat the additive-store capacity wall by making the store bigger or the codes sparser alone — it defeats it by **changing which problem the store has to solve at query time**. Four brain mechanisms compound into one answer: (1) the hippocampus stores a **sparse INDEX/pointer, not the content** (Teyler-Rudy indexing theory) — so the thing that gets superposed and must survive crosstalk is a small, fixed-cardinality pointer set, never the full semantic content; (2) **systems consolidation into cortical schema** (McClelland-McNaughton-O'Reilly; Tse et al.) means that as the store matures, most queries are answered by **projecting onto a low-dimensional learned schema manifold**, not by decoding a superposition at all — schema-consistent items are assimilated near one-shot once the schema exists, at a cost that does not grow with total store size; (3) **expand-then-sparsify pattern separation** (dentate gyrus; Willshaw-net asymptotic capacity theory) shows that a store's absolute crosstalk-free capacity scales as `~N / (a·ln(1/a))` in coding level `a`, diverging as `a -> 0`, provided absolute active count is kept high via proportional expansion — this is a capacity LEVER, not a scale-invariance mechanism, and it composes with (4); (4) **modular/small-world community structure of semantic memory**, with the hippocampus itself learning to carve continuous experience into discrete community-scoped chunks (Schapiro et al.) and a coarse-to-fine long-axis gradient for gist-vs-detail (Poppenk et al.), means any single reasoning episode only ever activates a **bounded-size local community**, so the crosstalk-relevant `V` in `M < N/(2 ln V)` is the *activated-community size*, not the *total store size* — this is the one true scale-invariance mechanism among the four, and it is what should be built first.

Composed together and mapped onto certified substrate primitives: index-not-content (1) is a direct extension of the already-CERTIFIED correlation-hurts-store-capacity law (decouple retrieval-semantic codes from store/pointer codes — the hippocampal index IS that decoupled, near-orthogonal pointer space); schema consolidation (2) is a direct extension of the already-CG'd `replay_cycle` CLS primitive, reframed from "prevents forgetting" to "shrinks the fraction of queries that ever touch the raw superposed store"; expand-then-sparsify (3) is a direct re-application of the already-drilled DG-expansion law (2026-07-04) from the *encoder resolution* problem to the *store-scaling* problem; and community-bounded retrieval (4) is the one genuinely new mechanism for this substrate — a two-stage coarse-community-select then fine-index-decode retrieval architecture. Per lit-scan calibration discipline, all four threads are deflated 0.15-0.25 below naive lit-scan confidence; mechanism (4), being the only one with no existing substrate precedent, is capped at the 0.50 novel-synthesis ceiling.

## Cheap decisive test

**Single sharpest kill-test:** does end-to-end retrieval/reasoning fidelity stay FLAT as total store size `V` grows 10-100x, when the store is organized with (1)+(4) composed (index-pointer store + community-bounded two-stage retrieval), against a must-collapse dense-additive control that has no such structure.

**Design (cheap — synthetic, CPU, no real KB ingest needed to get the decisive signal):**
- Build a synthetic bipolar/HD codebook KB at `V in {580, 2900, 29000, 58000}` (baseline, 5x, 50x, 100x), organized with ground-truth community structure: `sqrt(V)` communities of `sqrt(V)` items each (small-world-consistent scaling), items within a community share a common low-rank "schema" component (simulates schema-consistent content), a fixed fraction (~10%) are schema-violating outliers (simulates novel/surprising items that must go through the raw hippocampal-index path).
- **CONTROL arm (must-collapse dense-additive):** single flat HRR/Hebbian superposition bundle over ALL `V` items; decode is a global order-statistic argmax over the whole codebook (current mechanism, extrapolated as-is). This is the arm that should reproduce the `M < N/(2 ln V)` prediction and visibly degrade as `V` grows — its purpose is a saturation-vacuous-smoke guard: if this control does NOT degrade, the harness isn't exercising the crosstalk regime and the test is void (per `[[feedback-saturation-vacuous-smoke]]`).
- **TREATMENT arm (index + community):** two-stage retrieval — (a) coarse community-gist codebook of size `sqrt(V)` (near-orthogonal community pointers, cosine-selects the right community in `O(sqrt(V))` comparisons or `O(log V)` if organized as a tree), then (b) fine decode restricted to the `sqrt(V)`-item community bundle only, using the existing peel/SIC readout for any residual multi-item collision within that bounded bundle.
- **Reasoning-chain variant:** repeat with a 2-3 hop composed query (matches the certified reasoning-loop's multi-hop composition primitive) so the test also exercises whether crosstalk compounds across hops when community-routing is used at each hop vs. globally.
- Paired seeds (>=3), matched query difficulty across `V` settings, cv <= 0.15.

**HARD-PASS threshold:** TREATMENT's fidelity-vs-`log(V)` slope is statistically indistinguishable from zero (relative degradation < 10% from `V=580` to `V=58000`), WHILE CONTROL shows >= 30% relative degradation over the same range, consistent in direction and rough magnitude with the `M < N/(2 ln V)` prediction. This is the joint condition — TREATMENT flat AND CONTROL collapses — required to claim the community/index mechanism is doing the work (not that the test scale is simply too small to show collapse anywhere).

**HARD-FAIL threshold:** TREATMENT's degradation slope is NOT statistically distinguishable from CONTROL's (within seed noise) — meaning community-routing + index-decoupling does not decouple effective-`V` from total-`V` on this substrate's actual codebook geometry (e.g., communities leak crosstalk into each other via shared schema components, or the coarse-select step itself degrades with `V` at a similar rate). This would mean the substrate must treat the crosstalk wall as a hard scaling limit requiring raw capacity engineering (bigger `N`, not structural workaround) for any KB beyond ~10x current scale.

**MIDDLE_BAND:** TREATMENT degrades at a materially slower rate than CONTROL (e.g. <= 50% of CONTROL's relative degradation) but is not flat — partial mechanism; route to a v2 sweep on community size and a second hierarchy level (community-of-communities, matching the hippocampal long-axis gradient) before concluding either direction.

## Falsifiable predictions (full list)

1. **PRED-1 (index vs content):** a two-tier store where Tier-1 (pointer/index, near-orthogonal, fixed small cardinality per query) is decoupled from Tier-2 (content, schema-structured, NOT superposed) will show retrieval fidelity that depends on the size of the *active working set of pointers* (bounded, e.g. <= 10-20 concurrently bound), not on total store size `V`. HARD-PASS: fidelity delta between `V=580` and `V=58000` at fixed working-set size <= 5%. HARD-FAIL: fidelity delta tracks the dense-store prediction (i.e., no benefit from decoupling).
2. **PRED-2 (schema assimilation cost):** the fraction of queries requiring the "expensive" raw hippocampal-index path (vs. cheap schema-projection) should SHRINK as the store matures/consolidates, even as raw `V` grows — measurable as "fraction of items >= N replay cycles old that resolve via schema-only lookup." HARD-PASS: this fraction is monotonically increasing with replay cycles and, at steady state, >= 70% of items resolve schema-only regardless of `V`. HARD-FAIL: the schema-only-resolvable fraction is flat near 0% regardless of replay count (schema mechanism inert on this substrate's codes) — this would directly contradict the c1 CLS finding (2026-06-22, see synthesis below) that codebook-NN cleanup is unexpectedly robust, so a HARD-FAIL here would need reconciling against that prior result specifically, not dismissed as ordinary noise.
3. **PRED-3 (expand-then-sparsify capacity law):** absolute crosstalk-free item capacity of a sparse index scales with coding level `a` roughly as `~1/(a·ln(1/a))` (Willshaw-net asymptotic form), so an index sparsified to `a=2%` should tolerate an order of magnitude more items before collision-onset than the current substrate's `a≈12.5%` operating point (K512/4096, per the 2026-07-04 DG drill), PROVIDED absolute active count is held constant via proportional dimension expansion. HARD-PASS: at matched absolute active count, `a=2%` index shows >= 5x the crosstalk-free item capacity of `a=12.5%`. HARD-FAIL: capacity scales with `a` alone (fraction) rather than with absolute active count (would falsify the "expansion, not just sparsification" reading of the DG mechanism, contradicting the already-grounded 2026-07-04 finding — treat any such result as a discriminator sanity failure to re-check first, not an automatic reversal).
4. **PRED-4 (community-bounded reasoning, the load-bearing new claim):** a reasoning chain composed entirely within one community (all hops share a community) should show flat fidelity as total-KB-`V` grows; a reasoning chain that must CROSS communities (hop 1 in community A, hop 2 requires a fact stored in community B) should show a fidelity cost proportional to the number of community-crossings, independent of total `V`. HARD-PASS: within-community chain fidelity flat (<=10% degradation, `V` 580->58000); cross-community cost is a FIXED per-crossing penalty, not a `V`-scaling penalty. HARD-FAIL: within-community chains ALSO degrade with total `V` (meaning the community boundary isn't actually isolating crosstalk — the coarse-select step itself is leaking global interference into the "isolated" community bundle).

## Thread-by-thread neurobiology (deep, brain-first)

### 1. Hippocampal INDEXING theory — Teyler & Rudy

Teyler & DiScenna (1986), updated as the "hippocampal indexing theory" (Teyler & Rudy 2007), and extended by Moscovitch's multiple-trace theory (Moscovitch et al. 2016): the hippocampus does not store episodic content. It stores a **sparse index** — a compressed, largely arbitrary/pattern-separated code (built via DG/CA3, thread 3 below) that is bound, via strong CA3-to-cortex and entorhinal-cortex loop connections, to whatever distributed set of cortical modules was active during the original experience. Retrieval works by re-activating the index, which then pattern-completes the FULL distributed cortical activity that was present at encoding — "index" in the literal computer-science sense: a small key that resolves to a large record stored elsewhere, not the record itself.

Why this sidesteps the additive-store capacity wall directly: the object that gets superposed and must survive crosstalk at scale is the INDEX, not the content. An index only needs enough entropy to be *discriminative among currently-relevant candidates* — it does not need to encode the full semantic richness of what it points to. Its cardinality can therefore be held roughly constant (or grow far more slowly than `V`) even as the total content store grows arbitrarily large, because content lives in cortex at a completely different (distributed, non-superposed, structured) representational format. This is the load-bearing distinction the prompt's crosstalk-wall framing misses if it treats "the store" as one undifferentiated bundle: biology never superposes the content itself into one bundle — it superposes only *pointers*, and looks the content up structurally.

**Substrate mapping:** this is a direct, almost literal, extension of the already-CERTIFIED correlation-hurts-associative-store-capacity law (`reference_correlation_hurts_associative_store_capacity_decouple_from_retrieval_2026-07-08`, confirmed via the ConceptNet semantic-seeded-beam HONEST-NEGATIVE + RANDOM_BEAM parity result): that law already says "keep store/composition codes near-orthogonal, keep semantic-correlated codes for retrieval only, decouple the two." Hippocampal indexing theory is the biological reason this decoupling exists AND gives the missing second half: the near-orthogonal store-codes should be **pointers into a separate structured content store**, not a second copy of the content itself superposed a different way. The substrate currently has the decorrelated-store half; it does not yet have the "content lives elsewhere, structurally, not superposed" half.

P (deflated, this specific mechanism reading is correct and buildable): ~0.45. Raw lit confidence in indexing theory itself is high (~0.70, it is textbook-level well-supported by lesion, connectivity, and computational-modeling evidence) — deflated for: (a) the specific quantitative claim that index cardinality can be held near-constant as `V` grows 10-100x is an extrapolation past what any biological study directly measured (biological "V" — lifetime episodic count — is not manipulated experimentally at this dynamic range); (b) building "content lives elsewhere, structurally" on an HD substrate is a nontrivial new component (a schema/graph-structured content store), not a drop-in change.

### 2. Systems consolidation into cortical SCHEMA — McClelland-McNaughton-O'Reilly; Tse & Morris

Complementary Learning Systems theory (McClelland, McNaughton & O'Reilly 1995) explains WHY there must be two stores at all: a fast hippocampal system that can bind arbitrary new associations in one shot (high plasticity, high interference risk) and a slow cortical system that extracts SLOWLY, over many interleaved replay exposures, the regular structure across many episodes into a stable, generalizing "schema." The key move for the scale question specifically is Tse, Langston, Kakeyama, Bethus, Spooner, Wood, Witter & Morris ("Schemas and memory consolidation," Science 2007; and the 2011 Science follow-up on rapid schema-dependent learning): once a schema exists (the rats' spatial-flavor-place associative framework, built over weeks), a BRAND NEW item that is consistent with that schema can be consolidated into cortex in as little as 48 hours — and in the 2011 follow-up, essentially a single trial — instead of the normal weeks-long systems-consolidation timeline. The mechanism proposed (and supported by immediate-early-gene / lesion evidence) is that a mature schema provides a low-dimensional set of "slots" (a manifold of expected relations) that a new, schema-consistent item can be assimilated INTO directly, cortically, with only minimal or no hippocampal involvement — it is not stored as a new arbitrary pattern requiring slow interleaved replay to avoid interference; it lands in a place in representational space the network already has structured connectivity for.

Why this attacks the scale wall specifically: as a knowledge store matures, an increasing FRACTION of new content is schema-consistent (this is close to definitionally true of any KB with real semantic structure — most new facts about an already-well-represented entity are typical/expected, not surprising). Each schema-consistent item's storage cost does not scale with total store size `V` — it is a small update within an already-learned, fixed-dimensionality manifold. Only the shrinking residual of schema-violating (surprising/novel) items must pay the expensive raw hippocampal-index cost. This is a genuinely different mechanism from indexing theory (thread 1) — thread 1 says "don't superpose content, superpose pointers"; thread 2 says "most content, over time, stops needing ANY superposition at all, because it is representable as coordinates on a fixed low-dimensional learned surface."

**Substrate mapping — and a cross-thread surprise worth flagging directly.** The substrate already has `hdlab/continual.py::replay_cycle`, CG'd/MEASURED_MECHANISM at +0.57 drift reduction, and the c1_cls_replay_continual_ingest cell (2026-06-22) delivered an HONEST_NEGATIVE that is, read correctly, actually informative here: task-A recall did NOT collapse even at alpha=0.5 (10 disjoint tasks, ~2050 items) under codebook-NN cleanup — "the codebook-NN's 'snap to nearest of ~2050 candidate value vectors' is FAR more forgiving" than raw Hebbian-superposition readout. A discrete codebook-argmax readout is, structurally, a crude schema — it constrains the decode to a small FIXED set of admissible answers (the codebook), which is exactly the kind of "low-dimensional manifold the new item lands on" schema-consolidation predicts. This reframes that 2026-06-22 result: it was not "the substrate got lucky and didn't forget," it may be indirect evidence that ANY discretizing/constraining readout structure (codebook-NN being the crudest possible schema: a flat unstructured set of candidates) already buys real scale-robustness, and a genuine schema (structured, relational, not just "pick nearest of a flat candidate list") should buy substantially more. This is a testable refinement of that prior result, not a new claim from nothing.

P (deflated): ~0.40. Raw confidence in the biological claim is high (~0.65 — Tse et al. is a strong, causally-manipulated, twice-replicated Science-tier result). Deflated for: (a) building an actual schema-manifold-projection encoder (not just a flat codebook) is new substrate machinery, closer to the still-open Spoke-3-style CLS composition work (`design_stage2_concept_encoder_spoke3...`, P_CG there was itself only ~0.10 post-deflation for the full DG/CA3/consolidation composition) than to something already proven; (b) the "most new content is schema-consistent" assumption needs to be verified against the ACTUAL ingested KB's structure (real KBs may have a heavier surprising/long-tail fraction than the idealized synthetic-corpus assumption).

### 3. Sparse coding / pattern separation capacity math at scale — Willshaw net; Tsodyks-Feigelman; dentate gyrus expansion

The classical asymptotic result for binary associative/content-addressable memories (Willshaw, Buneman & Longuet-Higgins 1969; formalized further by Palm, and by Golomb, Rubin & Sompolinsky 1990 for the finite-noise regime; Tsodyks & Feigelman 1988 and Amit & Fusi 1994 for sparse Hopfield-style attractor nets) is that the maximum number of patterns storable before crosstalk-driven collapse, per synapse, does **not** peak at moderate/dense coding levels — it is maximized as the coding level `a` (fraction of active units) goes to zero, scaling roughly as `1/(a·ln(1/a))`, diverging in the sparse limit, versus the flat ~0.14 patterns/synapse ceiling of dense (a=0.5) Hopfield-style storage. The catch, and the reason biological sparsity settles at "a few percent" rather than the theoretical zero-limit, is that making `a` smaller while holding total dimension `N` fixed drives the ABSOLUTE number of active units toward zero too, which destroys resolution/SNR at readout (too few active units to distinguish a correct completion from noise) — this is exactly the resolution-vs-separation distinction already nailed down in the 2026-07-04 in-substrate DG drill (`research_drill_dentate_gyrus_pattern_separation_resolution_at_2pct_2026-07-04.md`): "separation comes from the low FRACTION, resolution comes from the ABSOLUTE active count, kept high by EXPANSION." The dentate gyrus resolves this exact tension biologically by fanning EC's ~200k inputs out to DG's ~1-plus-million granule cells (a 5-10x expansion) BEFORE sparsifying to ~1-4% active — this keeps absolute active count in the tens of thousands even at extreme fractional sparsity, landing near the Willshaw-optimal capacity/synapse regime without sacrificing resolution.

Why this is a capacity LEVER for the scale question, not by itself a scale-invariance mechanism: it changes the constant in `M < N/(2 ln V)` favorably (bigger effective `N` per unit of stored information, at optimal sparsity), but it does not change the fact that capacity still eventually saturates as `V` grows without bound — it buys headroom, proportional to how much dimension-expansion budget is available, not unlimited scale-invariance. Its correct role in the composed answer is: apply proportional expand-then-sparsify to whatever index/pointer structure carries the store (thread 1), so that as total `V` grows the operating point stays near the Willshaw-optimal sparsity band (empirically ~1-4%, matching DG) rather than drifting toward the substrate's current, much denser, 12.5% operating point (K512/4096) that the 2026-07-04 drill already flagged as capacity-inefficient.

**Substrate mapping:** this is the DG-expansion lever from 2026-07-04 (P_deflated there was 0.42, LEVER 1 in that note: expand `total_dim` to 8192-32768 to bring K512 into the 1.5-3.1% sparsity band), now re-applied one level up — from "the retrieval-distillation head's own resolution" to "the KB-scale pointer/index dimension." Concretely: as ingested-KB `V` grows 10-100x, the index/pointer dimension should be expanded roughly in proportion (or sub-linearly, per the Willshaw asymptotic form) to hold sparsity in the empirically-optimal 1-4% band, rather than holding dimension fixed and letting effective density rise with `V`.

P (deflated): ~0.40. This reuses an already partially-validated lever (2026-07-04's own P was 0.42, itself already deflated) one level up in scope, so confidence transfers reasonably well, but the specific claim "proportional expansion keeps the operating point Willshaw-optimal as `V` scales 10-100x" is untested at that dynamic range and the expansion-cost budget (memory, compute) at 100x scale is a real engineering constraint the 2026-07-04 drill did not have to consider at its scale.

### 4. Modularity / small-world community structure of semantic memory — the scale-invariance mechanism

This is the thread that most directly answers "how does the brain keep `V_effective` small even as total `V` grows," and it is the one with the least existing substrate precedent.

Real semantic/associative memory is not a uniform random graph — it has strong **small-world** structure (Watts & Strogatz 1998 for the general graph-theoretic result; Steyvers & Tenenbaum 2005, "The Large-Scale Structure of Semantic Networks," directly measuring word-association and WordNet-style networks and finding high local clustering plus short average path length, with a heavy-tailed/scale-free-like degree distribution — a small number of highly-connected hub concepts and a large number of weakly-connected peripheral ones). Critically, this is not just a static description of the graph — the hippocampus itself actively **learns to carve continuous experience into discrete community-scoped chunks**: Schapiro, Rogers, Cordova, Turk-Browne & Botvinick ("Neural representations of events arise from temporal community structure," Nature Neuroscience 2013) show that when people are exposed to a continuous sequence generated by an underlying graph with community structure (dense local clusters, sparse cross-cluster edges), hippocampal representations spontaneously reorganize to reflect the LATENT community boundaries — items within a community become represented as MORE similar to each other, items across a community boundary become LESS similar, even when local transition statistics alone don't reveal where the boundary is. This is pattern separation and completion being deployed selectively AT community boundaries, not uniformly across the whole graph — the hippocampus is actively discovering and exploiting the graph's modularity to organize its own indexing.

Layered on top of within-community clustering, the hippocampus additionally has a **coarse-to-fine long-axis gradient**: Poppenk, Evensmoen, Moscovitch & Nadel ("Long-axis specialization of the human hippocampus," 2013) and Collin, Milivojevic, Doeller et al. (2017, "Memory hierarchies map onto the hippocampal long axis") show posterior hippocampus represents fine-grained, detailed, small-scale spatial/relational structure while anterior hippocampus represents coarser, larger-scale, gist-level structure — a genuine multi-resolution hierarchy, not a flat partition. A complementary cortical-hub literature (Patterson, Nestor & Rogers 2007, "Where do you know what you know? The representation of semantic knowledge in the human brain" — the anterior-temporal-lobe hub-and-spoke model) adds a second flavor of the same idea at the cortical-schema level: a single amodal convergence hub integrates gist across all modality-specific "spokes," so a coarse cross-domain similarity judgment can be resolved at the hub level without ever touching the full high-dimensional spoke-level detail.

Why this is the actual scale-invariance mechanism, distinct from levers 1-3: in `M < N/(2 ln V)`, `V` is "the size of the codebook the decode has to discriminate against." If retrieval is a single flat operation against the WHOLE store, `V` = total KB size, and it grows without bound as the KB grows. If retrieval is routed — first a coarse community/gist-level selection (against a MUCH smaller codebook of community-pointers, sized like `sqrt(V)` or `log V` depending on the branching structure, not `V` itself), THEN a fine decode restricted to just that community's bundle — the `V` that matters for the fine decode's crosstalk math is the COMMUNITY size, which can be held roughly constant (or grow only logarithmically) by adding MORE communities as the KB grows, rather than growing each community's size. This converts an ever-growing `V` into a `V` the design can hold fixed by construction. Buzsaki's global/rate remapping findings (place cells wholesale reassigning their tuning when spatial/task context changes) are the biological analog of "hard-switching which community is active" — reasoning in a new context doesn't gradually blend the whole store, it snaps to a different bounded working subset.

**Substrate mapping — this is the new mechanism, not a re-application of an existing certified primitive.** No existing substrate cell builds two-stage coarse-community-select-then-fine-decode retrieval. It composes cleanly with everything else in this note: the coarse community-gist codebook is exactly the kind of small, near-orthogonal pointer set indexing theory (thread 1) already motivates; the community-gist vectors themselves are natural candidates for schema-manifold coordinates (thread 2); each community's fine-decode bundle should itself be built at Willshaw-optimal sparsity (thread 3); and — the direct link to the reasoning-loop mechanism inventory (`research_neural_reasoning_loop_mechanism_inventory_2026-07-08.md`) — the "cue generator constructs a query per-step, biased by the current subgoal context" (that note's thread 1/5) is a natural place to ALSO carry "which community is currently active," so a multi-hop reasoning chain that stays within one community pays a flat cost regardless of total KB size, while a chain that must cross communities pays a bounded per-crossing cost (PRED-4 above) rather than a `V`-scaling cost. The certified peel/SIC readout is unaffected in its own math — it is simply applied to a much smaller (community-scoped) bundle instead of the global one, which is exactly what keeps its own crosstalk terms bounded as total `V` grows.

P (deflated): ~0.35, the lowest of the four threads, capped at the 0.50 novel-synthesis ceiling regardless. Raw lit confidence in the biological phenomenon itself is solid (~0.60 — Schapiro et al. is a real, replicated finding; small-world semantic-network structure is well-established structural fact, not a contested claim). Deflated heavily for substrate-implementation risk: (a) this requires building an entirely new component (community-gist codebook + routing step) with no existing substrate precedent to lean on, unlike threads 1-3; (b) it requires the ingested KB to ACTUALLY have exploitable community structure at the scale tested — real KBs (ConceptNet-style) do have this structure, but the substrate has not yet measured its modularity/clustering coefficient directly, so this is an assumption pending verification, not a confirmed substrate property; (c) the coarse-select step introduces its OWN new decode/crosstalk problem (selecting among `sqrt(V)` community pointers) that must itself stay resolution-adequate as `V` grows — the mechanism could in principle just move the crosstalk wall up one level rather than removing it, which is precisely what PRED-4's cross-community-cost falsifier is designed to catch.
