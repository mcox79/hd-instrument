## THE TEST CASE

**"Tom worked all summer to buy a bicycle, but he wasted his savings."**

A child comprehender must, in order, roughly: (1) parse clause 1 into a GOAL (bicycle-acquisition) held by an AGENT
(Tom), reached via an ATTEMPT (worked all summer) that implicitly produces a MEANS (savings — never stated, a
bridging inference from "worked" -> "earned money"); (2) register "but" as a signal that clause 2 will violate the
expectation clause 1 set up; (3) parse clause 2 into an EVENT — waste(Tom, his-savings) — resolving "he"/"his" back
to Tom; (4) know that "wasted" denotes consuming/losing a resource WITHOUT producing an offsetting benefit; (5)
value that event as bad; (6) bind that valuation to the standing goal — not merely "goal not yet met" but "the
MEANS toward the goal was destroyed," a stronger, more specific THWARTED reading than simple non-attainment. This
document traces exactly which brain component does which of these six steps, where each one's information comes
from, and where a text-only, ungrounded system would and would not succeed.

## COMPONENT-BY-COMPONENT TRACE

### 1. Anterior temporal lobe (ATL) transmodal hub + hub-and-spoke architecture

**Represents:** an amodal convergence point per concept ("waste," "savings," "bicycle," "work," "Tom") that a
concept's modality-specific features (spokes) feed into and that composition/similarity machinery reads out from.
**Neural code/format:** NOT a discrete feature list. Cox, Rogers, Shimotake, Kikuchi, Kunieda, Miyamoto,
Takahashi, Matsumoto, Ikeda & Lambon Ralph (2024, *Imaging Neuroscience*; bioRxiv 2022.10.27.514039 — HIGH,
directly fetched per the prior similarity audit) decoded vATL ECoG during picture naming with
representational-similarity-**learning**: continuous multidimensional target matrices fit vATL patterns 6-13x
better than binary present/absent feature matrices; multiple orthogonal semantic dimensions are decodable
simultaneously per concept, ~200-400ms post-stimulus. Patterson, Nestor & Rogers (2007, *Nat. Rev. Neurosci.*
8:976) is the canonical hub-and-spoke review establishing ATL as the transmodal integration site, evidenced by
semantic dementia's amodal, graded degradation pattern (a single hub lesion degrades ALL modalities of a concept
together, not one at a time). **How it computes:** modality-specific spokes (fusiform/vision, STG/audition,
motor/premotor/action) converge onto ventral ATL; Chen, Lambon Ralph & Rogers (2017, *Nat. Hum. Behav.* 1:0039)
show computationally that the amodal hub representation is not a stipulated layer but *whatever the network
converges to* as the bottleneck sufficient to translate between disparate spokes — convergence forces abstraction.
**Developmental origin:** built via Barsalou's accumulation mechanism (Barsalou & Wiemer-Hastings 2005 lineage,
HIGH, direct quote: "an increasingly entrenched associative network emerges... accumulating the aggregate results
of superimposing information on relevant neural systems time after time") — i.e. repeated grounded co-activation,
consolidated fast-hippocampal -> slow-cortical via replay (Complementary Learning Systems, McClelland, McNaughton &
O'Reilly 1995, MEDIUM). Never hand-authored; the feature ONTOLOGY itself is statistically learned (Colunga & Smith
2005's demonstration that the "shape bias" tracks real child-directed-vocabulary statistics, HIGH). **Role in test
case:** each content word ("waste," "savings," "bicycle") activates a hub node whose downstream value depends
entirely on what its spokes deliver — this component supplies the CONVERGENCE POINT, not the content. It is not
where "waste=bad" lives; it is where "waste," however it eventually gets valenced, gets bound into one addressable
concept.

### 2. The grounded spokes — sensorimotor, affective/valence, and how abstract/evaluative words ground

**Represents:** the modality-specific feature channels that feed the hub. For a concrete word like "bicycle":
visual-form (fusiform), motor/manipulation (premotor) — Pulvermüller (2005, *Nat. Rev. Neurosci.* 6:576,
"Brain mechanisms linking language and action" — MEDIUM, well-established, not independently re-fetched this
session) is the canonical evidence that action-word comprehension recruits somatotopic motor cortex. For an
evaluative/abstract word like "waste," the load-bearing spoke is NOT sensorimotor — it is the **affective spoke**:
OFC/insula/amygdala coding of benefit/harm/valence/arousal, one of Binder, Conant, Humphries, Fernandino, Simons,
Aguilar & Desai's (2016, *Cognitive Neuropsychology* 33:130 — HIGH, per the feature-acquisition audit) 14
experiential domains. **How abstract/evaluative words ground, precisely:** Kousta, Vigliocco, Vinson, Andrews &
Del Campo (2011, *JEP:General* — HIGH, fetched) Affective Embodiment Hypothesis: abstract words carry
disproportionately higher emotional valence than concrete words at matched frequency, and this affective content
explains RESIDUAL processing advantages after controlling for imageability — genuinely **contested** (Paivio 2013
dual-coding rebuttal, flagged in the feature-acquisition audit as a live controversy, not settled). Ponari,
Norbury & Vigliocco (2018, *Language, Cognition and Neuroscience* — not independently re-fetched this session,
MEDIUM, well-known in the developmental literature, cited by the task brief itself): valence is a STRONGER
predictor of children's word-acquisition age than concreteness/imageability for abstract vocabulary specifically —
i.e. kids learn emotionally-loaded abstract words (love, hate, fun, bad) EARLIER, relative to their abstractness,
than emotionally-neutral abstract words (idea, fact) — direct evidence affect is a PRIMARY, early-available
scaffold for abstract-word acquisition, arguably more available to a young child than fine sensorimotor grounding
for a word like "waste." **Developmental origin:** the affective/interoceptive system matures very early relative
to sensorimotor-conceptual sophistication (see Component 3/4's developmental findings below — differentiated
negative affect is present by 4-7 months) — meaning for THIS class of word, affective grounding is available to
the child developmentally BEFORE most of the word's eventual symbolic/economic content is acquired. **Role in test
case:** "wasted" does not ground primarily through vision or motor cortex (there is no canonical visual form of
"waste") — it grounds through the affective spoke: the word's meaning is anchored to something like the felt
badness of loss, not to a picture or a motor program.

### 3. OFC / vmPFC / ventral striatum — outcome valuation, reward common-currency

**Represents:** a domain-general, content-blind scalar VALUE assigned to whatever composed content arrives,
regardless of source. Padoa-Schioppa & Assad (2006, *Nature* 441:223; 2008 follow-up — HIGH, directly fetched by
this session's lit-scan) in macaque OFC: three dissociable cell classes — **offer-value** neurons (value of one
available good), **chosen-value** neurons (value of whichever good is selected, described in the primary text as
varying "in a rather non-specific way," i.e. abstracted from WHICH good), and separate **taste/identity** neurons
(binary, WHAT-coding). Value and identity are literally separate populations. The 2008 follow-up confirms
**menu-invariance**: a good's value-coding slope is unchanged by what else is on the menu — not re-scaled by
context, a genuine common-currency signature. Levy & Glimcher (2012, *Curr. Opin. Neurobiol.* 22:1027 — HIGH,
fetched) meta-analyze 13 human fMRI studies converging on vmPFC/OFC scaling identically across money, food, juice,
social reward, and pain-avoidance. Chib, Rangel, Shimojo & O'Doherty (2009, *J. Neurosci.* 29:12315 — HIGH,
abstract fetched): a single vmPFC region tracks value across food, non-food consumables, AND monetary gambles.
Tom, Fox, Trepel & Poldrack (2007, *Science* 315:515 — MEDIUM, triangulated) show the SAME gain-sensitive
regions monotonically DECREASE for losses — one continuous signed axis, not a dedicated "bad-detector" module;
this design is intrinsically reference-dependent (prospect-theory framing against a certain/no-loss baseline).
**How it computes — the sharpest new finding this session:** every study located values an ALREADY-IDENTIFIED,
already-categorized good (a specific juice, a specific dollar amount, a specific gamble outcome) — none tests
valuation running directly on raw lexical/word-form input. This supports a **staged architecture**: language
comprehension (Components 1/2/5/6/8) first resolves "he wasted his savings" into an abstract propositional/goal-
state representation (resource depleted, no offsetting benefit obtained); THAT composed representation, not the
words themselves, is what OFC/vmPFC assigns a signed scalar to, exactly as it would a monetary loss. This is a
genuine, if indirect, synthesis (MEDIUM confidence, no single paper proves it for linguistic material) — but it is
the load-bearing structural claim for the whole adversarial section below: **valuation is content-blind, but it is
NOT input-blind — it needs the input already turned into a proposition, which is language comprehension's job, not
valuation's.** **Developmental origin:** primary-reward valuation circuitry (OFC/ventral striatum) functions from
infancy — operant/appetitive conditioning works in newborns — but its coupling to SYMBOLIC/narrative content is a
later-maturing achievement layered on top of an ancient core (OFC is among the latest-maturing prefrontal regions
structurally, though this specific maturational timeline was not independently re-verified this session — flag
LOW-MEDIUM). **Role in test case:** once "his savings are gone, no bicycle was obtained" is composed (by Components
5/6/8, not by this one), OFC/vmPFC/striatum assign that state a NEGATIVE scalar on the same continuous axis it
would use for a real monetary loss — this is the component that actually computes "bad" as a graded, felt
magnitude rather than a symbolic label.

### 4. Dopaminergic reward-prediction-error — how value is learned and grounded from experience

**Represents:** a signed teaching/surprise signal comparing actual outcome value to a prior EXPECTATION.
**Neural code:** phasic midbrain dopamine (VTA/SNc) firing rate, broadcast to striatum/OFC/vmPFC. **How it
computes:** Schultz, Dayan & Montague (1997, *Science* 275:1593 — MEDIUM, formal TD equation triangulated via
secondary sources this session, primary PDF unparseable): delta = r(t) + V(t+1) - V(t); dopamine bursts above
baseline for POSITIVE delta (better than expected), dips below baseline for NEGATIVE delta (worse than expected or
an expected reward omitted). Crucially, this requires an existing PREDICTION V(t) to compare against — a goal
representation functions as exactly that reference point. Gläscher, Daw, Dayan & O'Doherty (2010, *Neuron* 66:585
— HIGH, abstract fetched this session): a dissociable STATE prediction error, localized to intraparietal
sulcus/lateral PFC (not ventral striatum), tracks abstract model-level transitions distinct from primary-reward PE
— direct evidence PE-computing machinery generalizes beyond raw sensory reward into abstract/cognitive-model
territory, though this session's lit-scan found no direct evidence it extends specifically to READING/narrative
content (an honest, flagged gap, not a negative finding). **Developmental origin:** the core dopaminergic RPE
mechanism functions from infancy (basic reward learning is present very early); its application to a STORY about
someone else's goal requires a further step — vicarious/simulated appraisal, i.e. the reader runs the narrative
through their OWN goal-valuation machinery as if it were their own plan, which depends on Component 7
(mentalizing) supplying the "this is Tom's goal" framing first. **Role in test case:** the goal clause sets up an
implicit expectation of success (narrative goal-attempt sequences carry a default expectation of resolution,
Trabasso & van den Broek 1985's Setting->Event->Goal->Attempt->Outcome schema, cited in the composition audit); the
outcome clause delivers a worse-than-expected resolution once Component 3 has valued it negatively — the mismatch
between expected (bicycle obtained) and actual (savings destroyed, no bicycle) IS the computational signature of
"thwarted," not merely "not yet achieved."

### 5. Hippocampus — relational/episodic binding, goal<->outcome linking, bridging inference

**Represents:** the machinery that binds the GOAL node (Tom wants a bicycle) to the OUTCOME node (savings wasted)
into one coherent episode, across a sentence boundary, with no lexical overlap beyond coreference. **Neural
code:** fast, sparse, near-orthogonalizing conjunctive coding in dentate gyrus/CA3 (pattern separation: Leutgeb et
al. 2007, *Science* 315:961; O'Reilly & Rudy 2001, *Psych. Rev.* 108:311 formalize this computationally — both
reused from the composition audit, not re-verified this session). **How it computes:** Eichenbaum's (2004,
*Neuron* 44:109) relational memory theory — hippocampus binds elements into events, events into episodes, episodes
into an inferable relational network, supporting exactly the backward retrieval this case needs: on encountering
"he wasted his savings," the reader retrieves the antecedent goal from working/episodic memory (Graesser, Singer &
Trabasso's 1994 Class-7 causal-antecedent bridging inference, *Psych. Rev.* 101:371, reused from the composition
audit — predicted ROUTINE/automatic for exactly this backward-retrieval class, per that audit's Section 3c).
**Developmental origin:** hippocampal relational-binding capacity has an early primitive form (present in some
capacity within the first year) but flexible, adult-like relational/episodic binding over longer spans continues
maturing through early-to-middle childhood (broadly consistent with the general late-maturing trajectory of
declarative/relational memory systems; not independently re-verified this session, flag MEDIUM). **Role in test
case:** this is the component that resolves "he"="Tom" and "his savings"="the money Tom's summer of work produced"
— and, more importantly, retrieves the STANDING GOAL so that the newly-valued outcome (Component 3/4) has
something to be compared against at all. Without this component, "wasted his savings" would be valued negatively
in isolation but never connected to "did Tom get his bicycle."

### 6. PFC goal-maintenance + goal-congruence evaluation

**Represents:** active, sustained maintenance of "Tom's goal = obtain a bicycle, via intact savings" across the
clause boundary, and the COMPARISON of the incoming outcome against that maintained goal. Miller & Cohen (2001,
*Annu. Rev. Neurosci.* 24:167, "guided activation theory," reused from prior in-repo drills, not re-verified this
session) is the standard account of PFC sustained top-down bias. **How it computes:** goal-congruence appraisal
(Scherer/Lazarus goal-conduciveness, already the disk-verified basis of this substrate's own
`congruence_decision()`, per `notes/deep_vet_comprehension_organ_vs_brain_2026-08-05.md`) — does the arriving
outcome state satisfy, block, or leave neutral the maintained goal. **The sharper point this drill adds:** the
correct comparison here is not simply "end-state achieved? yes/no" — it is congruence with the implicit MEANS
sub-state ("savings must remain intact until spent on the bike"). "Wasted his savings" doesn't just fail to
achieve the end state, it destroys the INSTRUMENTAL resource the goal depended on — a stronger, more specific
THWARTED reading than mere non-attainment, and this distinction can only be computed if the maintained goal
representation includes the implicit means-chain (working -> earning -> saving -> buying), not just the terminal
desired object. **Developmental origin:** primitive goal-maintenance over a short delay is present in infancy
(classic A-not-B and delayed-response paradigms, ~8-12 months); maintaining a multi-step, narrative-length,
means-end-structured goal representation is PFC-dependent and matures considerably later, continuing through
childhood (not independently re-verified this session, flag MEDIUM, well-established developmental-cognitive-
neuroscience consensus). **Role in test case:** this is where the comparison "goal vs. outcome" literally happens
— it is the computational site of "MET" vs "UNMET" vs (the sharper case here) "MET vs. actively DESTROYED."

### 7. mPFC/TPJ mentalizing — intention/goal attribution to a character

**Represents:** the attribution of an internal desire/intention state to Tom — "Tom WANTS a bicycle" — treating
his actions as goal-directed rather than a bare action sequence. **Neural code:** rTPJ, dmPFC (bilateral, rTPJ-
emphasized) — reused from the composition audit's Section 3e, not re-verified this session. **How it computes:**
constructing a model of another agent's mental state, distinct from the reader's own — this is the step that makes
"worked all summer to buy a bicycle" parse as GOAL-DIRECTED behavior at all, rather than a neutral fact sequence.
**Developmental origin:** explicit false-belief theory-of-mind (Wimmer & Perner 1983 Sally-Anne) emerges around
age 4, but a more primitive, IMPLICIT goal-attribution capacity (inferring WHAT an agent wants from HOW they act,
without full belief-reasoning) is present far earlier — Woodward (1998, *Cognition* 69:1, not independently
re-verified this session, MEDIUM, canonical finding) shows 5-6-month-old infants look longer when an agent reaches
for a NEW object at a new location than when they reach for the SAME object at a new location, i.e. infants track
the agent's GOAL (the object), not just the motion — and Fogassi, Ferrari, Gesierich, Rozzi, Chersi & Rizzolatti
(2005, *Science*, HIGH, per the feature-acquisition audit) show macaque parietal mirror neurons encode the
downstream GOAL of an action chain, prospectively, from kinematically identical initial movements — a plausible
evolutionary/developmental precursor to the later, more abstract mentalizing network. **Role in test case:** this
component is upstream of Component 6 — it supplies the very representation ("Tom desires a bicycle") that PFC then
maintains and compares the outcome against. Without it, there is no GOAL for anything downstream to be congruent
or incongruent with.

### 8. Language network (LIFG unification, pMTG lexical access) — combinatorial syntax, and its precise LIMITS

**Represents:** LIFG performs Hagoort's "Unification" — binding retrieved lexical/semantic frames into an
incrementally-built interpretive structure via sustained/reverberatory activity (Hagoort, PMC3709422, reused from
the composition audit's Section 1, HIGH); pMTG performs lexical retrieval. **How it computes composition:**
Ciapparelli, Marelli, Graves & Reverberi (2025, *Cerebral Cortex* 35:bhaf246, HIGH, directly fetched per the
composition audit) find MULTIPLICATIVE/elementwise-product composition models beat additive/vector-sum models in
real RSA, strongest in LIFG — composition is conjunctive, not summation. **Syntactic bootstrapping — what syntax
CAN deliver without grounding:** Naigles (1990, *Cognitive Development*, HIGH, well-triangulated): a novel verb's
transitive vs. intransitive frame alone flips its interpreted meaning (causal vs. non-causal), independent of any
grounded observation disambiguating it. Landau & Gleitman (1985): blind children acquire "look"/"see" on a normal
timetable from syntactic environment alone, without the matching sensory channel. Applied here: "X wasted Y"
(transitive: agent X acts destructively on patient Y) tells a learner the EVENT STRUCTURE (X did something TO Y,
Y underwent a change) even before "waste" is known to mean loss-without-benefit specifically.

**What syntax/discourse structure CANNOT deliver — the precise limit, newly drilled this session.** Lakoff (1971)
and Blakemore (1987, *Semantic Constraints on Relevance*; 1989, *Linguistics and Philosophy* 12:15; 2002,
*Relevance and Linguistic Meaning* — MEDIUM-HIGH, triangulated this session): "but" encodes **procedural**, not
conceptual, meaning — an instruction to derive an implication from clause 1 and then negate/eliminate it given
clause 2. Sanders, Spooren & Noordman (1992, *Discourse Processes* 15:1 — MEDIUM, triangulated this session) and
Mann & Thompson (1988, *Text* 8:243) formalize Contrast/Concession as a **structural POLARITY** primitive
(expectation-breaking), computed as a discourse-relation category independent, in principle, of the affective
CONTENT of the two clauses. Direct ERP confirmation this session: Xiang & Kuperberg (2015, *Language, Cognition
and Neuroscience* 30:648 — HIGH, full text fetched): a concessive connective ("even so") measurably reduces the
N400 to a real-world-inconsistent-but-connective-consistent continuation, and produces P600/sustained anterior
negativity when even the connective-primed reading is violated — direct neural evidence the brain uses a
connective to generate an ADVANCE PREDICTION about the upcoming clause. **But** — and this is the load-bearing
finding — in that same experiment the prediction operates over the relationship between TWO ALREADY-VALENCED
propositions ("failed"/"aced" carried the valence; "even so" only reweighted the probability of what comes next).
Applied to the test case: "but" reliably tells the reader "what follows will violate the success-expectation set
up by clause 1" — that much is free, zero grounding required. It is EQUALLY compatible with "...but he saved even
more than expected" (a violated-expectation-toward-positive) as with "...but he wasted his savings" — **"but"
signals that a reversal is coming, not which direction it goes.** The SIGN must come from somewhere else.

**Developmental origin:** syntactic bootstrapping is functional very early (Naigles 1990's subjects are
24-27-months-old). Adversative/concessive connective USE, by contrast, is later-acquired in child language relative
to additive ("and") and simple causal ("because") connectives — consistent with, though not independently
re-verified this session (flag LOW-MEDIUM), the broader connective-acquisition-order literature. **Role in test
case:** the language network parses structure, retrieves "wasted"'s stored entry, and "but" primes a
reversal-is-coming expectation — but per the finding above, this component structurally CANNOT deliver the
valence sign on its own; that has to come from Components 2/3 (grounded spokes -> valuation).

## THE ADVERSARIAL QUESTION

**Is the barrier really GROUNDING — or is it (a) representation-format, (b) a missing learning signal, (c) a
compositionality/inference gap, or (d) a data/exposure issue?** Arguing all sides in turn, then adjudicating.

### The strongest case FOR grounding being the wall

OFC/vmPFC valuation cells are content-blind but not input-generating — Component 3's own finding is that no study
locates valuation running on raw symbols; it needs a valence signal already attached to the input, and that signal
traces back, developmentally, to something FELT. The developmental evidence (Component 3/6/7's citations, sharpened
by this session's Lit-scan C) is decisive on timing: Stenberg, Campos & Emde (1983, *Child Development* 54:178 —
HIGH, directly verified this session) show differentiated anger to goal-blockage by 7 months via the classic
arm-restraint paradigm; Lewis, Alessandri & Sullivan (1990, *Developmental Psychology* 26:745 — HIGH, verified)
show contingency-extinction produces anger by 4-8 months; Lewis & Ramsay (2005, *Child Development* 76:518 — HIGH,
full text fetched) add PHYSIOLOGICAL (cortisol) confirmation, dissociating anger (persistence attempt) from sadness
(perceived loss of control). **"Blocked goal = bad" is grounded in a felt, endocrine-confirmed, pre-linguistic
affective response present by 4-7 months of age — many months before the child has any words at all**, let alone
"waste." The word arrives ~5+ YEARS later (Sehl, Friedman & Denison 2025, *Cognitive Development* — MEDIUM,
triangulated this session, N=514 ages 3-7: waste-minimizing judgment competence emerges reliably around age 5.5)
and simply gets ATTACHED to an already-existing felt category, not built from scratch by the word. A system that
has never had anything like this felt experience has no "there" for the word "waste" to refer to — it can only
ever manipulate a label with nothing underneath it (the classical Symbol Grounding Problem, Harnad 1990, not
independently re-verified this session but foundational and uncontested in the relevant literature). Sharpened
further by this session's distributional-affect finding (Lit-scan B): pure text co-occurrence achieves real but
BOUNDED accuracy at valence prediction (Turney & Littman 2003, *ACM TOIS* 21:315 — MEDIUM-HIGH, triangulated:
82.8% word-orientation accuracy; GloVe/skip-gram models: r=0.73-0.80 against Warriner, Kuperman & Brysbaert 2013
human valence norms) — and EVERY method that achieves this bootstraps from a small, hand-supplied seed set of
already-valenced anchor words (Turney & Littman's own 14 seeds: good/nice/excellent/positive/fortunate/correct/
superior vs. bad/nasty/poor/negative/unfortunate/wrong/inferior). This is forced by the "distributional paradox" —
antonyms are near-identical in raw co-occurrence space (they appear in the same contexts), so the SIGN cannot in
principle be recovered from distribution alone; it must be injected from outside. This is a precise, falsifiable,
mathematical argument that text distribution alone cannot ground valence — SOME grounding signal is load-bearing,
not optional, not a matter of scale.

### The strongest case AGAINST grounding being THE wall (the four alternatives)

**(a) Representation-format.** Maybe the deficit isn't that meaning must trace back to sensorimotor/affective
experience — maybe it's simply that this substrate's tags are DISCRETE SYMBOLIC LABELS (`POS_VALENCE` built
identically to `NAUTICAL`, per the similarity audit's own direct code-read finding) instead of dense, graded,
distributed vectors. The GloVe/skip-gram r=0.73-0.80 result above is nontrivial: a purely statistically-learned,
dense DISTRIBUTED representation gets most of the way to human valence judgments using nothing but text
co-occurrence plus a tiny seed. If "grounding" is functioning here as an informal proxy for "sufficiently rich,
high-dimensional, non-arbitrary representation" rather than a literal requirement for embodied causal history, the
FORMAT fix (stop using opaque symbolic tags, start using a big learned distributed vector) might do most of the
real work regardless of whether that vector's ultimate causal history touches a body.

**(b) Missing learning signal.** The substrate's tags are hand-supplied once and never updated by prediction
error — this is the OTHER half of the feature-acquisition audit's own two-part gap, and it is real independent of
grounding. Rogers & McClelland's (2004) PDP model derives graded typicality, illusory correlations, and category
structure with ZERO symbolic feature list and, notably, zero literal embodiment either — its "grounding" is just
whatever property/relation labels the training signal supplies. Applied here: an error-driven loop that predicts
STORY OUTCOMES (did the character's plan succeed or fail, from narrative cues) across a large corpus could in
principle induce a graded, valence-like hidden representation purely from getting that prediction task right many
times — no sensorimotor or interoceptive experience required, just the right training SIGNAL and enough exposure
to it.

**(c) Compositionality/inference gap — and here the substrate's own build history is decisive, disk-verified
evidence, not speculation.** The composition audit's own central finding, independently re-derived this drill:
the FHRR bind/bundle/role-keyed-register machinery is ALREADY brain-faithful (Lalisse & Smolensky 2021's
reanalysis of Frankland & Greene's fMRI data favors superposition-of-role-fillers over segregated registers,
P=0.55 deflated) — the barrier for the "good boy = goal met" class was purely the ABSENCE of a bridging-inference
mechanism connecting a later evaluative event to an earlier goal with no shared lexical content. **This was
BUILT and VALIDATED HARD_PASS end-to-end on real prose (commit 17dd3567b isolation, commit d157941c6 end-to-end,
per `audit_SYNTHESIS_semantic_meaning_barrier_2026-08-06.md`) using a hand-lexicon (`EVAL_POS`/`EVAL_NEG`) — i.e.
WITHOUT solving grounding at all.** This is a controlled, disk-verified demonstration, run inside this very
substrate, that fixing the INFERENCE layer produces real measured gains (organ owner-acc 0.50->0.60, zero
regression) completely independent of whether the underlying valence tags are grounded or hand-supplied. Grounding
and inference are empirically dissociable components, not one wall wearing two names.

**(d) Data/exposure.** Simplest alternative: maybe there's nothing deep here at all, just insufficient coverage.
The verb drill's own piloted Tier-2 similarity-extension mechanism (8/8 and 6/6 held-out) already demonstrates that
extending an already-supplied symbolic vocabulary to new words via similarity, Fazly, Alishahi & Stevenson
(2010)-style, closes real coverage gaps cheaply — this is real, but it only ever propagates EXISTING symbolic
labels further; it cannot manufacture a sign that isn't already present somewhere in the seed set (see (a)/the
distributional-paradox argument above) — so (d) reduces to (a)/(b) once you ask where the propagated signal
originally came from.

### Adjudication

**All four alternatives are correct for different SLICES of the pipeline, and treating "grounding" as one
monolithic wall is itself the error.** The sharpened, falsifiable decomposition this drill contributes:

1. **The SIGN of valence on a genuinely novel evaluative word cannot come from syntax/discourse-structure alone**
   (Component 8's "but"-boundary finding is precise and mechanistic: it delivers "reversal incoming," never
   "which direction") **and cannot come from pure text distribution with zero grounded input** (the distributional-
   paradox argument is a real, in-principle mathematical constraint, not an engineering inconvenience) — so (a)
   representation-format and (b) learning-signal fixes, however good, need SOMETHING grounded to seed from. This
   is the part of "grounding is the wall" that survives adjudication.

2. **But the grounded requirement is much SMALLER than "every word needs its own embodied history."** It reduces
   to a small set of PRIMITIVE anchor concepts — pleasure/pain, benefit/harm, goal-satisfaction/goal-blockage —
   that need to be grounded in something like reward-prediction-error/affective appraisal ONCE. This substrate
   already has a disk-verified HARD_PASS candidate for exactly this primitive layer
   (`pfc_gate_cfrpe_trained_v2`, per the feature-acquisition audit's Section 2/4). Everything ELSE — "waste,"
   "squander," "forfeit," "betray" — can, in principle, be reached from that small grounded core via (a)
   representation-format fixes (dense vectors, not opaque tags) plus (b) learning-signal fixes (similarity/
   analogy propagation, Fazly-et-al./NNSE-on-PPMI-style, already piloted at small scale) — exactly mirroring how a
   real child extends an early-grounded "goal-blocked=bad" core (present by ~7 months) to the specific word
   "waste" over the following 5-10 years, predominantly via LANGUAGE and cultural/economic-concept exposure
   (Berti & Bombi 1988; Bonn & Webley 2000, per Lit-scan C — profit not understood until ~11, banking/interest
   still consolidating at 9-14), NOT by re-grounding each new word in fresh embodied experience.

3. **(c) Compositionality/inference is a fully separate axis, and this drill's disk-verified evidence is the
   sharpest new fact here**: this substrate ALREADY shipped a real, measured win by fixing inference alone,
   leaving grounding untouched (hand-lexicon valence, HARD_PASS end-to-end). That is direct, controlled proof the
   two are dissociable — a system can score real gains on real prose from inference-fixing without grounding
   being solved, and conversely a perfectly-grounded valence signal would still fail every "good boy"-class item
   without the bridging-inference layer. Do not conflate them; do not gate one on the other.

4. **(d) is real but shallow** — it is not a fourth independent wall, it collapses into (a)/(b) once you ask
   where a propagated symbolic label's sign ultimately originated.

**Direct answer to "could it be known from language/syntax alone":** No — not fully, and Component 8 gives a
precise, mechanistic reason why (the "but"-boundary), not just an empirical trend. The BEST text-only systems
(GloVe/skip-gram, r=0.73-0.80) get impressively close, but every one of them is provably parasitic on a small
externally-supplied grounded seed (the distributional-paradox argument), so "language alone, with literally zero
grounded input anywhere in the pipeline" is not just empirically hard, it is mathematically underdetermined for
recovering sign.

## Cheap decisive test

Two small, disk-only, hand-authored-bank tests that discriminate the adjudicated "small-anchor + propagation"
architecture from the "every word needs its own grounding" and "syntax carries more than it should" alternatives:

**Test 1 — propagation from a small grounded anchor.** Build a held-out set of 15-20 OOV evaluative/outcome words
NOT in any existing hand-tagged lexicon (negative: waste, squander, spoil, forfeit, betray; positive: earn, treasure,
secure, achieve, redeem). Using ONLY the RPE-anchored core vocabulary already grounded via
`pfc_gate_cfrpe_trained_v2` (not a hand-labeled valence lexicon) as the seed, run a similarity-extension /
NNSE-on-PPMI-style propagation (the mechanism the verb-feature drill already piloted at small scale) to sign each
held-out word, and score against gold (hand judgment + Warriner-Kuperman-Brysbaert-style human norms where
available).
- **HARD-PASS:** >=75% of the held-out set correctly signed, matching or exceeding the GloVe/skip-gram
  distributional benchmark (r=0.73-0.80-equivalent), demonstrating the small-grounded-anchor architecture is
  sufficient without hand-tagging every word.
- **HARD-FAIL:** <=45% (near chance for a signed binary task) — would falsify "a small grounded core plus
  propagation suffices" and imply a much more expensive per-word grounding requirement than this drill's
  adjudication assumes.
- **MIDDLE-BAND:** 45-75%.

**Test 2 — does "but" carry sign information beyond reversal-is-coming?** Take a small hand-authored bank (N=15-20)
of goal-then-outcome pairs like the test case, in matched pairs: one version keeps the adversative connective
("...but he wasted his savings"), one replaces it with a neutral connective ("...and he wasted his savings") or
removes it, holding the outcome word fixed. Measure whether a sign-prediction mechanism's accuracy on the OUTCOME
word changes between the "but" and "and"/no-connective versions.
- **HARD-PASS (confirms Component 8's finding):** accuracy is statistically unchanged between conditions — "but"
  contributes zero sign information beyond what the lexical item itself carries, exactly as the Xiang & Kuperberg
  (2015) mechanism predicts (reversal-primed, not sign-primed).
- **HARD-FAIL:** accuracy drops meaningfully (>=0.20 absolute) when "but" is removed — would mean discourse
  structure is doing more sign-relevant work than this drill credits it for (plausibly via a learned script prior:
  "goal-narratives introduced with 'but' overwhelmingly resolve negatively," an Event-Segmentation-Theory-style
  prior, Baldassano et al. 2018, per the composition audit) — genuinely informative either way, cheap, no GPU.

## Substrate-product implications

- **Do not chase a bigger hand-tagged valence lexicon, and do not wait for a full embodied-grounding program
  before shipping open-vocab valence coverage.** The adjudicated architecture (small grounded anchor + cheap
  propagation) is buildable now from organs this substrate already owns (`pfc_gate_cfrpe_trained_v2` for the
  anchor, the already-piloted Tier-2 similarity-extension pattern for propagation) — Test 1 above is the concrete
  next increment and has not yet been run; it should be prioritized over further hand-tagging.
- **Keep the inference-layer program (bridging-inference for non-lexical evaluative constructions) and the
  grounding program on SEPARATE tracks.** The disk-verified evidence this drill leans on hardest is that
  inference-fixing alone already produced a real, measured win (owner-acc 0.50->0.60) with zero grounding
  change — do not gate the next inference build (affect-state bridging, negation-scope, per the SYNTHESIS note's
  own NEXT list) on solving grounding first, and do not treat a future grounding win as automatically fixing
  inference-shaped gaps (it will not — the "good boy" class has no valence-sign problem at all, its outcome word
  IS already known to be positive; its problem was purely the absence of a bridging path).
- **The "but"-boundary finding (Component 8) is a genuinely actionable, cheap, near-term test** (Test 2) —
  if confirmed, it rules out spending effort on discourse-connective-based valence shortcuts (they cannot carry
  sign by construction); if refuted, it opens a cheap, purely-syntactic partial signal (a script-prior: "but"
  after a goal-narrative predicts negative resolution) worth exploiting as a prior/bias term even before the
  lexical valence is resolved.
- **Sequencing implication from the developmental timeline**: per Lit-scan C's two-layer account, prioritize
  grounding/propagating words in the EARLY-available affective-core vocabulary (loss, blockage, harm, benefit —
  the ~4-7-month-grounded layer) before chasing words requiring later-maturing cultural/economic concepts (profit,
  interest, savings-as-deferred-value — not solid in children until ~9-11+) — the substrate should expect the
  SECOND class to need either more context/scaffolding or explicit compositional decomposition into the first
  class's primitives (e.g. "wasted savings" = "destroyed a stored resource obtained through effort," expressible
  via the early-grounded core) rather than being separately grounded as its own primitive.

## THE CRUX IN PLAIN TERMS

A baby learns that losing something you worked for feels bad by actually feeling it — as early as seven months
old, well before any words, a baby gets visibly angry (and their stress hormones rise) when a goal gets blocked.
The word "waste" comes along years later and just gets a label stuck onto that already-felt badness. Our program
has never felt anything like that, so when it hits the word "wasted," it has no feeling to hang the label on — it
can spot the grammatical warning sign that a twist is coming (the word "but" is like a yellow flag saying
"something's about to flip"), but nothing in it tells it which way "bad" points. The fix isn't to teach it a
feeling for every single word — it's to give it ONE small grounded sense of "loss feels bad" (which this project
has already partly built, via a reward-learning signal) and then let it reason its way from that one anchor out to
new words by similarity, the same way a real child's grasp of "waste" grows outward for years from one early gut
feeling about a blocked goal.
