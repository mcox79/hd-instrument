## HEADLINE

**The three independent lit-scans converge on the same answer from three different angles, and it is NO, with high confidence: no published non-gradient/inspectable mechanism induces genuine hierarchical/relational structure from raw linear data without a hierarchical prior/hypothesis-space/model-class being handed to it in advance.** Every credible glass-box candidate is a **selector** (picks the best tree/rule-set/cluster from a pre-given hierarchical hypothesis space) not an **inducer** (builds the concept of hierarchy from an unstructured substrate). This is not a single contested paper — it is the structural conclusion of the MDL literature, the Bayesian-program/structural-form literature, the EM-based grammar-induction literature, the iterated-learning literature, AND the direct "any credible counter-example?" search, independently. It also matches, from the outside, our own atom 29443 (representable-but-not-learned by a linear readout) and atom 29440 (linear atomize+sleep = fixed-similarity kNN): **every mechanism we and the field have tried bottoms out at the same wall** — hierarchy has to already be in the toolkit before data arrives; data only selects among what's already representable.

The one open thread that is genuinely NOT closed by the lit-scan (and is where the honest minimal test should go before conceding the tradeoff) is **ABL / ADIOS-style discrete, non-EM, non-POS-tag pattern-nesting from raw substitutability/co-occurrence statistics** — these are the two mechanisms in the entire survey that get closest to bottom-up assembly of nested structure without a hand-specified grammar template, and they are fully discrete (no continuous optimization anywhere). But the literature is equally blunt that both are **empirically fragile/data-hungry and degrade sharply on large-vocabulary, low-repetition natural text** (WSJ-scale corpora) relative to small/constrained/synthetic corpora (van Zaanen & Adriaans comparison; Solan/Horn/Ruppin/Edelman follow-up skepticism on ADIOS's stronger claims). That fragility signature is itself a specific, falsifiable, cheap thing to check on the real Linzen agreement testbed before treating the question as closed.

## 1. BRAIN: how do humans induce structure from raw input without a teacher?

**Established, uncontested base phenomenon:** infants track local, linear, adjacent transitional probabilities to segment continuous speech into chunks (Saffran, Aslin & Newport 1996; Aslin, Saffran & Newport 1998 — confirmed the tracked statistic is conditional/transitional probability specifically, not raw frequency). This is mechanistically a first-order Markov-style computation over a 1-D sequence — flat, local, no hierarchy. Nobody disputes this piece, and nobody disputes that, alone, it does not produce constituency/dependency/recursive structure.

**Chunking accounts** (Perruchet & Vinter 1998 PARSER; Christiansen & Chater 2016 "Now-or-Never bottleneck") are explicitly flat/associative, sequential chunk-formation-and-competition processes. Christiansen & Chater claim repeated/recursive chunking of chunks-of-chunks yields a "hierarchy of increasingly abstract representational formats" — but this is contested directly in a BBS commentary that argues memory-constraint chunking is empirically too weak/variable a mechanism to derive real syntactic hierarchy, and that hierarchy (if real) is more plausibly located in utterance-planning subgoal structure than in perceptual chunking. Treat "chunking → hierarchy" as an unresolved, actively disputed claim, not an established result.

**The central contested debate** (does linear/distributional learning ever yield genuine hierarchy, or is a hierarchical/structural bias required?) has real researchers on both sides, not a settled majority view:
- *For* (statistics can go far, possibly all the way, without a language-specific structural bias): Bod's U-DOP (Bod 2006) — an unsupervised algorithm that induces trees from raw strings via fragment-reuse statistics; Frank, Bod & Christiansen 2012 (*Proc. Roy. Soc. B*) — argues hierarchy is invoked "too hastily," much of language use is explicable sequentially (note: this is explicitly a minority/contrarian position by its own authors' framing, not a consensus view); Perfors, Tenenbaum & Regier 2011 (*Cognition*) — an ideal Bayesian learner with only a domain-general simplicity/MDL-like bias assigns higher posterior to hierarchical over flat grammars given ordinary child-directed speech.
- *Against* (a structural/hierarchical bias or a qualitatively different mechanism is necessary): Marcus, Vijayan, Bandi Rao & Vishton 1999 (*Science*) — 7-month-olds generalize an abstract identity/variable-binding relation (ABA vs ABB) using entirely novel syllables, which cannot be transitional-probability-based by construction; robustly replicated at the phenomenon level (Geambașu et al. 2023 multicenter replication), but the *interpretation* — does it require genuinely algebraic/symbolic computation, or a rich-enough similarity/feature space? — remains actively contested (Seidenberg & Elman 1999; Altmann & Dienes 1999 connectionist rebuttals; Rabagliati et al. 2019 meta-analysis showing the effect is stimulus-type-dependent, not a uniform "algebra module"). Poverty-of-stimulus arguments (Crain & Nakayama 1987 auxiliary-fronting) are the classic structural-bias case (see §3 below for the direct rebuttal/complication).
- **Crucially, the strongest "for" result has a self-undermining caveat, confirmed by one of its own authors:** Amy Perfors, in a later solo piece ("Bayesian Models of Cognition: What's Built in After All?", *Philosophy Compass* 2012), argues explicitly and generally that *any* Bayesian learner (including her own 2011 PTR model) can only ever **select** among hypotheses already representable in its pre-specified latent hypothesis space — it cannot manufacture a new representational primitive (like "hierarchy") that wasn't already latent in the model's architecture. Applied to her own 2011 result: the model could favor a hierarchical grammar only because a hierarchical grammar was already a candidate the model could represent and score before any data arrived. This is the single most direct and citable statement, from inside the strongest pro-induction literature, that the apparent counter-evidence is actually a selection result, not a construction result.

**Is the brain's structure-induction plausibly inspectable (glass-box) vs opaque/gradient-like?** Genuinely two-level, not a clean answer. The *computational-level description* of statistical learning (a co-occurrence/transitional-probability table, or PARSER's chunk-strength counters) is discrete and inspectable in the abstract. But the *neural implementation* is distributed, graded, largely implicit/non-reportable population activity — i.e., glass-box at the level of "what statistic is being tracked," not glass-box at the level of "how the brain computes it." No source claims MDL/Bayesian model-comparison is the actual psychologically-real mechanism running in an infant's brain — that framing (Perfors/Tenenbaum) is offered as a rational/computational-level *learnability* argument (an existence proof), not a mechanistic/neural claim. This gap between ideal-observer models and implementational mechanism is worth being honest about rather than conflating.

## 2. Glass-box (non-gradient) structure-induction prior art — survey table

| Mechanism | Hierarchical-from-scratch or hierarchical-prior-required? | Inspectable / glass-box? | Requires |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MDL compression** (de Marcken 1996; Stolcke & Omohundro 1994) | Prior-required: recursive rewrite-rule representation assumed by construction; *which* merges/rules is genuinely discovered | Mostly yes (discrete greedy merge search) — **caveat**: Stolcke & Omohundro bolt on an Inside-Outside/EM post-pass (continuous re-estimation) | Pre-given recursive grammar formalism + repeated substructure in the data to compress |
| **Bayesian structural-form / BPL** (Kemp & Tenenbaum 2008; Lake, Salakhutdinov & Tenenbaum 2015) | Prior-required, strongly: fixed hand-built menu of structural forms, or a hand-authored compositional program grammar; model selects, does not invent | Readable generative model, but **BPL explicitly uses continuous numerical parameter optimization internally** — not gradient-free in practice | Hand-designed hierarchical hypothesis space; little data needed (nearly all the work is the prior) |
| **Iterated learning** (Kirby 2001; Kirby, Cornish & Smith 2008; Griffiths & Kalish 2007) | Partially prior-required: learner's hypothesis space must already support compositional representations; meaning-space is typically already independently structured (shape x color x motion) — contested how much is "emergent" vs pre-installed | Yes for symbolic/Bayesian computational variants; **N/A for the flagship 2008 PNAS human-subject result** — the "learner" is a human brain, not an algorithm, so inspectability doesn't apply to the headline evidence | Representational capacity for composition + a transmission bottleneck + (typically) a pre-structured meaning space |
| **EM-based dependency/constituency induction** (DMV, CCM — Klein & Manning 2002/2004) | Prior-required, cleanly: tree/bracketing structure IS the generative model's definition; EM learns only continuous parameters selecting the specific tree | Caveat, not clean yes: EM is a continuous, iterative likelihood-ascent procedure, well-documented as prone to local optima/initialization sensitivity | POS tags (non-trivial supervision) + fixed generative template + treebank-scale corpora |
| **Seginer's CCL (2007)** | Prior-required: outputs a dependency-like tree by design | Yes — purely greedy, deterministic, discrete heuristics, no EM/likelihood step | Raw text, no POS tags, moderate-large corpora |
| **ADIOS** (Solan, Horn, Ruppin & Edelman 2005, PNAS) | Closest to genuine discovery: no a-priori grammar template, but the capacity to recursively re-nest its own extracted patterns is a designed feature of the search | Yes — discrete significance-test pattern extraction, no EM/gradient — **but its stronger hierarchy/generalization claims are contested in follow-up comparative work** | Large raw corpus for statistical-significance testing of recurring patterns |
| **Brown clustering** (Brown et al. 1992) | Neither — bottoms out at flat bigram statistics; output "tree" is a lexical taxonomy over word types, not syntax | Yes, fully discrete | Large corpus, pre-chosen cluster count K |
| **ABL** (van Zaanen, Alignment-Based Learning) | Best case for bottom-up assembly: nested bracketing assembled from raw substitutability evidence (Harris 1951); still assumes "valid output = single nested tree" as its output format | Yes — fully symbolic (alignment + heuristic selection), no continuous optimization anywhere | Many near-duplicate/overlapping sentence frames; **well-documented fragility/data-hunger on large-vocabulary natural corpora (WSJ-scale)** |
| **EMILE** (Adriaans) | Prior-required: explicit CFG target, theory-laden substitution-class formalism | Yes, symbolic, learnability-in-the-limit proof | Even more data-hungry than ABL; degrades on messy natural text |

Modern gradient-based unsupervised parsers (DIORA/S-DIORA, ON-LSTM, PRPN, compound PCFGs) were also surveyed and are disqualified on TWO independent grounds, not one: their model class is already a tree (inside-outside chart enumerating all binary trees, or an explicit CFG), AND they are gradient-trained. Ponvert, Baldridge & Cook 2011 (cascaded finite-state chunkers) is the closest "flat-start" system in the modern NLP literature; it still hand-builds a recursive-cascade architecture (a hierarchical assumption at the architecture level) and its output is characterized as flat multiword chunks with a default right-branching structure imposed on top — not discovered head-dependent relations — and it underperforms models that assume an explicit tree outright. The field's own systematic review (Muralidaran, Spasic & Knight, *Natural Language Engineering* 2021, 33 systems surveyed) reports that a structural inductive bias — Bayesian priors, recursion-depth constraints, modified objectives — was present in essentially every successful system.

## 3. THE HIERARCHY-FROM-LINEAR CRUX

**Direct verdict from the dedicated crux scan: NO.** No credible non-gradient/inspectable mechanism was found in the literature that induces hierarchical/relational structure from raw linear data without a hierarchical prior/hypothesis-space/pre-structured target already built in. Supporting detail:

- **Elman SRNs** (1990/1993): the famous "hierarchical" cluster-plot readings are contested (Steedman 2002) — critics argue SRNs are functionally finite-state/sequential-statistics machines whose geometry *correlates* with hierarchical training-corpus structure without *representing* head-dependent relations as such. This is the same failure signature as our own atom 29440/29443: surface statistics that mimic structure-sensitive output without instantiating structure.
- **Reali & Christiansen 2005**: plain bigram/trigram models, trained on child-directed speech, assign higher likelihood to the correct (structure-dependent) complex-question form than to the naive linear-fronting alternative — a real result, but it shows surface linear statistics can *mimic* structure-sensitive behavior in English CDS (which happens to contain enough correlated local cues), not that the model thereby represents or has induced any dependency/embedding structure. Directly analogous to the Frank/Bod/Christiansen point: behavior consistent with hierarchy does not imply hierarchy was represented, let alone induced.
- **Perfors, Tenenbaum & Regier 2011** (the best positive-looking result): re-characterized above (§1) via Perfors's own 2012 argument as selection among a pre-enumerated hypothesis space (hierarchical CFG option vs regular/flat option both already representable), not construction of hierarchy from a hierarchy-free substrate.
- **Direct search for a counter-example** (glass-box mechanism inducing head-relations from raw text, no oracle parse, no hierarchical model class assumed): none found. Every unsupervised parser surveyed (DMV, CCM, DIORA, ON-LSTM, PRPN, compound PCFGs, cascaded finite-state chunkers) is either a tree-shaped generative/architectural model class fit to data (selector), gradient-trained, or both.
- **Iterated learning** (Kirby et al.): re-examined specifically for "does it start from BOTH a flat signal space AND a flat meaning space?" — no. Kirby's own "Evolution of Meaning-Space Structure" work treats meaning-space structure as an independent variable, and the 2008 PNAS result explicitly requires the meaning space to have "certain kinds of structure" for compositional signals to win the stability competition. Iterated learning stabilizes/selects a compositional mapping when a pre-structured target already exists to align to — it is a selector/stabilizer, not an ex-nihilo inducer.

**Single best citation for the refutation, chosen deliberately from inside the strongest pro-induction camp (not an external critic):** Perfors (2012), *Bayesian Models of Cognition: What's Built in After All?*, *Philosophy Compass* — a co-author of the leading positive claim (PTR 2011) arguing generally that no learner selects a hypothesis that wasn't already representable in its pre-specified hypothesis space, applied explicitly to her own team's result.

**On the "is handing a hierarchical prior a fair TAUGHT scaffold, or is it smuggling the answer" question:** the survey suggests a real gradient of fairness, not a binary:
- DMV/CCM/BPL/Kemp&Tenenbaum hand the model POS tags and/or the *entire specific* generative structure (a dependency-tree grammar, or a stroke/part/relation program grammar) — this is close to smuggling in the answer; only parameters are learned.
- ABL/ADIOS/CCL hand the model only the much weaker assumption "the output is some well-formed nested bracketing / some recursively-composable pattern set" — no POS tags, no specific tree shape, no lexical head labels. This is structurally closer to a fair scaffold (analogous to our own program handing "position" as a scaffold in 29443) than to smuggling the head label itself.

This distinction is exactly where the minimal glass-box test below should sit: use ABL/ADIOS-style discrete pattern/bracket induction (the "weak hierarchical-capacity scaffold" tier), NOT a DMV/CCM-style hand-specified dependency-tree generative model (the "smuggled answer" tier).

## Cheap decisive test

Reuse the validated 29443 real-text Linzen agreement testbed (subject-verb agreement; head-noun vs. linearly-nearest-noun discriminator; held-out lexeme/distance split). Do NOT re-attempt a flat prototype/geometry-reshaping consolidation (already refuted per the prior consolidation note) and do NOT hand the inducer POS tags or an explicit dependency-tree generative template (that would be the "smuggled answer" tier per §3, not a real test of induction).

1. **Pre-check (resolves the residual open risk flagged in the consolidation note):** confirm whether the 29443 testbed already supplies oracle dependency-head labels or POS tags as input features. If yes, any induction result is contaminated (the "hard part" was already handed in) and this test cannot run as designed — strip those features first.
2. **Induce, ABL/ADIOS-style, from RAW TOKENS ONLY:** run a discrete, non-EM, non-gradient alignment/substitutability procedure (Harris-style substitution-class detection, à la ABL) or recursive statistical-significance pattern-nesting (à la ADIOS) over the raw training sentences. Output: a set of nested bracketings / recurring pattern hierarchies per sentence, with NO externally-supplied notion of "head" or "verb agreement" — purely a bottom-up structural output.
3. **Define a head-candidate rule from the induced structure alone** (e.g., "the head of a nested constituent is the element whose substitution class recurs across the most distinct bracketed contexts" — an inspectable, auditable rule over the induced structure, not a hand-coded position rule).
4. **Test on held-out lexeme/distance combinations** (same split as 29443): does head-tracking derived from the induced structure beat the already-measured linear positional/count baseline?
5. **Content-dependence discriminator (signature-flip test, same family as prior atoms):** (a) scramble which tokens fill which slots while preserving the induced bracket/pattern structure — should hurt badly if the mechanism genuinely uses structure; (b) separately, check induced-bracket *coverage and stability* across the full held-out set (not just accuracy where it fires) — this is the fragility check the literature specifically predicts (ABL/EMILE degrade sharply on large-vocabulary, low-repetition natural text).

## Falsifiable predictions

**HARD-PASS** (glass-box structure-induction genuinely works on real text, no invariant tradeoff needed):
- Induced-structure-based head-tracking on held-out lexeme/distance combinations beats the linear positional baseline (29443 numbers) by a non-trivial margin (not noise-level).
- The content-dependence discriminator fires correctly: scrambling slot-fillers while preserving induced structure hurts accuracy substantially; the mechanism does not silently fall back to position/frequency.
- Induced-bracket coverage is broad and stable across the held-out set (not confined to a small repetition-favorable subset) — i.e., it does NOT show the fragility signature the literature predicts for ABL/EMILE on natural text.
- No continuous/EM/gradient step is required anywhere in the induction or head-candidate-rule pipeline (fully discrete and auditable throughout).

**HARD-FAIL** (matches the literature's predicted failure mode; this is the evidence that forces the glass-box-invariant tradeoff to USER):
- (a) Induced bracket/pattern coverage on the real (large-vocabulary, low-repetition) Linzen corpus is sparse/unstable — most held-out sentences get no confident bracketing, exactly as ABL/EMILE do at WSJ-scale relative to small/synthetic corpora (van Zaanen & Adriaans). This would mean the inducer cannot even produce a usable structural signal on real text, independent of accuracy.
- (b) The induced head-candidate rule correlates with linear proximity/token-frequency (not a genuine relational feature) — i.e., collapses back to the same positional/count heuristic already measured in 29443, just relabeled through an extra layer (the "edge-extraction regress" risk flagged in the prior consolidation note, now realized).
- (c) The content-dependence discriminator does not fire (scrambling slot-fillers doesn't hurt) — meaning the mechanism secretly still keys off position/frequency, and the "structural" framing is cosmetic.
- (d) A continuous/EM-like re-estimation step turns out to be necessary to get usable coverage at scale (reintroducing exactly the opacity the invariant is meant to exclude) — this would be the literature's DMV/CCM pattern re-appearing.

**MIDDLE (inconclusive, needs a harder split or more data):** induced structure beats the positional baseline narrowly, or coverage is moderate but not clearly stable — power issue, not yet a clean signature either way.
