## HEADLINE

**The Director's hypothesis is HALF RIGHT and HALF SELF-SERVING.** It is correct, and near-tautological, that ANY system that generalizes to a novel combination must apply SOME function uniformly at decode-time -- in that trivial sense "fixed algebra" is unavoidable and the brain is not exempt (Fodor & Pylyshyn 1988's systematicity claim is explicitly about representational FORMAT, a competence property, consistent with this). Read that narrowly, atom 29437's finding (free-by-construction) does not indict our substrate specially -- every compositional generalizer, brain included, is "free" in this weak sense. **But the Director's hypothesis over-reaches when it uses that truism to declare the CG target itself incoherent and close the search.** There IS a coherent, non-trivial sense of "learned, not algebra-given" that our substrate lacks and that Lake & Baroni's MLC (*Nature* 2023) genuinely instantiates: not "no fixed function exists" (impossible for any computer) but **whether the compositional capacity's EXISTENCE, SCOPE, and CONTENT-DEPENDENCE were themselves shaped by exposure to structured data, versus stipulated once by an engineer independent of any data.** FHRR circular-convolution binding is chosen and fixed before a single training example is seen; it works identically for structured or scrambled filler geometry (29437's own clincher: random beats real). MLC's meta-seq2seq network has no such operator engraved in advance -- its within-episode "rule" is inferred from a few support examples, and its very capacity to do this was acquired by exposure to thousands of systematic tasks. Smolensky's ICS program (and Chalmers 1990, "Syntactic Transformations on Distributed Representations") argued any connectionist system that generalizes systematically must be functionally isomorphic to a TPR *at some level of description* -- but isomorphic-to-a-TPR is not the same claim as "behaves identically regardless of whether the content it operates over is structured or scrambled." That second property is the one 29437 actually measured, and it is the one that distinguishes a stipulated operator from an acquired one.

## The discriminating test (the actual answer to "name a system that isn't reducible")

MLC is the candidate. It is not proven to pass the discriminator -- Lake & Baroni never ran a content-scrambling ablation -- but it makes a clean, opposite, falsifiable PREDICTION: a meta-learned compositional capacity should depend on the STRUCTURE of the content it was trained on to do its binding work, so scrambling/randomizing filler geometry should HURT a meta-learned operator (it has nothing else to exploit), not help it (as it does under free-algebra, where orthogonality minimizes crosstalk and structure is irrelevant or harmful). This is the same test already run in-house (atom 29437), just pointed at a meta-learned system instead of fixed FHRR. No paper reviewed (here or in the three prior 07-22/07-20 drills) has run this exact ablation on MLC or an equivalent meta-learner -- this is a gap, not a settled refutation.

The developmental/brain version of this claim (Frankland & Greene's role-specific populations are "extracted from experience," per the HP2 drill) is weaker evidence for the same point: it is inferred, not directly demonstrated by a content-dependence test in neural data (HP2 drill's own flag: absence-of-evidence risk on eLife's null binding-signature finding). Do not lean on it as confirmed.

## VERDICT

**REFUTED, narrowly and specifically.** The Director's hypothesis survives as a claim about OUR PARTICULAR result (29437 is genuinely free-by-construction; that finding is correctly banked MM, not overturned here) and survives as a truism (every generalizer has a fixed function somewhere). It is REFUTED as a claim that the CG target is *incoherent in principle* and that no coherent alternative exists to search for. The real remaining CG target is nameable and falsifiable: **a compositional capacity whose generalization survives or depends on content-structure in the OPPOSITE direction from 29437's signature** -- i.e., run the geometry-scrambling ablation on a meta-learned (MLC-pattern) operator, not a fixed-FHRR one. If real-geometry beats random-geometry there (the opposite of 29437), that is the "learned, not algebra-free" signature the program has been searching for, and it is a genuine architectural target, not a semantic dodge. If random ALSO beats real there, the Director's hypothesis strengthens considerably (no known system escapes the free-algebra signature) and should be promoted from HYPOTHESIS to program-level conclusion.
