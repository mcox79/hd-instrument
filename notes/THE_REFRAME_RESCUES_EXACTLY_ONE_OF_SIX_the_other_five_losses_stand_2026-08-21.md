## 2. WHY THE FIVE ARE DIFFERENT, STATED PLAINLY

**CA3's baseline was a DATABASE INDEX.** *Nothing in a brain enumerates its own memories and ranks
them. The comparison was category-mistaken.*

**The other four baselines are things brains verifiably do.** *Cortex tracks co-occurrence statistics.
Frequency shapes every reaction-time result in the literature. The visual word form area computes
orthography. These are not stand-ins for brain mechanisms -- they ARE brain mechanisms, and simpler
ones than what we built.* **Losing to them is a real loss and stays a real loss.**

**And the random-gate case is not a rival mechanism at all** -- *it is the ablation that isolates
SELECTION from RATE, which is exactly what a control is for. The finding it produced ("the gain is
rate, not prediction") is untouched by any of this.*

## TLDR

Last turn I found that one of our components only looked useless because it was being compared

**I said that was a question to ask of the others, not an answer to assume. I asked it. It does not

**Of six comparisons where something simple beat something we built, exactly one was unfair.** The
other five lost to things brains demonstrably do: tracking which words occur together, favouring
common words, and recognising spelling. **Those aren't cheap stand-ins for brain mechanisms — they

**And I'm recording this precisely because the finding was convenient.** Having found one unfair

everything excuses nothing.

**So the honest position is now split rather than uniform.** Where the system tries to work out
meaning from word patterns, it genuinely loses to simpler methods the brain also uses. Where it
rebuilds a memory from a fragment, it works — once we replaced a borrowed piece of mathematics and
measured it the way a brain would have to.

**And that split falls exactly where the earlier fidelity analysis predicted:** the failures sit on
components where science doesn't pin the equation and we invented one; the success came from fixing
precisely such an invention.
