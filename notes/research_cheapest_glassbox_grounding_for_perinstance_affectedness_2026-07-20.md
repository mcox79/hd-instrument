# Research: cheapest glass-box approximation of grounding for per-instance affectedness (2026-07-20)

Director synthesis of a 3x parallel Sonnet lit-scan (infant causal-perception minimal primitive; structured state-change annotation as a grounding proxy; synthetic/physics-sim/real-perception cost-ranking). Trigger: per-instance who-is-affected is now COMPREHENSIVELY WALLED for every cheap text+structured route (text-derivation closed `29375`; curated Levin/Dowty signal redundant-with-structure `29373`; VerbNet SELRESTRS coverage-blocked/empty even on core verbs `29392`; PropBank/ATOMIC wrong granularity, per `research_affectedness_decomposition_typelevel_lookup_vs_perinstance_2026-07-20.md`). This drill answers a narrower, harder question than "can text do it" (already no): **is there a CHEAP, glass-box-legal approximation of grounding that supplies the missing per-instance signal, short of full perception?** All prior work credited (learn-from/build-on, never "steal"). Deflated throughout per lit-scan-calibration.

## HEADLINE

**No off-the-shelf cheap option delivers the per-instance residual. The brain's own minimal grounding primitive (contact + motion-transfer, Michotte/Leslie-Keeble, present by 6.5 months, independent of object-identity) IS genuinely cheaper than full scene perception — but the literature only verifies it as sufficient for detecting THAT something changed, not WHICH of several type-compatible candidates changed or HOW (the actual linguistic-affectedness question). Every engineering approximation of that primitive inherits one of two failure modes: the annotation-based routes (ProPara/OpenPI-style per-instance state-change labels) are CIRCULAR and UNPROVEN to transfer to independent disambiguation benchmarks; the simulation-based routes (synthetic text-world/graph generators, physics-engine oracles) are glass-box-legal and offline but carry a WELL-DOCUMENTED, VERIFIED shortcut-learning risk across every close analog found (ALFRED, CLEVR/CLOSURE, Dyna-bAbI, Gururangan-style annotation artifacts). Real perception (image/video-text grounding) is the only route with no shortcut-risk caveat in what was found, but is orders of magnitude more expensive AND has zero verified evidence of solving per-instance affectedness specifically (only type-level/aggregate NLU gains). Net: the honest verdict is that "cheap-grounding-approximation" is not a free lunch — the cheapest PLAUSIBLE candidate (a synthetic procedurally-generated event-outcome corpus) is glass-box-legal and modest in raw construction cost, but only trustworthy if paired with mandatory anti-shortcut engineering (systematic-generalization held-out split + scrambled-generator must-fail control), which is itself a real, non-trivial addition to "cheap." This is a genuinely bigger and less-certain investment than the type-level lookup routes already tried and closed — Option B is NOT a cheap unlock; it is a real, moderate-cost, UNPROVEN bet that needs its own can-fail test before any larger commitment.**

## Cheap decisive test

Build the CHEAPEST candidate identified below — a small, procedurally-generated synthetic event-outcome text corpus (a simple templated "world" with N object-types, M event-templates, and a deterministic state-tracker emitting (event-sentence, ground-truth-affected-entity) pairs, analogous in spirit to bAbI/TextWorld/GTASA but scoped down to exactly the who-is-affected question) — and run it against the SAME `29375` eval item structure (or a matched synthetic-analog eval), not a new hand-picked showcase set.

**Design gate (per feedback-experiment-design-gate, verify at smoke before full run):**
1. **Real baseline** = the closed text-derivation signal from `29375` (0.474 argmax pick-gold) AND the type-lookup arm already design-gated in the affectedness-decomposition note — run the new grounded-signal arm on the identical held-out split where possible.
2. **Discriminator can-fail:** the grounded/synthetic signal must be able to score WORSE than the closed baseline — construct the eval so some items have the synthetic generator's "learned" cue absent or misleading (e.g., swap which template-frequent object plays which role) so a shortcut-only model fails visibly.
3. **Difficulty actually on:** use the type-INDISCRIMINABLE subset from the affectedness-decomposition note (same-type candidates, ~30-45% per-instance residual) — the exact bucket where type-lookup showed near-chance (53%, Chersoni/Bicknell) — not the easy type-discriminable majority.
4. **One variable differs:** only the presence/absence of the grounded synthetic signal (as a training feature or lookup edge) on top of the same closed baseline; same eval items, same gold labels, same scoring.
5. **MANDATORY must-fail control (new, load-bearing for this specific route, per the verified shortcut-learning literature below):** a SCRAMBLED-GENERATOR control — regenerate the synthetic corpus with object/role assignments randomly permuted relative to the templates that produced them, train/derive the same signal from the scrambled version, and confirm it does NOT beat chance on the held-out real eval. If the scrambled control also "passes," the signal is a generator-artifact, not a grounding signal (this is the Gururangan/Dyna-bAbI/ALFRED failure mode, verified below, not a hypothetical).
6. **Held-out systematic-generalization split** (CLOSURE/Dyna-bAbI/ALFRED-L style): hold out a combination of (object, role, template) never seen in training, to catch compositional-shortcut exploitation specifically — a simple train/test random split is NOT sufficient per the literature (models solve i.i.d. splits of exactly these generator-families while failing systematic splits, verified across 3 independent analogs below).

**Arms:** (a) closed text-derivation baseline alone [`29375`, real baseline]; (b) closed baseline + type-lookup edge [from the decomposition note, if not already run]; (c) closed baseline + synthetic grounded-signal edge (the new arm this drill proposes); (d) synthetic grounded-signal edge ALONE (isolate its standalone contribution); (e) scrambled-generator control [must NOT beat chance -- mandatory sanity gate]; (f) systematic-generalization held-out split of arm (c) [must not collapse the way Dyna-bAbI/ALFRED did, or the "win" is a shortcut].

## Falsifiable predictions

**HARD-PASS:** on the type-indiscriminable (per-instance-residual) subset, the synthetic grounded-signal arm (c) or (d) beats the closed baseline by a pre-registered margin (>= 0.05 absolute accuracy) on an i.i.d. held-out split, AND retains a non-trivial majority of that lift (>= 60% of the gain) on the systematic-generalization held-out split (f), AND the scrambled-generator control (e) stays at or below chance. This would mean: a modest, glass-box, build-time-only synthetic-grounding investment genuinely supplies part of the per-instance residual without full perception — a real, bounded win worth scaling.

**HARD-FAIL:** either (i) the synthetic arm shows no lift over baseline on the i.i.d. split (< 0.02 accuracy difference) — the generator's ground-truth-labeling doesn't transfer at all, matching this cycle's finding that NO paper anywhere demonstrates state-change/synthetic-grounding transfer to independent thematic-fit/SRL disambiguation; or (ii) the synthetic arm shows a lift on the i.i.d. split but that lift MOSTLY EVAPORATES on the systematic-generalization split (< 40% retained) — the "win" was a generator-artifact, exactly the Dyna-bAbI/ALFRED/CLOSURE pattern; or (iii) the scrambled-generator control (e) ALSO beats chance — the eval harness itself leaks the answer through frequency/template structure, independent of the hypothesis (a construction bug, not a result). Any of these three would mean: cheap synthetic grounding does NOT resolve the residual, and the per-instance affectedness gap genuinely requires either (a) real perceptual grounding (expensive, per the cost table below) or (b) accepting the residual as a structural bound, consistent with the brain's own known difficulty here (Bicknell's spillover-only, N400-only effects — this correction arrives "a beat later" even in humans, it is not instantaneous or free even biologically).

**Must-fail control (harness sanity, separate from the hypothesis test):** the scrambled-generator arm (e) must not beat chance on ANY subset — if it does, fix the eval before drawing any conclusion from (c)/(d).

## Cross-thread synthesis

### 1. The brain's minimal grounding primitive for affectedness -- genuinely cheaper than full perception, but only for a NARROWER question than the one we need answered

Michotte's launching-effect studies (and a 2025 registered replication, Royal Society Open Science) establish that the causal impression depends on exactly three purely kinematic parameters -- contact latency near-zero, a narrow launcher/target velocity ratio, same-direction motion continuation -- using abstract 2-D shapes with NO object-identity or scene context at all. Leslie & Keeble (1987, *Cognition* 25) show 6.5-month-old infants dishabituate to a reversed "direct launching" film more than to a spatiotemporally-matched non-causal control, using plain colored blocks -- i.e., the causal/launching percept is present before object-KIND recognition and does not require it. Spelke's core-knowledge contact principle (objects influence each other's motion iff they touch) is explicitly a primitive-physical-parameter claim, not an object-identity claim, operative from ~5 months. **This triangulates cleanly: "who/what was contacted and had motion transferred to it" is a genuinely minimal, developmentally-early, object-identity-independent perceptual primitive** -- the cheapest possible layer, confirmed by 3 independent converging literatures.

**The caveat that matters most for this drill's decisive question:** this minimal primitive answers "did contact/force transfer occur, and to what," which is necessary but not sufficient for the linguistic notion of affectedness we actually need (Dowty's proto-patient: undergoes CHANGE OF STATE, not just motion-transfer -- "break," "kill," "convince" involve a change in the entity's PROPERTIES, not just its trajectory). Spelke's own framework fixes THAT motion was transferred, not WHAT changed about the recipient. Woodward's infant goal-attribution work (1998, *Cognition* 69) shows a SEPARATE, richer primitive -- tracking the goal/recipient object of an agent's action -- that requires representing the actor as an agent-with-a-goal, not just detecting contact; this is plausibly closer to what "who is affected" actually needs linguistically, and it is NOT the same cheap primitive as Michotte's. Schlottmann's work (UCL) further shows perceiving causality and REASONING about it correctly (who is responsible, what changed) dissociate in children -- perception does not automatically deliver understanding. **No study directly tests whether the cheap contact/motion-transfer primitive alone suffices for the FULL linguistic affectedness/patienthood judgment, or whether the richer, later-developing goal/agency primitive is required** -- this is a synthesis inference from the sub-agent, explicitly flagged as such, not a landed finding. This matters directly for the design of any synthetic-grounding corpus: a generator that only encodes contact/motion-transfer (the cheap primitive) may be encoding the WRONG primitive for what "affectedness" needs (Woodward's richer goal/agency structure), which would explain in advance why a naive synthetic corpus might fail to transfer even without shortcut-learning being the cause.

### 2. Structured per-instance state-change annotation (ProPara/OpenPI) -- real candidate, but circular and unproven, not a bankable cheap win

ProPara (Dalvi et al. 2018, NAACL, arXiv:1805.06975) annotates existence/location per entity per step across 488 procedural paragraphs, 81,345 total annotations, inter-annotator agreement ~0.67; OpenPI (Tandon et al. 2020, EMNLP, arXiv:2011.08092) is similar at ~$3.6/article crowd cost, 810 WikiHow paragraphs, 29,928 tuples. Both are exactly the "per-instance state-change ground truth" this drill asked about, and both are static/CC-navigable/glass-box-legal build-time artifacts (same legal status as the COMET-offline-dump precedent already established). **But the specific comparison this drill needs -- does per-instance state-change training beat a verb-class/selectional-preference-only baseline on MULTI-CANDIDATE-SAME-TYPE disambiguation -- does not exist in the published literature.** ProPara's own baselines (feature-based LR+CRF 58.6% vs. ProLocal 62.7% vs. ProGlobal 63.0% vs. human 91.7% on Cat-1) show richer contextual features help general state-tracking, but none of those baselines is a VerbNet-SELRESTRS-style verb-class-only signal, and no follow-up paper runs that exact comparison. **The circularity concern raised in the task brief is real and stands UNREBUTTED in the literature**: crowd annotators must already correctly identify which entity is the patient/undergoer to label its state change -- the annotation re-encodes the ambiguity rather than independently resolving it for a system that doesn't yet have that identification. No cross-domain transfer evidence exists either (both corpora are procedural/how-to only; no test of transfer to narrative/news-domain patient disambiguation was found). This is a genuine, unclosed gap -- worth the design-gated test above precisely because it's cheap enough to try, but it should NOT be assumed to work; the honest prior, given the circularity and zero transfer evidence, is modest at best.

### 3. Synthetic procedurally-generated grounded corpora and physics-engine oracles -- glass-box-legal and cheap to build, but VERIFIED high shortcut-risk

No published "text-only CLEVR for events" (a generator emitting (event-sentence, ground-truth-affected-entity) pairs specifically for the who-is-affected question) was found to exist yet -- this is a genuine, confirmed gap, not a dismissal. The closest analogs (TextWorld, ALFRED/ALFWorld, ScienceWorld) are built for RL/instruction-following, not disambiguation-labeling, but they carry a DIRECTLY VERIFIED and highly relevant construct-validity warning: ALFRED-trained agents were shown to "exploit shortcuts and unintended biases without comprehending the underlying linguistic structure" (e.g., learning "microwave = heat anything" because microwaves lack visual diversity in the simulator, ignoring the actual held object) -- serious enough that a dedicated held-out test split (ALFRED-L, EMNLP 2022) had to be built specifically to catch it. CLEVR's own design motivation was to fix earlier VQA datasets' exploitable frequency/linguistic biases, yet CLOSURE later showed CLEVR-trained models still fail systematic compositional generalization. Dyna-bAbI (Tamari et al., arXiv:2112.00086, *StarSem 2022*) is the most directly on-point: models hit >99% on original bAbI 1.0 but collapse under compositional-generalization splits (EntNet 98.95%->72.0%, T5 99.8%->62.66% OOD) -- proof that bAbI's near-saturation reflected exploitable generator shortcuts, not the reasoning it claims to test. This is the SAME failure-mode family as Gururangan et al.'s classic annotation-artifacts finding (hypothesis-only SNLI/MultiNLI baselines hitting 53-67% accuracy, arXiv:1803.02324) -- i.e., this is not a one-off risk specific to any single dataset, it is a GENERAL, well-replicated pattern across every close analog checked (4 independent instances: ALFRED, CLEVR/CLOSURE, bAbI/Dyna-bAbI, SNLI/Gururangan).

A very recent (construction-year 2026, maturity/peer-review status unverified, flag accordingly) system called GTASA (arXiv:2604.10385) is structurally the closest match to "a physics/graph simulator that emits paired text + ground-truth who-affected-whom labels at build time" -- fully offline, deterministic, glass-box-legal in the same sense as COMET-offline-dump. Notably it reports the OPPOSITE problem from shortcut-gaming: existing video/text encoders currently score "barely above chance" on its who-is-near-whom probe, meaning (for that specific system) the task is currently too HARD, not too gameable -- but this also means no one has yet shown this route transfers usefully to any downstream text benchmark. PIGLeT (AI2-THOR-based, learns a physical-dynamics model from simulator interaction, then couples it to language) is the best-established precedent for "physics simulator as a grounding oracle for language," but per-instance patient-disambiguation numbers from it could not be verified this cycle -- flagged as inference-only that it is structurally the right shape, not a landed number.

### 4. Real image/video-text grounding -- gold standard, but no direct evidence for THIS specific capability, and orders of magnitude more expensive

Visual Genome's construction cost is directly documented: ~800K Mechanical Turk HITs, workers paid $6-8/hr, 6 months of collection after 15 months of schema iteration (arXiv:1602.07332) -- and comparable commonsense-graph annotation runs ~$4/image at $15/hr. This is 2-3 orders of magnitude more expensive than either synthetic route or the lexical-resource lookups already tried. Critically, even at this cost tier, **no paper found demonstrates a direct, per-instance affectedness/patient-disambiguation transfer gain from real image/video grounding.** "Is BERT Blind?" (arXiv:2303.12513) finds vision-and-language-pretrained text encoders beat text-only baselines on tasks requiring IMPLICIT visual reasoning but UNDERPERFORM on standard NLU benchmarks; LexiContrastive Grounding (ACL 2024 Findings) reports a modest ~5% perplexity improvement and gains on word-learning/sentence benchmarks -- both type-level/aggregate language-modeling gains, not verified per-instance who-is-affected wins. The runtime-vs-build-time distinction still applies here exactly as it did for COMET: an offline dump of image-text annotations used as static training/lookup data is glass-box-legal; a live perception pipeline queried at inference would violate the no-runtime-LLM/no-runtime-perception invariant.

## Substrate-product implications

- **This does not reopen `29375`'s text-derivation closure, `29392`'s VerbNet coverage-block, or `29373`'s curated-signal redundancy** -- all three stay closed; this drill is strictly about whether a NEW, grounding-adjacent route can supply the per-instance residual those three left unaddressed.
- **The honest verdict for the USER's "is Option B cheap-approximable" question: NO, not off-the-shelf.** The genuinely cheap routes (VerbNet, curated-signal) are already closed for THIS purpose because they are structurally type-level, not per-instance. The next-cheapest live candidate (ProPara/OpenPI-style state-change annotation) is real, static, glass-box-legal, and inexpensive to acquire (public datasets already exist, no new construction needed to TRY it) -- but it is unproven and carries an unrebutted circularity concern; it is worth a cheap can-fail test precisely BECAUSE the marginal cost of trying is low (existing datasets, no new annotation spend), not because the literature predicts it will work.
- **The synthetic-generator route is glass-box-legal and modest in raw construction cost, but is NOT "cheap" once mandatory anti-shortcut engineering (systematic-generalization held-out splits + scrambled-generator must-fail controls) is included** -- this is a real, moderate engineering investment, not a quick win, and the verified failure-mode literature (4 independent analogs) means skipping the anti-shortcut design would produce a result that LOOKS like a win and isn't.
- **Full real-perception grounding (image/video) remains the only route with no shortcut-risk caveat found, but is 2-3 orders of magnitude more expensive AND has zero verified evidence of solving this specific capability** -- committing to it on the strength of this literature alone would be an unjustified leap; if the synthetic/ProPara routes both HARD-FAIL at the design gate, the honest next step is to treat the residual as a genuinely BIG, uncertain investment (consistent with the standing pivot's build-the-missing-layer framing) rather than assume perception is a guaranteed fix.
- **Recommended sequencing (cheapest-first, per the table):** (1) try the ProPara/OpenPI-style per-instance state-change signal first (near-zero marginal cost, existing datasets, the design-gated test above) -- if HARD-PASS, this is the cheapest possible win; (2) only if (1) HARD-FAILS, invest in the synthetic-generator route WITH mandatory anti-shortcut design from day one (not as an afterthought); (3) only if (2) also HARD-FAILS or the anti-shortcut engineering proves prohibitively complex, escalate to considering real-perception grounding as the genuinely-big bet, with the explicit caveat that even that bet is currently evidence-free for this specific capability and would need its own dedicated derisking study before a full commitment.
- This keeps Option B's evaluation strictly sequential and can-fail at each stage, consistent with the standing design-gate discipline and the lit-scan-calibration penalty -- no stage is assumed to work, and the cheapest stage is tried first.
