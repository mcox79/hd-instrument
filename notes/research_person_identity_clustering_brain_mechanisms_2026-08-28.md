## (A) Ambiguous-surface-form disambiguation among competing same-type entities [PRIORITY 1]

**Named structures + computation:** Person Identity Node (PIN — Bruce & Young 1986, *Br. J. Psychol.* 77(3):305–327, verified) and the Interactive Activation and Competition model (Burton, Bruce & Johnston 1990, *Br. J. Psychol.* 81(3):361–380, verified) for the person-specific layer; Centering Theory (Grosz, Joshi & Weinstein 1995) and ACT-R cue-based retrieval / base-level activation (Lewis & Vasishth 2005, *Cognitive Science* 29:375–419) for the general reference-resolution layer.

**PINNED, from prior on-disk work + this session's own recent drills (not re-derived):**
- Grammatical role/structural salience dominates raw recency for the antecedent-choice decision (SOLVED.md: 0.6988 CI-separated on GAP, recency/frequency ablate to +0.0000 marginal *under the current, provably-flawed formula*).
- A full LV05-style cue-based-**activation** binder HARD_FAILED for the binding decision (−0.1348); a 2017 meta-analysis (Jäger, Engelmann & Vasishth) independently found the interference signature *absent* for reflexive/reciprocal-antecedent dependencies — the structural case closest to person-binding.
- **Iterative settling/attractor relaxation is NOT supported for the pick itself** — three converging sources (Li, Luh, Pylkkänen, Yang & Hale 2020 bioRxiv naturalistic-listening brain-fit; Chow, Lewis & Phillips 2014 *Frontiers*; the substrate's own on-disk LV05 HARD_FAIL) all say the mechanism is fast, one-shot, feed-forward score-and-argmax, not multi-cycle relaxation. **Do not build a settling mechanism for this decision.**
- The current salience formula (`count + capped exponential tiebreak`) has a **proven mathematical defect**: a 1-mention frequency lead can never be overturned by recency, no matter how long ago. The literature-correct replacement, already derived but not yet built, is ACT-R base-level activation: `B_i(t) = ln(sum_k w_role(k) . dt_k^-d)`, a genuine frequency/recency trade-off. `d` must be swept (`{0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 1.0}`), never adopted.

**NEW this drill (IAC-model extension to the surname-competitor case — architecture-derived prediction, flagged SPECULATIVE/INFERENTIAL by the lit-scan, no direct published test found):** the IAC model's node pools are Face/Name Recognition Units, PINs, and Semantic Information Units (SIUs — shared traits: occupation, family, **surname**), with excitatory between-pool and inhibitory within-pool connections. Two PINs that share an SIU (a surname) both receive excitatory spread from that *same* shared unit — the architecture predicts **elevated mutual cross-activation/confusability specifically when two people share a surname**, exactly the organ's own measured failure mode (bare "Miss Bennet" completing to the wrong sister). This reframes the over-merge finding: it is not an implementation bug, it is the *expected* behavior of a PIN/IAC-shaped system when the disambiguating SIU (which specific sister) is absent from the surface form — the fix has to come from an *independent* channel (grammatical-role/recency salience, i.e., lever A's decay score), not from the surname/gender features themselves, which the IAC model says will always partially cross-activate.

**Concrete implementable operation:** Build the ACT-R base-level decay score exactly as pre-registered in `research_pronoun_activation_dynamics_2026-08-27.md`, but apply it as the tiebreak **specifically gated on same-surname/same-gender candidate sets** (the organ's own named failure case), not just pronouns — same mathematical primitive, two consumers, build once. Role weights reuse the SOLVED.md grammatical-prominence hierarchy; `dt_k` is sentence-distance; sweep `d`.

**Skeptical read:** this is the strongest lever of the five because it is (a) a proven mathematical fix to a known defect, (b) already partially validated on a related task (pronoun binding), and (c) targets LitBank, which this SOLVER is *already* using — near-zero additional corpus risk relative to the prior note's own uncertainty (which was about finding a suitable long-narrative corpus at all). It will **not** by itself close the whole over-merge gap — the IAC-model reading above says some residual cross-activation between same-surname sisters is architecturally expected, not eliminable — but it is the correct next build, not a re-hash.

**P_deflated = 0.45** (matches the directly-analogous prior pronoun-binding prediction; deflated for the same reasons — untested transfer of a proven mathematical form to a new corpus population, in this case NAME-mentions rather than pronouns specifically).
**HARD-PASS:** on the same-surname/same-gender ambiguous-tie subset, the decay-tiebreak beats the current organ by a CI-separated B-cubed-precision margin, AND does not regress overall B-cubed F below the current 0.862, AND beats a shuffled-mention-order control (info-free twin — destroys decay structure, keeps counts/roles) CI-separated.
**HARD-FAIL:** no separation from the current formula, or the shuffled-order twin ties/beats it — would mean the decay form does not carry information at LitBank's scale for NAME-mentions even though it may for pronouns, a real and useful negative that would flip this lever's priority down.

## (B) Nickname/diminutive unification [PRIORITY 3, cheap high-precision sub-lever available now]

**Named structure/theory:** not a memory-system question but a phonological-competence one (McCarthy & Prince 1986 prosodic morphology; Lappe 2007, *English Prosodic Morphology*) plus a direct PIN-level finding: Stevenage & Lewis (2005, *QJEP*, "By which name should I call thee?") show experimentally that a formal name and its nickname converge on **one** Person Identity Node (elevated familiarity, competitor cost) — i.e., the brain does *not* store two linked entries, it stores one identity with multiple output name-forms, which licenses merging alias-forms into a single node once bound, rather than a "linked-pair" relation.

**PINNED, hybrid verdict:** a productive truncation(+y/-ie) template — find the primary-stress syllable, extend to the maximal legal English monosyllable coda, optionally suffix -y/-ie — is real, generative, and **generalizes to unfamiliar names** (Will→Willy, Liz→Lizzy, Tom→Tommy, Ed→Eddie, Sue→Susie): this class needs **no lookup table**. A large, extremely common residual (Bob/Bobby←Robert, Peggy←Margaret, Jack←John, Sally←Sarah, Molly←Mary, Ted/Ned←Edward, Nan←Ann, Hank←Henry, Dick←Richard) is historically opaque (rhyming-nickname substitution, medieval juncture reanalysis) and has **no synchronic phonological derivation** — every practical record-linkage system surveyed (genealogy/census tools) handles this class only via an explicit stored-pairs dictionary; no phonological-similarity metric (Soundex, Jaro-Winkler, Double Metaphone) recovers it.

**NEW, independent, zero-lookup-table mechanism (this drill's highest-confidence single recommendation for this lever):** discourse apposition-pattern detection for explicit author-signaled aliasing — `NAME1 [,] (called|known as|nicknamed|or) NAME2` / `NAME2, as PRONOUN was (called|known)`. This is a fully deterministic lexico-syntactic pattern (Hearst-pattern-style, well-precedented in NER/alias-extraction engineering), orthogonal to phonology, and directly targets exactly the construction 19th-century narrative prose commonly uses to introduce a nickname ("Elizabeth, or Lizzy as her sisters called her").

**Concrete implementable operation, in order of build cost:** (1) apposition-pattern detector (cheapest, highest precision, catches only explicitly-signaled aliases — a recall-limited but false-positive-free channel); (2) truncate(+y) generative matcher for the productive class (generalizes, moderate precision risk — verify against a held-out gender/name-type check before merging); (3) a small (~50–150 entry) stored table for the closed historically-opaque class, sized to common 18th/19th-century English given names (LitBank's register), not a general nickname dictionary.

**Skeptical read:** do not oversell a single generalizable mechanism — the evidence is explicit that phonological similarity alone misses roughly half of common English nicknames. Given the organ's dominant failure mode is already over-merge, be careful that the truncate(+y) matcher's false-merge rate (e.g. spuriously matching unrelated short names) is bounded before shipping; the apposition detector carries no such risk and should ship first regardless of the other two.

**P_deflated = 0.40.**
**HARD-PASS:** apposition detector recovers ≥50% of the SOLVER's measured nickname residual (~10% of stragglers) at ≥90% precision; truncate(+y) matcher recovers an *additional* ≥20% of the remainder without regressing precision.
**HARD-FAIL:** apposition detector recovers <10% (construction too rare in this corpus — a real, useful negative) AND the phonological matcher's false-merge rate on the full same-gender candidate pool exceeds a 2% budget (would *worsen* the organ's already-dominant over-merge problem, making it net-negative to ship).

## (C) Nominal/definite-description/epithet binding [PRIORITY 2 by request, but genuinely harder — budget accordingly]

**Named structure/theory:** this is **not** "bridging" in the modern technical sense. Clark (1975) originally used "bridging" broadly, but the field has since split it (Hawkins 1978 "associative anaphora"; Prince 1981 "Inferrables"; modern ARRAU/GUM annotation explicitly excludes identity-coreference from the term over time) into (i) associative/new-entity inference (a genuinely different, costlier mechanism — Burkhardt 2006 ERP: bridged referents show an added P600/late-positivity integration cost even when N400 is reduced) versus (ii) identity re-description of an *already-explicit* entity, which is the SOLVER's actual case ("the girl" = the already-introduced Elizabeth). The right formal apparatus for (ii) is Heim's (1982) Novelty/Familiarity condition + van der Sandt's (1992) Binding-and-Accommodation Theory, operationalized in DRT (Kamp & Reyle 1993) as: **bind** a definite to an existing discourse referent if the referent's accumulated file-card properties are compatible with/entail the description's content; **accommodate** (create new) only if no compatible referent exists.

**PINNED (direct answer to "is this the same organ or genuinely separate," medium confidence, triangulated across three independent evidence lines rather than one direct study):** **same organ as name-clustering, plus an added semantic-type compatibility gate** — not a separate bridging-inference module. This follows the Stanford deterministic multi-pass sieve architecture (Raghunathan et al. 2010; Lee et al. 2013) — which *won CoNLL-2011 outright* over every contemporary statistical competitor using exactly this shape — where head/relaxed-head match runs first and a **compatibility-gated fallback pass** (gender/age/animacy filters from gazetteers, Bergsma & Lin gender-noun lists) handles cases with zero head-noun overlap, using the *same* salience/recency ranking as every other pass, not a separately-ranked mechanism.

**Minimal brain-faithful cue set, converging across Heim/DRT, Vieira & Poesio (2000), and the Stanford-sieve engineering line:** (1) semantic-type/category compatibility as a **hard gate** — human-hypernym + gender/age compatibility between the description's head noun and each candidate node's inferred gender/age (reuse the organ's existing gn-agreement filter, extended from proper-name gender to definite-description head-noun gender, e.g. WordNet person-hypernym + a gendered-common-noun gazetteer for "girl/lady/suitor/gentleman/old man"); (2) recency/salience window — **reuse the exact same ranking built for lever (A)**, do not build a second ranking mechanism; (3) gender/number/animacy agreement, already owned; (4) uniqueness-in-window — if ≥2 compatible candidates survive, **ABSTAIN** rather than guess (this mirrors Vieira & Poesio's own low inter-annotator agreement on exactly this mention type, and matches the SOLVER's own complete-vs-separate discipline).

**Honest, load-bearing caveat (do not oversell):** Vieira & Poesio's own taxonomy treats head-noun-overlap cases ("a house… the house") as the *easy* case; the SOLVER's actual examples ("the girl", "this suitor", "the well of solicitors") have **zero** head overlap with any proper name and fall into the genuinely harder residual class their paper says needs lexical/world knowledge — a WordNet-hypernym-based system for the closest comparable task (associative bridging specifically, Hou 2018) reports only **~40% antecedent accuracy**, far below typical name/pronoun coreference. This is a *different, numerically worse* task than levers (A)/(B); the SOLVER's own framing of it as "seemingly a different competence" is partially right — not architecturally separate, but empirically and psycholinguistically **costlier** (the ERP evidence above), so it should not be expected to close most of the 76%-of-stragglers residual on a first pass. Budget it as a real but *partial* recall win, gated hard on precision so it doesn't create new over-merge errors on top of the existing ones.

**P_deflated = 0.35** (capped low relative to (A)/(B): this is the SOLVER's largest residual bucket, the literature is explicit that the comparable task tops out well below proper-name coreference accuracy, and no direct study was found testing exactly "definite-description-to-explicit-named-antecedent" — the triangulation is medium-confidence, not a direct hit).
**HARD-PASS:** the semantic-type-gated nominal channel (reusing the (A) salience ranking) recovers ≥25% of the nominal/epithet residual (76% of stragglers) at ≥85% precision on this channel specifically (matching, not regressing, the organ's existing precision floor).
**HARD-FAIL:** recovers <10% of the residual, OR this channel's own precision drops below 70% — given how large this residual bucket is, a low-precision nominal channel would be net-harmful to ship even if it improved raw recall.

## (D) Complete-vs-separate default bias and gating signal

**Named structures:** dentate gyrus sparse expansion coding (separation, architecturally upstream/default-on) vs. CA3 recurrent autoassociation (completion, engaged once an attractor is caught) — Marr 1971; O'Reilly & McClelland 1994; Yassa & Stark 2011. Comparator: CA3 novelty/identity-detector cells (Vinogradova 2001, her last paper) and/or CA1 comparing CA3-derived prediction against direct entorhinal input (Kumaran & Maguire — **two separate, both verified, real papers**: *J. Neurosci.* 27(32):8517–8524, 2007, "Match–mismatch processes..."; *Hippocampus* 17(9):735–748, 2007, "Which computational mechanisms..."; note the commonly-quoted title "Novelty signals: a window into hippocampal information processing" is **Kumaran alone**, *TiCS* 13(2):47–54, **2009**, not co-authored with Maguire — correct this if it propagates further).

**CONTESTED — there is no single fixed default; the theory explicitly denies one.** Complementary Learning Systems theory (O'Reilly & McClelland) and Hasselmo's ACh/theta-phase work say the DG/CA3 operating point is **continuously state-tuned**: high acetylcholine / theta-peak (active encoding/exploration) suppresses CA3→CA1 completion pathways while sparing direct entorhinal input, biasing toward separation/new-encoding; low ACh / theta-trough lets CA3 recurrence dominate, favoring completion. **Reading a novel is, on this account, itself an encoding-attentive, separation-favoring state** — which argues *against* a fixed completion-bias.

**But the best-matched *empirical* analogy (ambiguous-similarity judgment under weak evidence) points the other way.** Stark-lab mnemonic-similarity-task work (aging/degraded pattern-separation populations) and the DRM false-memory paradigm both show that when discrimination evidence is weak, the observed failure is **over-completion** (false "old"/merge), not over-separation — no comparable literature reports the opposite failure direction under weak evidence.

**Honest synthesis (this drill's own reconciliation, flagged as an engineering judgment, not a single literature verdict):** these two lines of evidence are in real tension, and forcing a clean answer would be dishonest. The actionable resolution: **the organ's current over-merge bias is exactly what brain-typical behavior looks like under weak discriminating evidence** (the failure-mode literature) — it is not evidence the threshold is set wrong. **Do not "fix" this by shifting the complete/separate prior toward separation as a blanket policy** — that would fight the brain's own weak-evidence default and risks breaking cases where merge genuinely is correct. Instead, per the state-tuning literature, the lever that should change is **the quality of the discriminating signal itself** — i.e., lever (A)'s decay-based salience score is the correct target of investment, not the threshold. This is a substantive, non-obvious reframe: **D is not a separate build, it is the reason A is the right priority.**

**On the explicit "hold ambiguous / defer" state:** **brain-motivated but not literally brain-observed.** Yonelinas' dual-process signal-detection model (1994) and diffusion-model accounts of recognition memory both confirm a real, graded low-confidence ("familiar-but-not-recollected") zone exists in the underlying decision variable, but every formal model **forces a categorical response per trial** — there is no persistent third "unresolved" bucket in the brain's own account. The legitimate engineering translation: implement DEFER as a **revisable, weakly-weighted soft-link** (strengthened into a firm merge, or split off, as later evidence — more mentions — arrives), not a frozen, permanently-suspended third state. This matches the SOLVER's own complete-vs-separate framing better than a static "unresolved" flag would.

**Concrete implementable operation:** (1) do not move the complete/separate threshold toward separation; (2) use a continuous graded overlap/match score for the comparator (no study reports a fixed numeric threshold — mismatch strength scales continuously with prediction-outcome dissimilarity), consistent with whatever scoring the organ already uses; (3) implement DEFER/hold as a decaying soft-merge that gets confirmed or split by subsequent mentions, not a static third bucket.

**P_deflated = 0.30** (the lowest of the five — the two literatures genuinely conflict, and this is closer to an engineering judgment call informed by biology than a literature-derived prediction).
**HARD-PASS (reframed as a non-manipulation test, per the honest synthesis above):** leaving the current merge-biased threshold **unchanged** while shipping lever (A) improves recall on the ambiguous-tie subset without a *further* precision loss beyond the current 0.858 baseline — i.e., confirms the "fix the signal, not the threshold" reframe.
**HARD-FAIL:** shipping lever (A) at the current threshold *still* over-merges at the same or worse rate — would mean the over-merge problem is not (primarily) a discriminating-signal-quality problem after all, and the threshold-direction question would need to be reopened directly (test both directions explicitly rather than assuming this note's reframe).

## (E) ATL semantic-hub convergence vs. hippocampal episodic complete/separate — structured vs. bag-of-features

**Named structures:** PIN (Bruce & Young 1986) is itself already a **hybrid**: one localist gateway node per identity, fed by distributed, weighted Semantic Information Unit pools — neither pure bag-of-features nor pure structured slots at the person-recognition level. Patterson, Nestor & Rogers (2007, *Nat. Rev. Neurosci.* 8(12):976–987, verified, DOI 10.1038/nrn2277) ATL hub-and-spoke: modality-specific "spokes" converge on a bilateral (often left/right-asymmetric) anterior-temporal "hub"; semantic dementia (progressive ATL atrophy) causes loss of stored/famous-person semantic knowledge with relative sparing elsewhere. Laterality is **PINNED** (Gainotti, multiple reviews e.g. *Cortex* 2007): right ATL damage impairs face/person *familiarity* (perceptual convergence), left ATL damage impairs proper-*name* retrieval specifically — a genuine double dissociation by hemisphere, confirmed via mirror-image face-vs-name patterns in right- vs. left-predominant semantic-dementia patients.

**Crux double dissociation (ATL stored-knowledge vs. hippocampal new-binding, specifically for person identity): PINNED in direction, CONTESTED/never demonstrated head-to-head on matched person stimuli.** The evidence comes from two separate literatures that were never run against each other on the same task: semantic-dementia patients show relatively spared hippocampus and can show preserved new-item recognition despite catastrophic semantic loss (Graham & Hodges 1997), and separately recognize *personally-familiar* (non-famous, recently-encountered) people better than famous ones — but no study directly tests whether an SD patient can track a **brand-new** person's identity within a single conversation, which is the closest analogy to the SOLVER's task. Classic MTL amnesia impairs new face-name binding (confirmed via fMRI encoding studies) but the picture for *remote* person-knowledge sparing is not clean — retrograde loss shows a temporal gradient rather than uniform preservation. **Treat the double dissociation as real in direction, not as a clean, task-matched proof.**

**Decisive point for the structured-vs-bag-of-features design question (this drill's own synthesis, moderate-to-high confidence — a timescale argument, not a direct citation of a study on this exact question):** systems consolidation from hippocampal-style rapid conjunctive binding to cortical, ATL-hub-like distributed representation is a **slow, months-scale process** (Squire & Alvarez 1995; McClelland, McNaughton & O'Reilly 1995 complementary learning systems). A single novel-reading pass — the SOLVER's actual task — **never reaches the timescale at which ATL-hub machinery would be the operative representational regime for a newly-introduced fictional character.** The brain-appropriate analogy for this task is therefore **hippocampal rapid, one-shot, conjunctive/structured binding** (McClelland/O'Reilly-style arbitrary-feature binding: given-name + surname + title + context bound together in one act), not the ATL's slowly-built distributed convergence code.

**Direct answer to the SOLVER's design question:** **keep the structured given-name/surname/title/gender slots; do not move toward a bag-of-features sparse code.** This is a genuine, literature-grounded reversal of what a naive reading of "ATL = person-specific semantic hub" (as framed in the SOLVER's own brief) would suggest — the ATL hub is the right structure for a reader's *lifetime-accumulated* knowledge of a recurring or real person, but the wrong timescale/regime for tracking a person across the ~2,000 words of a single novel excerpt. The IAC model's SIU-pool structure (surname as a literal shared semantic feature node, see lever A) is itself evidence that even the ATL/PIN system's own internal architecture treats "surname" as a **discrete, named slot**, not an unstructured feature — reinforcing rather than undercutting the structured-slot design.

**P_deflated = 0.55** (higher than the accuracy-levers above because this is closer to a confirmed structural/timescale argument than novel synthesis, but still capped below the accuracy levers since no study directly tests this exact design comparison on fictional-character tracking).
**HARD-PASS (framed as a design-confirmation, not an accuracy lever):** if ever ablation-tested, replacing the structured given-name/surname/title slots with an unstructured bag-of-token-features code does **not** improve B-cubed F on LitBank and most likely regresses it (predicted, not yet run — flagging as a standing design constraint so a future solver doesn't silently re-attempt the "simplify to bag-of-features" refactor without re-deriving this reasoning).
**HARD-FAIL:** an unstructured bag-of-features ablation matches or beats the structured design — would be a genuinely surprising result given the timescale argument above and would warrant re-opening whether the hippocampal-conjunctive-binding analogy actually applies here.

## Cheap decisive test (single pre-registered LitBank run, extends the SOLVER's existing organ)

Add levers incrementally, **measuring each channel's precision/recall separately, never blended into one aggregate number** (per the honest-caveat discipline above — (C) especially should not be allowed to hide behind an aggregate that masks its lower per-channel accuracy):

2. **Arm 2 (lever B):** add on top of Arm 1: (a) apposition-pattern nickname detector, (b) truncate(+y) generative phonological matcher, (c) small stored irregular-pairs table. Report nickname-residual recovery rate and precision separately from overall F.
3. **Arm 3 (lever C):** add on top of Arm 2: semantic-type-gated nominal/epithet channel, reusing the Arm-1 salience ranking + a hard gender/age/human-hypernym compatibility gate + abstain-on-tie. Report nominal-residual recovery rate and this channel's own precision separately — **do not blend into overall F** given the expected lower accuracy.
4. **Arm 4 (lever D, exploratory only):** holding Arms 1–3 fixed, sweep the complete/separate prior itself (toward-merge vs. toward-separate) on the ambiguous-tie subset only, to directly test whether "fix the signal, don't move the threshold" (this note's D-synthesis) actually holds, or whether the threshold itself also needs adjustment.
5. **Arm 5 (lever E, structural, lower priority):** an ablation swapping structured slots for a bag-of-features code, run only if Arms 1–3 land and there is appetite to validate the structural design claim — not a near-term priority given (E)'s confirmatory framing.

## TLDR

We looked at five separate brain-science questions about how people tell characters apart in a story. The clearest, most useful finding: the tool that already exists for fixing "who does 'he' refer to" (a formula for how strongly a person is remembered, based on both how important their grammatical role was and how recently they were mentioned) is the same fix needed here for "which Bennet sister does a bare 'Miss Bennet' refer to" — it was designed once, for pronouns, and was never built; it should be built once and reused for both jobs. Second: the fact that the system currently confuses same-surname sisters is not really a bug — it matches how a well-known brain model of recognizing people (which represents family members as sharing a "family" tag that spreads activation between them) predicts they SHOULD be confusable from surname alone; the fix has to come from a different clue (the recency/role formula above), not from tightening the surname/gender check itself. Third: turning a vague phrase like "the girl" into "Elizabeth" is a real but genuinely harder problem than matching names — outside research on almost the same task tops out around 40% accuracy, so we should expect a partial win here, not a full fix, and should protect against it creating new wrong-merges. Fourth: nicknames are a mixed bag — about half are formed by a simple, teachable pattern (chop the name down, maybe add a "-y") that a computer can do generally, but the other half (Bob, Jack, Peggy, Sally) have no logical connection to the full name and must simply be memorized as a short list; separately, a cheap and very reliable trick is to watch for the author directly telling us the nickname ("Elizabeth, or Lizzy as her sisters called her"). Fifth: on whether to represent each character with separate slots (first name, last name, title) versus one blended fingerprint — the science says keep the separate slots, because the "blended fingerprint" style of memory takes months of real-world exposure to build, far longer than the time spent reading one novel.
