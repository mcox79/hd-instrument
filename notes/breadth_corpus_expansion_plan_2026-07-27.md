# Breadth corpus expansion plan — feed the substrate diverse experience (encoder AND reader)

Filed by: research (Sonnet lit-scan x2 in parallel + on-disk measurement, this session). READ-ONLY scoping — no cells built, no training run launched.

## HEADLINE

**Wikipedia (Simple English first, then full English) is the correct next corpus, and it is the only shortlisted candidate that feeds BOTH halves of the CLS loop well** — it is clean enough for the READER (situation_reader -> clarify_gate -> condenser -> learner MDL sleep -> `cskg_foundation_v1`) to extract explicit facts/relations from, and broad enough for the ENCODER (MLM representation training) to gain topic diversity beyond ARC's science bias. Obtain via `load_dataset("wikimedia/wikipedia", "20231101.en")` / `"20231101.simple"` on HuggingFace (pre-extracted, zero WikiExtractor step) — Simple English alone is a low-risk ~150-400M-token first pull (range, unverified exact count; measure on arrival with the same coverage-check script used for ARC).

**Coverage reframe (measured this session, on-disk, not modeled):** the naive worry that ARC's science register leaves everyday/social/emotional concepts near-ZERO is **not what the token-count data shows** — a direct grep-count of 40 probe words (`family`:102,473, `mother`:50,343, `respect`:15,979, `freedom`:9,456, `happy`:15,596 mentions) across the 237.7M-token ARC corpus finds most of them at high raw frequency (ARC's "science search results" construction pulls in social-studies/civics/health passages too, not pure natural science). **The real gap is REGISTER, not raw presence**: these words are hypothesized to occur in ARC mostly in expository/definitional framing ("family Felidae", "respect for the environment") rather than experiential/narrative framing (a child's relationship with their mother, feeling embarrassed at school) — this is NOT yet measured (would need per-mention context classification) and is flagged as the concrete first check to run before assuming Wikipedia "fixes" anything. The genuinely near-zero-count gap words in the probe were the low-frequency long-tail (`doorbell`:98, `apologize`:666, `wallet`:528, `etiquette`:515) — real but narrow.

**Biggest structural finding this session (USER course-correction, mid-task):** the 237M-token ARC scale run fed the ENCODER only (MLM representation learning). It never ran through the READER (`hdlab/situation_reader.py` -> `hdlab/clarify_gate.py` -> condenser -> learner MDL sleep) to extract explicit knowledge into `cskg_foundation_v1`. Every corpus recommended below is scoped for BOTH paths, not just bigger encoder training — see Section 5.

## 2. Coverage analysis — what ARC leaves uncovered

Measured this session, on-disk, streaming pass over `data/corpora/arc/ARC-V1-Feb2018-2/ARC_Corpus.txt` (237,666,846 alpha tokens) against 40 hand-picked probe words spanning four concept classes hypothesized to be under-served by a science-search corpus:

```
social:          friend 20148  family 102473  mother 50343  father 33131  neighbor 3227
                 stranger 2172  conversation 4952  promise 4726  apologize 666  greeting 855
emotion:         happy 15596  sad 4579  angry 4355  afraid 5211  jealous 1143
                 proud 3970  embarrassed 680  grateful 1966  lonely 1755  excited 4765
everyday_object: kitchen 5629  bedroom 2217  wallet 528  umbrella 1833  sandwich 1871
                 sidewalk 663  grocery 2214  doorbell 98  suitcase 280  pajamas 174
abstract_social: fairness 593  kindness 1341  honesty 975  respect 15979  freedom 9456
                 justice 9538  loyalty 1922  forgiveness 881  gossip 691  etiquette 515
```

**Honest read:** raw mention COUNT is mostly fine — ARC's "search-engine results for science search terms" construction pulls in social-studies/health/civics passages, so common social/emotional words are NOT near-zero. The real, unmeasured-but-plausible gap is **register**: these mentions likely skew toward expository/definitional sentences ("family Felidae", "respect for the environment", "the greenhouse effect makes the Earth warmer") rather than experiential/narrative sentences (a character feeling embarrassed, a promise between friends). This matters because the CLS reader extracts SITUATION MODELS (entities/events/time/causation) — a corpus that mentions "promise" 4,726 times inside science-explanation prose gives the reader almost no promise-KEEPING or promise-BREAKING events to extract, even though the word is "covered" by frequency.

**The concrete gap that IS clearly real (near-zero tail):** long-tail everyday nouns tied to specific physical/social scenes — `doorbell` (98), `wallet` (528), `etiquette` (515), `apologize` (666) — genuinely under-covered even at ARC's 237M-token scale, consistent with the prior session's finding that ARC's zero-mention tail was dominated by taxonomic Latin genus names (a different long tail, same shape: whatever a science-search corpus doesn't sample, stays thin regardless of overall corpus size).

**Recommended first check on Wikipedia arrival (cheap, before any training):** re-run the existing coverage-check script (validated on ARC, ~82s/pass) against the same 24,275-concept `cskg_foundation_v1` vocabulary, PLUS extend it with a lightweight register classifier (does the sentence contain a first/second-person pronoun, a past-tense narrative verb, an emotion/dialogue verb like "said"/"felt"/"asked"?) so the coverage table reports not just mention-count but REGISTER MIX per concept. This directly tests the hypothesis above rather than assuming it.

## 3. Integrity + mixing

**Per-corpus cleaning (reuse the ARC-plan's integrity shape, `notes/scale_corpus_and_data_integrity_plan_2026-07-26.md` section 2):**
- Wikipedia: drop disambiguation pages (by title pattern / Wikidata instance-of), drop list/index pages, strip residual infobox/template fragments and "See also"/category boilerplate, length filter (<4 tokens).
- OpenSubtitles: dedupe near-identical subtitle lines (same line reused across many films/episodes is extremely common — a naive dedup pass will collapse a large fraction of the raw line count), strip timing/formatting artifacts, filter non-English.
- Gutenberg/SPGC: strip PG header/footer boilerplate (standard in `pgcorpus`), drop non-English books, flag pre-1900 OCR noise for a lighter weight or quality-score-based downweighting rather than hard exclusion (real prose, just noisier).
- FineWeb sample: already passes Gopher/RefinedWeb-style heuristic filters + per-snapshot MinHash dedup (5-gram, ~0.75 Jaccard threshold) upstream — accept as-is, just verify the streamed sample isn't dominated by one snapshot year.

**Cross-corpus dedup:** exact-line-hash pass across the FULL mixed pool (a Wikipedia sentence quoted verbatim inside a book or subtitle is not rare) + MinHash-LSH near-dedup at paragraph granularity using `datasketch` or `text-dedup` (same tools the FineWeb/Dolma pipelines use) — run this AFTER per-corpus cleaning, BEFORE mixing, so a single duplicated passage cannot get 2-3x sampling weight by appearing in more than one source corpus.

**Mixing proportions (recommendation, not yet empirically tuned):** target a domain-balanced mix rather than raw-token-proportional, since Wikipedia + FineWeb will otherwise dwarf everything else by 1-2 orders of magnitude. Suggested first-pass target (tunable): ARC 15% / Wikipedia 45% / OpenSubtitles 15% / Gutenberg 15% / textbook+worldtree+mcguffey/graded (existing small corpora) 10% — upsample the small high-quality corpora, downsample the largest pool, consistent with brain-consistent "varied daily experience" rather than one register dominating exposure. Treat this as a first guess to revisit once the coverage-check script reports the actual register mix (Section 2) — if Wikipedia alone already fixes the register gap, the OpenSubtitles/Gutenberg proportions can be smaller.

**Reuse the concept-level leak-proof scrub verbatim** (per `scale_corpus_and_data_integrity_plan_2026-07-26.md` section 2e): build ONE held-out-concept list across the WHOLE mixed corpus (not per-source), scrub every corpus's mentions of held-out concepts (and lemma/inflection variants), and re-verify the zero-overlap witness (0/N) on the FINAL mixed stream, not per-source before mixing — mixing itself can reintroduce leakage if two sources aren't scrubbed against the identical concept list.

## 4. Concept-set expansion — what's missing and where to source it

`cskg_foundation_v1` (24,275 single-token concepts, degree>=2) is science/commonsense-leaning by construction (built from ConceptNet/CSKG/ATOMIC, cross-referenced 53.7% against Lancaster experiential norms). Already-on-disk assets not yet exploited for concept-set breadth:

- **`data/corpora/base_vocabulary/`** (measured this session): SUBTLEX-US frequency backbone (74,286 words, from 51M tokens of film/TV subtitles — itself a proxy for everyday spoken register) + Kuperman AoA ratings (31,317 words, learn-order) + Ogden Basic English 850 (minimal core-concept set) + Dolch sight words (221 service words + 97 nouns, graded). This is a ready-made, already-licensed, already-merged EVERYDAY/FUNCTION-WORD vocabulary list that has NOT yet been cross-referenced against `cskg_foundation_v1` for concept-gap analysis — recommend running that overlap check next (cheap, both files on disk) to get an exact "how many of the ~5,000 most common English words are NOT yet foundation concepts" number, the direct concept-count analog of Section 2's mention-count table.
- **`data/grounding_testbed/`** (~40k words, Lancaster/Brysbaert/Warriner/Kuperman norms): already the experiential-grounding source; any base-vocabulary word missing from `cskg_foundation_v1` but present here is a cheap concept to ADD (grounding data already exists, just needs a node).
- **Wikipedia article/redirect TITLES**: the single largest source of proper-noun and named-entity concepts (people, places, organizations, historical events) that ConceptNet/CSKG structurally under-represent (those are common-sense-relation KBs, not entity KBs) — mine titles + first-sentence definitional gloss as new candidate concept nodes, gated by the same grounding/relation-density bar used to build `cskg_foundation_v1` in the first place (don't admit every title; require some minimal relational connectivity).
- **Missing concept TYPES flagged qualitatively this session:** emotional-state concepts (embarrassed, grateful, jealous — present as words per Section 2 but likely thin as RELATIONAL nodes, i.e. connected to few edges in the KB), social-script concepts (apologizing, greeting, small talk — process/event concepts, which ConceptNet under-represents relative to object concepts), and specific everyday objects tied to domestic/social scenes (doorbell, wallet, etiquette — confirmed genuinely thin even in raw text, Section 2).

## 5. Encoder + reader unified ingestion (CLS — both halves, not encoder-only)

**Why this section exists:** the 237M-token ARC scale run trained the ENCODER (MLM/predict-context representation learning) only. It never ran through the READER (`hdlab/situation_reader.py` -> `hdlab/clarify_gate.py` -> condenser -> learner MDL sleep-consolidation -> `cskg_foundation_v1`) to extract explicit facts/relations. That is using each corpus for only half its value — the brain's CLS (complementary learning systems) architecture this project is explicitly built around (per `THE_PLAN_learned_grounded_representation_foundation_2026-07-26.md`) requires BOTH the cortical (slow, distributed, encoder-side) and hippocampal (fast-write, reader/sleep-side) pathways over the SAME experience stream, not one or the other.

**Current reader state (verified on disk this session, not modeled):**
- `hdlab/situation_reader.py` is a **glass-box symbolic** extractor (coref backbone + predicate/agent/patient event extraction + tense/connective temporal ordering + causal-network links, all composed from already-banked modules) over a Cowan-4-bounded working memory. It does NOT currently use any trained neural encoder for comprehension — it is pure structural/rule extraction. This means "the trained scale-encoder becomes the reader's comprehension engine" (the course-correction framing) is a **proposed integration, not yet built** — see the wiring proposal below.
- `hdlab/clarify_gate.py` is a stateless two-threshold conformal gate (`clarify_tau=0.35`, `refuse_tau=0.55`) that intercepts middle-confidence outputs and emits a CLARIFY/turn-taking signal instead of acting — currently calibrated for the M1.6 router's `max_sim` confidence score, not yet wired to any encoder-based unknown-concept score.
- **Known issues to fix BEFORE scaling the reader loop** (verified this session in `data/exp_ingest_learn_sleep_loop_cycle2_v1/metrics.json` and `cycle3_ppattach_v1/metrics.json`, not from memory):
  - **cycle2 = HURT_REGRESSION** (`partB_feedback_ablation.verdict`): the AGGRESSIVE clarify-gate override (fires on every >=2-candidate item) hurt accuracy (`acc_without_knowledge=0.9826` -> `acc_with_aggressive_override=0.6445`, delta -0.338) by overriding a correct structural prior; the CONSERVATIVE gate (override only when the structural pick is selectionally unsupported) was less bad but still negative (delta -0.146) on this cycle's item mix. `sleep_class_generalized_a_rule: False` — sleep consolidation did NOT generalize a rule from this cycle's reading, it only compressed per-item facts (`sleep_class_compression_ratio: 0.9075`, `n_rules: 34`, all per-item, no class-level generalization detected).
  - **cycle3 (PP-attachment) = MIDDLE_BAND**, `rise=+0.0157` (`acc_ingest_full=0.6794` vs `acc_structure=0.6637`), diagnosed explicitly in the cell's own verdict as `COVERAGE_GAP_or_underpowered_low_active_count` — only `coverage_frac=0.316` (141/446 test items had any reader-acquired knowledge active at all); where knowledge WAS active, the lift was real and larger (`active_lift=0.0496`), but the reader's throughput over its (small, graded-reader-scale) input corpus was too low to cover most of the eval set.
  - **The pattern across both cycles**: the reader mechanism itself shows real signal when it fires (cycle3's active-subset lift, cycle2's PASS_KNOWLEDGE_MOVES_PROBE part D at delta +0.25), but (a) gate aggressiveness needs to stay conservative (cycle2's lesson) and (b) coverage/throughput is the binding constraint (cycle3's lesson) — exactly the kind of problem more DIVERSE, higher-VOLUME reading material (this plan's whole point) is positioned to fix, provided the reader can actually process that volume (next point).
  - **Reader throughput at scale is itself an unbuilt capability, not an assumption.** `situation_reader` has only ever been run over small graded-reader/OneStop-scale passages (hundreds to low-thousands of sentences per cycle). Scaling symbolic coref+event+causal extraction to Wikipedia-article volume (283,711 Simple-English articles alone) requires a BATCH pipeline (chunked multi-process extraction, not the current per-passage interactive-scale use) — flag this explicitly as a required build step for whoever picks up this plan, not something that falls out of "just point the reader at a bigger corpus."

**Proposed wiring (design, not yet implemented) — how each corpus should flow through BOTH paths:**
1. **ENCODER path (unchanged in kind, bigger in scale):** corpus -> concept-level held-out scrub (Section 3) -> BPE tokenizer trained on the post-scrub stream -> MLM/predict-context training, exactly the `scale_meaning_learn_arc_heldout_v2` pipeline already in flight (`data/exp_scale_meaning_learn_arc_heldout_v2_smoke/ckpt_seed_7.pt` is the reusable checkpoint format). This yields the encoder checkpoint.
2. **READER path (the new wiring):** corpus (Wikipedia first — cleanest, most fact-dense, best-suited to symbolic extraction) -> `situation_reader.read(passage)` at BATCH scale (build step above) -> per-passage `SituationModel` (entities/events/time/causation) -> `clarify_gate` flags passages/concepts where extraction confidence is middle-band (candidate unknowns, driving WHAT to acquire next, i.e. an active-reading signal rather than passive corpus consumption) -> condenser -> learner MDL-gated sleep-consolidation into `cskg_foundation_v1` (new nodes/edges, or edge-weight reinforcement of existing ones).
3. **The coupling point (where the encoder becomes the reader's comprehension engine):** feed the ENCODER's trained token/passage representations into `clarify_gate` as an additional confidence signal alongside the existing structural `max_sim` score — an encoder-embedding novelty/OOD score (distance from the nearest known concept's learned representation) is a natural, non-borrowed (own-trained) unknown-concept detector, replacing or supplementing whatever heuristic currently decides "this concept is under-specified." This keeps the reader glass-box at the SYMBOLIC-STRUCTURE level (coref/event/causal extraction stays rule-based and auditable) while letting the encoder's distributional knowledge decide WHEN to trigger clarify/acquire — consistent with the charter's "no borrowed embeddings AS the encoder" invariant, since this is our own scale-trained encoder, not a borrowed one.
4. **Which corpora suit which path:** Wikipedia (both — fact-dense AND broad, best first target for the reader's batch-extraction build); Gutenberg/SPGC narrative (reader-favored — real coref/event/causal structure across coherent multi-sentence passages, better exercise for the situation-model machinery than Wikipedia's list-like fact register); OpenSubtitles and FineWeb (encoder-only for now — dialogue turns and open-web text are not well-formed situation-model passages for the current symbolic reader; revisit once/if the reader gains a dialogue-specific or looser extraction mode).

## 6. Practicalities

- **Download size/time:** Simple English Wikipedia parquet via HF ~is small (low GB, minutes on any reasonable connection); full English Wikipedia parquet is tens of GB (hours); OpenSubtitles English subset via OPUS is GB-scale; Gutenberg/SPGC full snapshot is ~65GB all-granularities / 3.6GB unigram-only (pull a curated top-N-books subset first, not the full crawl, to control disk and cleaning effort).
- **Disk:** local machine + remote RTX 4060 Ti 8GB. Recommend staging raw+cleaned corpora on the LOCAL machine (same discipline as `data/corpora/arc/`, gitignored/local-only per the existing store discipline) and SCP only the final tokenized/scrubbed training shards to remote for the GPU run — mirrors how the ARC corpus + foundation were staged for `scale_meaning_learn_arc_heldout_v1`. The 8GB GPU is a training-compute constraint, not a data-size constraint; the coverage-check/dedup/scrub preprocessing is CPU-only and can run local before anything touches the GPU.
- **Recommended FIRST broad run:** Simple English Wikipedia alone (not yet mixed with anything else), same architecture as `scale_meaning_learn_arc_heldout_v2` (from-scratch transformer, concept-level leak-proof scrub, checkpoint-saving), token target = whatever Simple-Wiki actually measures out to after cleaning (expect same order of magnitude as ARC's 237M, verify don't assume) — run the coverage-check + register-mix extension (Section 2) and the dedup/leak gates (Section 3) as a CPU preflight BEFORE the GPU run, exactly the four-gate cheap-decisive-test discipline used for ARC. In PARALLEL (non-competing, CPU-local), build and smoke-test the reader BATCH pipeline (Section 5) over a small Simple-Wikipedia sample (e.g. 1,000 articles) to validate throughput and re-check the cycle2/cycle3 gate-aggressiveness and coverage issues before committing to a full-corpus reader pass.
- **Expected coverage lift:** unverified until measured on arrival (do not assume); Wikipedia's breadth argument rests on TOPIC diversity (all fields vs ARC's science-search skew) more than raw token-count, since Section 2 showed ARC's raw mention-counts for common words are already reasonable — the lift to look for is in the LONG TAIL (proper nouns, non-science domains, the specific thin words identified in Section 2) and in REGISTER diversity (narrative/experiential sentences vs expository ones), not in fixing a token-count cliff that mostly isn't there.

## Cheap decisive test

Before committing to a full training run on any new corpus: (1) run the existing ARC-validated coverage-check script against the new corpus, extended with the register-mix classifier proposed in Section 2 (first/second-person pronoun + narrative-verb + dialogue-verb heuristics) — must report BOTH mention-count AND register mix per concept, not count alone; (2) exact-dup + sampled near-dup rate on the corpus (Section 3), below threshold; (3) verified-zero-overlap held-out-concept leak check on the FULL mixed stream (not per-source), exactly 0 leaked windows; (4) a small (1,000-item) reader-batch-pipeline smoke run, checking that clarify_gate stays in its CONSERVATIVE regime (cycle2's lesson) and that coverage_frac clears some pre-registered floor (cycle3's lesson — e.g. >0.5, not 0.316) before trusting a full reader pass. A failure at any gate blocks the full run.

## Substrate-product implications

If Wikipedia clears the coverage/register gates and the reader-batch pipeline hits a workable coverage floor, this is the path that turns the substrate from a science-narrow demonstrator into something that can plausibly converse about everyday/social topics — directly serving the north-star (glass-box substrate you can converse with). Equally important: if the reader-batch pipeline can NOT be made to clear a reasonable coverage floor even on clean Wikipedia prose (i.e. cycle3's coverage_frac=0.316 problem persists at scale), that is a decisive, cheap-to-discover signal that the READER side of CLS (not the encoder side) is this project's next real bottleneck — a different, structural finding from "we need more data," and one this plan's Section 5 preflight is specifically designed to surface early rather than after a full corpus commitment.

## Calibration (per lit-scan calibration-penalty discipline)

P(Simple-English-Wikipedia clears the coverage+register+leak gates and improves held-out-NEW-concept generalization over ARC-only, same margin class as the ARC win) = **0.40** (deflated from a naive-higher read; Wikipedia's clean license and topic breadth are near-certain, but whether TOPIC breadth translates into a measurable generalization gain — versus register/mention-count already being adequate per Section 2's surprising result — is genuinely unmeasured). Both are novel-synthesis design estimates (capped, per discipline, well under 0.50).

**HARD-FAIL for the reader half specifically**: if a 1,000-article Simple-Wikipedia reader-batch smoke test still shows coverage_frac <= 0.316 (no improvement over cycle3's graded-reader-scale result) AND/OR the conservative clarify-gate still regresses accuracy (repeat of cycle2's HURT_REGRESSION direction, any magnitude), that is evidence the reader's bottleneck is NOT corpus scale/diversity but the extraction/gate MECHANISM itself — redirect the next research/build cycle to the reader's internal design (event/coref extraction recall, gate calibration) rather than sourcing yet more text.
