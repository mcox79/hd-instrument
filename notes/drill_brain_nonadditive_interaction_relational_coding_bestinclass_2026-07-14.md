## HEADLINE

The brain does not solve interaction/asymmetry with one universal trick — it layers **two separable mechanisms** that map onto two separable fixes for our substrate:

1. **Genuine non-additive (AND/XOR/configural) structure** is represented by **conjunctive units** (hippocampal DG/CA3 sparse conjunctive cells; PFC "nonlinear mixed selectivity" neurons) that multiplicatively/nonlinearly combine inputs, expanding dimensionality so a purely **linear readout downstream can now separate what raw additive inputs could not** (this is the textbook resolution of the XOR problem: expand nonlinearly, then read out linearly). Critically, in cortex this nonlinear expansion is believed to be substantially **UNSTRUCTURED/RANDOM at the mixing stage**, with **only the readout weights learned** (Fusi/Rigotti's "why neurons mix" argument) — i.e. CONSTRUCT the expansion, LEARN the readout.
2. **Genuine order/role-asymmetry** ("A relates to B" != "B relates to A") is represented by **multiplicative/bilinear interactions** — parietal **gain fields** (eye-position x retinal-position, multiplicative not additive) are the clearest documented neural instance of an asymmetric, non-commutative combination rule, and they are known to be acquired/calibrated through sensorimotor experience (i.e. **LEARNABLE**, not fixed at birth).

**Top-ranked brain-aligned mechanism for our substrate: LEARNED low-rank bilinear/gated multiplicative binding** — replace or augment the current elementwise-product bind `z = a ⊙ b` with a trained factored-bilinear form (e.g. `z = (Pa) ⊙ (Qb)` with P, Q learned projection matrices, per Kim et al. 2016 "Hadamard Product for Low-rank Bilinear Pooling," arXiv:1610.04325, and the Memisevic factored-gating family). This is **LEARNABLE** (not merely CONSTRUCT): the plain Hadamard product is confirmed in the literature to be a special-case, expressively-limited **low-rank approximation** of the full bilinear/outer-product interaction — i.e. today's substrate bind is already "bilinear pooling with P=Q=identity." Making P and Q learned parameters is a minimal, well-precedented upgrade, not a new operator class. It is brain-aligned via two independent lines of evidence: (i) parietal **gain fields** (Zipser & Andersen 1988, *Nature* 331:679-684) are a multiplicative, asymmetric, experience-calibrated combination rule, and (ii) PFC neurons conjunctively/multiplicatively coding rank × item-identity for sequences (Dehaene, Meyniel, Wacongne, Wang & Pallier 2015, *Neuron* 88:2-19) are explicitly characterized as a gain-field-like mechanism generalized from spatial coordinates to abstract order — both are learned/plastic, not fixed at the mixing stage. Our substrate already possesses a related but heavier non-commutative primitive (GHRR matrix-vector bind, validated in the June 2026 pilot — see Cross-thread synthesis); the low-rank bilinear-pooling framing is the cheaper, better-precedented, and more directly brain-matched way to reach the same non-commutative + interaction-capable destination.

A second, cheaper, narrower fix targets ONLY the order/asymmetry half of the gap (not full AND/XOR interaction capacity): apply a **fixed random permutation** to one operand before binding (standard VSA practice — Gayler's MAP architecture names Permute as a third first-class primitive alongside Bind/Bundle specifically for this; Plate's circular-convolution HRR bind is already "neither commutative nor associative in general," per the Schlegel & Neubert 2020 VSA survey, arXiv:2001.11797, and permutation sharpens/guarantees the asymmetry). This is **CONSTRUCT**, near-zero-cost, and well-established, but is a narrower fix (breaks symmetry/order) than the learned-bilinear mechanism (which additionally provides genuine multiplicative interaction capacity for AND/XOR-class problems).

## (a) How the brain represents non-additive/interaction and asymmetric/ordered relations

### A1. Conjunctive coding (hippocampal DG/CA3) — the substrate for "AND, not sum"
Dentate gyrus grants sparse, high-dimensional conjunctive codes; CA3 recurrent collaterals bind arbitrary co-active inputs into a single attractor pattern. The classic computational argument (O'Reilly & McClelland 1994, "Hippocampal conjunctive encoding, storage, and recall: avoiding a trade-off," *Hippocampus*) is that DG's extreme sparsity buys **pattern separation** — two overlapping input patterns are driven to near-orthogonal outputs — which is precisely what lets CA3 store the conjunction "A-and-B-together" as a code that does NOT overlap with "A alone" or "B alone." An additive/linear combination of A's code and B's code cannot do this: it necessarily sits at the vector sum, correlated with both individual codes.

Behaviorally, this conjunctive machinery is the proposed substrate for **configural association** (Sutherland & Rudy 1989, "Configural association theory: the role of the hippocampal formation in learning, memory, and amnesia," *Psychobiology*): hippocampal lesions selectively impair tasks that require treating a compound stimulus as a unique whole (negative patterning, biconditional discrimination — see (a3) below) while sparing simple elemental (single-cue) discriminations.

### A2. PFC nonlinear mixed selectivity — the dimensionality-expansion argument
Rigotti, Barak, Warden, Wang, Carnevale, Miller & Fusi (2013), "The importance of mixed selectivity in complex cognitive tasks," *Nature* 497:585-590, is the load-bearing reference here. The precise mathematical claim: neurons with **nonlinear mixed selectivity** — firing rate depends on a NONLINEAR (multiplicative/conjunctive) combination of two or more task variables, not their weighted sum — populate a representational space whose dimensionality is high enough that ANY downstream input-output mapping, including non-linearly-separable ones like XOR, becomes solvable by a single downstream LINEAR readout (a perceptron). Pure linear mixed selectivity (weighted sums of the same variables) caps the achievable dimensionality and provably cannot support such mappings. Follow-up: Fusi, Miller & Rigotti (2016), "Why neurons mix: high dimensionality for higher cognition," *Current Opinion in Neurobiology* 37:66-74 — argues the nonlinear mixing can be largely **random/unstructured** (not task-specifically hand-tuned) and still confers the needed expansion, as long as the final readout is trained on the task. This is the direct neural analog of "random nonlinear feature expansion + learned linear readout" (see (b) and A4/computational section).

### A3. Configural learning and the XOR-equivalence of negative patterning
Pearce (1994), "Similarity and discrimination: a selective review and a connectionist model," *Psychological Review* 101:587-607, formalizes **configural theory**: an animal treats a compound stimulus (AB) as generating its own unique internal representation, distinct from the sum of A's and B's individual associations — in contrast to Rescorla & Wagner's (1972) **elemental** theory, where compound learning is strictly a linear sum of separately-updated element-outcome associations.

The decisive behavioral test is **negative patterning** (A+, B+, AB-) and **biconditional discrimination** (AB+, CD-, AC-, BD+). These tasks are the animal-learning analog of XOR: an elemental/linear model necessarily predicts the compound response as V(A)+V(B) (both driven positive by A+ and B+ training), which cannot simultaneously satisfy AB- — the four training points are not linearly separable, the same proof Minsky & Papert (1969, *Perceptrons*) used for a single-layer unit and XOR. Yet rats, pigeons, rabbits, honeybees, and humans reliably learn negative patterning and biconditional discriminations (biconditional is the harder four-term generalization and is learned more slowly than negative patterning — Rescorla 1972, "Analysis of the unique cue in configural discriminations"). This is strong empirical proof the brain's associative-learning machinery is NOT restricted to additive/elemental combination.

**Important caveat surfaced on cross-check:** the hippocampal-necessity claim for negative patterning specifically is **contested, not settled**. Sutherland & Rudy's original 1989 Configural Association Theory proposed hippocampal necessity for configural discriminations, and McDonald and colleagues found fornix/hippocampal lesions impaired several configural-relational tasks; but Rudy's own later group found hippocampal-lesioned rats *could* still acquire negative patterning under some conditions, and **Rudy & Sutherland (1995), "Configural association theory and the hippocampal formation: an appraisal and reconfiguration," *Hippocampus*, explicitly walked back the strong all-or-none hippocampal-dependency claim.** The broader claim that configural/relational learning recruits the hippocampal formation in many paradigms (spatial biconditional, conditional-contextual discrimination) has better support than the specific "hippocampus is necessary for negative patterning" claim. Treat the hippocampal-specificity of A1/A3 as probable-but-not-certain; the underlying XOR-equivalence / conjunctive-representation-is-required argument itself is NOT in question — only which brain structure supplies the conjunctive unit is contested.

Also relevant: the elemental-vs-configural debate is not fully resolved either — hybrid "unique-cue" accounts (originally Rescorla 1972, sometimes attributed to Wagner in secondary sources) modify Rescorla-Wagner by adding an extra, separately-learned "unique cue" representing the specific compound, which supplies the same nonlinear/XOR-solving power while staying closer to elemental machinery; Melchers, Shanks & Lachnit (2012) argue for combined elemental-representation-plus-configural-mapping accounts. All variants agree a genuine conjunctive/interaction term is required somewhere in the system — they differ on where it lives, not on whether it's needed.

### A4. Combination-sensitive neurons — relations as first-class neural objects
Mustache-bat auditory cortex contains **delay-tuned "combination-sensitive" neurons** (Suga and colleagues, e.g. O'Neill & Suga 1979, *Science*, and later Suga reviews) that respond maximally to a SPECIFIC TIME DELAY between the emitted echolocation pulse and the returning echo — i.e. the neuron encodes the RELATION between two events (pulse, echo), not either event's presence alone, and not their sum. This is a clean existence proof that cortex can hard-wire a genuine two-argument RELATION as a single unit's tuning function, and that such tuning is measurably non-additive (response is not predicted by summing pulse-alone and echo-alone responses) and — because it's a *delay*, i.e. pulse-then-echo, not echo-then-pulse — inherently **order-sensitive**.

### A5. Gain fields — the clearest asymmetric/multiplicative binding mechanism
Parietal cortex neurons combine retinal position and eye position **multiplicatively** (gain fields: first characterized by Andersen & Mountcastle 1983; formalized computationally by Zipser & Andersen 1988, "A back-propagation programmed network that simulates response properties of a subset of posterior parietal neurons," *Nature* 331:679-684; basis-function theory in Pouget & Sejnowski 1997, "Spatial transformations in the parietal cortex using basis functions," *J. Cognitive Neuroscience*). The multiplicative combination `f(retinal) x g(eye-position)` is a **basis function** whose value depends jointly on BOTH variables in a way a weighted sum cannot reproduce: additive combination of two tuning curves just gives a wider/shifted single-variable response (the two inputs are NOT separably recoverable), whereas the multiplicative/basis-function joint code preserves a nonlinear joint representation from which both the original variable AND its transform can be linearly extracted downstream — and, because it cannot be decomposed back into its factors without already knowing one of them, it is a genuinely conjunctive, order/role-sensitive bind, not a symmetric sum. This exact multiplicative "gain-field" logic has since been found generalized from spatial coordinates to abstract sequence order: PFC neurons (SMA/pre-SMA/SEF/LPFC) conjunctively code rank x item-identity for sequences (Shima & Tanji 2000; Berdyyeva & Olson 2009, 2010), explicitly characterized as a gain-field-like mechanism by Dehaene, Meyniel, Wacongne, Wang & Pallier (2015), "The Neural Representation of Sequences: From Transition Probabilities to Algebraic Patterns and Linguistic Trees," *Neuron* 88:2-19.

### A6. Oscillatory phase coding for sequence order
Lisman & Idiart (1995), "Storage of 7 +/- 2 short-term memories in oscillatory subcycles," *Science* 267:1512-1515, and Lisman & Jensen (2013), "The Theta-Gamma Neural Code," *Neuron* 77:1002-1016, propose that **theta-gamma phase coding** in hippocampus encodes SEQUENCE ORDER: each item's assembly fires in a distinct gamma sub-cycle nested at a specific phase within the theta cycle (item 1 early, item 2 later, etc.), so "A-before-B" and "B-before-A" correspond to physically distinct phase-assembly bindings — natively order-sensitive by construction. Direct human empirical support: Heusser, Poeppel, Ezzyat & Davachi (2016), "Episodic sequence memory is supported by a theta-gamma phase code," *Nature Neuroscience* 19:1374-1380 — items in different sequence positions show reliably ordered gamma-power phase differences. Caveat: this mechanism is well-evidenced for *temporal/sequential* order specifically, but the literature does not establish it as a general-purpose mechanism for arbitrary *semantic* role-asymmetry (subject-vs-object, cause-vs-effect) independent of presentation order — generalizing beyond sequence memory is speculative, and there is no established discrete-VSA analog, so it is ranked lowest in the shortlist below.

### A7. Tensor-product / role-filler binding — the connectionist-modeling bridge
Smolensky (1990), "Tensor product variable binding and the representation of symbolic structures in connectionist systems," *Artificial Intelligence* 46:159-216, is the foundational CONSTRUCTED mechanism: bind role r and filler f via outer product `r (x) f`, a matrix whose (i,j) entry is `r_i . f_j` — preserving the FULL cross-product of every component of r against every component of f (not a per-coordinate product), and inherently non-commutative (`r (x) f != f (x) r`; row-role vs. column-filler are structurally distinct slots). This directly generalizes to genuine k-ary interaction (a binary relation instance embeds as a rank-3 tensor), at the cost of dimensionality growing as the PRODUCT of role-dim x filler-dim (the classical "combinatorial explosion" critique that motivated Plate's compressed HRR alternative). Conjunctive coding (A1) is the literature's proposed biological substrate for TPR-like binding: a neuron/population jointly sensitive to BOTH a role signal and a filler signal instantiates one "slot" of the tensor product (Nematzadeh, Ahmed & Miikkulainen, "Learning to Perform Role-Filler Binding with Schematic Knowledge," PMC8019313). Eliasmith's Semantic Pointer Architecture uses circular convolution (a compressed/lossy relative of the outer product) at ~2.5M-neuron scale specifically because literal TPR does not scale biologically (Crawford, Gingerich & Eliasmith 2016, "Biologically Plausible, Human-Scale Knowledge Representation," *Cognitive Science*).

### A8. Role-filler asymmetry in hippocampal/PFC relational memory (systems-level evidence)
Eichenbaum's relational memory theory holds the hippocampus rapidly forms associations among disparate elements and supports comparing those relations against new input (reviewed in Konkel & Cohen 2009, *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience*). The key behavioral signature is **transitive inference**: from overlapping pairs A>B and B>C, the hippocampal system supports the novel *ordered* inference A>C — intrinsically asymmetric, since "A>B" is not the same fact as "B>A." The **Tolman-Eichenbaum Machine** (Whittington et al. 2020, *Cell* 183:1249-1263) formalizes this computationally by factorizing memory into a structural/relational (grid-like) code conjoined with sensory content — a direct model analog of role-filler binding, where the same content bound to different structural/relational codes yields different composite representations. Dorsolateral PFC separately encodes relationships between working-memory items and promotes subsequent memory for those relationships (Blumenfeld, Parks, Yonelinas & Ranganath work, PMC3970078). Caveat: the primary hippocampal-physiology literature uses "relational binding"/"ordered inference," not an explicit role-R1-vs-role-R2 formalism — the tight role-filler framing is more native to the VSA/cognitive-modeling literature (A7) than to primary hippocampal physiology.

## (b) How is it learned? (random expansion + linear readout vs. learned interaction)

The literature does NOT resolve to a single answer — direct evidence exists for both, and for a middle path where the "fixed expansion" is itself partly learned:

- **Fixed/unstructured nonlinear expansion + learned linear readout** (Fusi/Rigotti's account of PFC mixed selectivity, A2 above) is the textbook computational-sufficiency account: nonlinear mixing can arise from largely random synaptic connectivity, with all task-specific learning in the readout weights — mathematically the same strategy as **random-feature methods / reservoir computing**: Rahimi & Recht (2007), "Random Features for Large-Scale Kernel Machines," NeurIPS, show a fixed random nonlinear projection + learned linear model approximates arbitrary kernel machines (the standard kernel-trick resolution of XOR-class non-separability); Jaeger's (2001) echo-state networks and Maass, Natschlager & Markram's (2002) liquid state machines are the recurrent instantiations (fixed random recurrent expansion, trained linear readout).

- **Direct evidence the "fixed" expansion is itself partly LEARNED, not purely random:** Dang, Jaffe, Qi & Constantinidis (2021), "Emergence of Nonlinear Mixed Selectivity in Prefrontal Cortex after Training," *J. Neuroscience* 41:7420, directly tested pre- vs. post-training monkey PFC and found nonlinear mixed selectivity INCREASED significantly with training for a spatial working-memory task (6.2% -> 12.3% of neurons, p=1.13e-7) but showed no significant increase for a shape/feature task (5.8% -> 6.7%, p=0.31) — task-selective but genuine evidence the interaction structure itself is experience-dependent, not fixed at birth. Lindsay, Rigotti, Warden, Miller & Fusi ("Hebbian Learning in a Random Network Captures Selectivity Properties of Prefrontal Cortex," PMC4125086) show Hebbian rewiring of an initially random recurrent network reproduces PFC-like mixed-selectivity statistics BETTER than a purely fixed-random network. Cerebellum-like expansion-coding theory shows the same pattern: the classical Marr-Albus view (fixed random granule-cell expansion, trained Purkinje-cell readout only) is now supplemented by models showing Hebbian plasticity WITHIN the expansion layer itself improves generalization relative to a strictly fixed-random expansion (reviewed via Litwin-Kumar and colleagues' cerebellar-expansion work).

- **Learned/plastic interaction structure for configural/conjunctive learning** (A1, A3): Pearce's configural nodes are proposed to develop THROUGH experience as specific compounds are encountered, not sampled at random up front; the unique-cue models (Rescorla 1972) likewise LEARN the added conjunctive term via ordinary trial-outcome updates. Gain fields (A5) are calibrated/re-calibrated by sensorimotor experience (prism-adaptation, strabismus, developmental tuning studies) — the multiplicative interaction itself, not just a downstream readout, is plastic.

**Bottom line for our substrate:** treat "fixed random expansion, learned readout only" as a real but INCOMPLETE account — the more current picture (Dang et al. 2021, Lindsay et al.) is that biological circuits use a broad, largely-random-at-onset nonlinear/multiplicative scaffold that is ALSO reshaped by experience where it matters behaviorally. This favors testing the fixed-expansion hypothesis FIRST (cheapest, isolates whether expansion alone suffices) but not stopping there if it partially fails — the brain's own account predicts a learned-interaction layer is needed on top for the harder cases.

### How to test the top 2 in our shared-code+readout harness

- **Rank 1 (learned low-rank bilinear bind):** Replace the current elementwise-product bind `z = a (.) b` with a trained factored form `z = (Pa) (.) (Qb)` (P != Q, both learned low-rank projection matrices), trained end-to-end via gradient descent on the relation-prediction objective in the SAME shared-code+readout harness used for the commutative-bind baseline. Measure held-out novel-pair accuracy on (i) the existing abelian/commutative relation set (must NOT regress) and (ii) a constructed asymmetric-relation set + an AND/XOR-equivalent (negative-patterning-style) synthetic relation set (must newly pass). **HARD-PASS: novel-pair accuracy >= 0.70 on the asymmetric/interaction set, with <5% relative regression on the existing abelian set. HARD-FAIL: <= 0.20** (indistinguishable from the current commutative-bind failure floor), or >15% relative regression on the abelian set (a genuine architectural tradeoff, not just "didn't help").
- **Rank 2 (fixed random permutation to break symmetry):** Apply a frozen random permutation `pi` to one operand before binding (e.g. `z = a (.) pi(b)`), zero new trainable parameters beyond the existing readout. Test on a NARROWER synthetic set: pure order-flip discrimination (A-relates-B vs. B-relates-A on relations that are otherwise additive, NOT a full AND/XOR interaction task, since permutation alone does not add genuine multiplicative interaction capacity). **HARD-PASS: order-discrimination accuracy >= 0.70. HARD-FAIL: <= 0.20** (chance-level for a binary order-flip task).

Running both in the same cycle is cheap (both reuse the existing harness/readout) and cleanly separates the two sub-problems named in the task trigger: rank 2 alone tests whether order/asymmetry is fixable near-zero-cost; rank 1 tests whether genuine non-additive interaction (the harder AND/XOR-class problem) requires a learned bilinear interaction, which per (b)'s Dang et al. 2021 finding is the more brain-consistent expectation.

## Cheap decisive test (single recommended first move)

Run **Rank 2 (fixed random permutation) and Rank 3 (fixed random bilinear expansion + learned readout) FIRST, in parallel with Rank 1** — both rank 2 and rank 3 are strictly cheaper than rank 1 (zero or near-zero new trainable interaction parameters) and isolate whether cheap, brain-precedented fixes (symmetry-breaking alone; dimensionality-expansion alone) already close some or all of the gap before committing to the heavier learned-bilinear-pooling (Rank 1) path. Per (b), the current brain evidence (Dang et al. 2021; Lindsay et al.) predicts ranks 2-3 will likely handle PURE order/asymmetry but NOT the full AND/XOR-class interaction problem — Rank 1 is expected to be necessary for the harder cases regardless, so running all three in one cycle (cheap, shared harness) is more efficient than a strict waterfall.

## Falsifiable predictions

- **HARD-PASS (Rank 2, permutation-only symmetry-breaking):** pure order-flip discrimination accuracy >= 0.70, zero new trainable parameters.
- **HARD-FAIL (Rank 2):** <= 0.20 on order-discrimination -> even the cheapest fix fails; proceed to ranks 1/3.
- **HARD-PASS (Rank 3, fixed random bilinear expansion + learned readout):** novel asymmetric/AND-XOR-equivalent pair accuracy >= 0.60 with zero new trainable interaction parameters.
- **HARD-FAIL (Rank 3):** <= 0.20 (no better than current commutative-bind floor) -> expansion alone is insufficient, Rank 1 is required.
- **HARD-PASS (Rank 1, learned bilinear pooling):** novel asymmetric/AND-XOR-equivalent pair accuracy >= 0.70 AND no regression (<5% relative drop) on existing abelian/commutative relation accuracy.
- **HARD-FAIL (Rank 1):** <= 0.20, OR a regression >15% relative on the existing abelian set (would indicate the learned bilinear form destabilizes the already-working commutative-discovery capability — a genuine architectural tradeoff, not just "didn't help").
