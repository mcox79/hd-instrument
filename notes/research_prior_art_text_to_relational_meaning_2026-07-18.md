## HEADLINE

Text -> structured relational meaning is mature **as a representation-design problem** (AMR-style predicate-argument graphs, PropBank/FrameNet role backbones, DRS discourse-referent structures are all settled, decades-old, well-benchmarked targets -- adopt, don't reinvent) and mature-with-caveats **as a fully-supervised parsing problem** (AMR ~83-88 Smatch, SRL 78-88 F1, DRS ~90-96 F1 English -- all trained on large hand-annotated treebanks: 59k AMR sentences, PropBank, 1,200+ FrameNet frames built by expert lexicographers over years). None of that supervision regime is ours. The regime that IS ours -- denotation/weak/grounded supervision with little-to-no hand-labeled logical form -- is also a real, working, decades-old mainstream sub-field (Clarke/Liang/Berant/Artzi-Zettlemoyer), but its own literature is explicit that it reaches full-supervision parity ONLY in small closed domains (GeoQuery) and degrades hard as the domain opens (WikiTableQuestions ~37% baseline). Fully unsupervised structure induction (grammar induction, zero supervision) has sat 30-40 F1 points below supervised parsing for a full decade of architectural progress (DMV -> PRPN -> DIORA -> compound PCFG) -- ruling out "just induce it from nothing" as a shortcut. The one genuinely load-bearing, still-open finding for us: **compositional/systematic generalization is not a supervision problem, it's an architecture problem** -- flat seq2seq parsers (any supervision regime) fail SCAN/COGS-style systematic generalization (16-35% on COGS structural splits) while an explicitly COMPOSITIONAL parser (AM-parser) recovers to ~98% on the same benchmark, USING THE SAME SUPERVISED DATA. That is the one place the mainstream literature names an unsolved architectural gap that a native-compositional-binding substrate is a plausible (not yet proven) candidate to close, at open-domain generality and under a grounded/modest-supervision regime nobody in this scan has demonstrated together. Everything else (the target representation, the role-graph vocabulary, the "denotation-only signal works in narrow domains" learning philosophy) should be adopted and credited, not treated as novel.

## Cheap decisive test

Build a small, real (not construction-determined) **systematic-generalization split** in the style of COGS/SCAN, scoped to our grade-appropriate SVO/role-filler regime: hold out COMBINATIONS never seen together at training time (e.g., a known verb-frame applied to a role-filler pairing that never co-occurred in training; a role-reversal minimal pair; a familiar preposition-role mapping applied to a novel argument type). Compare, on the SAME held-out systematic-generalization split (not the in-distribution split):

1. **Baseline A (current hand-rule / pattern-match extractor)** -- the existing glass-box pipeline, unchanged.
2. **Baseline B (a flat/memorization-shaped scorer)** -- any component that scores role-filler fit by surface co-occurrence/frequency alone, deliberately built to mimic the flat-seq2seq failure mode the literature predicts.
3. **Candidate (native VSA compositional bind)** -- the same role-assignment logic, but role-filler combination is computed via native bind/unbind (compositional-by-construction) rather than pattern lookup or frequency scoring.

Score RELATION-F1 and comprehension-Q accuracy separately, in-distribution AND on the held-out combination split, per the fair-test design gate already established in the sibling relation-reader note (independent gold, real baselines, difficulty-on, one variable at a time).

**HARD-PASS:** native-bind candidate holds accuracy roughly flat (or degrades only mildly, e.g. <15 relative points) between in-distribution and held-out-combination splits, while Baselines A and B collapse by a COGS/SCAN-magnitude margin (>40-60 relative points) on the same held-out split. This would be the first demonstration in either this literature or ours that compositional binding delivers the SCAN/COGS-style generalization win at the text-to-relation-extraction stage, not just at the downstream symbolic-parsing stage (AM-parser) the literature already showed it for.

**HARD-FAIL:** the native-bind candidate degrades on the held-out split by a margin comparable to (or worse than) Baselines A/B. This would mean the compositional-generalization advantage the literature shows for structured *symbolic* parsers (AM-parser on COGS) does NOT transparently transfer to the upstream *encoder* problem (text -> which role does this filler occupy), and the real bottleneck is elsewhere (most likely the multi-cue role-assignment "encoder" stage already flagged as the sole real gap in the sibling thematic-roles note) -- refuting the strategic read above and redirecting effort away from "native binding solves generalization" toward the encoder-learning problem specifically.

## Falsifiable predictions (HARD-PASS / HARD-FAIL)

### Prediction 1 -- representation adoption is a non-event
**Claim:** adopting an AMR/PropBank/DRS-flavored target representation (predicate + numbered/frame-ish roles + reentrant coreference nodes + nested embedded propositions) costs nothing novel and should not be treated as a research contribution.
- **HARD-PASS:** our existing relation-store schema (per the sibling thematic-roles blueprint) already maps cleanly onto this vocabulary with no redesign needed (Kintsch proposition <-> AMR/PropBank predicate-argument node; situation model <-> discourse-referent accumulation).
- **HARD-FAIL:** if mapping our schema to this vocabulary requires structural redesign (e.g. we cannot represent reentrancy/coreference the way AMR does, or nested propositions don't compose the way DRS boxes do), that is itself a finding worth a follow-up drill -- flag, don't force-fit.

### Prediction 2 -- denotation/grounded supervision is the right learning-signal philosophy for us, at a cost
**Claim:** training/scoring the reader against comprehension-Q answer correctness (a checkable denotation) rather than requiring gold triples at every step is a mature, adoptable learning-signal choice (Clarke/Liang/Berant lineage), not a novel idea -- but the literature's own ceiling data (GeoQuery parity vs. WikiTableQuestions ~37%) predicts this will work well in narrow/closed domains and degrade as sentence/vocabulary diversity opens up.
- **HARD-PASS:** comprehension-Q-supervised role assignment matches or nearly matches a hand-checked-triple-supervised version on grade-appropriate narrow-vocabulary text (GeoQuery-analog regime).
- **HARD-FAIL:** comprehension-Q supervision alone collapses well below the hand-checked version even in our narrow/grounded domain (would mean our regime behaves more like WikiTableQuestions than GeoQuery -- i.e., the spurious-derivation problem the literature flags is worse for us than the closed-domain literature would predict, and extra machinery -- consistency constraints, abstract-example generation -- is needed sooner than expected).

### Prediction 3 -- pure unsupervised structure induction is not a viable shortcut
**Claim:** attempting to induce relational structure with zero supervision/grounding signal (DMV/DIORA/compound-PCFG-style) will underperform any grounded or denotation-checked variant by a wide, persistent margin, mirroring the 30-40 F1 gap that a decade of unsupervised-parsing architecture progress has not closed.
- **HARD-PASS/HARD-FAIL is definitional here, not empirical** -- this is a literature-consensus finding strong enough to treat as a standing constraint (don't spend cycles on a fully-unsupervised relation-induction path) rather than something we need to re-test ourselves, UNLESS a specific substrate-native affordance (e.g. grounding-as-implicit-supervision, already flagged as a real edge in the sibling note) changes the calculus -- in which case that IS testable per Prediction 2's design, not as a separate unsupervised-from-nothing claim.

## What to LEARN-FROM / BUILD-ON / CREDIT

1. **Adopt the AMR/PropBank-style predicate-argument-graph-with-reentrancy target representation** for the relation store (already effectively converged on independently per the sibling thematic-roles note's Kintsch-proposition framing) -- credit Banarescu et al. 2013, Palmer/Gildea/Kingsbury PropBank. Do not reinvent a representation vocabulary; this part of the field is settled.
2. **Adopt DRS/DRT's box-nesting/accessibility mechanism conceptually** for cross-sentence discourse-referent binding and quantifier scope, since AMR/single-sentence graphs don't natively solve this and our situation-model/coref-overlay design already needs exactly this -- credit Kamp & Reyle, Bos/Boxer, the Parallel Meaning Bank.
3. **Adopt the denotation/weak-supervision learning-signal PHILOSOPHY** (train/score against a checkable comprehension-Q answer, not a required gold triple per sentence) -- this is the single most directly-transferable idea for a "modest supervision, grounded-from-near-nothing" system, and it is mature, decades-old, working machinery (credit Clarke/Goldwasser/Chang/Roth 2010; Liang/Jordan/Klein 2011 DCS; Berant & Liang 2014; Artzi & Zettlemoyer 2013 for the grounded variant) -- but budget for its own known failure mode (spurious derivations) once vocabulary/domain diversity grows past a GeoQuery-scale narrow domain.
4. **Adopt PropBank's generic ARG0-5 role scheme over FrameNet's frame-specific roles for now** -- the accuracy/coverage cost of frame-specific richness (15-25 F1 points below PropBank-style SRL) is not currently worth paying, though FrameNet's frame-relation GRAPH (inheritance/causation/subframe) is a genuinely interesting structure to keep in mind for later, since it maps naturally onto compositional VSA binding (a frame-relation hierarchy is itself a compositional-binding target) -- credit Fillmore, Baker/Fillmore/Lowe, note frame-induction is too immature to adopt yet.
5. **Do not attempt fully-unsupervised grammar/structure induction as a shortcut** -- 30-40 F1 points below supervised parsing after a decade of architecture progress (DMV/PRPN/DIORA/compound-PCFG) is a stable, load-bearing literature constraint, not a target to beat with a clever new architecture in isolation from a grounding/supervision signal.
6. **Treat GrAPES's methodological lesson as a standing eval discipline** (already partially covered by our own fair-test-design-gate discipline, now literature-corroborated): a single aggregate overlap metric (Smatch, F1) can look saturated while fine-grained, phenomenon-targeted breakdown (coreference, ellipsis, structural generalization, rare items) still shows systematic failure -- budget granular test suites, not just one global score, for any relation-reader eval.
7. **The one place to actually claim novelty, tentatively:** the SCAN/COGS finding that architectural compositionality (not supervision quantity) closes the systematic-generalization gap is the field's clearest evidence that our native compositional bind/unbind is aimed at a REAL, currently-open mainstream problem -- but the AM-parser result is at the *symbolic-parsing* stage (already-tokenized/typed input -> compositional output), not the *text-to-role-assignment encoder* stage where the sibling note locates our actual gap. Whether compositional binding's generalization win transfers upstream to the encoder problem is UNPROVEN and is exactly the cheap decisive test above.

## HONEST VERDICT

**Text -> relational-meaning is mature as a representation-and-fully-supervised-parsing problem; it is NOT mature as a grounded/modest-supervision, open-domain, glass-box, compositionally-generalizing problem -- and that intersection is where our genuine novelty must live, not in the parse target itself.** Concretely: (a) the TARGET representation (predicate-argument graphs, role backbones, discourse-referent structures) is solved -- adopt it wholesale, this is not research; (b) parsing INTO that target under full treebank supervision is a mature, well-benchmarked, but NOT-OUR-REGIME solved problem (expensive hand annotation we are deliberately not doing); (c) parsing under denotation/weak/grounded supervision -- our actual regime -- is mature and REAL but demonstrated only in narrow/closed domains (GeoQuery-parity) or narrow/closed worlds (navigation grids); nobody in this literature has shown it working at open-domain generality; (d) fully-unsupervised structure induction, our fallback if grounding/supervision signal were absent, has a stable decade-old 30-40-point gap -- ruling that out as a shortcut; (e) the one genuinely unsolved mainstream problem this scan surfaced -- systematic/compositional generalization failing under flat architectures regardless of supervision regime, fixed only by architectural compositionality -- is where a native-VSA-bind, glass-box substrate has a plausible, unproven, testable edge, PROVIDED that edge is shown to hold at the text-to-role-assignment encoder stage (unproven in this literature and unproven by us) and not just the already-demonstrated symbolic-parsing stage (AM-parser). **Novelty verdict, capped P<=0.50 per calibration discipline: the SUBSTRATE/representation-target is NOT novel (borrow and credit); the specific combination of (grounded-from-near-nothing supervision) + (open-domain generality) + (glass-box inspectability) + (architectural compositional-binding as the generalization fix) is unclaimed territory in the literature surveyed here** -- consistent with, and now cross-validated against, the sibling thematic-roles note's independent conclusion that the encoder (text -> role assignment) is the one real gap, with everything downstream (storage, binding, coref, accretion) a substrate strength.

## Substrate-product implications

Per [[feedback-no-papers-product-only]]: none of this is about publishing a parser paper. The practical payoff is a build-order recommendation: (1) do not spend cycles re-deriving a relational-meaning representation vocabulary -- it is settled, adopt AMR/PropBank/DRS-flavored structure directly into the existing relation store and situation-model design; (2) score/train the reader against comprehension-Q answer correctness (a checkable, grounded denotation) as the PRIMARY learning signal for role assignment, not gold-triple matching, consistent with the modest-supervision philosophy already in force for the "PIVOT -- build the ideal knowledge foundation" program; (3) do not chase neural OpenIE's small F1 edge -- it costs the no-LLM-at-runtime glass-box invariant for a ~6-8 point gain that classical/rule-hybrid systems already come close to; (4) the cheap decisive test above (systematic-generalization split, native-bind vs. flat/memorization baselines) is the next concrete, buildable experiment that would either validate or refute the one place this scour found real, unclaimed novelty for the substrate-product -- compositional binding as a fix for a genuine, currently-open mainstream generalization failure, at the specific encoder stage nobody has tested it at yet.
