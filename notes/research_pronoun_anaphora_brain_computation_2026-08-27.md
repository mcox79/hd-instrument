## (a) HEADLINE

**The brain's antecedent-selection computation is a two-stage, asymmetric HYBRID — a near-categorical morphosyntactic filter (agreement) followed by a Bayesian prior×likelihood combination, NOT a single ACT-R content-addressable-retrieval activation sum.** The prior term is Centering-style structural salience (grammatical role > parallelism > first-mention > raw linear recency, in roughly that order of literature support); the likelihood term is implicit-causality/coherence-relation bias (Kehler & Rohde 2013/2019: `P(referent|pronoun) ∝ P(pronoun|referent) × P(referent)`), which is the one place the literature commits to an explicit, implementable equation. **Lewis & Vasishth (2005) cue-based-retrieval's activation-sum equation is real and well-validated — but for a different job**: agreement-attraction errors and filler-gap dependency cost, not open-set antecedent CHOICE. A 2017 meta-analysis (Jäger, Engelmann & Vasishth) found the interference signature itself is **absent** for reflexive/reciprocal-antecedent dependencies — the structural case closest to pronoun binding — which is a striking, literature-level explanation for why the substrate's own full cue-based-activation binder HARD_FAILED while plain salience/recency won. This is convergent evidence, not a coincidence: **the on-disk result is exactly what the literature would have predicted.**

P estimate: **P=0.50** (deflated from ~0.70; Järvikivi et al. 2005 found BOTH cues operate simultaneously rather than one cleanly dominating, which could mean a role-weighted design only modestly beats pure-recency rather than decisively — capped at the novel-synthesis ceiling because applying this design to the substrate's own narrower register is untested).

HARD-FAIL: no separation, or recency-only wins — would indicate this register's discourse structure doesn't exercise genuine role/recency conflict the way controlled psycholinguistic materials do.

**Prediction B — an implicit-causality/coherence-bias term measurably improves same-gender (GAP-style) antecedent accuracy over salience-alone.**

HARD-FAIL: ≤2pt lift — would mean this register's same-gender ambiguity is dominated by genuine world-knowledge residue (consistent with WSC's original design intent) rather than IC/coherence, and no cheap fix exists short of a broader knowledge resource.

**Prediction C (confirmatory, not novel) — a full LV05-style cue-based-activation mechanism does NOT outperform salience for the BINDING/antecedent-choice decision specifically, even though it remains the right mechanism for content-based prediction/retrieval elsewhere in the substrate.**

Not raised to 0.75+ because a single on-disk measurement plus one meta-analysis is not the same as a dedicated re-test on the SPECIFIC conflict/GAP fixtures this note proposes.)
HARD-PASS (i.e., confirms the prediction): re-tested on the new fixture sets, cue-based-activation binding still underperforms salience-based binding.
HARD-FAIL (i.e., refutes the prediction, genuinely interesting if it happens): cue-based-activation binding wins on the NEW fixtures even though it lost on the QA-SRL-derived set — would suggest the earlier HARD_FAIL was register-specific (coarse 12-dim grounded features, ~2 items/entity) rather than a general finding, and the mechanism deserves a second, better-resourced trial before being written off.

3. **`probe_fix_tier_verb_semantic_ceiling_flagged_pronouns_2026-08-02.md`** measured that verb-**selectional-preference/animacy** knowledge would fix only 5.9% of flagged-and-wrong pronoun errors and recommended NOT building that resource. This drill's Lane D finding is an important, non-obvious correction to how that result should be read going forward: **implicit causality is not verb-selectional-preference.** Selectional preference asks "is this noun a plausible ARGUMENT of this verb" (semantic plausibility); implicit causality asks "which argument does this verb's MEANING (the *cause* of the event it names) implicate" (e.g., "blame" implicates the object as cause, independent of either argument's plausibility). The 5.9% ceiling measured for the former does **not** bound the latter — they are different mechanisms with different evidence bases (Ferstl et al. 2011's 305-verb norm corpus shows a 65-90% graded bias effect for IC specifically) and IC/coherence has never been measured against this substrate's actual flagged-error population. This is this drill's most actionable correction: **do not treat the verb-semantic-ceiling probe as having closed the door on implicit causality** — it closed the door on a narrower, adjacent mechanism only.
4. **Direct reconciliation with the fresh 2026-08-27 `BRAIN_FOUNDATIONAL_AUDIT.md` entity-BINDING result** (see HEADLINE): the literature independently explains why cue-based-activation content-matching lost to salience for BINDING specifically, without needing to invoke the substrate's representation-quality caveats (12-dim grounded space, ~2 items/entity) as the primary explanation — though those caveats remain real and should still be controlled for before fully crediting the mechanism-level explanation.

## (e) Substrate-product implications

1. **Do not build a general LV05 cue-based-activation binder for antecedent CHOICE.** This is now a doubly-supported negative result (on-disk HARD_FAIL + literature explanation) — flag as a standing design constraint, not just a one-off failed experiment, so it isn't silently re-attempted by a future solver who only sees the raw metric.
2. **The single highest-value NEW build is an implicit-causality/coherence-bias term**, sourced as a norm-list lookup keyed on the pronoun's governing verb (or governing coherence connective — "because"/"so"/"but"), NOT a general semantic-plausibility/selectional resource (already measured low-yield). Cheapest implementation: a small hand-or-corpus-built IC-bias table (subject-biased vs. object-biased verbs, following the Garvey & Caramazza / Ferstl et al. class), applied ONLY when gender/number agreement leaves 2+ survivors (i.e., a genuine tie-breaker stage, not a universal rescoring).
3. **Keep recency in the model, but do not weight it as the dominant term.** The Ariel Accessibility corpus-validation finding (Lane B: distance/linear-recency was the *weakest* of the four accessibility factors tested; saliency/subjecthood and competition were the reliable predictors) is a concrete, actionable, literature-motivated PRIOR for how to initialize a weight sweep — recency should start LOW relative to role-prominence and parallelism, not be assumed default-dominant the way the current `count + β·exp(−λΔ)` salience formula (frequency+recency only, per the audit's "RIGHT-OP-WRONG-METRIC" finding) implicitly treats it.
4. **Add a parallelism feature if not already present** — this is the one salience component with the strongest, most literature-robust claim to being able to override recency outright (cross-linguistically replicated, English + German), and is comparatively cheap to compute (does the pronoun occupy the same grammatical role, in the same clause-position-relative-to-verb, as a candidate's most recent mention).
5. **Agreement stays a hard/near-categorical pre-filter, not a soft blended cue** — this is well-supported by early-ERP timing evidence (ELAN/LAN range, before N400/semantic-integration timing) and matches the substrate's existing Hobbs/Centering-derived design (`research_coreference_hobbs_centering_resolver_2026-07-16.md`). On GAP-style same-gender material this filter is a correct no-op (0 candidates eliminated) — the design should explicitly log/measure that as a distinct code path so a GAP-corpus test doesn't silently mistake "filter did nothing" for "filter is broken."
6. **A genuine, unmeasured residual will remain** — no study in this scan puts salience + IC + coherence + world-knowledge head-to-head on identical same-gender materials, and the WSC/GAP literature is itself CONTESTED on whether "true" world-knowledge is even required vs. exploitable surface/frequency statistics (2021-2024 critiques of WSC "solutions"). Do not expect the salience+IC design to close the full GAP-corpus gap to human ceiling; pre-register the expected plateau (§c Prediction B) rather than treating a partial win as a full solution.
