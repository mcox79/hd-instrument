# Research: is WORKING MEMORY the primary barrier for glass-box (no-LLM) real-prose parsing?

**Date:** 2026-07-17. **Filed by:** research (Sonnet lit-scan x3, Sonnet synthesis). **Trigger:** direct hypothesis test requested by Director following the 2026-07-16/07-17 glass-box-reading drills (`research_glass_box_reading_robust_parsing_ceiling_2026-07-16.md`, `research_classical_openie_glassbox_parsing_2026-07-17.md`), which diagnosed the current positional-SVO pipeline's HARD_FAIL as a **construction-coverage + closed-relation-vocabulary** problem. This drill asks a deeper, orthogonal question: is there ALSO a working-memory (WM) mechanism — an incremental partial-parse buffer, bounded-ambiguity holding, cue-based retrieval — missing from a feed-forward/positional reader, and would adding it be a comparably large lever?

**Method:** 3 parallel Sonnet lit-scan sub-agents (1: WM in human sentence parsing — Gibson DLT, center-embedding, Just & Carpenter, Lewis & Vasishth; 2: WM analogs in computational parsers — stack/buffer, beam search, stack-LSTM, memory-augmented nets, chart parsing; 3: classical CS chart parsing/memoization/backtracking + garden-path reanalysis models), synthesized here. Calibration penalty per [[feedback-lit-scan-calibration-penalty]] applied throughout (deflate 0.15-0.25, cap novel-synthesis P at 0.50).

## HEADLINE

**WM is real and mechanistically load-bearing, but the field's own internal debate refutes the naive "bigger buffer = better parsing" framing, and the specific lever is narrower and different from what the hypothesis names.** Three findings converge:

1. **The strong "single WM resource pool explains complex-sentence comprehension" claim (Just & Carpenter 1992) is NOT settled consensus — it is one of three live, actively-contested positions** (vs. Caplan & Waters' separate dedicated-resource account, vs. MacDonald & Christiansen's connectionist emergent-capacity account), and the strongest critique specifically shows general WM span correlates weakly with **online** (real-time, incremental) parsing difficulty — only with **off-line/controlled** comprehension. This directly undercuts the idea that "more WM capacity" is the fix for an incremental reader specifically.

2. **The empirically best-supported WM-adjacent mechanism is NOT a capacity/buffer-size construct at all — it is Lewis & Vasishth's (2005) cue-based, content-addressable RETRIEVAL model with similarity-based INTERFERENCE.** This reframes the failure mode from "ran out of slots" to "the wrong item got retrieved because multiple stored items matched the retrieval cues" (agreement attraction: "the key to the cabinets *were* rusty"). Even Gibson's own distance-based DLT is itself contested by surprisal accounts (Hale/Levy) on some data, though Levy/Fedorenko/Gibson (2013) shows memory-cost theories correctly predict *where* difficulty lands (at the verb) in object-relative clauses, which pure surprisal does not — so WM-style accounts are not dead, but the mechanism is retrieval/interference, not raw capacity.

3. **On the engineering side, the clearest, most directly comparable evidence says: an explicit STRUCTURAL memory of open/incomplete dependencies (a stack, as in any real shift-reduce dependency parser) categorically outperforms a flat/fixed-window classifier on the identical task** (Dyer et al. 2015 Stack-LSTM 93.1/90.9 UAS/LAS vs. Chen & Manning 2014 fixed-window feed-forward 91.8/89.6 UAS/LAS on English; gap is larger on Chinese, 87.2 vs 83.9 UAS) **— but this is exactly the same fix already recommended by the 07-16 drill** (adopt a real dependency/clause-type parser instead of positional-SVO), not a separate, additional WM-specific intervention. A real dependency parser IS an incremental structural-memory mechanism (it tracks a stack of open dependencies by construction) — the WM question and the "adopt ClausIE-style dependency parsing" recommendation are the **same lever wearing two names**, not two different levers.

4. **Bounded ambiguity-holding (beam search) gives only a modest gain alone (+0.64 UAS greedy-to-beam32) and its full benefit requires globally-structured scoring, not memory alone** (+1.66 UAS total with global normalization, Andor et al. 2016) — so "hold more parse hypotheses" is a second-order lever, not a primary one.

5. **Chart-parsing-style full memoization is confirmed, across both the CS and psycholinguistic literatures, to be an EFFICIENCY device, not an accuracy/capacity device** — a memoized and a non-memoized backtracking parser are extensionally equivalent in what they can eventually derive; only cost differs. This directly rules out "just give it a bigger/unbounded chart" as the fix — chart size isn't the bottleneck axis at all.

6. **Reanalysis (Frazier & Rayner; Fodor & Ferreira 1998) is the one place biology unambiguously requires a distinct memory-of-parse-in-progress**, not for capacity reasons but because reanalysis is *defined over* the abandoned structural commitment (which node was misattached) — a flat, non-incremental parser cannot represent "this was misattached" because it never built a structural commitment to begin with. This is the cleanest argument that SOME incremental structure-tracking (not necessarily bounded, not necessarily large) is a hard precondition for correct passive/relative-clause/long-distance handling — again convergent with, not additional to, the dependency-parser recommendation.

**Bottom line:** WM matters, but not as "add more buffer capacity." It resolves into two separable, already-partially-actioned components: (a) an incremental STRUCTURAL representation of open dependencies — which the current positional-SVO parser categorically lacks and which the already-recommended dependency/clause-type parser upgrade already supplies — and (b) content-addressable, interference-aware RETRIEVAL for resolving long-distance/non-local dependencies specifically, which is a genuinely NEW, not-yet-actioned mechanism beyond simple dependency parsing (a real dependency parser gives you the tree; it does not by itself give you similarity-aware conflict resolution among competing referents/arguments for non-local attachment, e.g. coreference-like or long-distance agreement cases).

## Cheap decisive test

Re-analyze the SAME extraction-miss log the 07-16 drill's cheap decisive test already calls for (no new build) with one additional cross-cut: **bucket misses by dependency locality, not just construction type.**

**Procedure:** for every logged extraction miss, in addition to the 07-16 construction-type bucket (coordination/relative-clause/passive/nominalization/coreference/parse-error/lexicon/unclassifiable), tag whether correct role assignment required resolving an argument **across >=1 intervening clause boundary** ("long-distance") vs. **within the same local clause** ("local"). Separately tag whether >=2 candidate referents/arguments of the same type (interference candidates) were present in the sentence.

**HARD-PASS (WM/retrieval is a separate, additional lever beyond clause-type coverage):** among misses that share the SAME construction-type bucket (e.g., both are "relative-clause" misses), long-distance instances fail at a rate >=15 points higher than local instances of the identical construction type, AND failure rate is higher when >=2 interference candidates are present than when only 1 plausible candidate exists at matched distance. This would show distance/interference is doing independent work beyond what a clause-type-only fix (07-16's roadmap) would close.

**HARD-FAIL (WM/retrieval collapses into the coverage fix, no separate lever needed):** failure rate is statistically indistinguishable between long-distance and local instances of the same construction type (once a real dependency parse is used) — meaning adopting the 07-16-recommended clause-typology parser fully closes the gap and no additional cue-based-retrieval/interference-resolution mechanism needs separate engineering investment right now.

MIDDLE otherwise: distance matters somewhat but interference-candidate count does not (or vice versa) — prioritize whichever sub-signal is load-bearing.

## Falsifiable predictions (HARD-PASS / HARD-FAIL)

**Prediction 1 — incremental structural memory (stack of open dependencies) beats flat/positional parsing on identical sentences, independent of clause-grammar coverage.**
P estimate: **P=0.45** (deflated; directly supported by the Dyer-vs-Chen&Manning same-task comparison, though that comparison isn't a perfectly clean single-variable ablation isolating memory from parameter count — flagged in the ML lit-scan).
HARD-PASS: switching from the current positional/single-pass classifier to an incremental stack-based structure-builder (even a simple deterministic shift-reduce parser, no beam, no neural component), holding the SAME rule/grammar vocabulary constant, yields >=10 points accuracy/F1 gain specifically on constructions requiring non-adjacent role resolution (passive, relative clause).
HARD-FAIL: <3 point gain — would indicate the 07-16 roadmap's expected gains are coming entirely from clause-type rule coverage (vocabulary of patterns), not from having an incremental/structural representation per se, and a flat rule-based clause-type classifier without real incremental stack tracking would perform just as well.

**Prediction 2 — the failure mode is retrieval-interference (Lewis & Vasishth), not raw distance (Gibson DLT), for long-distance cases specifically.**
P estimate: **P=0.35** (deflated; strongly precedented in human data — agreement attraction, gradient center-embedding difficulty — but untested whether an engineered symbolic system exhibits the analogous failure pattern at all, since it need not implement any decay/interference dynamics unless explicitly built in).
HARD-PASS: among long-distance dependency instances in the miss log, error rate is markedly higher (>=15 points) when >=2 feature-matching candidate arguments are present than when only 1 plausible candidate exists at equal distance.
HARD-FAIL: error rate is flat regardless of competing-candidate count — would falsify an interference-based account for THIS system in favor of a pure distance/coverage account, meaning any future coreference/long-distance-argument module needs only proximity heuristics, not similarity-weighted candidate scoring.

**Prediction 3 — bounded ambiguity-holding (beam-search-style, keep top-K partial parses) gives at most a modest, second-order gain, and only when paired with a scoring rule that can actually exploit held alternatives.**
P estimate: **P=0.30** (deflated; directly precedented — Andor et al. 2016's own numbers show beam alone contributes less than beam-plus-structured-scoring — but this substrate's rule-based scoring is not a trained/globally-normalized model, so the mechanism enabling the "structured scoring" benefit may not transfer).
HARD-PASS: adding bounded top-K ambiguity-holding to the parser (keep >1 candidate partial parse, resolve via an explicit tie-break rule) improves accuracy by >=5 points over strict greedy commit-on-first-match.
HARD-FAIL: <1 point gain, or a gain that requires exponentially more rule-authoring effort per point gained — would indicate ambiguity-holding is not worth the added complexity for this substrate's rule-based (non-learned) scoring regime, and effort should stay on coverage/retrieval fixes instead.

**Prediction 4 (substrate-native departure from the brain-faithful baseline) — an UNBOUNDED incremental structural memory (no artificial human-style ~2-3-deep capacity cap) should show NO accuracy degradation on deeply-nested/coordinated real-prose constructions, unlike the human center-embedding wall.**
P estimate: **P=0.40** (deflated; the mechanism argument is clean — chart-parsing/memoization literature confirms unbounded memoized structures are extensionally complete and only differ from bounded ones in cost, and Yngve/Resnik's stack-depth account of center-embedding difficulty is explicitly a BOUNDED-stack phenomenon that a deliberately unbounded engineered stack has no structural reason to inherit — but real-prose deep nesting is rare enough that the eval set may not exercise this path at all).
HARD-PASS: on real-prose sentences with 2+ levels of clause embedding/coordination (rare but present in general-register text), an unbounded-stack parser's accuracy on those sentences is statistically indistinguishable from its accuracy on single-embedding sentences of the same construction type.
HARD-FAIL: accuracy measurably DROPS on deeply-nested constructions even under an unbounded stack — this would be a more concerning finding than a simple capacity-bound story, since it would indicate the difficulty is genuine combinatorial/structural ambiguity (multiple grammatically valid attachments) rather than a memory-bound artifact, meaning depth itself (not artificial capacity limits) drives errors even for machines.

## Cross-thread synthesis

This drill directly extends and partially UNIFIES the two most recent glass-box-parsing drills (`research_glass_box_reading_robust_parsing_ceiling_2026-07-16.md`, `research_classical_openie_glassbox_parsing_2026-07-17.md`). Both prior drills independently converged on "adopt a real dependency/clause-type parser" as the primary fix for the positional-SVO pipeline's construction-coverage failure. This drill's engineering-literature finding (Prediction 1, Dyer-vs-Chen&Manning) explains WHY that fix works at a deeper mechanistic level: a real dependency parser is not just "more grammar rules," it is an incremental STRUCTURAL-MEMORY mechanism (a stack of open dependencies) that a positional/flat parser categorically cannot have — so the "WM barrier" hypothesis and the already-recommended clause-typology-parser fix are the SAME lever, confirmed from an independent literature angle. This is a genuine convergence, not a coincidence of framing: three separate lit-scans across three drills (biology of parse failure; classical Open-IE precision ceilings; and now WM/incremental-structure literature) all point at the identical missing component.

The genuinely NEW, not-yet-actioned finding from THIS drill is Prediction 2: content-addressable, interference-aware retrieval for resolving long-distance/non-local dependencies is a mechanism distinct from simply having a dependency tree, and it is not automatically supplied by adopting ClausIE-style clause typology. This is the drill's actionable delta over the 07-16/07-17 recommendations — it should be tested (via the cheap decisive test above) BEFORE committing engineering effort to a coreference/long-distance-argument resolver, since if Prediction 2 HARD-FAILs, a much simpler proximity-based heuristic suffices.

This also connects to the standing "two frontiers" doctrine ([[project_two_frontiers_brain_faithful_world_plus_substrate_native_world_later_thrust_USER_2026-07-16]]) and "nail the brain baseline first" discipline ([[feedback_nail_the_brain_baseline_first_then_shore_up_real_weaknesses_USER_2026-07-16]]): Prediction 4 is an explicit, well-motivated departure from brain-faithfulness — the human ~2-3-deep center-embedding wall is a BOUNDED-stack artifact (Resnik 1992's left-corner stack-depth account), and an engineered glass-box parser has no reason to inherit that specific bound; it should adopt the STRUCTURAL mechanism (stack of open dependencies, cue-based retrieval) without the CAPACITY BOUND that causes human failure. Getting this right avoids a subtle trap: copying "bounded WM" wholesale (including its bound) would be copying a human LIMITATION, not a human CAPABILITY — the mechanism (structure-tracking, interference-aware retrieval) is the transferable asset; the ~4-chunk/~2-embedding limit is not.

## Ranked actionable anchors (delivered inline per no-routing-file discipline)

1. **[Primary, near-zero-cost, do FIRST] Add a dependency-locality + interference-candidate cross-cut to the extraction-miss log already being triaged per the 07-16 drill's cheap decisive test.** Tag each miss by (a) whether correct role assignment crosses >=1 clause boundary (long-distance vs local) and (b) whether >=2 same-type candidate referents/arguments were present (interference vs no-interference). This is a second pass over output already being analyzed — near-zero incremental cost, and it is the gate for anchors 3 below (see Cheap decisive test above for HARD-PASS/FAIL bars).
2. **[Secondary, contingent on Prediction 1] Instrument (not necessarily separately build) whichever dependency/clause-type parser upgrade is already in flight to log an ablation: same rule vocabulary, positional-assignment-only vs. stack-based incremental dependency-tracking.** This isolates whether the expected accuracy gain comes from the incremental STRUCTURAL representation itself or from the expanded rule vocabulary that typically ships bundled with a real parser — cheap if folded into an already-planned build rather than run as a separate cell.
3. **[Tertiary, contingent on anchor 1 HARD-PASS] A lightweight, rule-based, similarity/interference-aware candidate-scoring step for long-distance argument resolution** (score candidates by feature-match count against retrieval cues, not proximity alone) — NOT a full coreference resolver. Directly relevant to the coreference gap already flagged in the 07-16 note; if built, should use interference-weighting from the start per Prediction 2's reasoning. Do not build before anchor 1 confirms it's load-bearing.
4. **[Design constraint, zero-cost, applies regardless of test outcomes] Do NOT cap any incremental-structure memory at a human-realistic bound** (no artificial ~2-3-deep stack limit). The human center-embedding wall is a bounded-stack artifact (Resnik 1992), not a capability worth copying — build unbounded from the start and log depth-vs-accuracy as a side-channel metric to opportunistically test Prediction 4 without a dedicated cell.

## Substrate-product implications

For the glass-box, no-LLM read->grow pipeline: this drill reinforces (does not redirect) the existing recommendation to adopt a real dependency/clause-type parser in place of the positional-SVO parser, and adds one concrete new consideration — plan for a lightweight, rule-based, interference-aware candidate-scoring step (not a full learned coreference resolver) when the pipeline needs to resolve WHICH of several same-type candidate referents/arguments fills a long-distance role, rather than assuming "nearest matching NP" (a pure-distance heuristic) will suffice. This is directly relevant to the already-flagged coreference gap (07-16 Prediction D) — the interference literature suggests the correct fix, when the resolver is eventually built, should score candidates by feature-match count/similarity, not by proximity alone. It also licenses a specific engineering simplification with reasonable confidence: the pipeline does NOT need to implement human-style capacity bounds (no reason to artificially limit stack depth or discard old structure to mimic center-embedding failure) — full incremental structural tracking, unbounded, is both cheaper to build correctly (no bound-tuning needed) and strictly more capable than a human-realistic bounded version, per Prediction 4's reasoning.

## VERDICT (one line)

**WM is CONTRIBUTING, not a novel PRIMARY barrier separate from what's already diagnosed** — the "bounded buffer capacity" framing is actively contested in the human literature (Just & Carpenter's single-resource story is NOT consensus) and is not the operative mechanism; the real, well-evidenced lever is **incremental STRUCTURAL memory of open/incomplete dependencies (a dependency-parser stack, which the positional-SVO parser categorically lacks and the already-recommended ClausIE-style upgrade already supplies) plus content-addressable, interference-aware RETRIEVAL for long-distance/non-local argument resolution (a genuinely new, not-yet-built mechanism, needed specifically for coreference-like/long-distance cases, NOT a bigger buffer or an unbounded chart)** — and the human ~2-3-deep capacity BOUND itself should NOT be copied (it is a limitation, not a capability, per the two-frontiers doctrine).
