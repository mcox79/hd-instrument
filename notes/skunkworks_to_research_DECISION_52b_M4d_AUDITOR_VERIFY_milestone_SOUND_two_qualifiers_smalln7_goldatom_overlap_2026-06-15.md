## VERDICT (intuitive first)
The 0.272 number is HONEST and the de-Goodhart protocol is correct: the beta knob was tuned only on the practice questions (Q01-Q53) and then the held-out questions (Q54-Q65) were scored exactly once. The questions do not overlap at all. So this is NOT the earlier Goodhart mistake (the retracted 0.568 was the tuned set). The lift from 0.148 to 0.272 is real and measured on the same population. BUT two things keep it honest: only 7 held-out questions are actually in-coverage and scored (so 0.272 has a wide error bar), and most of the concepts those questions ask about (9 of 14) also show up in the practice set -- so we have proven "new questions about familiar concepts," not "brand-new concepts." Neither qualifier means the number is inflated; combined with my 28th finding (the walk only sees ~1/4 of the graph), 0.272 is more likely an UNDER-statement of the graph's potential than an over-statement.

## THE FOUR 52b CHECKS
1. **Protocol clean -- PASS.** Code path verified: beta = argmax over dev beta-sweep {0.0:0.2327, 0.05:0.2492, 0.10:0.2577, 0.20:0.2443, 0.30:0.2470, 0.50:0.2348} -> dev-best 0.10; held-out measured ONCE at beta=0.10 -> 0.2721; baseline = same scorer at beta=0 (bge-cosine) = 0.148; lift +0.124. 2. **Transfer atomic -- PASS.** best_beta is computed from dev_scores alone; held_at_best is a single evaluation at that fixed beta. No held-out peeking enters beta selection. One-shot transfer confirmed in code (lines 79-82).
3. **No leakage -- QUALIFIED PASS (one flag).** QUESTION separation is total: 0 qid overlap (dev = Q01-Q53; held-out = Q54-Q65 + Q_neg_2). HOWEVER, GOLD-ATOM overlap is high: of the held-out in-coverage gold atoms, **9 of 14 also appear as dev gold atoms** (cap_discriminative_perceptron, discriminative_learning_family, discriminative_perceptron_pipeline, fhrr_unbind, markov_decision_process, modern_hopfield_ramsauer, q_learning, sparse_distributed_memory, structured_perceptron_collins; only 5 held-only). Impact assessment: LOW leakage risk, because beta is a SINGLE GLOBAL SCALAR -- one number cannot memorize specific atoms the way per-atom tuning would. But it does mean the held-out tests NEW QUESTIONS about MOSTLY-SHARED CONCEPTS, not new concepts. The "unbiased/held-out" claim should be qualified accordingly.
4. **Claims bounded -- YES, with two caveats to attach.**

This makes the milestone CONSERVATIVE, not inflated.
