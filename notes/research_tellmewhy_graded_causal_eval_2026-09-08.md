HEADLINE
======================================================================

Three findings, ranked by how much they should change the next build:

1. **The classic graded-causality precedent is real but narrower than the brief assumed -- one paper (Keenan, Baillet & Brown 1984) gives a true graded pairwise causal-strength rating with a monotonic reading-time function; the Trabasso papers (1985 x2) actually use a BINARY per-pair necessity test, with gradedness living in the resulting NETWORK's connectivity count, not in the pairwise judgment itself.** This is a correction to the brief, not a refutation of the "graded, not forced-choice" claim -- the connectivity count is itself graded/interval-scale, and importance/recall/summarization are demonstrably monotonic functions of it (R²=.32-.65, Trabasso & van den Broek 1985 Table 5) -- but a graded gold built for TellMeWhy should be modeled on Keenan et al.'s per-pair Likert/magnitude rating (or the modern continuous-edge implementations below), not on a misremembered "Trabasso rated each pair 0-4" methodology that does not exist in the primary sources.
2. **A frequency-weighted top-1 credit metric built from TellMeWhy's 3-annotator helpful-sentence flags is mechanically incapable of showing a MATERIALLY LARGER mechanism gain than the union-lenient credit already measured and already refuted (goal engine -0.055 CI-sep below base under answer-lenient gold) -- because frequency credit is a strict, provable tightening of union credit on the same argmax picks.** The genuinely new (and untested) lever hiding inside the same data is a full-ranking Spearman/Kendall correlation against the graded 3-way frequency distribution, which is a different statistic than top-1 hit-rate and is not mechanically bounded by the union result -- but it is gated by severe statistical power problems (n=3 raters -> only 4 possible gold values per sentence; 43.3% of all annotator answers cite zero supporting sentences).
3. **BCOPA (Kavumba et al. 2019), not raw COPA or e-CARE, is the correct primary clean-benchmark instrument** -- it inherits COPA's high-reliability filtered construction (kappa=0.965) while specifically neutralizing the one documented, quantified artifact class (single-token surface-frequency cues, e.g. token "a" at 21.2% coverage / 57.5% productivity) that could make a correlation-only mechanism look real or make a genuine mechanism look artifactually weak. It is a static, on-disk-obtainable dataset (GitHub + HuggingFace mirror), requiring no new annotation. e-CARE is a legitimate second instrument (adds a free-text conceptual-explanation layer, useful for introspecting a glass-box mechanism's stated reasoning) but its own paper reports a **residual** hypothesis-only baseline of ~57-58% vs 50.1% chance -- its AFLite filtering (against a RoBERTa adversary) did not fully clean it, and Kavumba's own alternatives-only ablation should be re-run on OUR mechanism, on RAW COPA, as a mandatory control regardless of which clean benchmark is used as the headline instrument.

A fourth, sharper finding earns its own paragraph because it changes how a COPA/e-CARE win should be interpreted, not just whether to expect one: **a positive result on COPA/BCOPA, if it happens, will be structurally ambiguous between "the TellMeWhy tie was an eval-ceiling artifact" and "the TellMeWhy tie was an edge-extraction-difficulty artifact that COPA's task format removes by construction."** COPA presents exactly 2 pre-filtered, topically-matched-but-causally-clean alternatives per item; TellMeWhy presents up to ~5 naturally-occurring, non-adversarially-cleaned narrative sentences as candidates. The substrate's own 11th-triangulation finding (`CAUSAL_NETWORK_RESULT_2026-09-08.md`: "noisy edges corrupt a global centrality computation... MORE than a local pairwise judgment") is explicitly an edge-count/noise-degree finding, not a pure eval-methodology finding -- so a mechanism that discriminates on COPA's 2-clean-candidate format but ties on TellMeWhy's ~5-noisy-candidate format is equally consistent with "the mechanism is fine, TellMeWhy's harder combinatorics were the actual wall" as with "the TellMeWhy eval was too leaky to see a real gain." Disambiguating these two readings needs an explicit control (Section E).

(A) THE PINNED GRADED CAUSAL-SELECTION MEASURE
======================================================================

**Trabasso & Sperry (1985)**, *Journal of Memory and Language* 24(5):595-611, and **Trabasso & van den Broek (1985)**, same journal 24(5):612-630 (primary-fetched, scanned-PDF full text read pp.612-630) -- edges in the causal network are established by a **binary** counterfactual-necessity test per Mackie's (1980) "necessity in the circumstances" criterion: *"Event A is necessary for event B... if event A had not occurred, then, in the circumstances of the story, event B also would not have occurred."* Coding was exhaustive (all 2088 possible pairwise connections examined across 9 story versions; 234 direct causal connections identified) and highly reliable (**kappa = .96, p<.01**, proportion agreement .88; causal-chain-membership kappa=.76). **The gradedness lives one level up**: "number of connections" is recorded on an **interval scale** per event (a graded/count-valued network-degree variable), and judged importance (itself collected on a **7-point scale**) plus recall/summarization probability are **monotonic functions** of that connectivity count. Exact numbers (Table 3): causal-chain-event mean importance rank falls monotonically 3.55 -> 2.74 -> 2.57 -> 2.04 -> 1.29 as connection count rises 1 -> 2 -> 3 -> 4 -> 5-7; dead-end events show the same monotonic pattern (4.93 -> 4.35 -> 4.31 -> 3.46). Regression R² = .32-.65 across four dependent measures (immediate recall, delayed recall, summarization, importance rating), all p<.01.

**Keenan, Baillet & Brown (1984)**, *Journal of Verbal Learning and Verbal Behavior* 23(2):115-126 (secondary-corroborated -- ScienceDirect paywalled/Cloudflare-blocked; corroborated via Mulder 2008 PhD dissertation, which reproduces the original 4-level stimulus set verbatim, plus multiple independent secondary summaries in agreement) -- **this is the actual graded-pairwise-rating precedent.** Four ordered causal-strength levels for the same target sentence ("Joey's body was covered with bruises") preceded by causes of decreasing strength (brother punched him repeatedly > fell off bike > mother got angry > went to play at a neighbor's) were **independently validated by a separate human rating task** ("the researchers' intuitions that the relations differ in terms of causal strength is confirmed by participants in a rating task"), and reading time on the target sentence increases **monotonically** (secondary sources converge on "linear relationship") as rated causal strength decreases. The exact numeric bounds of the rating scale used in that norming task could not be primary-fetched -- flagged unverified. A direct follow-on, **Myers, Shinjo & Duffy (1987)**, JML 26:453-465, reportedly used continuous pretested causal-relatedness scores as a regression predictor (secondary mention only, not fetched).

**Modern computational graded-network implementations exist and directly answer "how would I build this today":**
- **CRAB** (Romanou et al. 2023, EMNLP, arXiv:2311.04284, primary-fetched) -- defines causality between event pairs as "**a continuous value from 0 to 100**," collected from **7 workers per pair**, averaged into one scalar, then binned into 4 classes (No/Low/Medium/High). Krippendorff's alpha = .28 overall (.38 on extremes only, .70 on an expert-validated subset) -- news-event domain, not narrative, but the exact "multi-rater-averaged continuous causal score" pattern requested.
- **Antony, Lozano, Dhoat, Chen & Bennion (2024)**, *Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience* 36(11):2368-2385 (primary-fetched, PMC) -- the clearest modern instance of a **continuous-edge-weight Trabasso-style network**, explicitly citing both 1985 papers. 7 raters score every pair of narrative/film scenes on a **directional 0-10** "how strongly did A cause B" scale, averaged into a weighted directed graph; **weighted in-degree** predicts memorability (r=.48, p<.001) far better than out-degree (r=.12, n.s.). Inter-rater reliability of the 0-10 ratings: r=.10-.70 (mean .48) -- moderate, consistent with genuinely graded (non-forced) judgments.
- **GLUCOSE does NOT have a graded strength rating** (semi-structured slot-fill explanations + a worker-quality 0-3 rating, not a per-pair causal-strength score) -- confirmed primary-fetched, ruling this out as a reusable graded-gold source.

**Concrete, LLM-free construction recipe for a graded TellMeWhy gold (two complementary tracks, zero external-tool inference either way):**
- **Track A (zero new annotation, pure re-aggregation).** TellMeWhy already ships each of 3 annotators' independently-flagged `helpful_sentences` per question. Gold weight per candidate sentence = (# annotators who flagged it) / 3 -- a 4-point graded scale requiring no new human labor. This is Section (B)'s frequency gold, reused here as the graded-gold input.
- **Track B (small new annotation pass, modeled directly on Keenan/CRAB/Antony).** TellMeWhy stories are ~5 sentences, so each question has a tractable ~5 (question, candidate-sentence) pairs. Recruit 5-7 raters (matching CRAB's and Antony et al.'s panel sizes) to give a continuous 0-100 (or directional 0-10, Antony-style) causal-strength rating per pair; average into one scalar per pair, exactly as both modern precedents do.
- **Scoring a system against either graded gold (two metrics, both modeled on how the original papers themselves validated gradedness):** (i) **strength-weighted credit** -- score = the graded gold value of whichever candidate the system's argmax lands on (partial credit, not binary hit/miss against one designated annotator); (ii) **rank correlation** -- have the system score/rank ALL ~5 candidates by predicted causal relevance, compute Spearman's rho (or Kendall's tau, given the small candidate set) against the full graded ranking, averaged with a CI across questions -- this is the metric that tests whether the system's entire ordering tracks graded causal strength, the same logic Keenan et al. used to validate that reading time tracks the FULL graded scale, not just a binary split.

(B) CROWD-FREQUENCY / MULTI-REFERENCE SCORING ADAPTED TO SENTENCE-SELECTION
======================================================================

**ProtoQA** (Boratko, Li, Das, O'Gorman, Le & McCallum, 2020, EMNLP, arXiv:2005.00771, primary-fetched) -- exact mechanics confirmed. 100 crowd-workers answer each prototypical-reasoning question; answers are clustered (WordNet + human validation, inter-annotator BLANC=83.17) into weighted clusters (cluster weight = raw answer count, e.g. Shower=43, Breakfast=30, Dress=7 out of 100). **Exact metrics (verbatim from the paper):** "Matching an answer cluster gives the model a score equal to the cluster size." **Max Answers@k** = score achievable using up to k guesses, reported as % of max achievable; **Max Incorrect@k** = unlimited guesses, stop after k misses. Worked example: Max Answers@1 = 43/43 = 1.0; Max Incorrect@1 = (43+30)/(43+30+7) = 0.9125. Matching uses a Hungarian-algorithm optimal assignment against the cluster-size reward matrix -- credit is **literally proportional to the fraction of the 100-person crowd who gave that answer.** This is a free-text generative-answer-clustering task, not sentence/span selection.

**No precedent exists for this exact frequency-proportional credit mechanic applied to sentence/span selection.** Checked directly and confirmed absent in:
- **HotpotQA** (Yang et al. 2018, primary-fetched): supporting-fact scoring is single-gold EM/F1 only. A separate multi-annotator side-study (used only to compute a "Human UB" ceiling, not to score models) found "**crowd workers agree less on supporting facts [than on the answer itself]**" -- an explicit, primary-sourced statement that sentence-level evidence agreement is measurably noisier than answer-level agreement, a direct caution for any TellMeWhy sentence-frequency scheme.
- **SQuAD 1.1/2.0** (primary-fetched, official eval script): multi-reference scoring is `max(f1(pred, ref) for ref in gold_answers)` -- a **best-of-k ceiling**, not proportional credit.
- **FEVER** (Thorne et al. 2018, primary-fetched): "one complete set of sentences should be predicted" -- any ONE of several valid evidence sets counts in full, but **no partial credit** for an incomplete match. A regular-vs-super-annotator recall gap of 72.36% (super-annotators searching exhaustively find sentences regular annotators miss) is a further caution that a single annotator's non-flag is not strong evidence a sentence is truly unhelpful.
- **The human-label-variation literature** (Plank 2022 EMNLP, arXiv:2211.02570, primary-fetched; Uma, Fornaciari, Hovy, Paun, Plank & Poesio 2021 JAIR "Learning from Disagreement," secondary-corroborated via Plank's detailed citations; Nie, Zhou & Bansal 2020 EMNLP "ChaosNLI," primary-fetched; Pavlick & Kwiatkowski 2019 TACL, primary-fetched) has a well-established, named family of soft/distributional metrics -- **cross-entropy against the human label distribution**, **KL/Jensen-Shannon divergence** (ChaosNLI: `JSD(p||q) = sqrt(1/2(KL(p||m)+KL(q||m)))`, m=(p+q)/2), **entropy-stratified reporting** (bin items by human-distribution entropy, report accuracy per bin rather than one pooled number -- ChaosNLI shows near-ceiling accuracy on low-entropy bins collapsing to near-chance on high-entropy bins, an effect invisible in the pooled number) -- **but every cited instance is a classification/label task** (NLI, hate speech, WSD, POS). Plank's own appendix of 30+ multi-annotator datasets contains **zero** extractive span/sentence-selection tasks. This is a genuine, confirmed gap: the "credit proportional to annotator endorsement" idea is well established for labels, essentially untested for spans/sentences.

**TellMeWhy-specific facts confirmed (Lal, Chambers, Mooney & Balasubramanian 2021, ACL-IJCNLP Findings, primary-fetched):** 3 independent annotators per question, each writes a free-text answer AND independently flags `helpful_sentences`. **43.3% of all 91,557 collected answers (39,661) cite zero supporting sentences** -- a large structural floor on how much of the dataset a per-sentence frequency gold can even be nonzero for. The paper's own diversity statistic (measured on answer TEXT, not sentence-selection sets, so a proxy not a direct measurement): only 150/30,055 questions have >90% answer overlap across all 3 annotators; 4,243 more have 2-of-3 >90% overlap; the remaining **26,068 questions (~85%) average only 26.12% answer overlap**. The literal pairwise-Jaccard-of-helpful-sentences statistic is **not reported anywhere in the paper** -- it would need fresh computation from the released annotations (github.com/StonyBrookNLP/tellmewhy) before this scheme can be built; flagged unverified pending that computation.

**Direct answer to the coordinator's sharpened question -- does frequency credit recover valid-outside-union causes the union-lenient measurement already missed? No, and this is a provable, not merely likely, result for the TOP-1 formulation.** Union-lenient credit already measured (per the build-queue note: answer-lenient gold, goal engine still -0.055 CI-sep below base) counts a system's argmax pick as a full hit if it appears in ANY of the 3 annotators' flagged sets. Frequency credit assigns that same hit a value in {1/3, 2/3, 1} instead of a flat 1. **Frequency credit is therefore a strict, monotone-nondecreasing tightening of union credit on the identical set of argmax picks** -- for any fixed set of system predictions, `E[frequency credit] <= P(union hit)`, with equality only in the degenerate case where every hit is unanimous (3-of-3). It is mechanically impossible for the top-1 frequency-credit number to show a MATERIALLY LARGER mechanism gain than the union number already showed, because it can only ever be equal or smaller on the same predictions -- the "gap" a real mechanism opens up under union credit cannot be enlarged by fractionally discounting some of the hits that already counted. **The lever frequency data can add is a genuinely different statistic, not a bigger number on the same one**: (i) the strength-weighted variant from Section (A) applied here is the same top-1 credit and inherits the identical mechanical ceiling; (ii) the **rank-correlation** variant (Spearman rho of the system's FULL candidate ranking against the 4-level frequency distribution) is NOT bounded by the union result, because it scores the whole ordering, not just whether the top-1 pick cleared a threshold -- this is the only genuinely untested, potentially-informative reformulation, and it inherits the n=3 statistical-power problem (only 4 distinguishable gold values) and the 43.3%-empty-set problem (most items contribute a near-degenerate ranking target) as compounding reasons to expect, at best, a real-but-narrow signal on the minority high-agreement subset (~14.6% of questions per the >90%-overlap-proxy figures above), not a full-population effect.

(C) THE CLEAN-BENCHMARK LIST -- EXACT FORMATS, WHICH IS BEST
======================================================================

**COPA** (Roemmele, Bejan & Gordon 2011, AAAI Spring Symposium on Logical Formalizations of Commonsense Reasoning; Gordon, Kozareva & Roemmele 2012, SemEval-2012 Task 7, ACL Anthology S12-1052 -- both primary-fetched). **Format**: premise sentence + prompt ("What was the cause of this?" / "What happened as a result?") + exactly 2 alternatives; pick the more plausible one. Verbatim example: *"Premise: I knocked on my neighbor's door. What happened as a result? Alternative 1: My neighbor invited me in. Alternative 2: My neighbor left his house."* **Size**: 500 dev + 500 test (original 2011/2012 release, no train split); SuperGLUE's later repackaging uses 400 train / 100 val / 500 test -- specify which release when citing a number. **Construction**: hand-authored by the dataset creators; incorrect alternatives deliberately written "similar in form... and somewhat related to the premise, but with no obvious direct causal connection" -- NOT algorithmically adversarially filtered. **Reliability**: Cohen's kappa = **0.965** (10 raters, 200 questions each, 2 raters per question) on selecting the correct alternative -- 26 disagreement items were removed/replaced at construction time, confirming the brief's citation exactly.

**BCOPA** (Kavumba, Inoue, Heinzerling, Singh, Reisert & Inui, 2019 -- **venue correction**: this is the **COIN workshop (Commonsense Inference in NLP) co-located with EMNLP-IJCNLP 2019**, ACL Anthology D19-6004, arXiv:1911.00225, NOT *SEM 2019 as the brief guessed; primary-fetched). **Superficial cues found**: single-token surface frequency imbalances between correct/incorrect alternatives -- token "a" has 21.2% coverage with 57.5% productivity (i.e., when "a" appears asymmetrically, the alternative containing it is correct 57.5% of the time, vs. 50% chance); other flagged tokens: "the" (17.0%), "to" (16.4%), "was" (11.0%), "in" (9.4%). Demonstration: a model trained on **alternatives alone, premise stripped out**, still scores **59.6% (+/-2.3)** vs. 50% chance -- proving the cues carry real signal with zero causal reasoning. Easy/Hard split: BERT scores 83.9%/71.9%. **Construction method -- correction to a brief hypothesis**: BCOPA is built by **manual mirroring, NOT AFLite/algorithmic adversarial filtering**: 5 annotators write a NEW premise per item such that the ORIGINAL item's wrong alternative becomes correct under the new premise, reusing both original alternative sentences so each occurs exactly once-correct and once-incorrect across the balanced set (worked example: "The stain came out of the shirt" [correct: bleached] mirrors to "The shirt did not have a hole anymore" [correct: patched]). **Publicly available**: yes, static XML on github.com/Balanced-COPA/Balanced-COPA, mirrored at huggingface.co/datasets/pkavumba/balanced-copa (1000 dev-set items: originals 1-500 + mirrors 1001-1500) -- confirmed a standard HuggingFace `datasets` loader exists via the HF mirror. "**BCOPA-CE**" is referenced in later downstream literature but its originating paper could not be pinned down this cycle -- flagged unverified provenance, do not cite a specific source for it without further confirmation. (Separately: **COPA-SSE**, Brassard et al. LREC 2022, github.com/a-brassard/COPA-SSE, is a distinct semi-structured-explanation extension of COPA -- do not conflate with BCOPA-CE.)

**e-CARE** (Du, Ding, Xiong, Liu & Qin, 2022, ACL, ACL Anthology 2022.acl-long.33, arXiv:2205.05849 -- **author-list correction**: 5 authors, Harbin Institute of Technology; no "Ke/Wan/Chen" as the brief's guessed author list had, primary-fetched). **Format**: same premise + cause/effect prompt + 2-hypothesis forced choice as COPA, **plus** a free-text "conceptual explanation" annotation describing the causal mechanism connecting premise and correct hypothesis -- two defined tasks, Causal Reasoning (selection) and Explanation Generation. **Size**: 21,324 total instances (13,048 unique explanations, many shared across instances); splits train 14,928 / dev 2,132 / test 4,264. **Construction**: sourced from GenericsKB (top-1%-quality generic statements) -> crowdworkers write cause/effect pairs -> **algorithmic AFLite-style adversarial filtering** (RoBERTa-base adversary, 25 iterations, sigmoid temperature schedule 1.0->0.2, K=20 candidates/instance) explicitly following Bhagavatula et al. 2019 / Sakaguchi et al. 2020 (WinoGrande) lineage -- the one of the three that uses algorithmic debiasing. Human validation: 92% accuracy / kappa=0.935 on causal labels, 89.5% / kappa=0.832 on explanation validity. **Residual bias, confirmed from the paper's own numbers**: post-filtering hypothesis-only baseline still scores ~57-58% vs. 50.1% chance -- **cleaner than raw COPA's 59.6% but not fully clean**, and filtering against one adversary model class (RoBERTa) does not guarantee robustness against a structurally different (non-neural) mechanism's own failure modes. No dedicated third-party Kavumba-style critique paper targeting e-CARE specifically was found this cycle (absence not exhaustively confirmed -- a small number of targeted queries only).

**Not substitutable, checked and ruled out**: **WIQA** (Tandon et al. 2019, EMNLP-IJCNLP, D19-1629) is a 3-way perturbation-effect classification (MORE/LESS/NO-EFFECT), not a 2-way clean forced choice -- different task shape. **TimeTravel/Counterfactual Story Rewriting** (Qin, Bosselut, Holtzman, Bhagavatula, Clark & Choi 2019, EMNLP-IJCNLP, D19-1509) is an open-ended generation task (minimally rewrite a story under a counterfactual), not a selection task at all.

**Which is best, and the recommended sequence**: **BCOPA is the correct primary instrument** for the stated purpose (separate mechanism quality from an eval-ceiling artifact using strict argmax on unambiguous items) -- it inherits COPA's kappa=0.965 filtered-construction reliability while specifically closing the one documented, quantified artifact class that could make either a spurious mechanism look real or a genuine mechanism look artifactually weak depending on which direction the surface cues happen to point on a given item. **e-CARE is the correct secondary instrument** if the free-text conceptual-explanation layer is wanted for introspecting a glass-box mechanism's own stated reasoning (a genuine complementary use, not redundant with BCOPA) -- but the ~57-58%-vs-50.1% residual means it is not as clean as its own "adversarially filtered" framing implies, and the same alternatives-only ablation control is needed there too. **Raw, unfiltered COPA should still be run alongside BCOPA, not skipped** -- as the required denominator for Kavumba's own cue-exploitation ablation (Section D), so any gap between raw-COPA and BCOPA performance is directly attributable and reported, not hidden. On-disk-obtainability confirmed this cycle for COPA (SuperGLUE `copa` config, standard HF `datasets` loader) and BCOPA (HF mirror `pkavumba/balanced-copa`); e-CARE's standard-loader status was **not verified this cycle** (its GitHub repo was not checked for an HF `datasets` config) -- confirm before build, cheap one-line check.

(D) ADMISSIBILITY AS AN OFFLINE EVAL INSTRUMENT + ARTIFACT GUARDS
======================================================================

**Confirmed legitimate, by direct ML-methodology reasoning** (not something requiring a citation): scoring a fixed, non-trained, glass-box mechanism against a held-out labeled test set (COPA/BCOPA/e-CARE) purely to measure selection accuracy, with **zero parameter fitting/gradient updates against that dataset's labels**, is exactly the standard zero-shot/rule-based-system evaluation use case these benchmarks and their train/dev/test splits were built to support -- categorically identical to how the original COPA/SemEval papers themselves scored unsupervised PMI-based baselines from day one, predating today's fine-tuned-LLM baselines. This is a held-out gold-metric file, not a runtime tool and not a foundation-training asset -- it satisfies this project's own FOUNDATION/RUNTIME admissibility split (an offline static asset used only to score is neither "at inference" nor "training," it is measurement).

**What WOULD be illegitimate, stated for completeness**: (i) fine-tuning any parameter, threshold, or classifier head on COPA's/e-CARE's train or dev portion before reporting test accuracy -- this crosses from evaluation into training and the reported number then reflects fitting to that dataset's idiosyncrasies, including any residual surface cues; (ii) using an external LLM at inference time to answer benchmark items -- this tests the LLM's (possibly memorized) knowledge, not the mechanism, and violates the project's own no-external-LLM-at-inference invariant regardless of whether the eval question was asked. A related, previously-unflagged risk worth stating explicitly even though it does not apply to OUR mechanisms: these are old, widely-circulated public benchmarks almost certainly present in any large pretrained LM's training corpus -- a genuine memorization/contamination concern for any LLM-based COMPARISON baseline that might eventually be placed in the same results table, though irrelevant to our own LLM-free glass-box mechanisms.

**Required artifact guards, concrete and mandatory**:
1. **Kavumba's alternatives-only ablation (premise stripped) must be run on OUR mechanism, on RAW COPA, as a required info-free-style twin** -- exactly the same twin-control discipline this project's own causal-selection cycles already used to certify every prior result (forward-transition, LOO-necessity, causal-network structure all failed or passed specifically because of a twin-beating test, not a raw score). This certifies a COPA/BCOPA win is not itself a hidden surface-cue artifact in the OTHER direction (a mechanism accidentally exploiting the same token-frequency cues Kavumba documented, rather than genuinely reasoning about cause).
2. **Report BOTH raw-COPA and BCOPA numbers side by side, and grade on BCOPA** (the harder/cleaner number) -- directly the same "report-both, grade the modern/harder number" discipline this project already applies to the 19th-century-vs-modern-gold corpus question (McGuffey/LitBank banned from requirements in favor of UD-EWT/QA-SRL/GUM).
3. **Run the same hypothesis-only ablation on e-CARE** -- its own paper's 57-58%-vs-50.1% residual means the guard is arguably more urgent there than on BCOPA, not less, despite e-CARE's "adversarially filtered" framing.
4. **Do not cite a specific paper for "BCOPA-CE"** without confirming its origin first; treat it as an unverified pointer in downstream literature only.

(E) CALIBRATED P
======================================================================

**(a) A graded/frequency eval on TellMeWhy reveals a materially larger mechanism gain than single-gold argmax.** Split into the two distinct formulations Section (B) separates:
- **Top-1 frequency-credit variant**: **P_deflated = 0.10.** This is not merely a low empirical estimate -- it is close to a formal impossibility given the already-measured union-lenient result, because frequency credit is a provable, monotone tightening of union credit on the same predictions (Section B). Treat this specific formulation as already effectively HARD-FAILED by existing measurement; do not spend a build cycle re-confirming it.
- **Rank-correlation-against-graded-gold variant** (the genuinely new, untested statistic): **P_deflated = 0.25** (capped per novel-synthesis policy; undeflated naive estimate ~0.40-0.45 before the mandatory 0.15-0.20 deflation for an untested reformulation in a domain -- extractive sentence selection -- where the wider human-label-variation literature has never applied this style of scoring). **MIDDLE_BAND is the more likely honest outcome**: real, CI-separable signal plausible only on the minority high-agreement subset (~14.6% of questions, per the >90%-overlap-proxy statistics), not full-population, given the n=3 statistical-power ceiling (only 4 distinguishable gold values per sentence) and the 43.3% empty-flag-set rate.

**(b) The mechanisms discriminate on a clean benchmark (COPA/e-CARE/BCOPA) where they tied on TellMeWhy.** **P_deflated = 0.30** that SOME discrimination appears; **P_deflated = 0.20** for the stronger, decision-relevant claim that a COPA/BCOPA win cleanly PROVES the TellMeWhy tie was an eval-ceiling artifact specifically (as opposed to an edge-extraction-difficulty artifact that COPA's easier 2-clean-candidate format removes by construction, per the HEADLINE's fourth finding). The two readings are observationally similar (both predict "wins on COPA, ties on TellMeWhy") and require an explicit disambiguating control before either can be claimed: **the same mechanisms should also be run on a difficulty-matched TellMeWhy subset** (e.g., non-adjacent questions where the story has been artificially reduced to exactly 2 candidate sentences, matching COPA's degree) -- if the mechanism discriminates on 2-candidate TellMeWhy items as well as on COPA, the eval-ceiling reading is supported; if it still ties even at COPA's own candidate-count, the edge-extraction-difficulty reading is supported instead, and COPA's clean construction (not its narrower answer-set size) would be doing the work, which is a different and more specific finding. This control is cheap (a re-slicing of already-available TellMeWhy data, no new annotation) and should be the FIRST cell built from this note, ahead of acquiring BCOPA/e-CARE.

Substrate-product implications
======================================================================

In plain terms: right now, when the causal-selection mechanisms are graded against one single "correct" answer per story, they come out tied with a much simpler baseline -- but we already know, from reading the human instructions given to the people who built that answer key, that real people were never asked to pick ONE irreplaceable cause; they were asked for their best plausible explanation, and different people reasonably gave different answers. This note lays out two honest, LLM-free ways to check whether the tie is really about our methods or about the scorecard: (1) build a graded answer key from the SAME people's already-collected disagreements (free) and a small graded rating pass (cheap), and score by how close a system gets to the whole spread of human answers, not just whether it matches one; (2) test the same mechanisms on cleaner, pre-filtered story pairs (like COPA, once we remove a known "cheating by counting words" shortcut some other systems were caught using) where there's no ambiguity about the right answer. The honest risk to flag: if the mechanisms still tie on the clean benchmark too, that is a real, useful, different finding -- it would mean the limitation is not the messy scorecard, but something about how well the mechanisms identify TRUE cause-and-effect links in the first place, which points back to a bigger, separate, already-known open problem (building a better cause-detector) rather than a quick eval fix. Either outcome (a) tells us to fix the ruler, or (b) tells us to fix the reasoning -- either way, this is the cheapest possible way to find out which one.

### TLDR (plain English)

We checked whether our cause-and-effect detector's "tie" result is a problem with our detector or a problem with the test. Two honest, no-AI-shortcut fixes exist: (1) build a graded answer key from human raters' own disagreements (the classic psychology studies that first showed causal importance is a matter of degree, not yes/no, give the exact recipe) and score partial credit instead of all-or-nothing; (2) test the same detector on a cleaner, pre-checked set of question pairs (like the well-known COPA test, once we correct for a documented "shortcut" some systems were caught using, by testing our own detector for that same shortcut first). One thing we can already prove without running anything new: a specific "give partial credit for how many of 3 people agreed" idea we considered cannot possibly show a BIGGER improvement than a more lenient test we already ran and that came up empty -- the math rules it out, so we shouldn't spend time building that exact version. A more useful variant of the same idea (score the WHOLE ranking, not just the top pick) is still open and worth a small, cheap try. If the cleaner test still comes up tied, that's a real, useful answer too -- it would mean the limitation is in finding true cause-and-effect links in messy text, not in how we're grading the test.
