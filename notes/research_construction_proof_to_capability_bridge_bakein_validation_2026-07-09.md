## HEADLINE

Three independent literatures (ML/interpretability epistemics, developmental/core-knowledge neuroscience, and compositional-generalization benchmark design) converge on the **identical three-part criterion** for "construction-proof" vs "genuine capability," and by that criterion **all three current bake-in MMs are correctly tiered as construction-proofs, not capability wins — and none of them has been tested against it, because none has been asked to run through a channel it wasn't built to satisfy.** The bake-in track is not over-credited in its VET tiering (the landed-VET call on same-different was the RIGHT call), but it IS at risk of being over-credited in program narrative if "3 bake-in MMs" gets counted as capability progress before any of them clears a capstone. The fix is structural, not rhetorical: every bake-in mechanism needs one capstone test, pre-registered before it's built, that routes the primitive through a downstream task it was never handed the answer to.

## The crisp criterion: construction-proof vs. capability

Three independent literatures name the same three necessary conditions, converging from completely different starting points:

1. **Different channel.** The test must exercise the primitive through a behavioral/task channel that is NOT the one used to construct or calibrate it. (ML: Hewitt & Liang 2019 "control tasks" / selectivity = accuracy(real task) minus accuracy(control task) — a probe/test that passes regardless of what's really encoded has zero selectivity. Brain: the Baillargeon-vs-Diamond/Munakata fault line is exactly this — looking-time is the SAME channel object-permanence representations were built to satisfy; manual search/reaching is a DIFFERENT channel, and that's precisely why the field treats the reaching gap as diagnostically important rather than explaining it away. Compositional-generalization: COGS reports 96-99% in-distribution vs 16-35% on held-out novel combinations for the same primitives — proof that "works on the channel it was built for" and "works on a different, novel-combination channel" are empirically dissociable.)
2. **Live alternative / could-fail.** A degenerate or shortcut solution path must exist and be checked for, so a pass is informative rather than guaranteed. (ML: Geirhos et al. 2020 *Nature Machine Intelligence*, "Shortcut Learning in Deep Neural Networks" — the general diagnosis that high accuracy can come from a spurious path uncorrelated with the intended capability; the antidote is a stress/OOD/control condition where the shortcut and the intended mechanism are decoupled. Brain: the VOE-skeptic camp, Sirois & Jackson 2007 and Paulus 2022 ("Should infant psychology rely on the VOE method? Not anymore," *Infant and Child Development*) argue looking-time alone cannot rule out low-level perceptual novelty-detection as the real explanation — i.e. the test as usually run has no live alternative built in.)
3. **Necessity, not just correlation.** Ablating/removing the primitive must measurably degrade the downstream task specifically. (ML: causal scrubbing (Redwood Research; Chan et al. 2022) and Geiger et al. 2021 NeurIPS "Causal Abstractions of Neural Networks" (interchange interventions) formalize necessity vs. sufficiency as logically distinct and empirically checkable — many hand-authored circuit claims fail this test when actually run, which is exactly what makes passing it informative. Brain: Diamond's account of the A-not-B error treats successful action as requiring the representation to WIN against a competing, prepotent response — i.e., necessity under real competition, not mere presence.)

**Construction-proof = same channel + no live alternative + no necessity check.** That is precisely `exp_same_different_bakein_relational_tier_v1`: measured on item-level same/different classification (the exact channel the comparator was built to satisfy), with no alternative solution path possible (the algebra guarantees the answer), and no ablation. Deterministic algebra passing its own definitional test is not evidence of anything beyond "the arithmetic is correct" — which is real and worth having, but is not a capability claim.

## Honest audit verdict

**Is the bake-in track appropriately valued, or over-credited?**

- **Tiering-level: appropriately valued.** The landed-VET call (HARD_PASS -> MEASURED_MECHANISM, not a capability tier) is exactly the right call under all three literatures above. This is the system working as designed — the VET caught a construction-proof before it got mis-labeled as a capability win. No correction needed to that specific call.
- **Track-level (program narrative): at real risk of over-crediting.** All three bake-in MMs (dual-number, object-permanence, same-different) share the same described character — "structural comparator/counter over the existing representational space, no learned readout" (verbatim from the same-different note's own product-implications section). That is a description of a shared construction-proof shape, not three independently-validated capabilities. If the running tally anywhere says "3 bake-in capability wins" rather than "3 construction-proofs, 0 capstones cleared," that is over-crediting by exactly the gap this drill surfaced. Recommend the cap_map / tracking language be changed now, structurally, to separate the two counts, and that **no bake-in mechanism graduates from MEASURED_MECHANISM to a capability tier without a capstone test passing.**
- This is not a reason to distrust the bake-in track's DIRECTION — brain-innate priors are real, and the literature (Spelke core-knowledge, Marcus's algebraic-mind program) says baked-in structural mechanisms are exactly how biological systems get early competence cheaply. The issue is purely that "cheap and structurally guaranteed" and "capability" are different claims, and the track has been producing the first without yet testing for the second.

## Cheap decisive test (the capstone design)

**Task: distractor-merge gate inside multi-hop retrieval.** Use the existing multi-hop query pipeline (already built per the same-day reader-crosstalk thread). At one intermediate hop, before the next bind/unbind step, the pipeline must decide whether two retrieved candidate atoms are the SAME underlying entity (merge/coreference) or DIFFERENT entities (keep separate). The same-different comparator is installed as this internal gate.

Critical design choices that satisfy all three criteria above:
- **Different channel:** the measured output is final multi-hop query ANSWER ACCURACY, never the same/different judgment itself. The comparator's correctness is never directly scored — it's an unlabeled internal step.
- **Live alternative:** run three conditions per trial batch — (i) REAL gate (the comparator), (ii) ABLATED gate (uniform-random merge/keep decision, same call signature), (iii) chance/no-gate baseline (always merge, or always keep, whichever is cheaper to compute). Random-gate is the Hewitt & Liang "control task" analog and the Geirhos "shortcut" check in one.
- **Necessity, with dose-response (avoids a saturation-vacuous smoke, per existing substrate discipline):** sweep distractor count / hop depth. At low distractor count the task should be solvable even under the ablated/random gate (ceiling for everyone — this is the sanity check that the task itself isn't trivial). At higher distractor count, only the REAL gate should hold accuracy up; the ablated condition should measurably degrade. The GAP between real-gate and ablated-gate accuracy, growing with distractor count, is the necessity signature — a static gap that doesn't grow with stress is itself suspicious (could be a fixed offset artifact, not use).
- **Novel-context requirement:** all entity pairs used in the capstone are freshly-drawn atoms never used in constructing/calibrating the same-different primitive itself — this keeps the "novel items" claim honest rather than filler-invariance-by-construction.

### Falsifiable predictions

**HARD-PASS (capability, not just construction-proof):**
At a pre-registered distractor count D* where the ablated/random-gate condition's accuracy has dropped to within a chance-baseline margin (e.g., ablated accuracy <= 0.60 on a task where chance = 0.50), the REAL-gate condition must:
- hold accuracy >= 0.85, reproducible across >= 3 seeds with non-overlapping 95% CIs vs. the ablated condition at the same D*, AND
- show the real-vs-ablated accuracy GAP monotonically increasing over at least 3 distractor-count steps (the dose-response / necessity signature — rules out a fixed, construction-driven offset that doesn't reflect actual use under stress).

**HARD-FAIL (still a construction-proof, not a capability — informative, not a program-ending result):**
- Real-gate accuracy tracks ablated-gate accuracy within noise at every distractor count (comparator present in the pipeline but the pipeline doesn't actually rely on it — same-different is available but not deployed), OR
- The task is solvable at ceiling by the ablated/no-gate condition at ALL tested distractor counts (task design failure — the multi-hop pipeline never stresses the gate; needs a harder task, not evidence against the primitive).
A HARD-FAIL here should route to redesigning the task's stress point (more entities, tighter similarity margins between distractors), exactly the same discipline already applied to relational same-different in the prior drill — not to abandoning the same-different primitive.

## Intuitive summary

The Director's question was sharp and the answer turned out to be unusually clean: three completely different fields of research — how we grade AI language models, how we know what a baby's brain actually does versus what it just notices, and how we test whether a computer program truly combines ideas versus just plugging in numbers — all independently landed on the exact same three-part test for "is this a real ability, or just proof the math works." The test is: (1) check the ability on a DIFFERENT task than the one it was built to pass, (2) make sure there was a real, checkable way for it to fail, and (3) confirm that if you remove the ability, the bigger system actually gets worse at something it wasn't explicitly told to use the ability for. Judged against that test, our three "baked-in" wins so far (a number-sense, an object-permanence sense, and now same-different matching) are all still stuck at step zero — each one was only ever checked on the exact task it was built to satisfy, with no way to fail, and no removal test. That doesn't mean they're wrong or wasted; the brain research says cheap, built-in shortcuts like these are exactly how real brains get a head start. It means we've only proven the wiring works, not that the wiring gets recruited and relied upon. We designed one concrete next step to close that gap for same-different specifically: bury the same-different judgment as an invisible helper step inside a bigger multi-step retrieval task (never scored directly), then check that the whole task gets measurably worse — and increasingly so as the task gets harder — when we swap the real judgment for a coin flip. If that happens, we've proven use, not just construction. If it doesn't, we've learned the primitive is present but not yet load-bearing, which is exactly the kind of honest, informative negative this project is built to chase down rather than paper over.
