# Research: multi-attribute fusion for robust grounding — the ATL hub-and-spoke lever

Date: 2026-07-10
Mode: self-executed lit-scan (explicit no-sub-agent-fanout instruction). Generic psycholinguistic/neuroscience/ML terms only used in all external queries — no substrate-novel mechanism names, configs, or numbers went off-platform.

## HEADLINE

The brain's answer to "how do you get a robust concept from noisy grounding signals" is never one channel — it is convergence of many independent weak channels onto a shared hub, and the neuroscience literature has a named principle (**inverse effectiveness**) stating the weaker each individual channel is, the *larger* the proportional gain from combining it with others. This is a strong, literal justification for multi-attribute fusion as the next lever. BUT the human-norms literature also delivers an unwelcome fact: several of the obvious candidate attributes are **not independent** (imageability/concreteness correlate at r=-0.8 in the one fetched cross-norm study), so naive fusion of concreteness + AoA + imageability adds far less than sqrt(K) would predict — the effective independent channel count is smaller than the nominal attribute count. Valence/arousal/dominance and the Lynott-Connell 5-sense perceptual-strength norms are the more promising *additions* because they are reported as measuring something concreteness/imageability do not (sensory norms are explicitly reported to outperform concreteness/imageability as predictors in some tasks — evidence of non-redundant signal). Verdict: multi-attribute fusion is the right lever, but only if channel independence is measured and enforced before fusing, and only if coverage (not just channel count) is addressed in parallel — a 4th correlated attribute over a 74%-coverage gradient is a much weaker bet than a genuinely orthogonal 2nd channel over denser coverage.

## 1. The brain's multi-channel grounding blueprint

**Architecture.** The hub-and-spoke model (Patterson, Nestor & Rogers 2007, "Where do you know what you know?"; Lambon Ralph et al. 2017 hub-and-spoke review) descends from Damasio's 1989 convergence(-divergence) zone theory: a transmodal hub in the ventrolateral anterior temporal lobe (ATL) receives converging projections from modality-specific "spoke" regions (vision, sound, action/motor, emotion/interoception, praxis) and distills them into a single amodal concept representation. The 2016-era "graded hub" refinement (Cerebral Cortex 2016, distortion-corrected fMRI) shows the ATL is not monolithic — the ventrolateral center-point is the omni-category transmodal convergence point, with peripheral sub-regions weighted toward whichever spokes they're most anatomically connected to. [jneurosci.org/content/36/5/1490](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/36/5/1490), [PMC6006425](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6006425/), [Cerebral Cortex 2016](https://academic.oup.com/cercor/article/26/11/4227/2374073), [convergence-divergence zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convergence-divergence_zone)

**Is the robustness precisely BECAUSE it integrates many independent noisy channels? Yes — this is a mechanistically established principle, not speculation, at the single-neuron level.** The multisensory-integration literature (superior colliculus, Stein and colleagues; independently converged on by the cue-combination psychophysics literature, Ernst & Banks 2002) establishes two load-bearing facts:
1. **Maximum-likelihood cue combination**: the brain weights each channel in inverse proportion to its own noise variance (reliability-weighted averaging), which is the textbook mechanism for turning several individually-noisy estimates into a jointly lower-variance estimate — the same math as ensemble/signal averaging (variance of an average shrinks ~1/K for K independent, reliability-matched channels; SNR gain ~sqrt(K)). [PMC9393257](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9393257/), [Models and processes of multisensory cue combination](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24709599/)
2. **The law of inverse effectiveness**: multisensory neurons in the superior colliculus show *proportionally larger* enhancement precisely when each unimodal input is weak — i.e., the combination gain is largest exactly where a single channel is least informative on its own, and degrades toward the simple superposition/no-gain regime as any one channel becomes independently sufficient. This is the single most directly relevant biological fact for our situation: **a single MARGINAL attribute is exactly the regime where the biology predicts the largest relative payoff from adding a second independent channel**, not a sign the lever is played out. [PMC5375642 — cellular mechanism for inverse effectiveness](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5375642/), [law of inverse effectiveness, neurons and behaviour](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0028393207002345)

**Does the SD (semantic dementia) literature corroborate "many weak channels, none individually necessary"?** Yes, indirectly. Semantic dementia (bilateral ATL atrophy) produces *graceful* degradation — concepts degrade toward prototypes/superordinates rather than collapsing catastrophically, and typicality/frequency-weighted features survive longest. This is consistent with (not direct proof of) a distributed, redundant, many-weak-features representation: if the hub's robustness came from one dominant channel, damage would produce sharp category-specific deficits rather than the observed graceful, redundancy-consistent decay. [PMC2805958](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2805958/), [Lambon Ralph 2008](https://nyaspubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1196/annals.1440.006)

**What this says about our marginal single-attribute result.** The biology is unambiguous on the qualitative direction: K independent measured channels should compound, and should compound *most* where any one channel (like our concreteness signal) is weak — which is exactly the regime we are in (mean lift 0.059, four of five seeds ~0.04). The open empirical question the biology does NOT answer is the magnitude — real biological "channels" (vision, audition, touch) are far more mutually independent than psycholinguistic norms turn out to be (Section 2a). The brain's channels are independent almost by physical construction (different transduction physics); our candidate attributes are independent only to the extent that different *human raters* were asked different *questions* about the same referent, which is a much weaker independence guarantee.

## 2. The multi-attribute fusion hypothesis

**(a) Are the candidate attributes genuinely independent?** Mixed, and this is the load-bearing finding of this drill:
- **Concreteness and imageability are essentially the same factor**, not independent channels: the one fetched cross-norm dataset with an explicit correlation table (ANPW_R, Polish, 4900 words, 8 co-collected dimensions) reports r = -0.80 between imageability and concreteness. [Frontiers ANPW_R](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.01081/full), [PMC4947584](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4947584/) Fusing concreteness + imageability as "two channels" would be close to fusing a variable with a noisy copy of itself — expect near-zero incremental gain, consistent with Section 2(b) below.
- **Valence, arousal, and psycholinguistic variables (concreteness, imageability, familiarity, frequency, AoA) show only weak-but-significant correlations** in the same source — i.e., not collapsed into one factor, but not fully orthogonal either. Exact English-specific (Brysbaert/Warriner/Kuperman) correlation coefficients were not independently fetched this cycle (the search snippets did not surface the correlation table itself) — treat the "weak-but-significant, not collapsed" characterization as corroborated only by the Polish norms proxy dataset, not verified directly against the English megastudies we'd actually join. **This is a concrete, cheap, internal-data pre-flight check we can run ourselves** (Section on pitfalls) rather than trust the literature's approximate consistency across languages.
- **Sensory-modality norms (Lynott & Connell, Lancaster Sensorimotor Norms, ~40k English words, 5 modalities rated independently + action strength) are explicitly reported in the literature to carry information concreteness/imageability do NOT** — modality-specific measures "often outperform long-established measures such as concreteness and imageability" in predicting lexical-decision/naming/property-verification performance. This is the strongest available evidence that sensory-modality norms are a genuinely additional (not redundant) channel, not just concreteness restated. [Lancaster Sensorimotor Norms, PMC7280349](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7280349/), [Lancaster.ac.uk](https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/psychology/lsnorms/)
- AoA is reported elsewhere (general psycholinguistic consensus, not independently re-verified via a fetched correlation table this cycle) to correlate moderately with concreteness (concrete words tend to be acquired earlier) — flag this as a plausible-but-unverified prior, to be measured against our actual joined data before trusting it as a genuine 4th channel.

**(b) Expected effect-size gain from fusing K independent noisy channels.** Ensemble/signal-averaging theory is unambiguous for the *idealized* case: combining K independent, equally-reliable noisy estimators reduces variance ~1/K and grows effective SNR ~sqrt(K) (this is the exact mechanism cited for evoked-potential averaging, NMR/FTIR spectral averaging, and MLE cue-combination alike — the same formula recurs across every one of these literatures independently, which is reassuring convergent evidence for the *math*, not for our specific case). [Ensemble averaging in medical signal processing](https://towardsdatascience.com/ensemble-averaging-in-medical-signal-processing-17116d0cf0d2/), [Signal averaging overview](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/computer-science/signal-averaging) **The catch, confirmed independently by the multi-view learning literature (co-training theory, Blum & Mitchell 1998; and its real-world violation record, Nigam & Ghani 2000): the sqrt(K) gain is contingent on TRUE independence.** When views/channels are correlated, realized gain interpolates between "no gain" (fully redundant, ~ our concreteness/imageability case) and full sqrt(K) (truly independent, ~ our sensory-norms case, tentatively). There is no literature-derived formula for partial correlation in this specific setting; the honest statement is "somewhere between no-gain and sqrt(K), degrading toward no-gain as pairwise attribute correlation rises" — this must be measured on our own joined data, not assumed.

**(c) Concrete fusion mechanics — the literal ATL-hub-analog is the best-supported option.** Three architectures exist in the literature, mapped onto our diffusion/consolidation geometry:
1. **Concatenate as a multi-dim exterior channel** (early fusion). Cheapest: stack [concreteness, valence, arousal, dominance, 5x sensory-strength] as one wider exterior feature vector per concept, feed into the existing diffusion-with-restart loop unchanged. Risk: if any sub-dimensions are correlated (2a), the diffusion implicitly reweights toward whatever direction the correlated block spans, silently un-doing the "K independent channels" premise — a redundant pair counts almost as one vote, not two, unless explicitly decorrelated first (e.g. whiten/orthogonalize the joined attribute matrix before diffusion, or drop one of a highly-correlated pair).
2. **Separate per-attribute diffusion, then combine** (late fusion, MLE-reliability-weighted). Run the existing diffusion-with-restart independently per attribute, producing K separate relational-lift signals per concept, then combine via reliability-weighting analogous to Ernst & Banks MLE cue combination (weight each attribute's contribution by its own measured cross-validated reliability, i.e. inverse variance). This is the most literal translation of the multisensory-integration mechanism, and it has a direct, checkable diagnostic advantage over (1): per-channel ablation becomes trivial (zero out one attribute's diffused signal and measure the delta), which is exactly the anti-collapse / non-redundancy check flagged as the top structural risk in the companion consolidation-loop research note (`notes/research_offline_consolidation_multiview_degree_invariance_prior_art_2026-07-10.md`, Pitfall #3/#5).
3. **A literal hub node that all attributes connect to** (graph-native ATL-hub analog). Add one synthetic "hub" node per concept in the graph itself, with an edge from the hub to each attribute's value-node (or attribute-channel embedding), then let the existing diffusion machinery propagate through the hub as it already does through real ConceptNet edges. This is architecturally the closest to the biology (a literal convergence point that spokes connect to, rather than a vector-space concatenation) and reuses 100% of existing diffusion code — no new consolidation logic needed, only new graph edges. The multimodal-GNN fusion literature's "attention-based fusion block" (adaptive per-modality attention weights at each layer) is the ML-side analog of reliability-weighted hub convergence, and is a reasonable next step if uniform hub-averaging underperforms. [Multimodal Graph Attention Networks](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/multimodal-graph-attention-networks-mgat), [MAGNET missing-modality GNN](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.22901)

Recommended order: start with (2) [separate diffusion + reliability-weighted late combination] because it gives per-channel ablation for free (directly answers the "is this real fusion or fake" question), then graduate to (3) [hub node] only if (2)'s gains justify the added graph-construction complexity.

## 3. Why marginal / other levers

**(a) Coverage.** The missing-node-features / graph-feature-propagation literature is unambiguous that missing features "seriously hurt" GNN-style performance, and that performance degrades in proportion to feature sparsity — feature-propagation methods exist specifically because coverage gaps are treated as a first-order problem, not a rounding error. [Self-supervised guided hypergraph feature propagation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08250), [Dynamic graph structure evolution, missing attributes](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-09840-z) No source directly quantifies "74% coverage vs 95% coverage, same attribute" as an ablation, so we cannot cite a specific expected-lift number — but the qualitative direction from every fetched source agrees: **denser coverage of ONE attribute plausibly buys more than a second attribute layered on the same 74%-coverage subgraph**, because the diffusion mechanism's whole value proposition is propagating a known value into an unknown node's neighborhood — the more nodes start with an unknown value, the more the diffusion is doing unsupported extrapolation rather than propagation. This is a testable, cheap ablation we can run ourselves (vary synthetic coverage fraction on the existing single-attribute setup, holding attribute count fixed at 1, before touching multi-attribute fusion) and should arguably be run BEFORE multi-attribute fusion, since it is cheaper (no new attribute join) and disambiguates "was the marginal result attribute-count-limited or coverage-limited."

**(b) Consolidation hyperparameters (restart alpha, passes, kNN).** Personalized-PageRank/diffusion-with-restart theory gives a precise, literature-verified mechanism for the alpha parameter: lower alpha = the walk explores further before restarting = information diffuses further from the seed (deeper propagation, more nodes touched, more attenuation per hop); higher alpha = tighter, more local, less-diffused signal concentrated near attributed nodes. [Personalized PageRank restart probability](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/personalized-pagerank-algorithm) This gives a concrete, cheap sweep independent of adding attributes: if the current alpha is tuned for coverage=74%/single-attribute, it is very plausibly NOT the alpha that maximizes low-degree-tail lift — an alpha sweep is a same-day, zero-new-data experiment that could move the marginal 0.059 substantially before any multi-attribute work is needed. No source gives a closed-form "optimal alpha for degree-invariant lift" — this has to be swept empirically on our own data.

**(c) Is marginality intrinsic to thin symbolic data, or a bug?** The honest answer, triangulating across all three threads (biology, norms-independence, graph-ML): **single-attribute grounding is EXPECTED to be marginal — this is not evidence of a bug.** Every biological precedent (inverse effectiveness, MLE cue combination) explicitly describes single-channel signals as individually weak; the entire architectural point of the hub is that no spoke is independently sufficient. A single joined attribute achieving a marginal, seed-fragile lift is the textbook single-channel regime, not a sign something is broken. The graph-diffusion-can-only-propagate-where-dense-enough argument (3a) is real but orthogonal — it says WHERE the lift is attenuated (thin-coverage regions), not WHETHER a second attribute would help (the biology says it should, independent of coverage). **Multi-attribute fusion is the more fundamental lever (matches the brain's actual solution to this exact problem); coverage/hyperparameter tuning are cheaper, faster, and should be exhausted first because they're near-free relative to a new attribute join** and will clarify how much of the current marginality is "need more channels" vs "need denser single-channel propagation."

## 4. Pitfalls / what would fake a "fusion works" result

1. **Attribute inter-correlation faking fusion gains.** If concreteness+imageability (r=-0.8, Section 2a) or concreteness+AoA (plausible moderate correlation, unverified) are fused as if independent, an apparent "K-channel gain" can appear that is actually just re-weighting toward the shared underlying factor both attributes encode — indistinguishable from simply up-weighting the original concreteness signal. **Required gate**: measure pairwise correlation among all candidate attributes ON OUR OWN JOINED CONCEPTNET DATA (not assumed from the literature) before fusing; if |r| > ~0.5 between any pair, treat them as one channel (pick one, or explicitly orthogonalize) rather than two.
2. **Overfitting the fusion weights.** The ensemble-fusion literature explicitly documents that reliability/fusion weights tuned via grid search or a meta-classifier on a small validation set "occasionally overfit the validation set," especially producing inflated results that don't transfer, particularly when the validation distribution differs from where the method is actually deployed. [Multi-modal fusion, inter-modality correlations](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11687621) **Required gate**: hold out a genuinely separate validation split for weight-tuning distinct from the seeds/split used for the HARD-PASS/FAIL decision; report weight stability across seeds (if the learned per-attribute weights swing wildly seed-to-seed the way the current single-attribute lift does, that alone is evidence of overfitting, not real fusion).
3. **Per-attribute fairness-gate violations — some attributes may be degree-loaded or graph-determined.** The companion consolidation-loop note's #1 identified risk directly generalizes here: popular/high-degree concepts plausibly have MORE recorded attributes across ALL norm databases (richer Wikipedia/ConceptNet presence correlates with richer psycholinguistic-norm coverage too), meaning attribute RICHNESS itself may already be degree-correlated before any diffusion happens. **Required gate**: run the existing scrambled-attribute control (which already correctly fired at -0.54 for the single-attribute case) independently for EACH new attribute before trusting its individual contribution, plus a joint fairness check that fusion doesn't relaunder a degree signal that any single attribute's gate would have caught alone.
4. **Silent leakage in the multi-attribute version.** More attributes = more joins = more surface area for one attribute's raw value (rather than its diffused/consolidated value) to leak directly into the eval signal, especially if any attribute happens to be measured densely exactly where the held-out eval set concentrates (e.g. common/high-frequency words are both easiest to norm AND likeliest to be oversampled in a naive eval split). **Required gate**: re-run the same held-out/eval construction discipline from the single-attribute cell (whatever prevented raw-value leakage there) for every new attribute individually, not just for the final fused result.
5. **The "one lucky seed" pattern repeating.** The single-attribute result already shows this fragility (one seed at 0.135 driving the mean over the 0.05 bar while 4/5 average ~0.04). Multi-attribute fusion, done incorrectly (redundant channels, overfit weights), could easily produce an even MORE seed-fragile result that looks stronger in aggregate purely because more knobs give more chances for one seed to align well by chance. **Required gate**: report the full per-seed distribution for the fused result, not just the mean, and require the bar to clear on the MEDIAN or on 4-of-5 seeds individually, not the mean alone (this is a stricter bar than the single-attribute result cleared, deliberately, precisely because Section 4 identifies several fusion-specific ways to inflate the mean without genuine robustness gain).

## BUILD RECOMMENDATION

**Yes — the next cell should be MULTI-ATTRIBUTE FUSION**, but sequenced behind two cheaper, faster diagnostic steps that the biology and graph-ML literature both independently flag as prerequisites, not optional extras:

**Rank-ordered anchor candidates (no inline experiment design — cell-author's call on concrete pre-reg/thresholds):**

1. **[Do first, same-day, near-zero new cost] Alpha/hyperparameter sweep on the EXISTING single-attribute cell.** Per Section 3(b): sweep restart-alpha (and kNN/passes if applicable) on the current concreteness-only setup before touching multi-attribute. Disambiguates "the marginal 0.059 was a hyperparameter artifact" from "the marginal 0.059 is the honest single-channel ceiling" — directly informs how much of the observed marginality Section 3 attributes to this lever vs Section 2's.
2. **[Do second, cheap, no new attribute join] Coverage-density ablation on the existing attribute.** Per Section 3(a): synthetically vary coverage fraction (subsample the existing concreteness join at 40%/55%/74%/90%) holding attribute count at 1, to separate "coverage-limited" from "channel-count-limited" marginality. This is strictly cheaper than joining a new attribute and answers a live, otherwise-unresolved question in Section 3(c).
3. **[The main lever, do third] Multi-attribute fusion: concreteness + valence/arousal/dominance (Warriner) + AoA (Kuperman) + 5-sense perceptual-strength (Lynott & Connell), via architecture (2) from Section 2c — separate per-attribute diffusion, reliability-weighted late combination (MLE/Ernst-Banks-analog).** Why this ordering, why this architecture: (a) late fusion gives free per-channel ablation, directly answering "is this genuine K-channel gain or one channel doing all the work" (Pitfall #1/#5); (b) reliability-weighting is the single most literature-validated combination rule available (recurs independently across cue-integration, ensemble-averaging, and signal-processing literatures); (c) sensory-modality norms are the best-evidenced genuinely-non-redundant addition (Section 2a) and should be prioritized over adding more concreteness-adjacent attributes (imageability) that are likely to just restate the existing signal.
4. **[Gate, must run alongside 3, not after] Pre-flight pairwise-correlation matrix among all candidate attributes measured on OUR OWN joined ConceptNet data** (Pitfall #1), **plus the per-attribute scrambled-control fairness gate** (Pitfall #3) for each new attribute individually before trusting the fused result. This is infrastructure, not a claim-bearing step, but per the companion consolidation-loop note's cross-thread synthesis, skipping this is the single most common way multi-channel results get faked across every literature surveyed this cycle.

**Expected effect-size gain, honestly bounded**: between "near-zero" (if the added attributes turn out correlated with concreteness the way imageability is, r~-0.8-tier) and "roughly sqrt(K)-scaled" (if valence/arousal/dominance + sensory-strength turn out close to independent, as the Lynott-Connell "outperforms concreteness/imageability" finding tentatively suggests). For K_effective ~ 2-3 genuinely-independent channels (a plausible outcome after pruning correlated attributes), sqrt(K) implies roughly 1.4x-1.7x variance-reduction-equivalent, which — IF it translated linearly to the lift metric (unverified assumption, flagged explicitly) — would move the mean from ~0.059 toward something in the ~0.08-0.10 range and, more importantly, should narrow the seed-to-seed spread (the more diagnostic target, since the current result's problem is fragility, not just magnitude).

## IS THIS THE RIGHT LEVER — verdict

**Yes, directionally** — this is the single strongest, most literature-convergent answer available to "what would make grounding robust rather than marginal": every biological precedent for robust grounding (hub-and-spoke, convergence zones, MLE cue combination, inverse effectiveness) is fundamentally a multi-channel story, not a single-channel-tuning story. But the honest caveat, driven entirely by this cycle's own findings (not hedging for its own sake): the naive version of "add more attributes" is weaker than it sounds, because several of the most obvious candidate attributes (concreteness, imageability, plausibly AoA) are reported to be substantially correlated, meaning the effective K is smaller than the nominal attribute count, and the sqrt(K) SNR-gain formula this whole recommendation leans on has never been validated for partially-correlated psycholinguistic channels specifically — it is imported wholesale from cue-integration/ensemble-averaging literatures where "independence" is closer to physically guaranteed. This is genuine novel-synthesis territory (no prior-art match found for "multi-attribute human-norm fusion via diffusion consolidation for KG degree-invariance" as a combined system) per the lit-scan calibration discipline.

**P_deflated = 0.40** (below the novel-synthesis cap of 0.50, deflated further because: (i) the core independence claims for the specific attribute set are corroborated by a non-English proxy dataset, not directly verified on the English megastudies we'd actually use; (ii) the sqrt(K) effect-size expectation is imported from adjacent literatures, not measured in this exact setting; (iii) the fusion-mechanics recommendation is architecturally well-motivated but entirely unproven in a diffusion-with-restart-on-ConceptNet setting specifically).

## Cheap decisive test (pre-registered)

Per BUILD RECOMMENDATION ordering: (1) alpha sweep on existing single-attribute cell (same-day); (2) coverage-density ablation, same attribute (cheap); (3) pairwise attribute-correlation matrix on our OWN joined data for the 4 candidate attributes (concreteness, VAD, AoA, sensory-strength) — cheapest possible falsifier for the whole multi-attribute hypothesis, since if attributes turn out highly mutually correlated on our actual graph, fusion is moot before any diffusion loop is re-run.

## Falsifiable predictions

**HARD-PASS (fusion is real, robust, and worth keeping) — require ALL of:**
1. Pairwise |correlation| between every pair of the 4 candidate attributes, measured on our joined ConceptNet data, is < 0.5 for at least 2 of the 4 attributes relative to concreteness (evidence of genuine non-redundancy for at least a 3-channel effective fusion, not a 1-channel-restated fusion).
2. Fused mean relational lift on the low-degree tail clears the existing 0.05 bar on the **median** seed (not just the mean) — a strictly harder bar than the single-attribute result cleared, per Pitfall #5.
3. Per-channel ablation (zero out any one attribute's diffused contribution) degrades the fused result by a nontrivial amount (>=15% relative) for at least 2 of the fused attributes — proves the fusion isn't secretly running on one dominant channel.
4. Seed-to-seed spread (max-min across 5 seeds) of the fused lift is NARROWER, in relative terms, than the single-attribute result's spread (0.018-0.135, a 7.5x range) — the primary evidence that fusion bought robustness, not just a marginally higher mean.
5. Per-attribute scrambled-control fires (goes clearly negative, analogous to the existing -0.54 single-attribute control) for every individual attribute before it's trusted in the fused combination.

**HARD-FAIL (reject fusion as the lever, or reject this specific attribute set) — ANY of:**
1. All 4 candidate attributes pairwise-correlate at |r| > 0.6 on our own joined data (the "collapsed into one factor" outcome) — fusion is moot, re-derive candidate attribute set or pivot to coverage/hyperparameter levers instead.
2. Fused result's seed-to-seed spread is WIDER (in relative terms) than the single-attribute result — fusion made things more fragile, not less; likely symptom of overfit weights (Pitfall #2) or a redundant-channel artifact (Pitfall #1).
3. Zeroing out 3-of-4 attributes changes the fused result by <=5% relative — the fusion has collapsed onto one dominant channel (equivalent to the multi-view "informational collapse" failure mode from the companion consolidation note).
4. Any individual attribute's scrambled-control fails to fire clearly negative — that attribute is degree-loaded or graph-determined and is relaundering a shortcut, not contributing genuine grounding signal (Pitfall #3).

## Cross-thread synthesis

Every literature drilled this cycle — neuroscience (hub-and-spoke, inverse effectiveness, MLE cue combination), psycholinguistics (norms intercorrelation), and graph-ML (multimodal GNN fusion, missing-feature propagation) — independently converges on the same two-part structure: **(1) multi-channel combination is the correct, well-precedented lever for turning a weak single signal into a robust one, and (2) the entire value of that lever is contingent on a genuine-independence premise that must be actively verified, never assumed** — every one of the three literatures has its own named failure mode for exactly this (redundant-cue non-improvement in psychophysics, co-training's real-world independence violations, GNN multi-view "informational collapse"). This mirrors, almost exactly, the cross-thread finding of the companion same-day consolidation-loop research note (`notes/research_offline_consolidation_multiview_degree_invariance_prior_art_2026-07-10.md`) — that note's Pitfall #3 ("verify neither channel predicts the other above chance before trusting agreement") is the SAME structural requirement as this note's Pitfall #1 ("measure pairwise attribute correlation before fusing"). The two notes are not independently arrived-at coincidences; they are the same underlying discipline (verify non-redundancy, don't assume it) surfacing at two different levels of the same architecture (structural-vs-attribute channel independence in the consolidation loop; attribute-vs-attribute independence within the exterior-channel fusion). Any future cell that fuses multiple sources of grounding signal, at any level, should treat "measure independence on our own data before trusting agreement/fusion" as a standing pre-flight gate, not a one-off check.

## Substrate-product implications

For the next cell: this is license to build multi-attribute fusion (three independent literatures converge on the *shape* being biologically and statistically well-motivated), but the FIRST two artifacts should be the cheap disambiguation steps (alpha/coverage sweep on the existing single attribute) before spending any new-data-join effort, and the pairwise-correlation pre-flight gate must run BEFORE the fusion loop, not as a post-hoc audit. None of this is a claim that our substrate replicates ATL function — it's an engineering discipline (verified non-redundancy as a precondition for combination) borrowed from three converging literatures, applied to a concrete, cheap, falsifiable next cell. The framing throughout is product-relevant: a robust (not marginal, not seed-fragile) grounding signal is what would let a downstream consumer of the relational-lift trust it without per-seed caveats — that is the actual bar "robust" needs to clear, not merely a higher mean.
