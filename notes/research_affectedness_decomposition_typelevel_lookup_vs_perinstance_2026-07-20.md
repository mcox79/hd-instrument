# Research: does affectedness decompose into a lookup-able TYPE-LEVEL component + a genuinely per-instance residual? (2026-07-20)

Director synthesis of a 3x parallel lit-scan (brain type-vs-instance split for patienthood; structured-resource lookup-ability of type-level affectedness; residual per-instance ambiguity quantification), built directly on four same-day prior notes: `research_brain_patienthood_affectedness_grounding_2026-07-20.md`, `research_functional_vs_topical_encoding_dependency_context_2026-07-20.md`, `research_plausibility_web_engineering_resources_adoptable_foundation_2026-07-20.md`, `research_brain_building_event_plausibility_web_2026-07-20.md`. All prior work credited (learn-from/build-on, never "steal"). Deflated throughout per lit-scan-calibration.

**FRAMING (load-bearing, stated up front): this drill does NOT reopen atom 29375's TEXT-DERIVATION real-null.** 29375 tested whether 6 self-supervised TEXT signals could DERIVE per-instance who-is-affected; that stays closed. This drill tests a DIFFERENT route — structured LOOKUP of a decomposed, TYPE-LEVEL piece of the same problem, reusing the single-edge lookup-store-recall-generalize machinery (atom 29390) that already works for VerbNet-sourced edges, and the location/artifact derived-typing precedent (atom 29391).

## HEADLINE

**Affectedness genuinely decomposes into two difficulty classes, and the split is asymmetric in a way that favors partial recovery via lookup.** (1) A TYPE-LEVEL selectional-preference component — verb-class predicts the SEMANTIC TYPE of its typical affected argument (break→breakable-object, give→animate-recipient, pour→liquid-theme) — is well-supported as verb-general/stable in the psycholinguistic literature (Ferretti/McRae/Hatherell 2001's strong verb-to-patient priming, contrasted with weak verb-to-location priming; Dowty's 1991 proto-patient entailments) AND is directly lookup-able from at least three structured resources (VerbNet selectional restrictions on Patient/Product roles, PropBank Arg1 roleset-level typing, ATOMIC-2020 xEffect/oEffect), each of which maps cleanly onto the same storable-edge shape already validated for location/artifact typing (29391). (2) A genuinely PER-INSTANCE residual — which of two candidates satisfying the SAME type constraint was actually affected in this specific event — stays event-combinatorial (Bicknell et al. 2010's agent×verb×context finding) and is NOT resolved by type lookup; this is the harder class the lit independently converges on (Kauf et al. 2023's possible-vs-impossible / likely-vs-unlikely dissociation lands in almost the same place). The estimated split (below) suggests the lookup route recovers a real, non-trivial minority slice of who-is-affected failures — enough to be worth a design-gated can-fail test on the 29375 items, not enough to close the wall.

## Cheap decisive test

Build a **type-level affectedness lookup edge**: for each verb (or VerbNet class), look up the Patient/Theme/Product role's selectional restriction (VerbNet `+animate`/`+concrete`/`+liquid`/etc., or PropBank Arg1 roleset gloss, or ATOMIC-2020 xEffect/oEffect outcome type) and store it as a static edge exactly like the 29391 location/artifact type-edge (verb-class → affected-role-TYPE). At inference, on the same 29375 / LCCP gold-patient eval items, score: does the type-edge alone (a) rule OUT candidates whose semantic type doesn't match the restriction, and (b) among remaining candidates, does an argmax-by-type-fit beat the closed text-derivation baseline (argmax pick-gold 0.474 vs. structural 0.684)?

**Design gate (per feedback-experiment-design-gate, verify at smoke before full run):**
1. Real baseline = the EXISTING closed text-derived signal from 29375 (not a strawman) — run on the identical item set/split.
2. Discriminator can-fail: the type-edge must be able to score WORSE than the closed baseline (many items will have both candidates of the same coarse type, e.g. two animate NPs under a "hurt" verb — the type-edge should show ZERO discriminating power there, not get a free pass).
3. Difficulty actually on: use items where BOTH candidates are syntactically plausible objects (the actual ambiguous cases 29375 failed on), not the easy semantically-unambiguous subset.
4. One variable differs: only the ADDITION of the type-lookup edge as a candidate-filter/re-ranker on top of (or in place of) the closed baseline; same eval items, same gold labels, same scoring.

**Arms:** (a) closed text-derived baseline alone [=29375, the real baseline]; (b) type-lookup edge alone (VerbNet/PropBank/ATOMIC selectional-restriction filter + argmax-by-type-fit); (c) type-lookup edge as a FILTER/prior combined with the closed baseline (type-edge narrows candidate set, baseline breaks remaining ties); (d) random-type control (a scrambled verb→type mapping — must NOT beat chance, sanity check for label leakage in the eval harness).

## Falsifiable predictions

**HARD-PASS:** on the subset of 29375 items where the two candidates have DIFFERENT semantic types under the verb's selectional restriction (the type-discriminable subset), the type-lookup edge (b) or combined (c) resolves who-is-affected at accuracy meaningfully above the closed baseline's 0.474 argmax-pick-gold rate — by a pre-registered margin (>= 0.05 absolute accuracy on that subset) — AND does not regress accuracy on the type-INDISCRIMINABLE subset (same-type candidates) below the closed baseline's own performance there. This would mean: a real, bounded slice of who-is-affected is recoverable via lookup without re-litigating the per-instance residual.

**HARD-FAIL:** the type-lookup edge shows no meaningful lift over the closed baseline even on the type-discriminable subset (< 0.02 accuracy difference), OR the type-discriminable subset turns out to be a small minority of items (<15-20% of the 29375 failure set) such that even a clean win there doesn't move the aggregate needle. Either result would mean the TYPE/INSTANCE split, while real in principle, does not carve the 29375 item distribution usefully — most of 29375's actual failures live in the type-INdiscriminable (same-type-candidates) bucket, and the lookup route is a genuine-but-small win, not a strategic reopening.

**Must-fail control:** the random-type-mapping arm (d) must not beat chance on the type-discriminable subset — if it does, the eval harness has a leakage/construction problem independent of the hypothesis.

## Cross-thread synthesis

### 1. Brain: the type-vs-instance split for patienthood (drilled deeper this cycle)

Already established in `research_brain_patienthood_affectedness_grounding_2026-07-20.md` (Dowty 1991 proto-patient entailments as verb-class-general; Ferretti/McRae/Hatherell 2001 strong verb→patient priming vs weak verb→location priming) and `research_functional_vs_topical_encoding_dependency_context_2026-07-20.md`. This cycle's sub-agent (litscan-typeinstance) drilled for a QUANTITATIVE split and for Bicknell/GEK ablation evidence.

**Confirmed, with numbers, not just qualitative contrast.** Bicknell, Elman, Hare, McRae & Kutas (2010, *JML* 63:489) is the direct empirical test: verb+patient plausibility HELD CONSTANT, only the AGENT varied ("the mechanic/journalist checked the brakes/spelling"). The instance-specific (agent-conditioned) residual produced real but MODEST effects — ~20ms/18ms self-paced-reading spillover facilitation (p<.002/.003) and an N400 amplitude difference of ~1.8µV (p<.05), riding on top of what the design implies is a much larger baseline plausibility effect set by verb-level expectation (the manipulation only shows up at spillover, not at the patient word itself — i.e. the initial patient-fit computation is fast/verb-driven; the agent-integration correction arrives a beat later). Rissman & Majid (2019, *Psychon. Bull. Rev.*) adds a useful further distinction inside "instance-level" machinery itself: Agent/Patient are supported cross-linguistically/cognitively as abstract, general categories, while Instrument encoding is comparatively verb-specific (Koenig et al.'s "behead" vs "kill" contrast) — i.e. even within thematic roles, Patient sits closer to the abstract/type-general end than some other roles, which is a point IN FAVOR of patient-type being unusually lookup-friendly relative to, say, instrument-type.

**No paper gives an explicit percentage split** between type-level and instance-level contribution — the sub-agent is explicit that its proportional framing ("type-level does the majority of discriminative work in typical/plausible cases; instance-integration is a real but secondary refinement, most necessary in reversal/ambiguous cases") is its own synthesis, not an established number. Talmy's (1988) force-dynamics connection to "affectedness is verb-class-predictable" could not be independently re-verified from accessible text this cycle and stays flagged unverified (consistent with the prior note's treatment of Talmy as the embodied-primitive layer, not a quantitative claim).

### 2. Structured resources as lookup-able type-level affectedness — weaker fit than hoped for 2 of 3 candidates

Builds on `research_plausibility_web_engineering_resources_adoptable_foundation_2026-07-20.md`'s resource inventory. This cycle's sub-agent (litscan-lexicons) drilled specifically for whether each resource's restriction is genuinely CLASS-level (shared across many verbs, hence a compact, generalizing, storable edge) or effectively per-item. **The answer is asymmetric, and it changes the design:**

- **VerbNet — the only clean class-level fit.** `SELRESTRS`/`SELRESTR` XML elements attach `type` (animate/concrete/location/organization/etc.) + polarity to each thematic role (Patient, Theme, Product) PER VERB CLASS, confirmed via VerbNet's own schema and the NLTK VerbNet reader — a class like `send-11.1` supplies one shared restriction to every member verb, which is exactly the "verb-class → affected-role-TYPE" edge shape hypothesized. Caveat (unverified, secondary-source only): the restriction set is coarse (a handful of binary features) and reportedly incomplete/inconsistent across classes; **no paper was found that directly scores VerbNet SELRESTRS as a standalone predictor of gold patient semantic type** — this specific number does not exist in the literature and would need to be measured at the design-gate, not assumed.
- **PropBank Arg1 — mostly verb/roleset-SPECIFIC, NOT class-general.** This is a genuine downward revision from the framing in the task brief. Arg1's meaning is fixed per-roleset in individual frame files (e.g. `give.01` Arg1 = "thing given") and the SRL literature explicitly states no consistent cross-verb generalization holds for higher-numbered arguments — Arg1 is only loosely "proto-patient" in the Dowty sense, not a fixed cross-verb ontological type. Standard SRL argument-labeling accuracy (~85-91 F1, CoNLL-2009-era) measures assigning the Arg1 LABEL to an already-identified span, not predicting the FILLER'S semantic type — a different task than what this drill needs. **PropBank alone does not give the compact, generalizing edge; it would need VerbNet-alignment (via SemLink) to inherit class-level generalization, which the resource-inventory note already flagged as the intended glue.**
- **ATOMIC-2020 xEffect/oEffect — event-level, not verb-class-level.** Effects are tied to a specific EVENT PHRASE ("PersonX votes for PersonY" → "PersonY feels honored"), with no verb-class index; near-paraphrase events get independent entries unless COMET's neural generalization is invoked (which reopens the offline-vs-online glass-box distinction already resolved in the companion note — offline-dump-only). This is a weaker fit for "verb-class → type" than assumed; ATOMIC is better suited as an outcome/effect lookup for SPECIFIC events already identified, not as a compact class-level typing table.
- **FrameNet** — only a "small portion" of frame elements carry an explicit WordNet-synset type; most are prose-gloss only, so machine-readable type lookup is sparse. Partial class-level sharing exists through frame inheritance (Undergoer↔Patient), similar in spirit to VerbNet but less complete.

**Net effect: of the three hypothesized lookup sources, only VerbNet cleanly delivers the class-level, compact, storable edge this drill's mechanism requires; PropBank and ATOMIC-2020 need SemLink-mediated inheritance from VerbNet (or event-specific rather than class-general use) to contribute the same way.** This narrows, but does not eliminate, the lookup route — it means the design-gate cell should build the VerbNet-SELRESTRS edge FIRST and treat PropBank/ATOMIC as secondary/supplementary rather than co-equal sources.

**Ceiling numbers for selectional-preference-only disambiguation (directly relevant, verified):** Resnik (1997, ACL SIGLEX W97-0209) — unsupervised WordNet-class selectional-association for sense disambiguation: 44.3% mean accuracy (verb-object) vs. 28.5% random baseline; Resnik's OWN conclusion flags limited broad-coverage utility because "many verbs and modifiers simply do not select strongly enough" — an explicit, author-stated ceiling caveat, not a lit-scan inference. Heinzerling, Moosavi & Strube (2017, arXiv:1707.06456) — selectional-preference features for coreference CANDIDATE DISAMBIGUATION (structurally the closest published analog to "which of several candidates does this fill") gave only a marginal CoNLL F1 gain (65.60→65.90), with the authors themselves questioning whether such a small improvement is practically meaningful. Zapirain et al. (2013, *Comp. Ling.* 39(3):631) reports much larger SP gains (~20-40 F1) but for standard role CLASSIFICATION (labeling an already-segmented argument), not type-prediction/candidate-disambiguation, and the primary source could not be fetched this cycle (403-blocked) — flagged unverified/secondary-only, do not treat as certain.

### 3. The residual: how much of 29375's failure is genuinely per-instance

This cycle's sub-agent (litscan-residual) found the single most directly load-bearing new number for this drill: **Chersoni et al. (2017, "Is Structure Necessary for Modeling Argument Expectations in Distributional Semantics?", arXiv:1710.00998), using the Bicknell et al. 2010 stimulus set directly (100 plausible/implausible triples, 50 verbs × 2 agents, patient held type-compatible with BOTH agents by construction).** On "Accuracy 1" — differing-by-patient at fixed agent+verb, i.e. exactly the scenario where the verb's typical/type-level expectation is satisfied by BOTH candidates and only agent-specific integration can resolve it — the best STRUCTURED (dependency-based) model reached only **62%**, and an UNSTRUCTURED bag-of-arguments model (the closest available proxy to "type/verb-alone" information) scored **53%, essentially chance**. This is a direct, verified, quantitative demonstration that on the maximally-ambiguous-by-construction subset (both candidates type-compatible), type/verb-level information alone carries almost no discriminating power — confirming the HARD-FAIL risk this drill's design gate explicitly pre-registered.

Important scope caveat (flagged by the sub-agent and independently by this synthesis): the Bicknell/Chersoni stimulus set is DELIBERATELY constructed to be maximally type-ambiguous (that is the point of the experimental design) — it is a worst-case bound on type-alone performance, not necessarily representative of the general population of who-is-affected items in ordinary text, where many verb+candidate pairs will NOT be so finely balanced. Pedinotti et al. 2021 (already credited; re-verified with numbers this cycle via ar5iv full text) adds a complementary data point: on PatientDTFit (323 typical/402 atypical pairs), binary typicality classification reaches 0.85-0.91 accuracy — high — but drops to 0.63-0.73 on the harder Wang2018 patient set and to near-chance (0.37-0.53) on a low-frequency-synonym diagnostic control, showing current models lean on surface/lexical shortcuts rather than genuine type-vs-instance integration even where they look accurate.

**No study — this cycle or prior — gives a naturalistic base rate for how often real (non-constructed) who-is-affected items fall into the type-discriminable vs. type-ambiguous bucket.** This is the single largest open gap in the evidence base for this drill's central design-gate question (what fraction of 29375's actual item set is type-discriminable). Gordon & Van Durme (2013, AKBC@CIKM Best Paper) supports reporting bias as a general phenomenon (generic/type-level facts are themselves distorted in frequency relative to real-world base rates) but does NOT give a verified number specifically comparing instance-level vs. type-level underreporting — the sub-agent flags its extension to that specific comparison as its own inference, not the paper's claim.

### Estimated decomposition (synthesis — explicitly flagged, not a literature-stated split)

Triangulating: (i) the brain/priming evidence (Ferretti et al.'s strong verb→patient effect, Rissman & Majid's Patient-as-abstract-category standing) favors type-level information doing real discriminative work in the GENERAL case; (ii) the Chersoni/Bicknell number (53% ≈ chance on maximally-ambiguous-by-construction items) is a verified worst-case floor showing type-alone information is genuinely useless on the hardest, deliberately-balanced subset; (iii) two of the three hypothesized lookup sources (PropBank, ATOMIC-2020) turn out weaker/less class-general than assumed, so the realistic lookup route is narrower (VerbNet-led) than the task brief's three-source framing suggested; (iv) no naturalistic base-rate exists for the actual mix in real text or in the 29375 item set specifically.

**Honest estimate: roughly 55-70% of who-is-affected instances are plausibly type-discriminable (candidates differ enough in VerbNet-restriction-covered semantic type that a lookup edge could in principle disambiguate), with a genuine 30-45% residual that is per-instance/type-ambiguous and NOT addressable by lookup** — lower confidence and a narrower type-resolvable majority than the residual sub-agent's own looser 70-85%/15-30% guess, specifically discounted for (a) VerbNet restriction coverage/coarseness being unverified and likely incomplete, (b) the Chersoni floor showing the ambiguous bucket is not a rare edge case when it occurs, it is near-total type-information failure, and (c) PropBank/ATOMIC's weaker class-generality meaning less of the "70-85%" majority is actually LOOKUP-ABLE even if it is nominally type-discriminable in principle. This estimate should be treated as a hypothesis to be measured at the design-gate (the type-discriminable-fraction check is itself part of the HARD-FAIL band), not a bankable number.

## Substrate-product implications

- If HARD-PASS on the type-discriminable subset: this is a genuinely CHEAP, glass-box, build-time-only addition — reuse the exact single-edge lookup-store-recall-generalize machinery already validated (29390) with a new edge source (VerbNet/PropBank/ATOMIC selectional restrictions), no architecture change, no runtime LLM, no grounding/perception escalation for this slice of the problem.
- If HARD-FAIL or the type-discriminable subset is a small minority: this cleanly BOUNDS the lookup route's contribution and correctly keeps the bulk of who-is-affected in the per-instance/grounding-adjacent bucket where 29375 already left it — diagnostic progress either way, not wasted effort.
- Either outcome keeps 29375's TEXT-DERIVATION closure intact and correctly scoped — this drill is strictly about a DIFFERENT route (structured lookup) on a DECOMPOSED sub-problem (type-level), not a re-litigation of whether text alone can derive per-instance affectedness (it cannot, per 29375).
- Consistent with the standing pivot to build the missing self-monitoring/learning layer from adopted prior art (engineering recombination, not novel research): VerbNet/PropBank/ATOMIC-2020 are all static, license-navigable, glass-box-legal resources already inventoried in `research_plausibility_web_engineering_resources_adoptable_foundation_2026-07-20.md`.

## P (deflated per lit-scan-calibration; novel-synthesis capped at 0.50)

- **P(the LOOKUP route reopens a MEANINGFUL, actionable chunk of who-is-affected on the 29375 item set, i.e. HARD-PASS band fires): 0.30** (novel synthesis, well under the 0.50 cap). Built up from: type/instance decomposition is real in principle (brain evidence solid) → but only 1 of 3 hypothesized lookup sources (VerbNet) is a clean class-level fit → and even class-level selectional-preference signal has a literature-documented, author-acknowledged ceiling (Resnik 44.3%/28.5%; Heinzerling's near-flat 65.60→65.90 F1 on the closest analogous task) → and the worst-case/maximally-ambiguous subset shows near-chance (53%) type-alone performance (Chersoni/Bicknell) → and no naturalistic base rate exists for how much of the ACTUAL 29375 item set is even type-discriminable. This is higher than the sibling dependency-PPMI note's affectedness-transfer P=0.12 (that mechanism was a weaker, purely distributional context-shape change; this mechanism directly injects curated selectional-restriction knowledge as an edge, a stronger intervention) but still well below even-odds given the resource-generalization and ceiling findings above.
- **P(VerbNet SELRESTRS alone, without PropBank/ATOMIC supplementation, gives a usable class-level edge for MOST verbs in a typical eval set): 0.40** (restriction coverage/completeness is unverified and flagged as likely incomplete by secondary sources; this is the load-bearing uncertainty for arm (b)/(c) even succeeding at all before considering whether it beats the baseline).
- **P(the type-discriminable subset is >=20% of the 29375 failure set, i.e. large enough that even a clean type-lookup win moves the aggregate number): 0.45** (genuinely unknown; the 55-70% estimate above is speculative synthesis, and the HARD-FAIL band explicitly treats <15-20% as a null result on relevance grounds even if the mechanism "works").
- **P(this result, whichever direction, correctly keeps 29375's text-derivation closure intact and does not require reopening it): 0.90** (high confidence — this drill is scoped to a structurally different route by design, and nothing found this cycle bears on whether text alone can derive per-instance affectedness).
