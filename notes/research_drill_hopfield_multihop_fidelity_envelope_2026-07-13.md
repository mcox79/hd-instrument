# Research drill: does the dense/Hopfield fidelity-vs-retrieval negative have a multi-hop escape hatch?

**Filed by:** research sub-agent. **Trigger:** direct envelope-push dispatch on a VET-confirmed negative (dense/modern-Hopfield super-quadratic readout lifts reconstruction-fidelity capacity but not retrieval/identification; single-hop link-prediction only needs retrieval; the fidelity lift did not deploy and vanishes at large scale). Task: find whether a MULTI-HOP/COMPOSITIONAL/SEQUENTIAL regime exists where fidelity (not retrieval) becomes the binding constraint, because an intermediate entity's reconstruction quality must survive to feed the next hop and errors compound.

**Method:** brain-first (1 lit-scan), then field-specific dense-Hopfield/multi-hop scan (1 lit-scan), then generic chained-error-propagation math scan (1 lit-scan), all Sonnet, generic public terms only, no substrate-novel names off-platform. Then synthesis against existing on-substrate multi-hop work.

## HEADLINE

**The envelope-push is real IN PRINCIPLE but CONDITIONAL, and the condition is a number we already have and haven't checked.** Two independent, directly-evidenced formal results (not analogy) establish that per-step error SHAPE stops being irrelevant and starts dominating once chain length crosses a threshold set by the per-step error rate itself:

1. **Ross & Bagnell 2010 (DAgger paper, imitation learning):** naive step-wise chaining where each step's output feeds the next gives an end-to-end error bound of **min(H, epsilon*H^2)** for horizon H and per-step error rate epsilon — i.e. QUADRATIC in chain length once H gtr-approx 1/epsilon, vs. a corrected O(epsilon*H) linear regime below that. This is the crispest available "fidelity/shape starts to matter more as chains lengthen" theorem in the literature, extended from discrete actions to continuous regression (fidelity-style) settings by Venkatraman/Hebert/Bagnell 2015.
2. **Density evolution (Richardson-Urbanke, LDPC/turbo decoding):** soft (real-valued, high-fidelity) message passing has a strictly better noise threshold than hard-decision (identification-only) message passing, and — critically — this gap is an ITERATION-DEPTH phenomenon: at 1 iteration hard vs. soft barely differ; the advantage compounds as iterations/hops increase.

Brain side: CA3 attractor pattern completion is architecturally built for high-fidelity reconstruction, not coarse classification (Rolls 2013; Neunuebel & Knierim 2014), and the sharpest, best-evidenced neuro finding is the ASYMMETRY itself — single-item recognition tolerates coarse familiarity signals, while multi-step/relational/compositional recall (transitive inference, novel-trajectory planning via replay — Pfeiffer & Foster 2013 Nature) requires a usable, higher-fidelity intermediate. This is a real, well-supported single-vs-multi-step asymmetry, though the specific "fidelity budget grows with chain length" curve is not directly measured in hippocampal circuits (best direct analog for graded error-with-length is grid-cell path-integration drift, which is a different mechanism, not memory-chain recall).

Field side (the sobering part): **zero papers were found that isolate dense/sharp-energy Hopfield readout as the causal variable in a genuine multi-hop benchmark.** The field's own self-framing (Millidge/Salvatori/Song/Bogacz/Lukasiewicz, "Universal Hopfield Networks: A General Framework for **Single-Shot** Associative Memory Models") treats classical/modern/dense Hopfield as single-shot machinery — this absence is itself informative (a heavily-studied area has not needed to test this). The closest direct evidence (Hoover et al. 2024, "Dense Associative Memory Through the Lens of Random Features") proves retrieval error accumulates over iterated relaxation steps TOWARD ONE attractor, modulated by inverse-temperature beta — a real fidelity-compounds-with-iteration result, but about internal descent dynamics to a single stored pattern, not chained retrieval across distinct facts. A 2026 attractor/fixed-point reasoning paper (Fein-Ashley & Rashidinejad, "Solve the Loop") shows iterated attractor refinement decisively winning genuinely multi-step compositional tasks (Sudoku-Extreme, Maze-Hard) with a tiny model beating larger LLMs — supportive but doesn't isolate reconstruction-fidelity as the mechanism vs. other explanations (e.g., adaptive computation depth). A standing counter-hypothesis in the compositional-generalization literature holds that discrete/structural/symbolic correctness, not continuous vector fidelity, is the real multi-hop bottleneck — no direct Hopfield-specific refutation of this was found either way.

**Bottom line: the negative does NOT automatically extend to multi-hop, but it also isn't automatically rescued.** Whether fidelity binds at 2-3 hops depends entirely on whether our own single-hop per-step error rate epsilon is small or large relative to 1/(chain length) — a number we can check off existing metrics before running anything new.

## Cheap decisive test

We do not need a new experiment infrastructure — this reuses the already-existing multi-hop chain eval (per `notes/research_reader_decisive_multihop_win_engineering_2026-07-09.md`, Stage-4 `exp_grounding_multihop_perhop_cleanup_gate_v1` already has fid1/fid2/decay_slope on real chains) plus the already-pre-registered but not-yet-landed Stage-5 `exp_grounding_multihop_decisive_win_v1` cell, which already contains a `DENSE_HOPFIELD_CLEANUP` arm (its own Prediction 2).

Two-part cheap test, in order:

**Part A (zero new compute, pure arithmetic on existing metrics.json):** Compute our own per-hop pairwise error rate epsilon_pairwise from the already-landed Stage-4 data (`fid1`, `fid2`, `decay_slope`). Compute 1/epsilon_pairwise and compare to the tested chain lengths (2-3 hops). If 1/epsilon_pairwise >> 3, we are still in the DAgger LINEAR regime at accessible chain lengths — predict the negative EXTENDS (fidelity still won't bind at 2-3 hops, matching single-hop). If 1/epsilon_pairwise is within an order of magnitude of 2-3, we are near/past the QUADRATIC crossover — predict fidelity starts to bind. This costs ~zero compute and should gate whether Part B is even worth dispatching.

**Part B (only if Part A says we're near the crossover, or as a cheap confirmatory run regardless):** Amend the already-designed Stage-5 `DENSE_HOPFIELD_CLEANUP` arm (currently measures raw cosine fidelity, fid2) to ALSO report end-to-end RETRIEVAL/rank accuracy (correct-entity-at-rank-1, or MRR) at hop1, hop2, hop3 — not just reconstruction fidelity — because the single-hop negative was specifically about retrieval-rank, not raw fidelity, and the two must be measured side-by-side to know whether a fidelity lift actually converts into a rank/retrieval lift at longer chains. Compare DENSE_HOPFIELD_CLEANUP vs PLAIN_CLEANUP on this retrieval metric across hop count — the diagnostic signature we want is the DIFFERENTIAL (delta grows with hop count), not a flat overall boost (a flat boost at every hop count would be the same noise-floor-lift confound that killed the single-hop version).

## Falsifiable predictions (HARD-PASS + HARD-FAIL)

**Prediction 1 (regime check, Part A):** Our own epsilon_pairwise (single-hop pairwise retrieval error rate, already measured) puts 1/epsilon_pairwise within 3x of the tested chain length (2-3 hops).
- HARD-PASS: 1/epsilon_pairwise is between ~2 and ~10 (i.e., 2-3 hop chains are plausibly near or past the DAgger quadratic-crossover point) — proceed to Part B with real expectation of a signal.
- HARD-FAIL: 1/epsilon_pairwise > 20 (chain lengths tested are deep in the linear, retrieval-dominated regime) — the multi-hop fidelity envelope is very unlikely to fire at 2-3 hops; would need much longer chains (5+) to test at all, which is likely not worth building given cost. This alone would let us STOP without spending Part B compute.

**Prediction 2 (the actual multi-hop fidelity win, Part B, only if Prediction 1 doesn't HARD-FAIL):** DENSE_HOPFIELD_CLEANUP's retrieval/rank accuracy (not just raw fidelity) beats PLAIN_CLEANUP's, and the GAP GROWS with hop count (hop3 gap > hop2 gap > hop1 gap approx 0).
- HARD-PASS: hop3 retrieval-accuracy delta (dense minus plain) is >= 2x the hop1 delta, AND hop1 delta is within noise of zero (confirms this is a genuinely NEW multi-hop-specific effect, not a re-discovery of the already-negative single-hop fidelity lift).
- HARD-FAIL: delta is flat across hop1/hop2/hop3 (same small or zero gap at every hop count) — this would mean the single-hop negative fully extends: dense readout never converts to a retrieval win regardless of chain length at accessible scale, and the fidelity-vs-retrieval distinction is a red herring for our regime specifically.

- If Prediction 1 HARD-FAILs (likely, pending the actual arithmetic check): this closes the multi-hop rescue cleanly and cheaply (zero new compute) — the dense-readout negative is confirmed to be architecture-general, not just a single-hop artifact, and further engineering effort should go to the already-identified alternative levers (path-as-object encoding, N-scaling, write-rule fixes) rather than a dense-Hopfield-readout retrofit.
