# Research: is HARD-RULE-vs-PREDICTIVE the barrier for glass-box real-prose reading, and is glass-box probabilistic parsing the fix?

**Date:** 2026-07-17
**Trigger:** direct follow-on to the Rung 5-8 read->grow chain (`exp_read_grow_realprose_*_rung{5,5b,6,7,8}_v1`, commits 7da8a4c8 / 64568e7b / c71a9eec7 / a1780b46 / current) and the adjacent `notes/research_classical_openie_glassbox_parsing_2026-07-17.md` drill, which recommended a classical rule-based clause-typology toolchain + a hard full-slot-match abstain gate. This drill asks the sharper question that note only touched in passing: is the pipeline's ceiling actually about RULE COVERAGE (more constructions, better morphology), or is there a genuinely distinct barrier — HARD-RULE commit-or-abstain vs GRADED/PREDICTIVE disambiguation — and can the graded version be built glass-box (no LLM, fully inspectable)?
**Method:** 3 parallel Sonnet lit-scan sub-agents (psycholinguistics of graded vs hard-commit parsing; ML history of glass-box probabilistic parsers and their accuracy ceilings; classical rule-based-to-statistical transition and the ambiguity-explosion problem), synthesized here against our OWN rung 5-8 error taxonomy (a rare case where we have real internal failure-mode data to check the external literature against, not just analogy). Calibration penalty per [[feedback-lit-scan-calibration-penalty]] applied throughout (deflate 0.15-0.25, cap novel-synthesis P at 0.50).

## HEADLINE

**Yes — hard-rule-vs-predictive is a real, well-evidenced distinction, and it is NOT the same axis as "rule coverage."** Three independent lines of evidence converge:

1. **Biology**: human disambiguation is explicitly graded/probabilistic (surprisal theory, parallel constraint-satisfaction, predictive-processing N400/P600 literature) — multiple candidate parses held in parallel with continuously-weighted evidence, not one rule firing or abstaining. This is the field's actual consensus model, not a fringe reading (deflated P ≈ 0.62-0.68 per the bio sub-agent, mainly for PDF-verification and one open 2026-preprint wrinkle about *how peaked* the distribution is — not whether it's graded at all).
2. **ML history**: every glass-box statistical parser (PCFG, lexicalized PCFG, MaxEnt, CRF, supertagging) is a table or linear-feature-weighted sum of named, human-readable numbers — fully non-neural, fully auditable — and these systems reached 88-92% F1 on clean text and 75-85% on web-register text, categorically above pure hard-rule structural heuristics tested on the *identical* decisions (Hindle & Rooth: ~55% for Right Association / Minimal Attachment vs ~80% for lexical-statistical scoring on PP-attachment — a ~25-point jump from probability alone, no new rules added). Charniak (1996) directly measured that missing rules were NOT his parser's bottleneck (reparsing training data recovered almost nothing); ambiguity-resolution among already-admissible parses was.
3. **Our own data**: the Rung 5-8 chain independently reproduces this exact split. Most of the 0.18->0.29->0.38->0.64->0.53 precision zigzag across rungs came from morphology/lookup COMPLETENESS bugs (irregular-verb lemma lookup, compound-noun head selection, negation parsing) — genuinely fixable by MORE hard rules, no probability needed (Rung 7 proved this: three deterministic bug-fixes took gated precision 0.38->0.91 before the imperative-coverage tradeoff). But Rung 5b's VET explicitly DECLARED one residual failure mode that neither gate catches: **reduced relative clause on the subject, no relativizer, finite embedded verb** — this is the textbook garden-path construction ("the horse raced past the barn fell"), and it is structurally different from the other bugs: there is no hard rule that distinguishes "raced" as main-verb vs "raced" as reduced-participle without a probability/preference judgment (verb-transitivity bias x reduced-RC base rate — literally Jurafsky's 1996 82:1 / 6.4-bit calculation on this exact sentence type). Compound-noun head selection (HTC Evo vs "Evo"; customer service vs "service") is a second, related case: a fixed left/right rule cannot get both right, but a frequency-based statistical head-finder can.

**Net: the ceiling has TWO distinct components, empirically separable in our own error log — a hard-rule-fixable coverage/completeness tail (most of what we've hit so far, and it responds to more rules exactly as predicted) and a genuinely-different disambiguation tail (reduced-RC, compound-head, and by extension PP-attachment/coordination-scope once we get there) that hard rules structurally cannot resolve regardless of how many are added, and that the literature says requires a graded probability/preference computation.** Glass-box probabilistic parsing is achievable for the second tail: PCFG rule-probabilities, MaxEnt/CRF feature weights, and simple frequency-ratio scorers (Jurafsky/Hindle-Rooth style) are all human-readable numbers, no learned distributed representations, no LLM.

## (a) Biology: graded/probabilistic, not hard-commit

Surprisal theory (Hale 2001; Levy 2008) frames processing difficulty as `-log P(word | context)` computed from a probability distribution maintained over multiple simultaneously-entertained parses — not a rule match/no-match signal. Parallel constraint-satisfaction models (MacDonald, Pearlmutter & Seidenberg 1994; Jurafsky 1996) explicitly reject Frazier's earlier serial hard-heuristic model (Minimal Attachment/Late Closure, one parse, revise-on-failure) in favor of weighted-evidence combination across several live candidates. Jurafsky's own worked example is literally our Rung 5b residual: for "the horse raced past the barn fell," he computes the (wrong) main-verb reading is favored over the (correct) reduced-relative reading by roughly **82:1** (base-rate of reduced RCs x verb-transitivity bias for "race"), i.e. ~6.4 bits of surprisal resolved at "fell" — a graded quantity, not a binary trigger. N400/P600 literature (Kutas & Federmeier; DeLong et al. 2005, with a documented partial replication failure at the phonological grain, Nieuwland et al. 2018; Aurnhammer et al. 2023 on P600-as-continuous-integration-index) corroborates a continuously-varying internal quantity computed before any observable difficulty signal, not a post-hoc pass/fail flag.

One flagged, very recent wrinkle (2026 preprint, Timkey/Dillon/Linzen, unreplicated): humans may show sharper/more peaked commitment to the single locally-preferred parse than an "ideal" graded Bayesian observer — meaning the open question is *how peaked* the graded distribution is, not whether gradedness exists at all. This does not rescue a hard-rule account; it only says human gradedness may be closer to "one dominant hypothesis with residual mass on alternatives" than to a flat distribution.

Deflated confidence: **P ~= 0.62-0.68** that graded/probabilistic disambiguation is the correct architectural characterization of human parsing (vs a fringe/contested reading) — deflation driven by inability to verify primary-source text directly (WebFetch failures on PDFs) and the DeLong replication crack, not by any credible hard-rule counter-account surfacing in the literature.

## (b) ML: glass-box statistical parsers are legitimately non-neural and reached real accuracy above hard rules

PCFG rule probabilities are literally `count(rule)/count(LHS)` (Charniak 1996's "tree-bank grammars," inside-outside estimation per Baker 1979) — a hand-auditable table. Lexicalized PCFG (Collins 1997: ~88% F1; Charniak 1997/2000: ~89.5-90% F1 on WSJ §23) added bilexical head-word statistics, still zero neural components. MaxEnt/CRF parsers (Ratnaparkhi 1997; Finkel, Kleeman & Manning 2008 CRF parsing, 90.2% F1) are globally-normalized linear models over named, enumerable features with scalar weights — the same "linear combination of named quantities" paradigm PCFG's log-probabilities already are, just discriminatively trained. Supertagging (Bangalore & Joshi 1999) gives the cleanest internal ablation of "how much does probability specifically buy, separate from rule/constraint coverage": symbolic structural filters alone cut ambiguity 47->25 supertags/word; adding a probabilistic trigram model on top then reaches 92.2% per-word accuracy (vs 77.2% frequency-only baseline) — the probabilistic step does most of the remaining disambiguation work, and the authors explicitly frame their own approach against Constraint Grammar (a hard rule-based competitor) on exactly this axis: "tagging is done in a probabilistic setting in the supertagging approach while it is rule based in the constraint grammar approach."

Deflated confidence: **P ~= 0.65-0.70** that probability-based disambiguation (not incremental rule/grammar coverage) was the dominant driver of the historical accuracy gains from hand-written-grammar-era to ~88-92% F1 classical-statistical parsing — deflation for lack of a single controlled modern rules-vs-statistics head-to-head benchmark on identical naturally-ambiguous sentences (the field simply stopped maintaining rule-based baselines once statistics won).

## (c) Classical history: ambiguity explosion is the actual wall, not coverage

Church & Patil (1982) show PP-attachment ambiguity grows as Catalan numbers (1,2,5,14,42,132,429,1430,...) with each additional PP — a genuine combinatorial explosion a hard-rule tie-breaker cannot resolve by adding more rules, because every one of those parses is already grammatically licensed; the problem is not "what's possible" but "what's intended." Abney (1996) states this dichotomy explicitly (grammaticality vs naturalness/preference). Hindle & Rooth (1993): purely structural heuristics (Right Association, Minimal Attachment — hard rules, zero statistics) plateau near ~55% on PP-attachment; adding lexical co-occurrence statistics (a PMI-like association score, computed from >200,000 sentences) lifts the SAME decision to ~78-80% — a ~25-point jump from probability alone. Notably, the field's actual best engineering path (Riezler et al. 2002, LFG+discriminative disambiguation) was hybrid: keep hand-built grammars for coverage, add statistics only for the choose-among-admissible-parses layer — which is precisely the two-tier structure our own rung data independently reproduces (coverage bugs respond to more rules; the reduced-RC/compound-head tail needs probability).

Deflated confidence: **P ~= 0.65-0.70**, same caveats (exact historical figures partially secondary-sourced; PDF fetch failures on several primary texts).

## Cheap decisive test

Build ONE minimal glass-box statistical disambiguator, scoped narrowly to the two NAMED residual failure modes already declared-but-unfixed in the Rung 5b/7 VET record (not a general PCFG rebuild):

1. **Reduced-relative-clause-on-subject scorer**: a frequency-ratio score (main-verb-reading-prior x verb-transitivity-bias) vs (reduced-RC-reading-prior), computed from a small corpus count table (transitive-vs-intransitive usage rate per verb lemma, reduced-RC base rate per verb class) — literally Jurafsky's 82:1 calculation, generalized to a lookup-and-multiply scorer, not a new hard rule. Every number in it is a named count, auditable by hand.
2. **Compound-noun head-selection scorer**: a frequency-based left-vs-right head preference (e.g. corpus bigram/trigram counts distinguishing proper-name compounds, first-word-head, from descriptive-noun compounds, last-word-head) replacing the current fixed left/right rule.

Run both on the SAME simple-register UD-EWT slice (Rung 6/7/8's n=300 pool) plus a small hand-built battery of nonce sentences exercising each construction, measuring construction-scoped precision (not overall corpus precision, which is dominated by unrelated coverage bugs already fixed in Rung 7).

## Falsifiable predictions — HARD-PASS / HARD-FAIL

### Cell 1: reduced-RC probabilistic disambiguator
- **HARD-PASS:** construction-scoped accuracy on reduced-RC-on-subject sentences (hand-built nonce battery + any real corpus instances) rises from the current 0% (declared not-caught) to >= 60%, using ONLY named, auditable frequency counts (no learned embeddings, no LLM), with zero regression on the existing 27/39 correctly-extracted Rung-7 sentences.
- **HARD-FAIL:** accuracy stays < 35% even with the probabilistic scorer, OR the scorer requires semantic/world-knowledge features beyond simple verb-transitivity + RC base-rate counts to get there — this would indicate the disambiguation genuinely needs deeper context than a Jurafsky-style two-factor score provides, a real (not implementation) ceiling for this specific construction at this scope.

### Cell 2: compound-noun head-selection scorer
- **HARD-PASS:** accuracy on a held-out battery of proper-name-compound vs descriptive-compound nonce sentences (extending the HTC-Evo / customer-service cases from Rung 8) rises to >= 70% using a frequency-based left/right preference score, beating the current fixed-rule baseline (effectively 50%, a coin flip between two fixed rules) by a wide margin.
- **HARD-FAIL:** accuracy stays <= 55% (no meaningfully-better-than-chance improvement over the fixed rule) — would indicate compound-noun headedness needs a real named-entity/proper-noun-detection signal (a different, orthogonal fix), not a frequency-preference score.

### Cell 3 (framing check): is hard-rule-vs-predictive actually the DOMINANT remaining bottleneck, or a minority tail?
- **HARD-PASS:** after Cells 1-2 land (if they do), overall gated precision_on_attempted on the Rung 7/8 pool clears >= 0.70 (beating the Rung 7 HARD-PASS bar of 0.60 by a real margin), confirming the reduced-RC + compound-head tail was a meaningfully-sized chunk of remaining error, not noise.
- **HARD-FAIL:** overall precision moves by < 0.03 absolute even if Cells 1-2 individually pass their construction-scoped bars — would mean these two constructions are too rare in the eval pool to matter in aggregate, and the REAL dominant remaining bottleneck is still coverage/completeness (more hard-rule fixes), not disambiguation. This is an important a-priori-plausible outcome given Rung 8's finding that most remaining errors were newly-exposed OLD bugs (irregular plurals, OOV verb spelling, do-support negation), not new ambiguity cases.

## Cross-thread synthesis

This sharpens, rather than contradicts, `notes/research_classical_openie_glassbox_parsing_2026-07-17.md`: that note's Cell 2 (strict full-slot-match abstain gate) is precisely the HARD-RULE horn of this drill's dichotomy — a substrate-native precision lever the brain does not use, good for cheaply suppressing spurious firing on genuinely out-of-scope constructions, but structurally incapable of resolving in-scope AMBIGUITY (two grammatically-licensed readings of the same admissible pattern). The Rung 5-8 chain is the direct empirical record of exactly the two-tier split this drill predicts: Rungs 6-7 (morphology/lookup completeness) responded to more hard rules exactly as the coverage literature predicts; Rung 5b's declared-uncaught reduced-RC residual and Rung 8's compound-head errors are the disambiguation tier this drill targets. This also connects to `notes/research_surprise_decomposition_unexpectedness_vs_importance_2026-07-16.md`: the substrate ALREADY implements a Bayesian-surprise-style quantity elsewhere (`KL(P(M|D)||P(M))`, a model-space belief-shift, glass-box by construction) for the memory-ingest gate — the same mathematical primitive (log-probability-ratio scoring over named hypotheses) that a glass-box predictive parser needs. This is not a new mechanism class to invent from scratch; it is the SAME primitive (probability-ratio / log-odds scoring over a small set of named, auditable alternatives) redeployed at the sentence-construction-disambiguation grain instead of the memory-gating grain.

## Substrate-product implications

The pipeline does not need a general statistical-parser rebuild (PCFG/CRF over the full grammar) to make progress right now — it needs two narrowly-scoped, cheap, fully-auditable frequency scorers targeting the two NAMED residual failure modes already isolated by VET. This keeps the no-LLM-at-runtime invariant intact (every number in both scorers is a named corpus count, inspectable by a human) while directly testing whether disambiguation-via-probability is real leverage on OUR corpus, not just in the literature. If Cell 3 HARD-FAILs (tail too small to move aggregate precision), the honest reading is that coverage/completeness bugs remain the dominant lever for now, and a general probabilistic-disambiguation layer should be deferred until the hard-rule coverage tail is closer to exhausted — avoiding over-investment in the more architecturally novel (but currently minority) fix.
