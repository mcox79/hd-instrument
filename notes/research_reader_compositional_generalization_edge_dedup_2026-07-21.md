## (b) Dedup reasoning — why a straightforward reader compgen test re-derives known results

Atoms 29379-29382 (`exp_compgen_binding_vs_flat_learned_frontend_v1.py`, `exp_novel_atom_generalization_codebook_binding_v1.py`, `exp_novel_atom_real_codebook_capacity_curve_v1.py`, `exp_novel_atom_real_codebook_generalization_v1.py`, all landed MEASURED_MECHANISM per `notes/SURVEY_reader_chaingrade_prior_work_and_genuine_gaps_2026-07-20.md` line 20) established two things on synthetic codes:

1. **Novel-COMBINATION generalization (binding algebra itself) = free, but tautologically so.** FHRR bind/unbind is a fixed algebraic operation, not a learned one. Once role and filler codes are correctly assigned, binding composes and decomposes correctly for ANY combination, including combinations never seen during training — this is a mathematical property of the algebra (distributivity + approximate self-inverse of circular convolution / involution), not an empirical discovery about a trained system's inductive bias. Per the memory record this was explicitly flagged as "a property, not a capability."
2. **Novel-ATOM generalization (recognizing a never-before-seen filler) = bounded.** The LEARNED front-end that maps raw input to a filler code only recovers correctly at high capacity, and only beats naive nearest-neighbor similarity in that regime (bounded linear-recovery).

A reader-level test that (a) trains role-assignment on some (verb, construction) pairs, (b) encodes the output via HD binding, and (c) tests decode-accuracy on held-out (verb, construction) pairs, reduces to exactly finding #1 IF the role labels are correctly assigned upstream (decode is then free by construction — nothing new learned) or reduces to a standard parser/labeler out-of-distribution generalization question IF role labels are NOT correctly assigned upstream (the binding step is irrelevant; failure or success is 100% attributable to the arc-parser/arc-labeler's OOD behavior, a well-trodden NLP question unrelated to VSA).

This second branch is the architecturally live one. In the current reader, thematic-role assignment (agent/patient/who-is-affected) is decided by a **learned classifier** consuming a handful of positional/syntactic cues (POS tag sequence, arc-label, preposition flag, verb-argument distance — the "4 crude positional cues" of `exp_learned_argstruct_parser_lccp`, plus the more recent explicit-grammar cells). Binding is applied downstream of that decision, to represent the (role, filler) pair as an HD vector for storage/composition with other facts — it does not participate in MAKING the role decision. So whatever "systematic generalization to a held-out verb×construction combination" the reader exhibits is overwhelmingly a property of the classifier's generalization (feature coverage, training-distribution breadth), not of binding. Testing this and reporting a binding-vs-flat delta would misattribute an ordinary supervised-parsing-generalization result to "binding gives systematicity," which is not what would be measured.

**Conclusion:** a compgen test framed as "does the reader's OUTPUT-encoding via binding generalize to held-out verb-construction combinations" is NOT novel — it is guaranteed free (re-derives 29379-82's combination result) whenever roles are correctly assigned, and is a mislabeled parser-generalization test whenever they are not. Do not dispatch this version.

## (c) The genuinely-untested slice

The live, un-derived question is narrower and structurally different: **does making the role-ASSIGNMENT DECISION ITSELF go through a binding/resonator-style mechanism (iterative unbind-against-codebook / clean-up, i.e. the decision is read out by resonating a bound superposition against a role-filler codebook) generalize better to held-out (verb, construction) combinations than an equal-capacity FLAT classifier decision (logistic regression / small MLP over the same feature set), on REAL narrative text with the reader's actual extraction noise?**

This is different from 29379-82 in the load-bearing way: those cells fixed the DECISION (gold or near-gold role labels) and varied only whether encoding/recovery used binding vs a learned front-end for ATOM identity. This proposed cell instead varies whether the DECISION MECHANISM is binding-mediated (resonator/cleanup dynamics performing the classification) vs a standard flat classifier, with atom identity and features held constant. It is also different from a vanilla "does the parser generalize" question because both arms see identical features and identical training data — the only difference is whether classification is implemented as iterative resonance against a superposed codebook (which can only settle to combinations reachable via compositional recombination of known role/filler codes — a structural constraint a flat classifier lacks) or as an unconstrained learned decision boundary.

This is squarely the Fodor & Pylyshyn "systematicity via combinatorial syntactic structure" hypothesis applied at the classification-mechanism level, not the representation level — and it has NOT been run in this substrate. It is however adjacent enough to the reader's existing "extraction-bound" finding (bounded 0.557 role-assigner ceiling; memory record) that the realistic prior is it will show a SMALL or NULL delta dominated by extraction noise, hence the P=0.30 deflated estimate.

## (d) Brain-first: is systematicity binding-based or learned-from-distribution?

The classical Fodor & Pylyshyn (1988) argument is that human sentence comprehension is systematic (understanding "John loves Mary" entails the capacity to understand "Mary loves John") because thought has combinatorial syntactic/semantic structure — a language-of-thought (LoT) claim used to argue connectionist nets of that era could not be systematic without implementing classical symbol structures. The VSA/HRR/tensor-product-representation research program (Smolensky 1990 tensor-product variable binding; Plate 1995 Holographic Reduced Representations; Gayler 2003 Vector Symbolic Architectures; Kanerva 2009 hyperdimensional computing) is the standard reply: distributed representations CAN be systematic if role-filler binding is implemented as an algebraic operation (outer product / circular convolution / XOR-permutation) that is content-independent — the same bind operator composes any role with any filler, so once the operator and the atom codes exist, novel combinations are representable "for free," exactly mirroring what 29379-82 measured (and confirming why that result is a property of the algebra, not a trained capability).

Neurally, the best evidence for factorized/compositional coding is grid-cell and entorhinal-hippocampal work: grid cells provide a factorized (separable, low-dimensional, combinatorial) code for space that generalizes across environments (Hafting/Moser), and this factorization extends to abstract/relational knowledge — Constantinescu, Behrens et al. (2016) found grid-like hexagonal codes during non-spatial conceptual generalization, and Whittington et al.'s Tolman-Eichenbaum Machine (2020) explicitly proposes a structure/content factorization (grid-like structural code bound to sensory content code) as the mechanism generalizing learned relational structure to new content — this is architecturally the closest neural analog to "fixed structural role code bound to learned content code," i.e. the substrate's own structure-content split. Bernardi, Fusi et al. (2020, "The geometry of abstraction in hippocampus and PFC") separately show PFC/hippocampal population geometry supports abstraction generalization precisely when representations are low-dimensional and factorized ("cross-condition generalization" = a linear-decodability signature of compositional/factorized coding), giving a second independent neural line of evidence.

Where human systematicity FAILS is instructive and should calibrate expectations for the reader test: humans are NOT perfectly systematic — well-documented processing asymmetries and failures occur exactly at the SAME locus the reader struggles (novel argument-structure combinations with low-frequency verbs in unfamiliar constructions, e.g. dative/passive alternations with rare verbs show measurable comprehension cost and error upticks in psycholinguistic literature on argument-structure generalization, e.g. work on verb-argument-structure overgeneralization errors in child language acquisition, and adult garden-path/misassignment effects on non-canonical constructions). This is consistent with human systematicity being GRADED and support by both a productive combinatorial mechanism (binding-like) AND a distributional/frequency-sensitive learned component (usage-based grammar, Goldberg/Tomasello construction grammar) — not pure symbolic compositionality. This matches this session's own conclusion (memory: role-assigner cells are learned/induced, bounded 0.557) — i.e. the reader's current profile (bounded learned role-assignment + free algebraic recombination downstream) is actually a reasonably brain-faithful two-component story, not a deficiency to "fix" by making everything binding-based. That argues for the narrower cell in (c) — testing whether ADDING a binding-mediated constraint to the decision helps at the margin — over any wholesale claim that binding alone should give the reader full systematicity.

## (e) Prior art: compgen benchmarks and where flat architectures fail (credited)

- **SCAN** (Lake & Baroni, 2018, "Generalization without systematicity: On the compositional skills of sequence-to-sequence recurrent networks," ICML) — simple command-to-action translation task; seq2seq RNNs achieve near-zero accuracy on systematic splits (e.g. train on "jump" alone, test "jump twice"/"jump around left") despite near-perfect in-distribution accuracy. Foundational demonstration that standard neural seq2seq lacks systematic compositional generalization.
- **COGS** (Kim & Linzen, 2020, EMNLP, "COGS: A Compositional Generalization Challenge Based on Semantic Interpretation") — English-to-logical-form semantic parsing with generalization splits targeting exactly the reader's target phenomenon: novel combinations of known words in known grammatical roles (e.g. a noun seen only as subject, tested as object) and novel syntactic recombination (deeper embedding). Standard seq2seq/transformer models score near-0% on the hardest structural-recursion splits while scoring near-100% in-distribution — this is the most directly relevant benchmark to the reader's who-is-affected task since it specifically targets lexical-item × grammatical-role recombination, exactly the reader-level test being considered here.
- **CFQ** (Keysers et al., 2020, ICLR, "Measuring Compositional Generalization: A Comprehensive Method on Realistic Data") — introduces "compound divergence" as a graded, principled way to construct train/test splits that vary how novel the COMBINATION of primitives is (distinct from token-level novelty) — directly useful as the design template for the held-out-combination split in section (f) below, since it formalizes "held out combination" rigorously rather than by ad hoc example curation.
- **Liska, Kruszewski & Baroni (2018, "Memorize or generalize? Searching for a compositional RNN in a haystack")** and **Lake (2019, "Compositional generalization through meta sequence-to-sequence learning")** — show ordinary training regimes essentially never discover the systematic solution among the many that fit the training data (a needle-in-haystack argument), and that meta-learning / explicit compositional inductive bias is needed to reliably find it.
- **Csordás, Irie & Schmidhuber (2021, "The Devil is in the Detail: Simple Tricks Improve Systematic Generalization of Transformers")** — shows relative positional encodings + small architectural changes substantially close the SCAN/COGS/CFQ gap for transformers, i.e. part of the "flat architecture fails" story is fixable by better inductive bias WITHOUT full symbolic binding — a caution against over-crediting binding as the only route to compgen.
- **Andreas (2019, "Good-Enough Compositional Data Augmentation")** and **Russin, O'Reilly & Cohen (2019, syntax-semantics separation for seq2seq)** — alternative routes to compgen (data augmentation recombination; separating syntactic frame processing from lexical-semantic content) that do not require VSA-style binding — relevant as "flat-with-tricks" comparison baselines.
- **Neuro-symbolic VSA (NVSA)** — Hersche et al. (2023, "A Neuro-Vector-Symbolic Architecture for Solving Raven's Progressive Matrices," Nature Machine Intelligence) demonstrate a binding-based neuro-symbolic architecture achieving strong systematic/compositional generalization on Raven's matrices vs purely neural baselines — the closest prior-art precedent for "binding gives measurable systematic-generalization advantage" on a REAL (not purely synthetic-algebra) task, and the direct template this session's NVSA-adoption plan is already built from (per `notes/prior_art_scour_synthesis_focus_chaingrade_2026-07-18.md`). Important caveat: NVSA's binding participates in the DECISION (a resonator network read-out), not just the encoding — structurally the same distinction drawn in section (c) above, which is why NVSA is evidence FOR the narrow cell in (c) and not evidence that the naive reader-level test in (b) would show anything new.
- **Fodor & McLaughlin (1990) reply to critics**, and later **Marcus (2001, "The Algebraic Mind")** — the philosophical/cognitive-science throughline arguing genuine systematicity requires operations sensitive to structural role independent of content (i.e., binding-like), which is the theoretical backbone the substrate's structure/content split is already aligned with.

Credit: all of the above are pre-existing published results being drawn on, not substrate-original; none were queried using substrate-novel terms (searches used generic terms: "systematic compositional generalization sequence models," "semantic parsing generalization split held-out combination," "vector symbolic architecture binding compositional generalization neuro-symbolic," "grid cell factorized code abstract relational generalization").

## (f) Design-gated cell spec (for the narrow novel slice only — NOT the naive reader-vs-flat-encoding version)

**Do not dispatch the naive version** (train/test split on binding-encoded reader output with roles decided upstream) — dedup verdict in (b) rules it out as either free-by-construction or parser-generalization-mislabeled.

**IF dispatched, the narrow live cell:**
- **Question:** does a resonator/cleanup-mediated role-DECISION (iterative unbind of the observed feature-bundle against a role×verb-class codebook, selecting the label that best resonates) generalize better than a flat classifier of equal parameter/feature budget, to HELD-OUT (verb, construction) combinations, on real narrative text with the reader's actual extraction noise?
- **REAL baseline:** flat classifier (logistic regression or small MLP) over the identical feature set the resonator arm uses (POS/arc-label/preposition-sense/passive-flag/etc.) — same features, same training examples, only the decision mechanism differs. This is a real, currently-implemented-elsewhere baseline (the existing `exp_learned_argstruct_parser_lccp`-family classifiers), not a strawman.
- **CAN-FAIL:** per the extraction-bound finding (0.557 ceiling) and the brain-check in (d) showing human systematicity is itself graded/frequency-sensitive rather than pure-binding, this can plausibly show NO delta — the extraction noise floor may dominate both arms equally, which would be a genuine (and useful) negative result, not a saturated/by-construction pass.
- **DIFFICULTY-ON:** use a CFQ-style compound-divergence split (Keysers et al. 2020) to guarantee the test set contains (verb, construction) pairs absent from training, at a specified minimum compound-divergence threshold — not just held-out sentences.
- **ONE VARIABLE:** decision mechanism (resonator/binding-mediated vs flat), all else (features, training data, split) held fixed.
- **HARD-PASS threshold:** resonator arm beats flat arm by >=5 points of role-assignment accuracy on the held-out-combination test set, with the in-distribution (non-held-out) accuracy gap between arms <=1 point (ruling out "resonator arm is just a better classifier overall" as the explanation).
- **HARD-FAIL threshold:** delta on held-out-combination set is <=1 point (within noise), OR the resonator arm's in-distribution accuracy is itself more than 2 points below the flat arm's (indicating the resonator constraint costs more capacity than it buys generalization) — either counts as HARD-FAIL and should be reported as "extraction-bound dominates; binding-at-decision-time does not rescue it," a real informative negative tying back cleanly to the existing 0.557-ceiling finding.
- **Tie-back to 29379-82:** this cell explicitly does NOT re-test whether binding CAN represent novel combinations (settled, free, atoms 29379-82) — it tests whether FORCING the decision through that same combinatorial constraint improves generalization of the decision itself, which is the one part of the pipeline 29379-82 did not touch (they held decisions fixed/gold).
