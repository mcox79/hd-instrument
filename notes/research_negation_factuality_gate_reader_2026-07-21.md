## (a) Brain-first: how humans process negation

**Two-step simulate-then-negate (contested, not settled).** Kaup, Ludtke & Zwaan (2006, *J. Pragmatics* 38(7), "Processing negated sentences with contradictory predicates: Is a door that is not open mentally closed?") and Kaup, Yaxley, Madden, Zwaan & Ludtke (2007, *QJEP*, "Experiential simulations of negated text information") found that shortly after reading "The door was not open," readers show a match effect for the AFFIRMATIVE state first (door open activates), which only later shifts to the negated/factual state (door closed) at longer SOA -- read as a two-stage process: simulate the to-be-negated state, tag false, then shift to the actual state. **This is directly contested**: 2024 eye-tracking work ("Mental simulation of the factual and the illusory in negation processing," *Sci. Reports*) and "Can We Prepare to Negate? Negation as a Reversal Operator" (*J. Cognition*) argue the two-step pattern is context-dependent, not obligatory -- direct simulation of the factual state is possible without a mandatory affirmative detour. Net: humans likely DO carry some cost/lag for negated-event representation, but the mechanism is not a clean fixed pipeline -- treat "compute affirmative then flip" as a plausible cheap approximation, not a proven brain algorithm.

**Processing cost / N400.** Fischler et al. (1983, *Psychophysiology*) found early semantic-mismatch detection is largely negation-blind (true-negative and false-affirmative pattern together at the initial N400 stage). Nieuwland & Kuperberg (2008, *Psychological Science*) is the key refinement: negation cost is not fixed -- when negation is PRAGMATICALLY licensed (rejecting a plausible claim in context), true-negative sentences pattern like true-affirmatives (low cost); unlicensed/context-free negation shows the classic elevated cost. Classic verification work (Clark & Chase tradition) finds negative-TRUE sentences take longer to verify than negative-FALSE ones, attributed to an extra polarity-coding step.

**Scope.** Thin direct human evidence. One relevant finding (Polish psycholinguistic study, plural nouns under sentential negation) shows scope resolution proceeds in phases, not instantaneously online -- consistent with scope being a real, non-trivial computation, not a free side-effect of detecting the cue. Scope-RESOLUTION is far more developed in computational/NLP work than in real-time human psycholinguistics (see (b)).

**Modality/counterfactuals/near-misses.** Counterfactual-conditional ERP work shows both the factual and hypothetical representations are concurrently activated. Gerstenberg (2016, Stanford CICL, "Understanding 'almost': Empirical and computational studies of near misses") frames near-miss events ("almost broke it") as counterfactual-proximity judgments -- how small a perturbation would have flipped the outcome -- i.e. "almost X" is represented as X's causal chain PLUS a distance-to-counterfactual-completion estimate, not simply "not-X." Factive-predicate work (Kuperberg lab, 2019, *Cognition*) shows factive complements ("know," "realize") generate presuppositions the brain commits to and maintains in the discourse model (inconsistent downstream input elicits a late P600), while non-factive complements ("think," "believe") do not -- direct brain evidence that VERB CLASS gates whether a complement event is treated as realized.

**Where it fails.** Multiply-negated ("depth-charge") sentences are frequently misread toward the pragmatically plausible reading rather than the literal logical one (Frontiers in Psychology 2019). Negation-plus-quantifier inverse-scope readings carry a documented processing cost and comprehenders default to surface-scope ("minimal effort") even when context supports the inverse reading.

**Brain-check verdict:** the brain does NOT get scope resolution "for free" from cue detection -- it is a genuine additional computation with its own cost and failure modes (embedding depth, multiple negation, pragmatic override). This matters for design: a scope-resolution step is expected to be non-trivial and itself failure-prone, not a rubber-stamp once the cue is found. Per [[feedback-every-negative-check-how-the-brain-does-it]] discipline (applied prospectively here, not retrospectively): if our glass-box scope resolver also struggles on deep embedding/coordination, that is a real structural bound shared with the brain's own limitation, not necessarily an implementation bug.

## (b) Prior art: glass-box negation-scope + factuality resolution

**NegEx (Chapman, Bridewell, Hanbury, Cooper & Buchanan 2001, *J. Biomedical Informatics*).** Regex-based pre-negation/post-negation trigger-phrase lists with a fixed-token-window scope heuristic (no parse required). Reported on clinical discharge summaries: sensitivity/recall 77.8%, precision (PPV) 84.5%, specificity 94.5%. Known failure mode, explicit in the follow-on literature: the fixed window is too crude for complex/coordinated sentences -- it crosses clause boundaries it shouldn't or truncates too early, and has no notion of syntactic attachment. **This exact weakness was already fixed by prior art, not hypothetically:** ConText (Harkema, Dowling, Thornblade & Chapman 2009) added temporality/experiencer dimensions; **DEEPEN** (Mehrabi et al. 2015) and **DepND** directly replaced NegEx's token-window with Stanford-dependency-tree-based scope rules, reducing coordination/apposition false positives -- i.e. the dependency-attachment design proposed below is not a novel idea, it is the field's own documented fix for NegEx's core limitation, and this drill builds on DEEPEN/DepND's precedent rather than reinventing it.

**\*SEM 2012 Shared Task + CD-SCO corpus (Morante & Blanco 2012, ACL Anthology S12-1035; corpus: Morante & Daelemans).** Two Conan Doyle stories, 1,056 negation instances / 3,899 sentences, split into cue-detection and scope-resolution subtasks. The winning system, **UiO1 (Read, Velldal, Øvrelid & Oepen 2012)**, used an SVM cue classifier then SVM-based discriminative ranking over constituency-parse subtrees for scope, reaching token-level scope-resolution F1 = 85.26 with predicted cues, ~88.2% with gold cues; the sibling UiO2 system used CRF sequence labeling over dependency-parse features. Cue detection (a near-closed lexical set) scored much higher across systems than full scope resolution -- confirming, with real numbers, that scope is the harder half of the problem, matching the psycholinguistic evidence in (a) that scope is a real additional computation, not free.

**Universal Dependencies.** Negation particles ("not," "n't," "never") attach via ordinary relations (`advmod`, with `advmod:neg` for clitic-like particles in some languages) to their governing predicate, carrying `Polarity=Neg` (UD deprecated the older dedicated `neg` relation). **This drill directly verified the attachment behavior on our own bundled UD-EWT source** (see Data note): dependency attachment gives cue-to-verb scope correctly even where surface word order would mislead (a `not` separated from its verb by an intervening adverb, or embedded inside a relative clause, still attaches by dependency edge to the correct target verb) -- confirmed against real corpus parses, not just asserted. But the raw UD edge only marks THAT a predicate is negated, not automatically what falls within scope across coordination/complement clauses -- **UDepLambda (Reddy et al. 2017, arXiv:1702.03305, "Universal Dependencies to Logical Forms with Negation Scope")** is direct, credited prior art for exactly the scope-propagation-over-the-UD-graph step this design needs; this drill's `conj`-propagation rule is a minimal instance of that general approach, not a novel mechanism.

**FactBank (Sauri & Pustejovsky 2009, *Language Resources & Evaluation*) + "Are You Sure That This Happened?" (Sauri & Pustejovsky 2012, *Computational Linguistics* 38(2):261-299).** Closed factuality-value set {CT+, CT-, PR+, PR-, PS+, PS-, U, Uu} (certain/probable/possible x polarity), built atop TimeBank, kappa=0.81. The compositional algorithm (implemented as the **De Facto** profiler) applies syntactic-construction markers, assigns a base value per event, then applies lexical markers (negation, modals like "almost"/"tried to"/"supposed to", conditionals) that shift the value across levels of embedding -- precisely the glass-box compositional-rule architecture this design reuses. **De Marneffe, Manning & Potts (2012, same *CL* issue, "Did It Happen? The Pragmatic Complexity of Veridicality Assessment")** is the important caveat: veridicality is not purely lexically determined, context/world knowledge matter, complicating any purely rule-based approach -- an honest ceiling on how far the glass-box modal/factivity half of this gate can go. Rudinger, White & Van Durme (2018, NAACL, "Neural models of factuality") is the modern upper-bound reference point but moves to learned models -- useful as a target, not a build-on component under the glass-box invariant.

**Single coherent glass-box pipeline precedent (directly answers task-brief question 5):** **Kilicoglu, Rosemblat, Cairelli & Rindflesch (2015, ACL Anthology W15-1303, "A Compositional Interpretation of Biomedical Event Factuality")** composes factuality by propagating negation/modality/hedging triggers through semantic-embedding levels -- exactly the cue-lexicon -> dependency-scope-propagation -> compositional-factuality-rule chain this design proposes, already built and published for biomedical text. **This is the closest existing template and this design should be read as directly building on Kilicoglu et al.'s three-stage compositional formalism, generalized from biomedical to general-domain text**, not as an independent Director invention. The implicative/aspectual-verb half of the modal lookup (try/manage/fail/almost) draws on Karttunen's classic implicative-verb classification -- not independently surfaced by either lit-scan sub-agent this cycle, flagged as not independently re-verified.

**Credited, not appropriated:** NegEx (Chapman et al. 2001); ConText (Harkema et al. 2009); DEEPEN (Mehrabi et al. 2015) and DepND; the \*SEM-2012/CD-SCO shared task (Morante & Blanco 2012; Morante & Daelemans) and UiO1/UiO2 (Read, Velldal, Øvrelid & Oepen 2012); UDepLambda (Reddy et al. 2017); FactBank + De Facto (Sauri & Pustejovsky 2009, 2012); de Marneffe, Manning & Potts (2012); Rudinger, White & Van Durme (2018); Kilicoglu, Rosemblat, Cairelli & Rindflesch (2015); Karttunen implicative-verb classes -- all prior academic art, credited and built on, not claimed as novel.

## (c) Design: the negation/factuality gate

**Signal (glass-box, parse-based, reuses the existing UD parser output already available to the reader):**
1. **Cue detection:** token with `lemma == "not"` and `Feats` containing `Polarity=Neg` (covers "not" and tokenized "n't" uniformly -- verified directly on UD-EWT: `did`+`n't` and `wo`+`n't` both tokenize the negator as its own PART token with `Polarity=Neg`, deprel `advmod`, head = the finite/content verb). Also lexicon-match `never`, `no` (determiner-negation, e.g. "no changes"), `nobody`/`nothing`/`neither`/`nor`/`without`/`cannot` as a fallback cue set for cases not carrying the UD feature.
2. **Scope attachment:** the cue's `advmod` head IS the negated predicate directly (dependency attachment, not surface distance -- confirmed on real examples where an intervening adverb or relative-clause embedding would defeat a word-order heuristic but the dependency edge still resolves correctly).
3. **Scope propagation:** if the negated verb has `conj` children (coordinated verbs sharing the same subject/aux chain), propagate NEGATED status to each `conj` child unless a child carries its own local negation/affirmation marker (the standard UD-linguistics assumption, confirmed on a real corpus item this cycle: "Saddam Hussein did **not** invade Kuwait, endanger Saudi Arabia and butcher his own people" -- all three coordinated verbs are within scope of the single "not").
4. **Modal/factivity lookup:** a small closed lexicon of implicative/aspectual predicates (try, attempt, manage, fail, almost, nearly, supposed to) plus conditional/hypothetical markers (`mark`-relation "if"/"unless" introducing the clause) that mark the embedded event REALIZED / UNREALIZED / GRADED-NEAR-MISS rather than a binary flip -- per Karttunen's implicative classes and Gerstenberg's near-miss framing in (a); this is the harder, lower-confidence half of the gate and should ship SECOND, after negation-only is validated.
5. **Output:** a 3-way (or graded) factuality tag per event -- REALIZED (default) / NEGATED (patient not affected) / HYPOTHETICAL-MODAL (event not asserted to have occurred) -- feeding the reader's existing patient-assignment vote as a gate/discount, and routing HYPOTHETICAL-MODAL cases toward the already-built abstain/confidence layer rather than a forced binary, consistent with the graded-not-categorical precedent already established for the affectedness gate (`research_verb_affectedness_type_gate_2026-07-20.md`).

**Design gate (per feedback-experiment-design-gate, verify at smoke before full run):**
1. **Real baseline** = current behavior: ignore negation entirely, always mark the event REALIZED and the grammatical patient affected (exactly today's FUNCWORD-discards-"not" behavior, confirmed at code level above) -- run on the identical item set.
2. **Discriminator can-fail:** the gate must be able to fail in TWO distinguishable ways, tracked separately -- (i) CUE-MISS (negation present but not detected: rare given `Polarity=Neg` is a first-class UD feature, but antonym-based implicit negation, e.g. "he broke it" vs "he fixed it" as opposites, is entirely outside this gate's scope and must not be conflated with a gate failure), and (ii) SCOPE-MISS (cue detected but attached to the wrong verb, or propagation over-reaches/under-reaches across coordination or embedding) -- this is where genuine failure risk concentrates, per the *SEM-2012 finding that scope resolution (not cue detection) is the harder half, and per the brain-check finding that human scope resolution itself is failure-prone on deep embedding/coordination.
3. **Difficulty actually on:** the eval MUST include, alongside clean affirmative items, (a) genuinely negated events attaching straightforwardly (COOR/LOCA/PREP items below), (b) coordinated multi-verb negation-propagation cases (the Saddam Hussein item is a real, already-in-gold example), (c) negation separated from its verb by an intervening adverb or embedded in a relative clause (the "will not soon be filled" item, also already in gold), and (d) genuine hypothetical/conditional/near-miss items (the modal-cue subset below) -- since a gate that only ever sees easy adjacent "did not V" cases would trivially pass without testing the scope-propagation or modal-lookup machinery.
4. **One variable differs:** only the addition of the negation/factuality gate (as a REALIZED/NEGATED/HYPOTHETICAL tag feeding the existing vote, not a new architecture) on top of the current reader; same items, same gold, same scoring.

**MEASURE:** does the gate correctly FLIP "affected -> not-affected / event-didn't-happen" on negated sentences (recall on the NEGATED subset) without breaking affirmative-item accuracy (false-positive rate on the affirmative majority)? Report cue-detection accuracy, scope-attachment accuracy, and end-to-end who-is-affected accuracy separately -- do not collapse into one number, per the *SEM-2012 two-subtask discipline.

## Falsifiable predictions

**HARD-PASS:** on the negation-balanced eval slice (see Data note), the gate (a) achieves cue-detection recall >= 0.95 (near-ceiling expected, since `Polarity=Neg` is a first-class, always-present UD feature on the tokens that carry it -- this is the easy half); (b) achieves scope-attachment accuracy (correct governing verb, including coordination-propagation cases) >= 0.85 on the difficulty-on subset described above; (c) end-to-end who-is-affected accuracy on the NEGATED subset improves by >= 0.30 absolute over the ignore-negation baseline (which should score near 0 on this subset by construction, since it always marks the event realized); (d) affirmative-item accuracy does not regress by more than 0.02 absolute (no false-positive cost).

**HARD-FAIL:** either (i) scope-attachment accuracy < 0.60 on the coordination/embedding difficulty-on subset -- meaning UD-attachment-plus-propagation is not sufficient and genuinely needs a more sophisticated resolver (this would be a real, informative negative, distinct from cue-detection failing); or (ii) the negation-balanced eval slice cannot reach n>=40 scoreable NEGATED items even after sourcing beyond the current 136-item construction gold (a DATA failure, not a mechanism failure -- see Data note); or (iii) affirmative-item accuracy regresses by > 0.05 absolute (the gate is net-harmful even though it fixes negated cases); or (iv) the modal/factivity lookup (implicative-verb lexicon) resolves < 30% of the hypothetical-cue subset -- a coverage failure for that half of the gate specifically, distinguished from the negation half per the design's phased-shipping order (negation ships first, modal lookup validated separately).

**Must-fail control:** a randomly-permuted cue-to-verb attachment (scrambling which verb each detected cue points to) must not achieve the same scope-attachment accuracy as the real dependency-edge attachment; if it does, the eval harness has a leakage or degenerate-majority-class problem independent of the hypothesis.

## Cross-thread synthesis

This gate is explicitly a SIBLING refinement to the already-delivered verb-affectedness gate (`research_verb_affectedness_type_gate_2026-07-20.md`), not a competitor: affectedness asks "IF the event happened, how much does it change the patient" (a verb-class-level, graded, type fact); this gate asks "DID the event happen at all" (an instance-level, per-sentence, polarity/modality fact). Both feed the same Step-2 integrated vote and the same already-built metacognitive abstain layer, reusing architecture rather than adding new decision branches -- consistent with the standing recombination-not-novel-research posture. It is also explicitly orthogonal to the word-order/extraction axis that the reader's crude cue features (`exp_learned_argstruct_parser_lccp_independent_gold_v1.py`) are bounded on (per the 07-20 LATE REFRAME): negation/factuality is a PARSE-DERIVED correctness dimension the reader has never been given at all (confirmed: "not"/"never" sit in the FUNCWORD noise-discard set), not a re-run of the word-order/grammar signal work already in flight on the explicit-grammar cell. This is a genuinely separate, additive lever, not a duplicate of the aa503aa2 explicit-grammar drill.

## Substrate-product implications

- Fully glass-box, build-time-cheap: reuses the existing UD dependency parse already consumed by the reader (no new parser, no LLM, no runtime cost beyond a feature check + a `conj`-edge walk + a small closed-lexicon lookup for the modal half).
- Ships in two independently-gated phases: (1) negation-only (cue + scope + propagation) -- higher-confidence, HARD-PASS thresholds set tighter; (2) modal/factivity lookup (implicative-verb lexicon) -- lower-confidence, coverage-gated, ships only if (1) passes and (2)'s own coverage check clears HARD-FAIL condition (iv).
- If HARD-FAIL on scope (condition i): this is informative on its own -- it would mean the reader needs a genuine scope-resolution component (CRF/classifier-style, per the *SEM-2012 best systems) beyond raw UD attachment + naive coordination-propagation, a bigger and more expensive build than currently assumed. Flag now rather than discover after a from-scratch scope resolver is half-built.
- If HARD-FAIL on data (condition ii): fixable by sourcing a negation-balanced slice (see below) -- a scoped, bounded data task, not a mechanism dead-end.

## Data note (the biggest risk, deflated, stated explicitly)

**This drill's own audit of `data/gold_construction_argstruct_ewt_v1/gold_construction_argstruct_ewt_v1.json` (136 items) found real, measurable negation+modal incidence: 16/136 (11.8%) items contain an explicit negation cue, 11/136 (8.1%) contain a hypothetical/conditional/near-miss modal cue, 0 overlap -- 27/136 (19.9%) combined.** This is enough to build a first eval slice without new data sourcing, and it is not a rare-event problem in principle. However, three real caveats, in descending order of severity:

1. **Not every negation-cue hit scopes over the item's LABELED verb.** Manual inspection of all 16 negation hits found 1 clear false-positive-for-this-purpose ("no matter what the liberal revisionist historians tell you" is a parenthetical idiom, not negating the labeled verb "fought") and at least one case ("not good" in "just covered in grease and not good") where "not" attaches to an adjective, not the labeled verb "covered." So the true "labeled-verb-is-negated" incidence is closer to ~13-14/136 (~10%), not 16/136 -- still workable but the HARD-FAIL n>=40 threshold (condition ii) will require pulling additional items from the same UD-EWT source (train+dev+test, not just the 136 currently sampled into the construction gold) using the same `Polarity=Neg`-attaches-to-labeled-verb selection rule, which is a bounded, mechanical data task, not new annotation.
2. **The McGuffey gold (`data/gold_mcguffey_lccp_argstruct_v1.json`, 114 items) has NO raw sentence text at all** -- it stores only extracted (verb, agent, patient) triples, not source sentences. Negation incidence cannot even be measured there without going back to the original McGuffey source text and re-extracting, which is a real, separate data gap for the negation gate specifically (the affectedness-gate note flagged a different McGuffey gap for light-verb constructions; this is a third, independent McGuffey gap on top of that one).
3. **The modal/hypothetical subset (11 items) is the more genuinely ambiguous half** -- several of the 11 modal-cue items ("if" introducing an unrelated clause, "supposed to" as a light epistemic hedge rather than marking non-occurrence) are borderline and will need the same kind of careful per-item scope check as the negation subset before being treated as clean HYPOTHETICAL-MODAL gold labels. This reinforces the phased-shipping design above (negation first, modal lookup second, separately gated).

## P (deflated per lit-scan-calibration; novel-synthesis capped at 0.50)

- **P(cue detection reaches HARD-PASS recall >= 0.95): 0.55.** UD `Polarity=Neg` is a first-class, near-ceiling-reliable feature on gold parses -- deflated from a naive ~0.85 prior because the reader's PRODUCTION path may not always have gold parses (a real parser-quality confound not tested this cycle).
- **P(scope-attachment accuracy clears >= 0.85 on the difficulty-on subset, i.e. HARD-PASS condition b): 0.45.** Set to match, not exceed, the published ceiling: UiO1's SVM-based scope resolver reached F1=85.26 (predicted cues) / ~88.2 (gold cues) on CD-SCO, and DEEPEN/DepND exist specifically because dependency-tree scope rules measurably beat NegEx's token-window on this exact failure class -- real, positive precedent, not a hunch. Still deflated below the published ceiling because our target domain (UD-EWT web text: emails, reviews, blogs) is noisier and more colloquial than Conan Doyle narrative prose or clinical notes, and this drill's own coordination-propagation rule works cleanly on only ONE hand-verified item so far, not yet at scale.
- **P(end-to-end who-is-affected lift on the NEGATED subset >= 0.30 absolute, i.e. HARD-PASS condition c): 0.45.** Conditional on (a)/(b) both clearing -- the ignore-negation baseline is expected to be near-zero on this subset by construction (it always marks the event realized), so the ceiling for improvement is high, but real-world scope-propagation errors will eat into it.
- **P(modal/factivity lookup coverage clears >= 30%, i.e. HARD-FAIL condition iv does not fire): 0.35.** The lowest-confidence number in this note -- the implicative-verb lexicon (Karttunen classes) was added from the Director's own cross-check, not independently verified by either lit-scan sub-agent this cycle, and modal-cue incidence in the gold is smaller (11 items) and more ambiguous than the negation subset per the Data note.
- **P(the negation-balanced eval slice reaches n>=40 without a real data-sourcing task, i.e. HARD-FAIL condition ii does not fire on the first attempt): 0.30.** Deflated hard: the current 136-item gold yields only ~13-14 clean labeled-verb-negation items after removing false-positive scope hits; reaching 40 almost certainly requires the bounded but real data-pull task described above BEFORE a full eval can run -- this is the single most likely near-term blocker, more likely to fire than any of the mechanism-level HARD-FAILs.

**Biggest risk (explicit, deflated):** the DATA gap, not the mechanism. The signal itself (UD `Polarity=Neg` + dependency attachment + `conj`-propagation) is directly verified against real corpus parses this cycle and is architecturally sound and cheap; the honest bottleneck is that the current construction gold's negation incidence, after removing non-scoping false positives, is likely too thin (~10%, ~13-14 items) to clear a statistically meaningful HARD-FAIL threshold on the first pass -- a negation-balanced data pull from the same UD-EWT source (bounded, mechanical, no new annotation scheme needed) is the realistic prerequisite, not a redesign of the gate itself.
