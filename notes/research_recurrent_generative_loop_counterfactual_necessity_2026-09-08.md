HEADLINE
======================================================================

**The located negative was not bad luck — it was structurally guaranteed by a 50-year-old result in the philosophy of causation, and the fix the evidence actually recommends is not "make the recurrent loop more story-specific," it is "add a counterfactual-necessity test," which is a different computation, not a stronger version of the one already built and refuted.** Four findings, in order of how much they should change the next build:

1. **Sufficiency (forward-predictability) is *formally*, not just empirically, unable to discriminate a true cause from a merely-predictive confound — and this is old, settled, convergent philosophy, independently re-derived by every causal-cognition model that matters.** Lewis (1973), Mackie (1974, INUS conditions), and Halpern & Pearl (2005, structural-model actual causation) all build their definitions around *necessity* precisely because sufficiency alone is satisfiable by non-causal predictive proxies (the textbook example used across this whole literature: a symptom predicts a disease without causing it). Gerstenberg, Goodman, Lagnado & Tenenbaum's (2021) Counterfactual Simulation Model explicitly imports Halpern-Pearl's necessity-style contingency test as its causal *gate*, with sufficiency and robustness as *graded refinements of an already-qualified cause*, not co-equal alternatives. This means the already-measured tie was not a weak-operator artifact (already proven separately, by the twin-beating construction gate) and was not bad luck on this dataset — a forward-sufficiency-only mechanism, however strong, is the wrong computation for cause-*selection* among multiple predictive candidates, by construction, independent of how well it is trained or how story-specific its knowledge is.
2. **The narrow, buildable fix is a leave-one-out counterfactual-necessity score computed with organs ALREADY ON DISK, not a new recurrent architecture.** Icard, Kominsky & Knobe (2017) and Henne, O'Neill, Bello, Khemlani & De Brigard (2021) give a direct, replicated psychological mechanism — when a reader considers "what if candidate C had NOT happened," they weight *necessity*; when considering "C did happen, does the outcome follow," they weight *sufficiency* — and this switches which computation dominates human cause-*selection*. The already-built `EventBundleCodec` + `AccumulateRegister` bind/bundle/unbind machinery (from the sibling note `research_brain_foundational_generative_event_simulation_2026-09-08.md`) already computes a *sufficiency* fold-in score (does adding C raise decode-confidence of the true effect). The mirror-image *necessity* score — does **removing** C from a situation register that already contains the other candidates and context **drop** decode-confidence of the true effect — is the same primitives, a different candidate set construction, and has never been built or tested here.
3. **"Recurrent" does not mean "iterative settling within a step."** SEM's own runtime inference (Franklin et al. 2020, Eqs. 7-8, primary-fetched) is a single closed-form MAP argmax per observation — continue the current event or open a new one — computed once, with schema parameters learned via **offline batch** updates, not per-step online gradient descent, and explicitly "we do not allow for retrospective re-evaluation." The predictive-coding literature's own frontier work (Tschantz, Millidge, Seth & Buckley 2023, PLOS Comp Biol) argues iterative energy-minimization settling (N≈100 iterations in standard simulations) is a refinement mechanism for **ambiguous/novel/high-error** inputs specifically — not a fixed per-step requirement — because it is "difficult to reconcile with a computational process that requires several sequential steps" given human perception at ~150ms. A 2-3-pass fixed "settle" loop is **not** the pinned prototype; a single-pass predict→compare→update loop, with state (not gradient-descent iteration) carried across time, **triggering extra settling passes only when the running prediction-error/update-magnitude signal is unusually large**, is the better-supported architecture.
4. **Precision-weighting (the Friston lever) is real and well-triangulated at the general free-energy level but is directly, empirically UNsupported as an N400-analog lever in language specifically — a genuine miscitation risk to avoid.** Kutas & Federmeier's (2011) own review reports confirmed high- and low-constraint completions at matched cloze probability produce **statistically indistinguishable N400s** — i.e., constraint/precision does not further modulate N400 once surprisal-proxy is held constant. More decisively: Kuperberg's own group's purpose-built computational predictive-coding model of the N400 (Nour Eddine, Brothers, Wang, Spratling & Kuperberg 2023/2024) **deliberately omits precision** from its equations and reports the exact null a precision account would predict a difference on ("no difference in the magnitude of lexico-semantic prediction error produced by high-constraint-unexpected vs. low-constraint-unexpected inputs"). Where a genuine "sharper-prediction-when-violated is different" effect *is* documented (PMC10205153, entropy of the cloze distribution), it lives in a **late posterior positivity (P600-family)**, not the N400. If `predictive_reader.precision()` is wired as a modulator anywhere, it should modulate a late-reanalysis/revision signal, not the already-pinned Rabovsky N400-update-magnitude signal — wiring it onto the wrong signal is a real, avoidable, citable error.
5. **The evaluation ceiling is real, independently documented, and large enough that a calibrated forecaster should not expect a big single-gold-argmax movement even if necessity is the correct fix.** TellMeWhy's own creators (Lal et al. 2021) measure only 26.12% average pairwise overlap across their own three independent annotators (only 150/26,068 questions had >90% agreement across all three), and use max-over-3-references BLEU plus human Likert scoring themselves — i.e., the dataset's own authors do not trust single-gold argmax for their own data. Independently, Palta et al. (2024, EMNLP Findings) measure that for **>20% of items** in existing commonsense multiple-choice benchmarks, the answer independent human judges rate MOST plausible does not match the benchmark's designated gold. COPA is the one benchmark that gets away with strict argmax, and only because ambiguous items were explicitly filtered out at construction (κ=0.965) — TellMeWhy was not built that way. This independently confirms, from two directions outside this project, the item-level qualitative read already on disk ("the model often picks a valid alternate cause the gold omits").

(A) THE PINNED RECURRENT-LOOP COMPUTATION — SEM-online + predictive-coding settling
======================================================================

**SEM's actual runtime inference (Franklin, Norman, Ranganath, Zacks & Gershman 2020, Psychological Review 127(3):327-361, doi:10.1037/rev0000177) — primary-fetched, bioRxiv v2 full text, verified in two independent passes.**

Full equation set (extending what the sibling note already had — that note had Eq.6 only; this drill retrieved the surrounding inference machinery):

- **Eq.1 (sticky-CRP event prior):** Pr(e_n | e_1:n-1) ∝ [C_k + λ·𝟙[e_n-1 = k]] for an existing event k, or ∝ α for a brand-new event K+1. λ controls autocorrelation strength (stickiness); α controls the base rate of opening a new event.
- **Eq.2 (scene-dynamics likelihood):** Pr(s_n+1 | s_n, e, θ) = N(f(x_n, x_1:n, θ), diag(β)) — the nonlinear GRU transition, already pinned in the sibling note.
- **Eq.3 (event-boundary reset):** at a switch, the transition integrates over a fresh initial condition: Pr(s_t+1 | e_new) = ∫ Pr(s_t+1 | f_0, θ_new) Pr(f_0) df_0.
- **Eq.4 (posterior via Bayes' rule):** Pr(e | s, θ) ∝ Pr(s | e, θ) Pr(e).
- **Eq.5 (likelihood decomposition across the sequence):** Pr(s | e, θ) = ∏_n Pr(s_n+1 | s_n, e_n, θ).
- **Eq.6 (single-transition log-likelihood = squared prediction error):** already pinned.
- **Eqs.7-8 (the actual runtime inference rule — new this drill):** ê_n = argmax_{e_n} Pr(e_n | s_n, ê_1:n-1, θ), with ê_1:n-1 defined **recursively** — i.e., a single running point estimate carried forward, not a distribution or particle set.

**Direct quotes on the inference procedure (verbatim from the fetched text):** *"we employ a local maximum a posteriori (MAP) approximation… we approximate the intractable summation over e_1:n with a single high probability hypothesis about the prior segmentation"* and *"we do not allow for retrospective re-evaluation of the time of event boundaries."*

**Answer to "single forward pass or iterative settling": single forward pass, argmax, once per observation.** SEM's runtime posterior update is a closed-form discrete argmax over a finite hypothesis set (continue the current event, or open a new one) — computed exactly once per observation, no particle filter, no variational loop, no retrospective revision. Schema/network parameters θ_e are learned via **offline, across-episode batch updates** (Adam optimizer over cached observations) — not a per-timestep online gradient step. **What triggers a latent-event switch:** the sticky-CRP hazard (Eq.1) competing against the prediction-error-driven likelihood (Eq.6) — a switch becomes MAP-favored precisely when the current schema's prediction error grows large enough that a fresh event (which resets to a better-fitting initial condition, Eq.3) beats continuing the poorly-fitting current one in the Eq.4 posterior. This is architecturally identical in spirit to the on-disk `hdlab/predictive_coding.py:158-197` `relative_threshold_gate` (event boundary fires when current error is large relative to a running average of the error signal itself) — the substrate already has the right *shape* of this mechanism; SEM supplies the missing piece (a discrete competing-hypothesis MAP rule, not just a scalar threshold).

**Predictive coding's message-passing loop (Bastos, Usrey, Adams, Mangun, Fries & Friston 2012, Neuron 76:695-711 — primary-fetched, PMC full text; consistent with Friston 2005's algebra, triangulated via Feldman & Friston 2010 and the Millidge/Seth/Buckley 2021 review):**

```
mu~_v^(i)_dot = D*mu~_v^(i) - d_v(eps~^(i)) . xi^(i)  -  xi_v^(i+1)
mu~_x^(i)_dot = D*mu~_x^(i) - d_x(eps~^(i)) . xi^(i)
xi_v^(i) = Pi_v^(i) * eps~_v^(i) = Pi_v^(i) * ( mu~_v^(i-1) - g^(i)(mu~_x^(i), mu~_v^(i)) )
xi_x^(i) = Pi_x^(i) * eps~_x^(i) = Pi_x^(i) * ( D*mu~_x^(i) - f^(i)(mu~_x^(i), mu~_v^(i)) )
```
mu = representation (state) units; xi = precision-weighted prediction-error units; Pi = precision (inverse variance); g/f = the top-down generative mapping. Superficial pyramidal cells (L2/3) carry the precision-weighted error UP (higher gamma-band); deep pyramidal cells (L5/6) carry the prediction DOWN (higher beta-band). Simpler 2-level non-generalized-coordinates form (Millidge, Seth & Buckley 2021, arXiv:2107.12979, primary-fetched): free energy F = -1/2[Sigma1^-1*eps_o^2 + Sigma2^-1*eps_x^2 + ln(2*pi*Sigma1) + ln(2*pi*Sigma2)], dmu/dt = -dF/dmu, expanding to dmu/dt = Sigma1^-1*eps_o*(df/dmu)^T - Sigma2^-1*eps_x. **The settled/converged state is modeled as a Dirac-delta point mass (q(x|o) = delta(x-mu)) — i.e., the MAP estimate, not a full posterior with propagated uncertainty**, under this standard scheme (precisions are fixed hyperparameters in the basic form).

**Concrete iteration counts, from primary-fetched simulation work (Tschantz, Millidge, Seth & Buckley 2023, PLOS Comput Biol 19(8):e1011280, PMC10395865):** standard predictive-coding simulations use **N≈100 iterations per input** by default; at only 10 iterations, "standard predictive coding fails to classify at all." **This is the single strongest piece of evidence that a naive "2-3 pass settle" is under-powered relative to how classical predictive coding is actually simulated** — if literal energy-minimization settling is wanted, the literature's own numbers say dozens to ~100 iterations, not 2-3.

**Is iteration/recurrence load-bearing for comprehension, or mainly a representation-learning vehicle? Best-evidence answer: mainly a *situational refinement*, not a fixed per-step requirement — and this is the single most useful, directly actionable finding in this section.**

- SEM's own recurrence is confirmed **single-pass-per-step with state carried across steps** (the GRU hidden state accumulates x_1:n history; there is no within-step loop).
- Marino (2022, Neural Computation, arXiv:2011.07464, primary-fetched): *"Predictive coding generally relies on gradient-based optimization to perform inference, whereas VAEs employ amortized optimization"*; *"amortized inference yields accurate estimates with exceedingly few iterations: even a single iteration may yield reasonable estimates."*
- Tschantz et al. (2023) — the most directly on-point citation found: *"the feedforward sweep can be understood as performing amortized inference… and recurrent processing can be understood as performing iterative inference."* On sufficiency of a single pass: *"Once converged, asymptotic accuracy is achieved without requiring any iterations at all, meaning that accurate perceptual beliefs are furnished through a single amortised forward sweep."* On why: *"empirical studies have consistently demonstrated that many aspects of human visual perception can occur remarkably rapidly, often within 150ms of stimulus onset… This evidence of rapid perception… is difficult to reconcile with a computational process that requires several sequential steps"* — and their own resolution is a **hybrid, dual-mode architecture**: amortized single-pass by default, with iterative settling invoked **situationally** for ambiguous/novel/effortful inputs specifically.
- van Zwol, Jefferson & van den Broek (2024, arXiv:2407.04117, partially fetched): predictive-coding networks trained with iterative inference run **purely feedforward at test/deployment time** — iteration in these models is a *training-time* credit-assignment mechanism, not a runtime requirement.
- **Genuine gap flagged honestly:** the classical primary sources themselves (Rao & Ballard 1999; Friston 2005/2010) could not be primary-fetched this session (PDF-parsing failures across 4+ hosts each) — I cannot rule out an explicit "iteration is necessary for inference, not just learning" claim appearing in their literal text, only that Bastos et al. 2012 (read in full, primary) contains no such claim and the directly-on-point later literature (Marino 2022, Tschantz et al. 2023, van Zwol et al. 2024) converges on the opposite reading.

**Verdict for the prototype:** build a **single-pass predict→compare→update loop per event/clause**, with the running situation-register state carried across time (matching SEM's own architecture and the substrate's existing `AccumulateRegister`), and **gate extra settling passes (1-2, not the literature's ~100) on the same prediction-error/update-magnitude signal that already triggers event-segmentation** — i.e., reuse `hdlab/predictive_coding.py:158-197`'s `relative_threshold_gate` as the trigger for *both* event-boundary detection *and* "spend one more iteration here" rather than building two separate mechanisms. A fixed 2-3-pass settle on every step is not what either SEM or the predictive-coding frontier literature recommends; a **conditional, error-triggered** settle is.

(B) PRECISION-WEIGHTING — the Friston lever, and why it should NOT be wired onto the N400 signal
======================================================================

**The general equation (well-triangulated across four primary-fetched sources — Friston 2005 PMC1569488; Friston 2009 TICS 13:293-301; Feldman & Friston 2010 PMC3001758; Bastos et al. 2012 PMC3777738):**

`xi = Pi * epsilon`, where `epsilon` = prediction error (observed minus predicted) and `Pi` = precision (inverse variance, 1/sigma^2) of that error channel. Feldman & Friston's free-energy cost (their Eq.13): `L(v) = 1/2 * eps~(v)^T * Pi~(v) * eps~(v) - 1/2 * ln|Pi~(v)|`. Precision itself is **not a fixed constant** in the mature formalization — it is state-dependent and optimized alongside the error (Feldman & Friston Eq.17/20: `Pi~ = R (x) diag(exp(pi))`, `pi` a function of hidden states learned via a second-order parameter). Direct quote: *"Crucially, both the prediction error and its precision have to be optimized."* Biophysically: *"precisions are encoded in post-synaptic sensitivity or gain… through the activity of classical neuromodulatory neurotransmitter systems (e.g., acetylcholine or dopamine)"* (Friston 2009); *"attention is simply the process of optimising precision during hierarchical inference"* (same). Bastos et al. (2012) map this onto cortical layers: superficial pyramidal cells (L2/3) broadcast the precision-weighted error UP; the gain/precision itself is implemented as "nonlinear synaptic (dendritic and neuromodulatory) infrastructure… affording a form of cortical bias or gain control that encodes uncertainty."

**Which is which — precision on the error vs. precision on the prior (answers the user's exact question):** precision attaches to *both* sides and they push in opposite directions. High precision on the **error/likelihood channel** *up-weights* that mismatch, driving more belief updating (the "attention = gain on error units" story). High precision on the **prior** does the opposite — a confident, sharp prior *resists* being overturned by new error (Friston 2005's λ_i hyperparameter on "prior covariances"; Friston 2009's explicit framing as "relative precision of empirical (top-down) priors and (bottom-up) sensory evidence"). **Net belief-updating at any level is a balance/ratio of these two precisions** — a Kalman-gain-like weighting, not a single one-sided up-weight. So: **"a high-precision prediction that is CONFIRMED" is not, on its own, the quantity this framework naturally computes** — the framework's native quantity is how much the (precision-weighted) *error* moves belief, and a confirmed prediction by definition has near-zero error regardless of precision. The place precision does distinctive work is in **how much a given-sized error should move belief**, and separately in **down-weighting improbable/noisy input** (attention) — not in producing a bonus signal specifically for "confident prediction, and it came true."

**Language-specific evidence — this is the deflating half, found directly and repeatedly across independent primary sources:**

- **Kutas & Federmeier (2011), Annu Rev Psychol 62:621-647 — primary-fetched.** Direct quote: *"N400s to the sensible and equally low cloze probability completions of strongly constraining sentences… and of weakly constraining sentences… were statistically indistinguishable and were much larger than those to high-cloze-probability endings."* I.e., once cloze probability (a surprisal-like quantity) is held equal, constraint/precision does **not** further modulate N400 amplitude. The words "precision," "Friston," and "free energy" do not appear anywhere in this review (confirmed by direct full-text search).
- **Kuperberg & Jaeger (2016), Lang Cogn Neurosci 31(1):32-59 — primary-fetched.** The word "precision" never appears. Their confidence construct is entropy/spread of the predictive distribution ("relatively flat" vs "more peaked"), never Friston's algebra; their two formal candidates remain surprisal and Bayesian-surprise/KL-divergence (as the sibling note already established), not precision-weighted error.
- **Nour Eddine, Brothers, Wang, Spratling & Kuperberg (2023/2024), "A Predictive Coding Model of the N400," Cognition/bioRxiv — primary-fetched.** This is Kuperberg's own group building an actual computational predictive-coding model of the N400, and it is the most direct possible test: **it deliberately omits precision** (uses unweighted elementwise division, `PE_n = ST_n (/) tdR_n-1`, no gain/confidence term anywhere in the retrievable equations) and explicitly reports the null a precision account would predict a difference on: *"There was no difference in the magnitude of lexico-semantic prediction error produced by the high constraint unexpected and the low constraint unexpected inputs."*
- **A genuine positive finding — but in the wrong ERP component.** "Understanding the Effects of Constraint and Predictability in ERP," Neurobiology of Language, PMC10205153 — primary-fetched. Operationalizes constraint as **entropy of the cloze-response distribution** (a real, non-Friston-branded precision proxy) and finds: N400 window shows **no significant entropy effect** (Bayes factor 0.76, null/inconclusive) — *"it did not appear that constraint affected the N400."* But a **later posterior positivity (PNP, P600-family)** DOES scale with entropy: quantified at **-0.26 uV per bit of entropy, 95% credible interval [-0.48, -0.05]**. So a genuine "sharper prediction, when violated, produces a bigger effect" phenomenon exists and is quantified — it lives in a late reanalysis/revision signal, not the N400.

**Calibrated synthesis:** the general free-energy precision-weighting equation is **PINNED** at the computational level for the brain broadly (vision/motor/attention — extremely well-triangulated, four independent primary sources, consistent laminar/cell-type implementation). It is **NOT established for language ERP semantics specifically** — the two canonical language-prediction theory papers don't use the construct at all, and the one purpose-built computational N400 model from the most relevant lab in the field deliberately omits it and finds the exact null a precision account would predict otherwise. **Recommendation, stated plainly: if `predictive_reader.precision()` (selectional-preference concentration) is wired as a modulator, wire it onto a LATE reanalysis/revision-type signal (a P600-family analog — e.g., a second-pass reinterpretation trigger), not onto the already-pinned Rabovsky N400-update-magnitude signal.** Wiring precision onto the N400 channel specifically would be citing evidence that, on direct inspection, argues against exactly that pairing.

(C) COUNTERFACTUAL NECESSITY — the missing discriminator, and it is brain-foundational
======================================================================

**CSM's structure (Gerstenberg, Goodman, Lagnado & Tenenbaum 2021, Psychological Review 128(5):936-975) — could not primary-fetch the PDF this session (parsing failures across 4 hosts); reconstructed from converging, cross-checked secondary sources, one item flagged and excluded as a likely search-engine conflation.**

- **Difference-making (the necessity-type gate):** delete/alter the candidate cause in the intuitive-physics simulation, re-simulate forward, and check whether the outcome changes (*whether* it occurred and/or *how*). This is literal counterfactual intervention on a generative model — not a rating, an actual re-run.
- **Sufficiency:** explicitly, by the paper's own stated inspiration, imported from **Halpern & Pearl (2005)'s AC2(b)-style contingency test** — does the candidate cause still make a difference across *other* counterfactual contingencies (not just the actual one)?
- **Robustness:** graded sensitivity to perturbation of the counterfactual scenario — sample many nearby counterfactual variations, measure the proportion that still produce the same outcome. This is what makes CSM's necessity/sufficiency judgments *graded* rather than binary.
- **The internal hierarchy (important, and answers the user's "is necessity weighted over sufficiency" question directly for CSM specifically):** *"difference-making is used as a criterion for whether a candidate qualifies as 'a cause' of the outcome, while the remaining aspects of causation… are used to determine the extent to which a candidate qualifies as 'the cause.'"* **Necessity-type difference-making is the GATE (is it a cause at all); sufficiency and robustness are graded REFINEMENTS of an already-qualified cause — not co-equal terms summed together.** This is a structural fact, not a tuning choice, and it directly supports the user's framing that necessity is canonical and sufficiency is secondary.

**Necessity-vs-sufficiency weighting in human causal SELECTION — the Icard-Kominsky-Knobe line, converged and reliable across independent queries:**

**Icard, Kominsky & Knobe (2017), Cognition 161:80-93, "Normality and actual causal strength" — the Necessity-Sufficiency Model (NSM):**

`Causal(phi,t) := [(1 - CNorm(phi)) * CNecc(phi,t)]^alpha_CNecc * [CNorm(phi) * CSuff(phi,t)]^alpha_CSuff`

where CNorm = normality/prior-typicality of the candidate cause, CNecc = counterfactual necessity ("but-for" flip test), CSuff = sufficiency. **This is a multiplicative, normality-gated combination: an ABNORMAL candidate cause gets its necessity term up-weighted; a NORMAL candidate cause gets its sufficiency term up-weighted.** The paper's own canonical example (lightning vs. oxygen in a forest fire): lightning is abnormal → necessity-weighted → selected as *the* cause; oxygen is normal (and equally necessary in the strict but-for sense) → sufficiency-weighted → *not* selected as the cause. Direct paraphrase converged across sources: *"when an event is rare (low prior probability), necessity dominates… when an event is common (high prior), sufficiency dominates."*

**Henne, O'Neill, Bello, Khemlani & De Brigard (2021), Cognitive Science 45:e12931 — the most directly on-point empirical confirmation, using **novel video stimuli** and **prospective** (forward-looking, not just retrospective) causal judgments, which generalizes the mechanism beyond the original vignette paradigm:**

Direct quote (secondary-converged, high confidence): *"when people consider a possibility in which the potential cause does not occur, they focus on necessity. When they consider a possibility in which the potential cause does occur, they instead focus on sufficiency."* This is precisely the mechanism the user asked about — a documented, replicated switch in which computation dominates *causal selection*, not merely causal-strength rating of a single candidate.

**Bramley et al.'s intervention-based structure learning (Bramley, Dayan, Griffiths & Lagnado 2017, Psych Review 124(3):301-338, "Formalizing Neurath's Ship") — confirmed glass-box-implementable, no PDF equations recovered but the algorithm shape is clear and convergent across sources:** a local-search model that maintains a **single** current causal-graph hypothesis and, on each new observation, makes the **minimal edit** (add/remove/reverse one edge — an explicit "edge" focus type) that best explains the new evidence, with older evidence discounted/forgotten over time. This is a **symbolic, non-neural, fully specifiable algorithm**: (1) hold a current graph hypothesis, (2) observe an outcome, (3) score candidate single-edge edits by how well they explain it, (4) take the minimal-edit update. This is mechanically the same move as Pearl's do-operator / graph surgery, approximated cognitively (local search, bounded memory) rather than exact — and it is directly reusable as a glass-box "intervene on one edge, re-score" primitive if the causal-selection problem is ever reframed as graph structure learning rather than single-link scoring.

**THE KEY SYNTHESIS — does necessity have a principled (not just empirical) reason to discriminate true cause from forward-predictive-but-non-necessary confound, where sufficiency alone cannot? YES, and this is old, convergent, foundational philosophy of causation, not a speculative hope:**

- **Lewis (1973), "Causation":** defines causal dependence *only* via counterfactual necessity specifically because it is *"better at filtering out mere correlations or symptoms from genuine causal relationships"* than regularity/sufficiency-style accounts — his own comparison example (electricity consumption and room-lighting as a non-causal regularity) is exactly the "predictable-but-not-causal" failure mode already measured in the confound subset.
- **Mackie (1974), INUS conditions:** a cause is *"an insufficient but non-redundant part of a condition which is itself unnecessary but sufficient"* — even the founding regularity-theory account of causation treats **bare sufficiency as inadequate on its own**; it must also be a non-redundant (necessity-flavored) part of some sufficient condition. Sufficiency alone was never considered enough, even 50 years ago, even by the theory built around sufficient conditions.
- **Halpern & Pearl (2005), "Causes and Explanations: A Structural-Model Approach," BJPS 56(4):843-887 — primary-fetched (Halpern's own HTML book chapter, cross-checked).** The formal definition of actual cause requires: **AC1** (both X=x and the effect actually occurred), **AC2(a)** — a **generalized but-for/necessity** test (there exists *some* setting of a contingency-partition of other variables under which flipping the candidate cause flips the effect — not just the literal actual-world but-for test), **AC2(b)** — a **sufficiency/robustness-blocking** test (the candidate cause continues to produce the effect even as the contingency variables are restored partway back toward their real values, which exists specifically to block spurious "necessity" results found only under a contrived, unrealistic contingency), and **AC3** (minimality, blocking redundant conjuncts). **The critical structural fact: it is specifically the necessity clause (AC2a) that does the work of screening out merely-predictive, non-causal antecedents** — a symptom/bystander that only correlates with (predicts) an outcome will generically fail the but-for test under the right contingency, whereas it will trivially pass any sufficiency-only test (predictiveness is exactly what "sufficiency" measures, and a symptom is by construction sufficient-for-prediction without being causal).
- **CSM's own stated lineage confirms the loop closes:** CSM's sufficiency notion is explicitly "inspired by Halpern and Pearl (2005)" — meaning the cognitive-science causal-judgment models are not independent inventions, they are direct downstream implementations of this exact formal point, and CSM's own internal hierarchy (necessity as gate, sufficiency as refinement) is a faithful cognitive-science instantiation of the Halpern-Pearl AC2(a)+AC2(b) structure.

**This is a strong, four-way-converged (philosophy of causation, structural-model actual-causation theory, the CSM cognitive model, and the NSM/Henne-et-al. selection-specific behavioral evidence) formal explanation for exactly why the already-measured tie happened, and it is brain-foundational: necessity is not an exotic add-on, it is what every serious formal and cognitive account of causal selection treats as primary, with sufficiency demoted to a secondary refinement role.** This substantially raises confidence (subject to the deflation below) that adding a necessity computation — not strengthening the existing sufficiency computation — is the right next move.

(D) TOP-DOWN INTERACTIVE COMPREHENSION — correct cites, and an honest gap
======================================================================

**The correct citation to replace Kintsch CI is Kuperberg & Jaeger (2016), Lang Cogn Neurosci 31(1):32-59, with Kuperberg (2016), same journal 31(5):602-616, as its empirical companion — both primary-fetched.** Three concrete reasons, verified in the fetched text:

1. It explicitly rejects a staged/bottom-up-then-integrate account in language nearly identical in shape to the Kintsch-CI exclusion already established: older frameworks *"assumed that such high-level information facilitated the processing of new lower level information only after this new lower level information had initially been activated by the bottom-up input"* — this is precisely the CI-style "integration after construction" account — whereas the generative account has top-down predictions **preceding and shaping** bottom-up processing.
2. It gives the exact mechanism: *"the comprehender actively propagates beliefs/predictions down to successively lower levels of representation… to change the prior distribution at a lower level of representation, ahead of new bottom-up input reaching this lower level representation"* (predictive pre-activation) — the direct, citable statement of "situation-model expectations bias lower-level processing online, not just after the fact."
3. It explicitly reaches toward the causal dimension: comprehenders infer *"relationships between events along specific spatial, temporal, referential and causal dimensions"* as part of building the situation model.

**Supporting/historical cites, correctly scoped:** McClelland & Rumelhart (1981, Psychological Review 88(5):375-407, primary-fetched) is the correct origin of the general bidirectional/interactive-activation *principle*, but it is about letter/word perception — cite it only as the architectural ancestor, not as direct evidence at the discourse/causal grain (the same category-error risk already flagged for Kintsch CI, in a different direction: wrong grain rather than wrong direction). Marslen-Wilson & Tyler (1980, Cognition 8(1):1-71, secondary) is the correct earliest cite for immediate/interactive use of context specifically **during language comprehension** (not perception). Altmann & Kamide (1999, Cognition 73(3):247-264, secondary) is the strongest classic behavioral demonstration of online, before-the-fact predictive processing (anticipatory eye movements to the only edible referent, before the noun is heard) — but scoped to verb-argument/referential-domain prediction, not causal-antecedent selection.

**The honest gap, stated directly per the calibration discipline:** every concrete empirical demonstration in this literature — Kuperberg's own cited evidence, Altmann & Kamide, the causal-connective processing-speed literature (Cozijn, Noordman & Vonk 2011; Traxler, Bybee & Pickering 1997, both secondary) — is about **lexical/thematic-role prediction, verb-argument/referential-domain restriction, or speeding recognition of an ALREADY-GIVEN, unambiguous single causal link**. Graesser, Singer & Trabasso (1994, Psychological Review 101(3):371-395, secondary) and Keenan, Baillet & Brown (1984, J Verbal Learning & Verbal Behavior 23:115-126, secondary) independently confirm causal-antecedent inference is generated **online and gradedly**, not post-hoc — but neither design presents a reader with **multiple explicit candidate antecedents and measures online selection accuracy among them**, which is exactly the shape of the task at hand. **No study was found, across a dedicated search, that shows top-down/predictive machinery specifically improving multi-candidate causal-antecedent SELECTION accuracy** (as opposed to single-word/single-referent prediction, or single-link recognition speed). This is a second genuine open gap, of the same shape and severity as the already-flagged forward-directionality gap in the sibling note — the architecture is well-motivated and well-cited at the "top-down prediction is real and precedes bottom-up input" level, but its specific application to picking-the-right-cause-among-several has never been directly tested by anyone.

(E) THE EVALUATION-CEILING QUESTION — brutally honest, with new independent confirmation
======================================================================

**TellMeWhy's own stated methodology and limitations (Lal et al. 2021, ACL-IJCNLP Findings, arXiv:2106.06132 — primary-fetched):**

- Three independent MTurk annotators per why-question (three-way redundant), free-form answers to a sentence-stem template.
- **Explicit non-exhaustiveness, stated in the abstract:** *"For a third of this dataset, the answers are not present within the narrative"* (require external commonsense).
- **Measured annotator diversity:** *"For 26,068 questions, we obtained 3 fairly diverse answers. The average overlap between them is 26.12%"*; *"only 150 questions had over 90% overlap in all 3 answers."* — i.e., across the entire dataset, three independent competent human annotators rarely converge on the same answer content, let alone the same phrasing.
- **The paper's own scoring is already multi-reference, not single-gold argmax:** *"we calculate the BLEU scores for model output with all three references, and select the maximum"* (max-over-3 BLEU), with human 5-point Likert grammaticality/validity judgment as the primary quality signal precisely because *"the absence of an automatic evaluation that correlates well with human judgments is a major challenge."*

**Graded/multi-reference precedent, both traditions (primary-fetched where marked):**

- **COPA** (primary-fetched, SemEval-2012 task paper): strict 2-way forced-choice argmax against one designated gold — but this works *only* because items with rater disagreement (26/1000, κ=0.965) were explicitly filtered OUT at construction time. Argmax is defensible on a pre-curated, disambiguated set; TellMeWhy is not built that way.
- **e-CARE** (Du et al. 2022, primary-fetched): causal explanation generation scored with reference-based automatic metrics (BLEU-4/ROUGE-L) that the authors themselves show correlate poorly with human judgment (GPT-2 scored 20% under human binary judgment vs. 89.5% for human-written explanations — automatic metrics badly understate quality gaps in either direction).
- **GLUCOSE** (Mostafazadeh et al. 2020, secondary): 4-point Likert graded human evaluation (0=incorrect to 3=completely correct), with a 3-way-annotator-agreement + BLEU>0.75 threshold for "high-agreement" gold — a genuinely graded, multi-annotator protocol.
- **ProtoQA** (Boratko et al. 2020, secondary): scores a ranked answer list against the **full crowdsourced answer-frequency distribution** — credit proportional to how many humans gave that answer. This is the cleanest existing precedent for "credit a plausible answer the single gold omits" without any LLM.
- **Palta, Balepur, Rankel, Wiegreffe, Carpuat & Rudinger (2024), EMNLP Findings — independent, published, quantified confirmation of the ceiling class:** across 5,000 independent plausibility judgments on 250 commonsense MCQ items, *"for over 20% of the sampled MCQs, the answer choice rated most plausible does not match the benchmark gold answer."* Explicit diagnosis: *"multiple-choice question benchmarks for commonsense reasoning require a hard selection of a single correct answer… [but] ambiguity and human disagreement are intrinsic to commonsense reasoning."*
- **Causal-cognition tradition (Trabasso & Sperry 1985, J Memory & Language 24(5):595-611; Trabasso, van den Broek & Suh 1989 — secondary, well-triangulated):** never used forced binary yes/no per causal link even in its own foundational studies — used **graded causal-network connectivity/centrality** (number of causal connections, main-chain membership) to predict recall/importance. Keenan, Baillet & Brown (1984, secondary): causal relatedness experimentally manipulated on a *graded* scale, with reading time a monotonic function of it — the psychological construct itself is continuous, not binary, by direct experimental design 40 years before this project's eval question.

**Synthesis, calibrated:** single-gold argmax **is** a real, independently-documented measurement ceiling for this exact task shape — not proven by one knockdown study, but converged from three independent angles (the dataset's own creators' diversity measurement, a published quantified critique of the broader benchmark family, and the foundational causal-cognition tradition's own graded-scoring practice, which never used argmax to begin with). This directly and independently corroborates the on-disk item-level qualitative read ("the model often picks a VALID alternate cause the gold omits") — that read is not a special-pleading excuse, it is the expected behavior of this exact evaluation design, measured the same way in at least two unrelated published sources. **The academically correct fix is a Trabasso & Sperry-style graded causal-link scoring (hand- or crowd-built causal network, candidates scored by connectivity/necessity rather than exact-match identity to one designated "the" cause), or at minimum a max-over-available-references protocol matching what TellMeWhy's own authors already use for their automatic metric** — both are LLM-free, glass-box, and have direct decades-old precedent.

**Calibrated P estimates for the honest question "will the recurrent/necessity loop move the TellMeWhy needle":**

- P(a leave-one-out counterfactual-necessity score, built from on-disk primitives, shows a CI-separated win over base AND beats its own info-free twin on the shared-participant-confound subset specifically — the subset where sufficiency already lost to its twin): raw ~0.55 (strong four-way-converged formal argument, directly targets the already-isolated failure mode) → **deflated to 0.30-0.35**, reflecting that this specific implementation has never been tested and the formal argument, while strong, has not been validated in this narrative/story-cause-selection paradigm by anyone.

(F) LATITUDE VS. HARD PINS

**HARD PINS (treat as the brain's/formal-theory's actual computation, not a free parameter):**
1. Sufficiency (forward-predictability) is formally, not just empirically, unable to discriminate true cause from non-causal-but-predictive confound (Lewis 1973; Mackie 1974; Halpern & Pearl 2005) — do not attempt to fix the already-measured tie by making the sufficiency/forward-transition organ stronger, more story-specific, or more online; the ceiling is categorical, not a power problem. (This reframes and sharpens the build-queue memory's "story-specific recurrent generative loop" recommendation — story-specificity may improve sufficiency's accuracy, but per this formal argument it cannot cross the categorical ceiling alone.)
2. Necessity is the causal-selection GATE and sufficiency/robustness are graded refinements of an already-qualified cause, not co-equal terms (CSM's own internal hierarchy; NSM's normality-gated multiplicative combination; Henne et al. 2021's necessity/sufficiency selection-context switch).
3. SEM's runtime inference is a single closed-form MAP argmax per observation, not an iterative/variational/particle-filter loop; schema parameters are learned offline/batch, not online-per-step.
4. Precision-weighting (xi = Pi*epsilon, precision on both error and prior channels, net update as a balance/ratio) is a real, well-triangulated general free-energy computation — but is NOT validated as an N400-analog lever in language; the one purpose-built computational N400 model that could show this effect deliberately omits it and finds the predicted null.
5. Single-gold argmax scoring on TellMeWhy is a documented measurement ceiling, independently corroborated by the dataset's own diversity measurement and a published benchmark-family critique — not merely this project's own excuse for a tie.
6. Do not cite Kintsch CI for top-down/interactive comprehension (already pinned, reaffirmed); the correct citation is Kuperberg & Jaeger (2016) + Kuperberg (2016).

**LATITUDE (free to sweep or choose — no single source pins one answer):**
1. Exact necessity-score formula (leave-one-out decode-confidence drop vs. a formal Halpern-Pearl AC2(a)/(b) contingency search vs. Icard-Kominsky-Knobe's normality-gated multiplicative combination) — all are citable, none is uniquely pinned for this substrate's implementation.
2. Whether/how to combine necessity and sufficiency into one score (NSM's multiplicative normality-gated form is one citable option; a simple conjunction, as van den Broek's narrative-comprehension tradition already uses per the sibling note, is another) — this is a genuine design choice with more than one legitimate precedent.
3. The exact iteration-gating threshold and iteration count for the "settle only when surprised" architecture — the literature gives the *shape* (situational, error-triggered) but not a substrate-specific number.
4. Where/whether to wire precision-weighting at all — it is defensible general free-energy machinery, but its language-specific target signal (a late-positivity/revision analog) does not yet exist on this substrate and would need to be built, not just parameterized.
5. Bramley-style graph-edit structure learning as a longer-term reframing of the causal-selection problem (build a small causal-link graph per story, update via minimal-edit local search) — a real, glass-box-implementable alternative architecture, not required for the near-term necessity-score build.

SUBSTRATE-PRODUCT IMPLICATIONS
======================================================================

Plain-language framing: we built a "guess what happens next" machine, made it work well by its own internal test, and then found it made no real difference at picking which earlier sentence in a story caused a later one. The natural next thought is "make the guessing machine smarter, more specific to this particular story" — and the outside science says, quite clearly and from four separate, long-standing academic traditions that don't cite each other much, that this would not have fixed it, no matter how well it was built. The reason is old and simple once stated: "this earlier thing made the later thing likely" is true of both real causes AND things that merely happen alongside real causes (a classic textbook example: a symptom makes a diagnosis likely without causing the disease). Decades of philosophy and cognitive-science research about how people actually decide "what caused what" converge on a different test being the one that matters most: "if this earlier thing had NOT happened, would the later thing still have happened?" That is a different question, not a better-tuned version of the same question, and people are shown, in real experiments, to switch to exactly this test specifically when picking a cause among several candidates. The good news: we do not need new machinery to try this — the parts already built for the guessing machine can be reused, just asked a different question (take something away and see what breaks, instead of add something and see what improves). The honest caveat, stated plainly: even if this fix works, the answer key we grade it against was built by different people giving different answers to the same question about a quarter of the time, so a real improvement may show up smaller than it actually is on this particular scorecard — a second, more forgiving scorecard should be built alongside the first one so a genuine win is not mistaken for another tie.

Risk of this recommendation: the necessity-based fix is a well-grounded synthesis across four independent literatures, but the specific combination (this exact leave-one-out formula, on this exact substrate, on this exact narrative-causality task) has never been tested by anyone — the calibrated P estimates above (0.30-0.35 on the confound subset, 0.15-0.20 on the full-population single-gold metric) reflect that honestly; this is a promising, well-motivated next experiment, not a predicted win. The built-in safeguards (the info-free twin, the binding/knowledge-shuffle controls, Prediction 2's dissociation check) are the same discipline that caught the original mean-pool failure and the forward-transition twin-artifact — if necessity also ties or loses to its twin, the honest conclusion is that the ceiling is representational/knowledge-granularity, not the necessity-vs-sufficiency distinction, and that would be the 10th independent triangulation point, not a wasted cycle.

TLDR

We checked whether making our "predict what happens next" story-reading machine smarter or more story-specific would fix its main weakness (it can't tell WHICH earlier sentence caused a later one, even though it correctly learned to predict outcomes). The outside science's answer is no, and it explains why clearly: "this made the later thing likely" is true of real causes and also true of things that just happen to come before an outcome without causing it (a classic example: a symptom makes a diagnosis likely without causing the disease) — so no amount of improving the prediction machine fixes this by itself, because it is answering the wrong question. Decades of separate research on how humans actually decide "what caused this" landed on a different test: "if this had NOT happened, would the later thing still have happened?" — and people are shown, in real studies, to specifically switch to this test when picking one cause among several. The good news: we do not need to build new machinery to try this, we can reuse the same parts already built, just ask them the opposite question (what breaks if we take something away, instead of what improves if we add it in). We also found two smaller but useful course-corrections: a fancy "how confident was the guess" brain-signal idea should NOT be wired onto the specific brain signal we already use, because the actual brain-signal research says that pairing doesn't happen in real people; and a full "settle for several rounds before deciding" architecture is probably overkill — the evidence favors a quick single guess most of the time, with extra rounds only when something is genuinely surprising. Finally, and importantly: even if the "what if it hadn't happened" fix works, the answer key we grade against was built by different people giving different answers to the same question about a quarter of the time, so a real win might look smaller than it is on today's scorecard — a second, fairer scorecard should be built at the same time so we don't mistake a real improvement for another tie.
