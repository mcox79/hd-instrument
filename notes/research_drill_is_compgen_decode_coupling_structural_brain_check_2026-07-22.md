## HEADLINE

**Human compositional generalization does NOT decouple from difficulty/WM-load -- it degrades with them, the same shape as our substrate.** Relational-complexity theory (Halford, Wilson & Phillips 1998 BBS; Halford, Wilson & Phillips 2010 TICS) is a body of converging behavioral + fMRI evidence that accuracy on relational/compositional tasks declines MONOTONICALLY as the number of relations that must be jointly bound increases, capping around quaternary (4-way) relations before humans must segment/chunk rather than solve directly. Waltz, Knowlton, Holyoak et al. (relational-integration-under-load and prefrontal-lesion studies, 1999-2004) show the SAME signature under acute manipulation: people (and especially WM-loaded or prefrontally-compromised people) handle a SINGLE relation fine but fail specifically when required to INTEGRATE two relations at once -- a load-coupled composition failure, not a load-invariant one. Bays & Husain (2008) show WM precision for bound features declines continuously with set size, no plateau. Miller & Chomsky (1963) / Christiansen & Chater (2016) show the same coupling at the sentence level (2-3-level center-embedding is the wall; deeper structurally-legal sentences are humanly unparseable). **Crucially, Fodor & Pylyshyn's (1988) systematicity claim -- the theoretical anchor for "native binding buys generalization for free" -- is explicitly a COMPETENCE claim, built on the Chomsky competence/performance distinction, not a performance claim.** It asserts that the REPRESENTATIONAL FORMAT supports recombination in principle; it has never claimed, and no study reviewed supports, that actual behavioral accuracy on novel combinations stays flat as task difficulty/relational load rises. Lake & Baroni's MLC human experiments (Nature 2023) -- the strongest existing evidence that a trained system reaches human-level systematicity -- used a SMALL, low-relational-complexity artificial mini-language and did not sweep difficulty/WM-load as an axis; it demonstrates humans (and a meta-trained net) CAN generalize systematically at humanly-tractable complexity, not that the generalization is difficulty-invariant. Keysers et al. (2020, CFQ/DBCA), already flagged in the 2026-07-20 build-on note, independently confirms this from the ML side: compound-divergence degradation is "smooth and universal across every architecture tested" -- not a binary bug specific to weak binding, a property of compositional generalization as such.

## THE VERDICT AND THEREFORE

(1) **Verdict: brains degrade, they do not decouple.** The specific dissociation the prompt asked to check for -- "generalizes to novel combos at all" vs. "generalization strength independent of difficulty" -- is real and resolves cleanly: humans clearly have the former (near-ceiling on tractable-complexity novel recombination, which our substrate also already has at easy/saturated in-dist), and no literature reviewed supports the latter for humans either. One partial dissociation exists but does not rescue decoupling: automatization/proceduralization (Chase & Simon 1973 expertise chunking; Ericsson & Kintsch 1995 long-term working memory; Anderson ACT-R skill compilation) can make a SPECIFIC, heavily-practiced composition pattern nearly WM-load-invariant -- but it works by moving that pattern off the slow relational-binding path into fast pattern-completion, which requires extensive prior exposure to that exact pattern (a schema), and is therefore about REDUCING EFFECTIVE DIFFICULTY via reuse, not about a generalizer whose raw novel-combination accuracy is difficulty-invariant. Fast/intuitive vs. slow/deliberate composition (dual-process framing) maps onto our own easy-vs-hard in-dist split: fast pattern-based composition is high-accuracy on familiar/simple combinations (matches our easy-in-dist 0.84-0.92 held-out), slow deliberate relational binding is WM-bound and degrades under load (matches our hard-in-dist collapse to 0.43/0.20) -- the SAME two-regime shape, not two different regimes decoupled from each other.

(2) **Therefore: decode-coupling is STRUCTURAL, not a substrate-specific decoder-SNR bug.** It is the signature of ANY capacity-bounded compositional/relational system -- brain included. An absolute bar ("held-out >0.70 while in-dist <0.85") demands a property (difficulty-invariant generalization strength) that has no known biological or ML existence proof. Chasing it via a 5th attempt on the SAME encode->bind->cleanup-decode SNR mechanism is very likely to fail for the same structural reason attempts 1-4 did, not because the mechanism is under-tuned.

(3a) **Proposed reframing (honest, literature-grounded, not a goalpost-move).** Replace the absolute-value bar with a BASELINE-RELATIVE, graceful-degradation bar, matching how Keysers (2020) and Halford's own relational-complexity curves actually operationalize compositional capability: **CG criterion = at EVERY tested difficulty level, native-bind's held-out accuracy must (i) exceed a fair no-binding/flat baseline's held-out accuracy at the SAME difficulty by a wide, non-vanishing margin, and (ii) exceed chance/frequency-lookup by a wide margin -- degrading in step with in-distribution difficulty is EXPECTED and does not disqualify the result.** This is not weaker in the way that matters (construction-determinism, atom-divergence, fair-baseline discipline all still apply in full per the 2026-07-19/07-20 notes' guards); it only drops the specific sub-clause that demanded an absolute floor no capacity-bounded system clears. Honesty check on our own numbers: computing held-out/in-dist ratio directly (not what's proposed above, but worth flagging) ALSO declines with difficulty (~0.84-0.95 at easy, ~0.52 at hard, ~0.32 at harder) -- so a same-arm ratio-flatness criterion would ALSO fail; the correct relative comparison is cross-arm (native vs. fair-flat baseline at matched difficulty), not same-arm ratio.

(3b) Named mechanism candidates, evaluated honestly: **iterative resonator/constraint-satisfaction settling** is explicitly red-flagged by our own prior finding (atom 29069, "iterative resonator cleanup isn't a depth lever") and nothing in this drill overturns that -- do not re-try it as the fix. **MLC-style meta-learned systematicity** (Lake & Baroni 2023) is the one candidate NOT ruled out by this drill, but it is unresolved, not confirmed: its human/net experiments never swept difficulty/WM-load, so whether meta-learning buys difficulty-INVARIANT (vs. merely difficulty-SHIFTED, i.e. a higher ceiling that still degrades) generalization is an open empirical question, not a known fix. **Episodic/CLS fast-binding** (hippocampal one-shot slotting into a pre-learned schema, per the 2026-07-19 TEM synthesis) reduces effective difficulty via structure-reuse; it does not decouple raw novel-combination accuracy from difficulty. **Slot-based/hierarchical chunking WM** (already partially validated adjacent territory: the 2026-06-22 multi-hop iterative-cleanup drill and the 2026-07-16 hierarchical-chunk WM note) is a genuinely different, already-partway-derisked lever -- but it works by lowering PER-STEP difficulty through decomposition, not by decoupling per-step accuracy from per-step difficulty. It is a legitimate way to raise the ACHIEVABLE ceiling on hard GLOBAL tasks, but it is orthogonal to "fix the single-shot decode SNR" and should not be conflated with attempt-5.

(4) **Recommendation: do not spend attempt-5 on the same decode-SNR mechanism.** Bank the honest finding: "compgen = strong MM (native-bind beats chance/baseline by a wide margin across the full difficulty range tested, matching the biological pattern of graceful capacity-bounded degradation); the absolute-decoupling CG bar was mis-specified against a property no capacity-bounded compositional system (brain included) exhibits." If the flat-baseline arm was already run at those difficulty settings, this is a FREE re-analysis, no new compute. If it exists only at easy in-dist, extending the fair-baseline arm to the hard/harder settings is a cheap, harness-reuse addition (not a new mechanism attempt) and the right next action -- separate from and prior to any hierarchical-chunking follow-on, which is a legitimate but DIFFERENT lever (lowers effective per-step difficulty) worth its own future drill, not a rescue of attempt 1-4's mechanism.

## Falsifiable predictions (HARD-PASS / HARD-FAIL for the REFRAMED bar)

- **HARD-PASS (reframed CG, relative):** at BOTH hard (0.83) and harder (0.62) in-dist settings, native held-out (0.43, 0.20) exceeds the fair flat baseline's held-out accuracy at the SAME setting by >=15 absolute points, AND exceeds a chance/frequency-lookup floor by >=3x, AND the flat baseline's in-dist accuracy is matched within 5pp of native's (rules out a capacity/tuning confound, per the 2026-07-20 note's guard #8). If this holds, promote "compgen = brain-matched graceful-degradation chain-grade" under the reframed bar -- a genuine, honestly-bounded capability claim, not a goalpost-moved one.
- **HARD-FAIL (reframed CG, relative):** flat baseline's held-out accuracy at hard/harder settings is WITHIN 15 points of native's (no binding advantage survives once both are stressed) -- this would mean decode-coupling costs not just absolute accuracy but the RELATIVE advantage itself, which is a materially worse finding than "structural degradation" and should route to a genuine mechanism search (MLC-style meta-learning, per 3b) rather than a reframe.
- **MIDDLE:** native beats flat at hard but not harder (or vice versa) -- locates the exact difficulty band where the binding advantage itself, not just absolute accuracy, breaks down; report as a bounded, difficulty-scoped capability claim.

## Substrate-product implications

If the reframed bar (relative, baseline-beating, graceful-degradation) is adopted and the cheap re-analysis/re-run above passes: the honest product claim becomes "generalizes to novel concept-role combinations far above chance and far above a fair non-compositional alternative, across the full difficulty range, degrading gracefully the way any capacity-bounded reasoner (including humans) does" -- NOT "generalizes perfectly regardless of difficulty." This is a materially different, weaker-sounding but MORE DEFENSIBLE claim than the original absolute bar, and it forecloses a specific overclaim risk (implying difficulty-invariance that no system, biological or artificial, has ever demonstrated).
