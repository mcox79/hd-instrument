## HEADLINE

Every angle converges on the SAME shape, not on a different bind operator: the brain (and the honest ML theory) never decorrelates a chain by making the whole code globally orthogonal. It inserts a **repeated, LOCAL, per-hop re-sparsification + comparator/novelty-gate bottleneck** between every step, and that bottleneck is deliberately built to strip only *incidental/statistical* redundancy — it does **not**, and by design **cannot**, decorrelate genuinely *semantic* correlation without destroying the information itself (Chalk/Marre/Tkacik PNAS 2018; V1 preserves-correlation literature). Separately, the VSA/resonator capacity literature confirms our own finding structurally: essentially all published resonator-network capacity guarantees (quadratic-in-N operational capacity, Frady/Kent/Olshausen/Sommer) are derived under i.i.d./quasi-orthogonal codebook assumptions; the correlated-codebook regime is an explicit, *unresolved* open question in that literature, not a solved problem we failed to find. So: Stage-3's wall is not a local implementation bug -- it is sitting exactly on a real, currently-open seam in the theory. That raises (not lowers) the value of a substrate-native cell here, but the ceiling section below is the honest read on how far cleanup alone can push it.

## Cheap decisive test

Build a minimal 2-hop associative chain over REAL learned (semantically-correlated) codes, exactly as in Stage-3's reach>=2 setup, and instrument ONE swap-in-swap-out per-hop cleanup gate (either candidate below) with the gate as a single on/off flag at matched compute/sparsity budget. This isolates "does a between-hop cleanup step recover fidelity" from every other Stage-3 variable (bind operator, resonator rank, dense/sparse choice) that has already been tested and ruled out as load-bearing.

## Falsifiable predictions

**Candidate 1 -- Per-hop DG-style re-separation gate** (hippocampal DG/CA3 + cerebellar-expansion grounded, NOT a bind-operator change): after each hop's associative retrieval produces a dense, correlated code, route it through a FIXED random sparse-expansion + k-WTA re-encoding (a fresh "granule-cell-style" sparse sketch, independent random projection per hop) before it is used as the next hop's cue. The chain then hops on freshly-separated sparse sketches, never on the raw correlated code directly.
- Discriminator (one line): reach>=2 chain fidelity with the gate ON vs. an ablated identity-passthrough control, matched compute/sparsity budget.
- HARD-PASS: gated fidelity exceeds ablated fidelity by a pre-registered margin (>=0.10 absolute) at reach=2, AND the gap does not shrink (ideally widens) at reach=3.
- HARD-FAIL: gated and ablated are statistically indistinguishable at reach=2 (paired-trial, matched seeds).

**Candidate 2 -- CA1-style comparator / residual gate** (hippocampal CA1 novelty-detector + predictive-coding explaining-away grounded, NOT a bind-operator change): after each hop's retrieval, compute the residual between the retrieved code and the direct (non-chained, single-hop-only) prediction from the current cue; forward only the residual/unpredicted component into the next hop's bind, suppressing the redundant already-explained component.
- Discriminator (one line): fidelity DECAY SLOPE across reach=1,2,3,4 with vs without the residual gate, matched budget.
- HARD-PASS: gate makes the decay slope sub-linear / measurably flatter (crosstalk growth rate reduced) at reach>=3.
- HARD-FAIL: slope unchanged, OR the gate makes fidelity worse (over-suppression of real signal is the specific failure mode the predictive-coding literature itself warns about -- Chalk/Marre/Tkacik show residual-forwarding is a normative trade-off, not an unconditional decorrelator).

P_deflated (either candidate recovers reach>=2 to a usable working fidelity, e.g. reach-2 fidelity within ~15% of reach-1): **0.35** (base intuition ~0.55-0.60 given strong cross-domain convergence on the mechanism SHAPE; deflated 0.20-0.25 per lit-scan calibration penalty because no direct precedent exists for this exact gate on our real learned/correlated codes, and because the VSA capacity literature itself treats correlated codebooks as an open problem rather than a solved one). Novel-synthesis cap of 0.50 applied and respected.

## Cross-thread synthesis

- Directly resolves the tension flagged in [[reference-crt-residue-helps-clean-encoding-hurts-noisy-readout]] and [[reference-correlation-hurts-associative-store-capacity-decouple-from-retrieval]]: those findings said "decouple store-codes (near-orthogonal) from retrieval-semantics (correlated)." This drill's convergent mechanism is the BIOLOGICAL IMPLEMENTATION of that decoupling: it's not a one-time store-vs-retrieve split, it's a per-hop, repeated split (fresh sparse/near-orthogonal re-encoding at every hop feeding a correlated/semantic associative step), plus a comparator that only lets NOVEL content through.
- Confirms the reader-arc's own sharpened diagnosis (director backup 2026-07-08): "reach ceiling = learned-code crosstalk/cleanup at graph scale, NOT the bind operator." All 3 priority lit-scans independently land on "insert a cleanup stage between hops," never "change the composition/binding algebra." This is now a 3-domain-plus-2-secondary convergence, the same shape [[feedback-dont-dismiss-adjacent-methods]] warns not to prematurely close off.
- Cerebellar angle (secondary, but concretely quantitative): Litwin-Kumar/Harris/Axel/Sompolinsky/Abbott (Neuron 2017) show expansion-layer dimensionality is a non-monotonic function of connectivity degree K, peaking at an intermediate, anatomically-matched optimum -- i.e. "more expansion/sparsity" is not free; there is a real optimum to search over for Candidate 1's projection width/sparsity, not an unbounded knob. ## Substrate-product implications

- This is not "add a denoiser" -- it's a specific, falsifiable claim: cleanup belongs BETWEEN hops (as a repeated gate coupled to a separate sparser subspace), not as a global end-of-chain pass and not as a change to the bind/unbind algebra itself. Stage-4 should build exactly one of the two candidates first (Candidate 1 is cheaper/more mechanical to wire; Candidate 2 requires a "direct single-hop prediction" side-computation to diff against).
- Product framing: if either candidate HARD-PASSes, the reader gets a genuine mechanism story ("multi-hop reasoning over real, correlated knowledge needs a cleanup stage between hops, exactly like hippocampal CA1/DG staging") rather than a parameter-tuned patch -- this is a better substrate-product narrative than another resonator-rank sweep.
- If both HARD-FAIL: that is itself a valuable, well-grounded negative -- it would mean our correlated learned-code regime sits past the point where per-hop local cleanup helps, and the honest ceiling (below) says the next move is capping practical chain depth + hierarchical chunking (landmark/hub codes), not further cleanup engineering.

## Honest ceiling

Three independent lines say the same thing, and none of them are optimistic about unlimited chaining:

1. **VSA/resonator theory:** every rigorous capacity-scaling result found (quadratic-in-N operational capacity; cleanup-rule capacity comparisons) is proven under i.i.d./quasi-orthogonal codebooks. The correlated-codebook case is an explicit OPEN question in the source literature itself (Resonator Networks 2 flags it as speculative, possibly even capacity-INCREASING under OLS weights in some settings, but with no general result). Proposed fixes (noise injection at init/iteration, ~50x capacity gain; stochastic thresholding; sparse block-code local factorization) are all validated on random codebooks only -- none has been tested against real semantic correlation structure. There is currently no theorem, in either direction, for our regime.
2. **Predictive-coding / efficient-coding theory:** decorrelation mechanisms (anti-Hebbian plasticity, divisive normalization, predictive residual-forwarding) are normatively optimized to strip INCIDENTAL/statistical redundancy, and multiple results (Chalk/Marre/Tkacik PNAS 2018; V1-preserves-correlation studies) show the brain deliberately PRESERVES informative/semantic correlation rather than removing it -- because removing it would destroy the content. This means there is a principled floor: you cannot decorrelate away genuinely-correlated semantic content without losing the semantics. Any cleanup gate we build should target the incidental/noise component of crosstalk, not try to force semantically-related concepts into near-orthogonal codes wholesale.
3. **Hippocampal attractor-capacity theory:** Treves & Rolls-style CA3 attractor capacity is a hard, quantified cliff (finite patterns per synapse, sharply degrading past a critical load -- the same shape as Hopfield capacity). Transitive-inference literature (Eichenbaum's relational-memory program) shows biological chains are behaviorally reliable only to roughly 4-7 serially-ordered items, with error rates rising with inferential distance (the symbolic-distance effect) even with the full DG/CA3/CA1/theta machinery running. The brain does not achieve unlimited-depth associative chaining.

**Verdict: the brain does not beat the correlated-crosstalk limit in an absolute sense -- it pushes the practical threshold back (via repeated per-hop cleanup targeting incidental noise) while accepting a hard floor set by how semantically correlated the content genuinely is, and it keeps functional chains SHORT, leaning on hierarchical chunking / landmark-hub structure for anything that looks like "long-range" retrieval rather than one long flat associative chain.** For the reader pincer: expect a per-hop cleanup gate to recover real but BOUNDED fidelity at reach=2-3, with diminishing (not free) returns beyond that -- reach>=4-5 on genuinely correlated learned codes should be treated as needing hierarchical chunking/landmarking as a SEPARATE, later mechanism, not as something more cleanup alone will solve.

## Intuitive summary

**What we did:** asked the brain (and the honest math literature) one question -- when your learned memories genuinely overlap in meaning, how do you still chain-retrieve through several of them in a row without the overlap scrambling the answer? We ran four parallel literature searches and got three back with real, cited sources.

**What we found:** everyone's answer has the same shape. Nobody solves this by making all memories maximally different from each other (that would destroy the meaning). Instead, the brain inserts a cleanup checkpoint BETWEEN every retrieval step -- a fresh, sparser "re-sorting" of the signal, plus a check for "is this actually new information or just the same thing I already predicted?" -- and that checkpoint is built to throw away only the accidental noise, never the real overlap that makes concepts meaningfully related in the first place. The best VSA/resonator math literature admits the same gap we hit: its strongest guarantees only hold when items are close to random and unrelated; nobody has solved (or definitively ruled out) the case where the items are genuinely similar, which is exactly our case.

**Why it matters:** this converts Stage-3's dead end into two concrete, buildable ideas for Stage-4 -- a "resort after every hop" gate and a "only pass along what's new" gate -- each with a one-line yes/no test. It also gives us an honest ceiling to pre-register against: even the brain, with all this machinery, only reliably chains about 4-7 steps before errors pile up, and it leans on chunking (grouping into short local chains linked by a few landmark memories) rather than one long chain. So the right bar for Stage-4 success is "meaningfully recovers reach=2-3," not "solves unlimited-depth chaining" -- and if both candidates fail, that failure itself would be a well-grounded result pointing straight at hierarchical chunking as the next mechanism to build, not a reason to keep tuning cleanup.
