## HEADLINE

**GO.** Subject-verb agreement-attraction is a well-precedented, publicly-benchmarked real-text phenomenon that cleanly satisfies both halves of the bar: (a) the surface/nearest-noun heuristic gives the WRONG answer on 40-46% of attractor sentences (Linzen, Dupoux & Goldberg 2016) -- a genuine, large, non-co-extensive conflict, not a rounding error -- and (b) the relational "verb agrees with the syntactic HEAD, not the linearly-nearest noun" feature has been directly shown LEARNABLE from raw token sequences with NO explicit parse given (Goldberg 2019's BERT result: 96-97% accuracy across 1-4 attractors, self-supervised masked-LM only; Hewitt & Manning 2019's structural probe shows tree-like distance structure emerges implicitly, not hand-installed). The frequency/lexical-memorization confound is independently closeable via the Gulordava et al. 2018 nonce-sentence variant (public dataset), which shows structure-sensitive performance survives even when every content word is replaced. **Calibration, not disqualification: the brain itself is NOT a pure hierarchical parser here -- it is a cue-based, content-addressable-memory retrieval system that is PARTIALLY similarity-sensitive by mechanism, not by bug (Wagers, Lau & Phillips 2009), producing attraction errors in a genuine minority of cases (roughly 5-20% in the potent singular-head/plural-attractor direction, near-0-4% in match conditions -- Staub 2009, Franck et al. 2002, Jäger/Engelmann/Vasishth 2017 meta-analysis).** The correct bar is therefore "structure dominates, cue-based/similarity-driven attraction errors occur in a real minority" -- NOT "zero attraction errors." A substrate that shows some human-like attraction-error residual while clearly beating the surface baselines is BRAIN-FAITHFUL, not broken; a substrate showing ZERO attraction sensitivity at all would be over-idealized relative to the human mechanism (an interesting but different finding, not required for HARD-PASS on this bar).

## 1. Brain mechanism (lead)

- **Bock & Miller 1991** (*Cognitive Psychology* 23:45-93), the foundational production study (preamble-completion paradigm): plural attractors ("the key to the cabinets") cause far more agreement errors than singular attractors ("the keys to the cabinet") -- the **attraction asymmetry** (roughly 4-8x in magnitude per the citing literature; exact original per-condition percentages not independently re-extracted this cycle from the primary PDF, flagged). Match conditions run near-floor. Their account: markedness -- plural is the actively-specified, "leakable" feature; singular is an unmarked default that rarely intrudes. Conclusion: attraction is not simple linear-distance decay (constituency matters, not just proximity) but also not perfectly hierarchical (some effect survives regardless). Very well-replicated (Bock & Cutting 1992; Bock, Eberhard & Cutting 1999; dozens since).
- **Eberhard 1999 / Eberhard, Bock & Cutting 2005** ("Marking and Morphing," *Psych. Review* 112(3):531-559): subject number is computed via a graded "notional number" (message-level, real-world plurality/distributivity) that "morphs" into the discrete grammatical feature used at production. This is why attraction tracks CONCEPTUAL number (distributive/collective readings of the attractor) not just its surface morphology. Well-replicated qualitatively; the precise quantitative morph-function is one-lab.
- **Wagers, Lau & Phillips 2009** (*J. Mem. Lang.* 61(2):206-237), the comprehension side: self-paced-reading/eye-tracking shows a **grammaticality asymmetry** -- a plural attractor produces an illusion of grammaticality (reduced disruption) specifically in UNGRAMMATICAL sentences ("...cabinets are rusty"), with no corresponding illusion in grammatical sentences. Their account, and the one load-bearing for our design decision: agreement checking at the verb is a **cue-based, content-addressable memory retrieval** operation -- cues are broadcast in parallel and a partially-matching memory chunk (the attractor) can be mistakenly retrieved instead of the true subject when correct retrieval fails. This makes the retrieval step *itself* similarity/cue-overlap-sensitive, operating ALONGSIDE (not instead of) correct structural representation-building. Very well-replicated (Dillon et al. 2013 ERP; cross-linguistic replications). Contested: whether attraction is mis-*retrieval* (dominant view) vs. mis-*representation* at encoding (still actively argued, e.g. representation-distortion accounts).
- **Franck, Vigliocco & Nicol 2002** (*Lang. Cogn. Process.* 17(4):371-404): nested-PP design dissociating hierarchical depth from linear distance -- the hierarchically-closer attractor (N2) produces a reliably larger mismatch effect than the more deeply-embedded one (N3), in both English and French, supporting a genuinely structural (not purely linear) component. **Directly contested**: a later replication/argument ("Against Structural Constraints in Subject-Verb Agreement Production," PMC4528622) reports the same data pattern is captured by a linear "scope of planning" account without positing true hierarchy, and finds no dissociation once certain confounds are controlled. Flag this as a live, unresolved debate in the field -- do not treat hierarchical-distance as settled.
- **Synthesis / correct bar**: best characterization is **(b) partly cue-based/content-addressable retrieval that is natively similarity-sensitive, layered on a structural computation** -- this is the dominant current view (Wagers et al. 2009; Dillon et al. 2013; Jäger, Engelmann & Vasishth 2017 Bayesian meta-analysis, small but reliable facilitatory interference effect, ~-7ms [-16,4] pooled). Human error-rate magnitude: near-floor (0-4%) in match/baseline conditions; the potent singular-head + plural-attractor mismatch condition runs roughly **5-20%** depending on materials (Staub 2009 Exp. 1: ~4% match vs. >18% mismatch; Franck et al. replications pool near 5% raw overall). **This is the number that calibrates our substrate's pass bar: humans are wrong a clear minority of the time, never near 50%, and the failure mechanism is itself partly similarity-driven -- so a substrate with a comparable modest residual error is not failing the brain-check, it is passing it.**

## 2. Is it a genuine surface-vs-structure conflict? (quantified)

**Yes, cleanly.** Linzen, Dupoux & Goldberg 2016 (TACL, arXiv:1611.01368; dataset/code public at http://tallinzen.net/projects/lstm_agreement) built ~1.35M subject-verb number-prediction instances from Wikipedia dependencies (121.5K train / 13.5K valid / 1.21M test). Key numbers:
- **Nearest-noun-number heuristic**: ~4.2-4.5% error overall (most sentences have no attractor so nearest noun = true subject) but **40-46.4% error specifically on attractor sentences** -- i.e. near-chance exactly where it is supposed to fail by construction. This is the number that establishes the surface heuristic gives the WRONG label on close to half of attractor cases -- a genuine, large, non-co-extensive conflict.
- **Majority-class baseline**: 32% error (32% of subjects are plural), a weaker unconditional reference point.
- **Supervised task-specific LSTM** (given explicit training signal on this exact sub-task): 0.4% error at 0 attractors, rising only to 17.6% error at 4 attractors -- degrading but staying far above chance.
- **Same LSTM used as a raw language model** (no task-specific supervision, next-word probability only): 7% error at 0 attractors -> 33% at 1 -> **70% error at 4 attractors (worse than chance)**. This is itself a second, harder surface-adjacent baseline any "learned structure" claim must also beat, not just nearest-noun.
- Marvin & Linzen 2018 (EMNLP, arXiv:1808.09031) targeted minimal pairs: LSTM LM accuracy drops to 0.50-0.61 (near chance) on long-distance/attractor-laden constructions (across a PP, across a subject/object-relative clause) versus 0.90-0.99 on local agreement; their n-gram baseline is "very close to 50%" on these items; humans score 0.82-0.88 on the same non-local items (i.e. humans are far from chance but also far from ceiling -- consistent with item 1's error-rate calibration).

## 3. Frequency/similarity confound + the Gulordava nonce variant

Gulordava, Bojanowski, Grave, Linzen & Baroni 2018 ("Colorless Green Recurrent Networks Dream Hierarchically," NAACL, aclanthology.org/N18-1108; code/data public at https://github.com/facebookresearch/colorlessgreenRNNs, includes human judgment data) substitute every content word (noun, verb, adjective, proper noun, numeral, adverb) with another content word matched for POS and morphology, leaving function words untouched and syntax fully intact -- producing "colorless green ideas"-style sentences that are semantically empty but frequency/collocation-neutral. This is exactly the right tool to CLEANLY guarantee condition (a) of the bar without any residual worry that a win reflects memorized lexical co-occurrence rather than abstract structure: if the model still tracks agreement when the words themselves carry no real-world meaning or learned collocational glue, the only thing left for it to exploit is structural position. **Key finding**: LSTM accuracy drops only modestly from real to nonce sentences (Italian 92.1%->85.5%; English 81.0%->74.1%; Hebrew 94.7%->80.8%; Russian 96.1%->88.8%) -- staying well above the 50% chance floor in every language. Human Italian speakers show a comparably-sized drop (94.5%->~85-88%), suggesting humans and the model rely on structure to a similar degree, not that the model is a frequency-lookup table wearing a syntax costume. Recommendation: use this as a CROSS-CHECK sub-split layered on the primary real-attractor-sentence design (item 6), not as the sole data source -- the nonce variant is the confound-killer, the real-sentence corpus is the larger, more natural, richer-effect-size primary evidence base.

## 4. Learnability / hand-built-vs-learned crux (most important for us)

This is the crux that must not be re-tripped (the prior trap: hand-building the relational feature = giving the parse = cosmetic "learning"). The evidence says the head-tracking feature is **learnable from raw sequences without an explicit parse**, though the strength of this claim depends on model scale/inductive bias:
- **Goldberg 2019** ("Assessing BERT's Syntactic Abilities," arXiv:1901.05287): BERT -- given ONLY raw tokens, self-supervised masked-LM objective, no parse or POS features -- reaches **96-97% accuracy essentially flat across 1-4 attractors** on Linzen-style stimuli, and 80-83% on Gulordava's nonce sentences (versus the LSTM's 74.1%). This is the single strongest piece of evidence that the relational feature is acquirable from data alone, not merely encodable once handed a tree.
- **Hewitt & Manning 2019** ("A Structural Probe for Finding Syntax in Word Representations," NAACL): a linear transform of BERT/ELMo embeddings recovers parse-tree distances -- i.e. tree-like structure emerges IMPLICITLY from self-supervised pretraining, it is not injected. This is the direct answer to "is it learned or hand-built": the structure is a byproduct of learning from data, extractable after the fact, not stipulated beforehand.
- **McCoy, Frank & Linzen 2020** ("Does Syntax Need to Grow on Trees?," TACL): explicit tree-structured architectures generalize hierarchically MORE RELIABLY than plain sequential RNNs -- but plain sequence models are NOT categorically incapable, they just need stronger inductive bias/more data to get there reliably. This frames the minimal-sufficient-scaffolding question as a matter of DEGREE (positional/sequential encoding + enough training exposure) rather than an absolute requirement for explicit tree input.
- **Linzen & Baroni 2021** (review, *Annual Review of Linguistics*): surveys this exact question across the above and concludes sequence/attention models CAN acquire substantial syntax from raw text with no explicit trees, imperfectly, with known failure modes under complexity (e.g. Marvin & Linzen's degraded long-distance cases for weaker models).
- **Caveat for our design (important, not in the sub-agent reports, my own flag)**: the strongest positive evidence (BERT) comes from a large-scale, richly-pretrained transformer -- a different regime from a small glass-box substrate's current learning mechanism. The McCoy/Frank/Linzen degree-not-kind framing is the relevant hedge: a smaller/weaker learner may need more explicit structural inductive bias (though NOT a hand-fed parse per se -- e.g. positional/order features, or a role-binding mechanism already in the substrate) to reach the same result. This is exactly the same tension flagged in the sibling fork-B note (does a small meta-learned operator behave like the free-algebra signature or like the content-dependent one) -- both forks bottom out on the same open question: at OUR scale, does the existing (or a modestly-augmented) learning mechanism clear this bar, or does it need the fixed-FHRR-style free-algebra shortcut that's already been shown NOT to satisfy the "learned, not given" property?

## 5. Fair-baseline design

Ranked baselines that must be beaten for a win to be attributable to learned structure, not surface cues:
- **(i) Nearest-noun-number / last-noun-before-verb** (these coincide in the standard construction -- verify identical operationalization in our split): **~40-46.4% error on the attractor subset** (Linzen 2016) -- **this is the STRONGEST, most standard surface baseline and the one that must be clearly beaten.** Near-chance specifically where the design targets it.
- **(ii) n-gram / frequency-only LM baseline**: ~50% (chance) on non-local/attractor items (Marvin & Linzen 2018); also relevant is Linzen's own finding that an LM-objective-only LSTM (no task supervision) degrades to 70% error at 4 attractors -- WORSE than chance. This is a second, independently-failing surface baseline any claimed win must clear, not just (i).
- **(iii) majority-class baseline**: 32% error unconditional -- a weak reference point, easy to beat, not diagnostic on its own; include but do not rely on it.
- **(iv) any-noun-agrees / vote-of-all-preceding-nouns**: not directly reported in the literature reviewed; flagged as a heuristic that could accidentally recover the correct subject in short sentences with few nouns (since "any noun matches" is a permissive OR-heuristic) -- must be sanity-checked separately in our design to make sure it isn't a hidden loophole baseline that looks structural but is actually just "guess whichever noun matches, and one usually does."
- **STRONGEST baseline to beat**: (i) nearest-noun/last-noun-before-verb at ~40-46% error on the conflict subset, AND (ii) the frequency/n-gram-only baseline at ~50%/chance -- both must be cleared, not just one.

## 6. GO / NO-GO + minimal design

**GO.** Recommend agreement-attraction (real Linzen corpus as primary, Gulordava nonce as a confound-killing cross-check) as the real-text relational-conflict LEARNING testbed for the named bar.

Minimal fair design:
- **Data**: Linzen, Dupoux & Goldberg 2016 Wikipedia agreement corpus, public at http://tallinzen.net/projects/lstm_agreement (primary; large, natural, well-characterized difficulty axis via attractor count 0-4). Cross-check sub-split: Gulordava et al. 2018 nonce sentences, public at https://github.com/facebookresearch/colorlessgreenRNNs (rules out lexical-memorization as an alternative explanation for any win).
- **Conflict split**: filter to sentences with >=1 attractor where nearest-noun-number != correct verb number (this is already the corpus's native attractor definition -- no new construction needed). Report performance binned by attractor count (0/1/2/3/4) matching the established Linzen/Goldberg reporting convention, so results are directly comparable to the published LSTM/BERT numbers.
- **Fair baselines to beat, both**: (i) nearest-noun-number heuristic on the conflict subset (~40-46% error to beat) and (ii) an n-gram/frequency-only baseline (~50%/chance to beat) -- per item 5.
- **The single variable that isolates LEARNED structure**: do NOT give the substrate a parse, dependency tree, or any hand-coded "syntactic head" feature -- feed it the SAME raw token sequence the baselines get. Whatever the substrate's current learning mechanism is (frequency-weighted collocation learner, role-binding/relational learner, or a to-be-added meta-learned operator per the sibling fork-B note) must be the thing that discovers, from many labeled agreement examples, that predictive weight belongs on the noun in subject-head position rather than the linearly-nearest noun. Use a held-out split by novel lexical items/attractor configurations (compound-divergence-style, reusing the same split discipline as the sibling fork-B note's harness) to rule out memorization rather than generalization.
- **Calibration note carried into the design**: do not require zero attraction-error for a PASS -- a modest (roughly 5-20%, human-comparable) residual error rate on the hardest (singular-head + plural-attractor) condition is BRAIN-FAITHFUL, not a bug, per item 1. The PASS criterion is "clearly beats both surface baselines," not "matches ceiling."
- **NO-GO fallback (not recommended, but named per the bar's own requirement)**: if agreement-attraction turns out to be infeasible to wire into the substrate's current input representation, the next-best real-text structural-conflict phenomena are reflexive/pronoun binding (Principle A/B licensing, e.g. "John said that Bill hurt himself/him" -- correct antecedent choice conflicts with linear proximity in some configurations) or filler-gap/wh-extraction dependencies (island effects). Both are covered, alongside subject-verb agreement itself, in a single public multi-phenomenon benchmark suite (Warstadt et al. 2020, "BLiMP: The Benchmark of Linguistic Minimal Pairs for English," github.com/alexwarstadt/blimp -- 67 paradigms including anaphor agreement, binding, filler-gap, island effects, subject-verb agreement, NPI licensing -- **this citation is recalled from background knowledge, NOT independently re-verified via WebFetch this cycle; flag as MODERATE confidence, well-known resource, verify before relying on its exact numbers**). Agreement-attraction is preferred over these alternatives because it has by far the most mature, publicly-available, numerically-characterized benchmark infrastructure (Linzen/Gulordava/Marvin-Linzen/Goldberg all report exact, comparable numbers) and the clearest brain-mechanism grounding (Wagers/Lau/Phillips cue-based retrieval account directly informs the calibration bar) -- reflexive-binding and filler-gap phenomena are real alternatives but with thinner, less numerically-precise prior art reviewed this cycle.

## Falsifiable predictions

- **HARD-PASS**: on held-out attractor sentences (>=1 attractor, nearest-noun-number != correct number, held out by novel lexical items/attractor configurations not seen in training), the substrate's learned mechanism -- given ONLY raw token sequences, no parse/head feature -- beats BOTH the nearest-noun baseline (~40-46% error) AND the frequency/n-gram-only baseline (~50%/chance) by a wide, statistically clear margin (e.g. >=15-20 percentage points over the stronger baseline), with degradation across attractor count (0-4) that stays well above chance at 4 attractors (per Linzen's supervised-LSTM reference point of 17.6% error, not the LM-only 70% error collapse). A modest (5-20%) residual error rate specifically on the hardest singular-head+plural-attractor condition is EXPECTED and consistent with PASS (brain-comparable cue-based retrieval sensitivity), not a disqualifier.
- **HARD-FAIL**: the substrate's learned mechanism ties or underperforms the nearest-noun/frequency baselines on the conflict subset (predictive weight tracks linear recency, not syntactic role) -- meaning the relational "head-tracks-verb" feature was NOT learned by the current mechanism from raw sequences, and would require either an explicit parse feature (reintroducing the hand-built-relational-feature trap this whole bar exists to avoid) or a materially different/stronger learning mechanism.
- **MIDDLE**: beats nearest-noun but not the frequency/n-gram baseline (or vice versa), or shows the LM-only-style collapse pattern (error rate scaling toward or past chance by 3-4 attractors rather than staying in the supervised-LSTM's ~18% range) -- inconclusive; would indicate partial structure-sensitivity without robust generalization, and the design likely needs either more training signal or a harder held-out split to have discriminating power.
