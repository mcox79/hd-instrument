## HEADLINE

**Two literatures (brain, graph-science) independently converge on the same structural claim: routing a long path through intermediate hub/landmark waypoints, rather than aiming continuously at a distant final target, is a real and well-precedented mechanism for exactly the failure mode just observed (a target-similarity signal that is too weak/noisy at long range to pick the right next step). The graph-science side has this as a mature, quantified engineering discipline (ALT, hub labeling, transit-node routing, highway hierarchies) built specifically to fix "greedy toward a distant target degrades" — it is NOT folklore, it is the reason production shortest-path engines exist. The brain side has a genuine, well-replicated direct-recording result for the adjacent claim (branch/choice points get special deliberative treatment: VTE + hippocampal forward-sweeps, Johnson & Redish 2007) plus one strong human fMRI analog for the landmark-as-subgoal claim itself (Balaguer et al. 2016, hierarchical subway-network planning via interchange/hub stations) — but the single most direct rodent single-unit test of "do place cells explicitly tag hub/gateway nodes" came back NEGATIVE (Duvelle et al. 2021, Current Biology): hippocampus encodes a single global reference frame, not a doorway/gateway-segmented one, despite rats behaviorally exploiting the connectivity. Both lit-scans also converge on the SAME honest caveat, independently: the mechanism's benefit is not free — it degrades to baseline (or worse) when landmarks are poorly chosen, symmetric/tied, or when the underlying graph lacks real bottleneck structure (a near-complete or expander-like graph). This is a substantive, falsifiable risk, not a footnote — it is the graph-science literature's own stated failure mode for the exact class of algorithm being proposed.**

**Net read: this is a well-precedented UPGRADE to try (2 of 3 angles converge cleanly on the mechanism; the brain angle converges on "choice points deserve special treatment" more strongly than on "hippocampus literally tags hub nodes"), but it is not risk-free, and its own failure mode is diagnosable cheaply BEFORE spending a build cycle (measure the KB graph's degree distribution / clustering coefficient — a hub-and-spoke graph predicts this helps; a near-uniform-degree, low-clustering graph predicts it won't, mirroring the plain-greedy result almost exactly).**

## The brain mechanism (honest convergence, explicit disagreement noted)

1. **Boundary-vector cells (BVCs) and landmark-vector cells (LVCs)** encode egocentric vectors to nearby boundaries/discrete landmarks (Lever, Burton, Jeewajee, O'Keefe & Burgess, *J Neurosci* 2009; iScience 2025 on LVCs persisting without direct visual input) — a nearby, locally-anchored reference is a basic, early-developing computational primitive (present in pre-weanling rat pups per Nat Comms 2024), not something built up only for long-range planning.
2. **But a separate, direct population-level goal-vector-field converging on a distant, currently-invisible goal ALSO exists** (honeycomb-maze CA1 recording, Nature 2022) — the brain does not exclusively decompose into landmark legs; direct-to-distant-goal vector coding coexists with landmark/boundary coding, likely at different scales/task regimes. **This is the single most important honest caveat from the brain angle: do not oversell "brain always routes via landmarks, never aims at the distant goal" — both mechanisms are directly observed.**
3. **Choice points get special, well-replicated deliberative treatment** — vicarious trial-and-error (VTE): rats pause and orient back-and-forth at T-junctions (Tolman 1930s; Muenzinger 1938), and Johnson & Redish (2007, *J Neurosci*) directly recorded hippocampal place-cell sequences sweeping ahead down each branch option in alternation during the pause, before commitment — a direct neural correlate of "trying out" each branch. van der Meer & Redish (2009/2010) tie this to striatal reward-prediction signals; Papale et al. (2016, *Neuron*) tie it to sharp-wave ripples. This is the single strongest, least-disputed piece of direct evidence that branch/bottleneck points (the biological analog of a graph node with multiple real neighbors) are computationally special — though it is about *deliberation at a branch*, not explicitly "landmark-as-subgoal."
4. **Human fMRI hierarchical-planning evidence for landmark-as-subgoal specifically**: Balaguer, Spiers, Hassabis & Summerfield (2016, *Neuron*) — subjects planning routes in a simulated subway network represent the plan as a sequence of line-segments plus interchange/hub stations, not a flat state-by-state plan; full flat planning was measurably more costly and not what the brain did. This is the closest direct match to the working hypothesis found anywhere in the 3-angle scan, though it is fMRI+behavior, not single-unit.
5. **DISCONFIRMING direct evidence**: Duvelle, Grieves, Liu et al. (2021, *Current Biology*) — rats behaviorally exploited doorway connectivity in a 4-room environment, but hippocampal place cells did NOT preferentially encode the doorways/gateways themselves; place cells coded a single global reference frame. If a hub/gateway representation exists, it is not (at least not simply) in hippocampal place cells. A modeling response (bioRxiv 2022, gateway-identity attractor model) attempts to reconcile this but is itself unconfirmed, not new data.
6. Janzen & van Turennout (2004, *Nat Neurosci*) and follow-ups: human parahippocampal gyrus selectively (and implicitly, even without conscious recall) tags objects located AT decision points versus equally-salient non-decision objects — landmark salience is concentrated at branch points, consistent with (but not proof of) landmark-as-subgoal.

## The graph/network-science + HRL mechanism (mature, quantified, convergent)

1. **This is a solved, production-grade engineering discipline for exactly this failure mode.** ALT (A*, Landmarks, Triangle-inequality; Goldberg & Harrelson 2005) precomputes distances from a small landmark set once, then uses the triangle inequality as an O(1) admissible heuristic at query time — this directly formalizes "aim at a nearby, precomputed reference instead of the raw distant target," and is the closest formal analog of the proposed substrate mechanism. Hub labeling (Cohen, Delling, Pajor & Werneck 2011; Abraham et al.) proves that any shortest path between two nodes passes through at least one shared hub in their precomputed labels. Contraction hierarchies, highway hierarchies (~2000x over Dijkstra), and transit-node routing (a further ~2 orders of magnitude, "millions of times faster than Dijkstra" on continental road networks) are the mature hierarchy of "collapse a long path into a short landmark-to-landmark sequence" — this exists specifically because "aim continuously at the distant target" (plain Dijkstra/greedy) does not scale.
2. **HRL bottleneck/landmark-subgoal discovery is the direct RL-native analog**: McGovern & Barto (2001, diverse density), Şimşek & Barto (2004/2005/2008, betweenness-centrality "access states" — the closest classical prior-art match to "precompute high-betweenness hub nodes as subgoals"), Machado, Bellemare & Bowling (2017, graph-Laplacian eigenoptions, spectral analog of the same idea). Modern (2019-2023) continuations directly relevant here: Eysenbach, Salakhutdinov & Levine (2019, "Search on the Replay Buffer") builds a graph over visited states and does explicit shortest-path search over intermediate waypoints rather than acting greedily toward the final goal — qualitatively the best-quantified precedent that graph-search-over-real-intermediate-states beats a raw goal-conditioned/greedy policy on long-horizon tasks. Kim et al. (2021, HIGL) and DHRL (2022) explicitly train a high-level policy to target sampled "landmark" states rather than the raw final goal.
3. **The convergent, explicitly-stated failure modes (this is the honest risk section, independently confirmed by two literatures):**
   - Landmark **selection quality is not free** — random landmark selection gives a weak heuristic that degenerates toward the no-landmark baseline; farthest-point or centrality-based selection is required for the benefit to materialize (ALT literature).
   - **Symmetric/tied regions cause landmark-distance degeneracy** — "multiple nodes may have the same distances to the same landmark set," stalling discrimination between candidate next-hops (the direct graph-science analog of the substrate's own already-diagnosed branch-point tie problem from the sibling-test drill).
   - **No clean bottlenecks (dense/expander-like graphs) makes the whole technique vacuous** — this entire class of method (hub labeling, highway hierarchies, betweenness-subgoal discovery) depends on the graph having hierarchical, non-expander structure (a small set of edges dominates long paths, as in road networks). In a graph close to a random expander, betweenness is nearly uniform across nodes and no landmark is meaningfully better than any other — the benefit should be expected to vanish, not just shrink.
   - Preprocessing/space cost is a real trade-off (heavier for the biggest speedups), though for a one-time offline KB precompute this is a minor concern relative to the failure-mode risks above.

## Convergent mechanism (>=2 angles agree)

- **Landmark/hub decomposition of long routes beats flat continuous aiming at a distant target.** Graph-science (strong, mature, quantified — this is the literal reason ALT/hub-labeling/transit-node-routing exist) + brain (Balaguer et al. 2016 human hierarchical subway planning) converge. 2/3 angles, one of them (graph-science) load-bearing-strength.
- **Branch/decision points deserve special computational treatment, distinct from ordinary hops.** Brain (VTE + Johnson & Redish 2007 forward-sweep, well-replicated) + graph-science (betweenness-centrality access-state subgoal discovery, McGovern/Şimşek-Barto/Machado) converge. This is effectively the same claim as the substrate's own already-diagnosed branch-point tie-degeneracy risk from the sibling-test drill, now confirmed from two MORE independent literatures — a third, cross-thread confirmation of the same underdetermination mechanism.
- **Landmark identity is not automatically "free" or given by the graph structure alone — it must be actively, correctly selected, and can fail silently if chosen poorly or if the graph lacks structure to exploit.** Graph-science (random-vs-centrality selection gap, expander-graph vacuity) + brain (Duvelle et al.'s null result — hippocampal place cells do NOT automatically/for-free tag gateway nodes, implying landmark-identification is not a trivial byproduct of ordinary spatial coding, consistent with needing an explicit, separate precompute/selection step) converge on this being a genuine, non-trivial sub-problem, not an afterthought.
- **Honest non-convergence, explicitly reported rather than papered over:** the brain angle does NOT support "always route via landmarks, exclusively" — direct-to-distant-goal vector coding is also directly observed (honeycomb-maze goal-vector-field, Nature 2022) and coexists with landmark/boundary coding. The proposed substrate design (below) mirrors this honestly: it falls back to direct goal-conditioning once close enough to the goal, rather than claiming landmark-mediation should replace direct goal-conditioning universally.

## ONE concrete substrate realization

**Flat, single-skeleton landmark-mediated routing, reusing only the two already-certified primitives — no new mechanism invented:**

1. **One-time landmark precompute (offline, cheap):** run the existing local-neighborhood-scoping primitive over every node once, count degree (`|neighbors|`), take the top-K nodes by degree as the landmark set. This is the degree-proxy escalation-path default — start here, not with betweenness. If the leg-length diagnostic below shows the degree-proxy landmark set is too sparse, escalate to sampled/approximate betweenness (repeated BFS-style expansion using the same existing primitive, tally which nodes recur most on sampled shortest-path frontiers) — still a one-time offline cost, still built entirely from the existing primitive, no new operator.
2. **Per-query landmark-skeleton construction (not per-hop re-nomination):** given current node C and final goal G, apply the already-certified goal-conditioning primitive with the CANDIDATE POOL restricted to the landmark set (instead of raw neighbors) and goal=G — argmax gives waypoint landmark L1. This is literally the same MM mechanism already certified for supplied-waypoints, just with a different candidate pool.
3. **Leg-walk:** walk C -> L1 using the EXACT already-certified supplied-waypoint mechanism verbatim (local-neighborhood scoping + goal-conditioning cosine-argmax), with L1 substituted for the externally-supplied waypoint. On reaching (or entering the local neighborhood of) L1, either terminate (walk directly to G if no remaining landmark beats direct cosine-to-G) or repeat step 2 among unvisited landmarks for L2.
4. **Recursion policy:** build the FLAT, 2-leg-capped version first (start -> L1 -> goal, or start -> L1 -> L2 -> goal). A well-chosen landmark set should place most nodes within short hop-distance of their nearest landmark by construction, so each leg should individually sit inside the SAME short-range regime where the supplied-waypoint mechanism already measures 0.499 — genuine multi-level recursion (sub-landmarks within a still-too-long leg) is a real possibility but should be a gated SECOND iteration, triggered only if the leg-length diagnostic in the risk section below says it's needed, not built preemptively.

This is structurally the ALT pattern (precomputed landmarks + triangle-inequality-style routing) transplanted from metric shortest-path search onto cosine-similarity-conditioned graph walks, and it is simultaneously the graph-native realization of the Balaguer et al. hierarchical-planning finding (route via hub/interchange stations, not a flat state-by-state plan) and the McGovern/Şimşek-Barto betweenness-subgoal HRL lineage. Zero new primitives; reuses local-neighborhood-scoping and goal-conditioning verbatim, just re-targeted onto a landmark instead of the final goal, then chained.

## Intuitive summary

**What we did:** the last experiment showed that having the substrate figure out its own route step-by-step by always aiming at the final, distant goal works better than random guessing, but falls well short of what's possible when someone hands it the correct halfway point. We asked: what if, instead of aiming at the far-off destination the whole way, the substrate picked a nearby, well-connected "hub" location first, walked there, and only then worried about the next leg? We checked this against how real brains navigate and how modern route-finding software (the kind behind GPS and maps) actually works.

**What we found:** GPS-style routing software solves EXACTLY this problem, and solves it by precomputing a small set of "hub" locations once, then routing trip-by-trip through the nearest useful hub instead of computing the whole trip in one shot — this is not a hunch, it is the reason modern route planners are fast at all. Human brains do something similar when planning complicated routes (a well-known subway-navigation study found people mentally chunk a route into "get to this hub station, then get to that one" rather than planning the whole trip in one flat pass). But we found an important, honest wrinkle: real brains ALSO have a completely separate way of aiming straight at a far-off, invisible goal, and a very careful rat study specifically checked "do brain cells specially mark the hub locations themselves" and found NO — they don't, at least not in the obvious place researchers expected. So hub-routing is a real, well-supported strategy, but it's not automatically free; something has to actually pick good hubs, in the brain and in our system alike.

**The plan we're proposing:** pick a handful of well-connected "hub" nodes once (cheap, one-time step, using tools we already have), then have the substrate walk toward the nearest useful hub first instead of the distant goal, switching to the next hub (or straight to the goal, once close) as it goes — reusing, unchanged, the exact same "which real neighbor looks most like my target" logic that already works well when handed a target directly. Nothing new is invented; we're just giving that existing logic a closer, easier target to aim at, one leg at a time.

**Why it matters, honestly:** if this works, it should recover most of the gap between "does okay on its own" and "does great with help," using tools we already built. But there's a real, checkable risk: if our underlying knowledge map turns out to be more like a tangled web with no obvious hub-and-spoke structure (rather than something with a few clearly important, well-connected nodes), this whole strategy could buy nothing at all — and the honest, useful thing to do is check which kind of map we actually have BEFORE spending effort building the hub-routing mechanism, not after.
