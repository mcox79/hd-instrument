## (a) HEADLINE

**The loop splits cleanly into two halves with opposite evidentiary status, and this split is itself the finding.** CONTENT growth via a scaffolded read→gap-detect→lookup→verify→bind loop is well-precedented and buildable: Share's self-teaching mechanism (1995, *Cognition*) shows a single successful decode installs a durable, item-specific orthographic trace with no external teacher needed for that item, and the bootstrapping literature (Snedeker/Gleitman/Brent 1999; Fisher, Jin & Scott 2019) independently shows how a small externally/observationally-seeded content store (concrete nouns, learned via cross-situational word-to-world mapping — no syntax required) becomes the substrate later rule-mechanisms lean on. **But RULES improving *from content growth alone* is the weak-to-negative half of the evidence, not merely under-evidenced:** explicit correction has a famously weak-to-absent effect on children's grammatical rule system (Brown & Hanlon 1970; Marcus 1993 — the classic "no negative evidence" finding); bare dictionary lookup often fails to install even usable item-level competence, let alone transferable skill (Miller & Gildea 1987); and the strongest empirical claim that would support a genuinely *compounding, accelerating* content→rules loop at the system level — the "Matthew effect" as a longitudinally *widening* achievement gap — is **not supported by 25+ years of direct empirical tests** (Pfost, Hattie, Dörfler & Artelt 2014, *Rev. Educ. Res.*, the field's major systematic review, found no consistent fan-spread pattern; Protopapas, Sideridis, Mouzaki & Simos 2011 directly failed to replicate it in a 5-wave longitudinal study). Where rule-level generalization DOES demonstrably improve from scaffolding, it is via **explicit strategy instruction with practice and feedback** (reciprocal teaching — Palincsar & Brown 1984), not from passive content accumulation, and even there the far-transfer effect is real but roughly a third the size of the near-transfer effect (Rosenshine & Meister 1994 meta-analysis: d=0.32 far-transfer vs d=0.88 near-transfer). The ML self-training/model-collapse literature gives the mechanistic reason this asymmetry should be *expected*: a fully closed, self-referential loop (rules updating from the loop's own generated/accumulated content, with no independent anchor) is the exact structural condition under which collapse/confirmation-bias/catastrophic-forgetting occurs across every domain checked (Shumailov et al. 2024, *Nature*; Alemohammad et al. 2023 ICLR; Gupta, Rao & Anumanchipalli 2024 ACL Findings on ROME/MEMIT catastrophic forgetting) — and is reliably *prevented* only by an independent, non-self-generated signal each round (fresh/accumulated real data — Gerstgrasser et al. 2024; or a verification gate external to the content being added — Wei et al. 2021's expansion-assumption theory of safe self-training). **This is good news for what we've already built** (the reliability/common-mode/metacognition self-monitoring layer is precisely this kind of independent-of-the-loop verification signal) **and bad news for the specific claim that content growth, by itself, sharpens parsing/combinatorial rules** — no literature in either domain (human or ML) shows that mechanism working; every case where "rules" (grammar, comprehension-monitoring strategy, model parameters) demonstrably improved required either (i) an independent structural anchor not itself produced by the loop (cross-situational noun seed; phonological-recoding-correctness as its own verification; fresh real data), or (ii) direct, explicit, feedback-supervised training on the rule-task itself (reciprocal teaching, curriculum learning) — never passive accumulation of more correct content.

## (b) Cheap decisive test

**Question:** does a content-growth loop (gap-detect → lookup → verify → bind, already designed in the active-learning companion note) measurably sharpen a RULE-LEVEL task the loop was never directly trained on — the substrate analog of "does knowing more nouns make verb-argument-structure disambiguation better," which is the actual empirical content of syntactic bootstrapping?

**Substrate framing note (load-bearing, not a caveat to skip):** per the standing memory anchor that the reasoning/binding free-algebra is CLOSED (fixed, brain-shared, not something that grows from data), "rules improving" in this substrate cannot mean "new combinatorial operators appear." The correct substrate analog of what syntactic bootstrapping actually claims — content narrows/selects among an *already-fixed* structural hypothesis space (which of several possible argument-role assignments is correct for this verb-context) — is **selection accuracy among fixed structural options**, e.g. the existing thematic-fit / affectedness / dependency-typed-role disambiguation tasks from the same-day companion drills, NOT growth of new binding capacity. This reframing is itself a finding of this drill, not an assumption.

**Design (three arms, one held-out eval set, same base reader, same codebook-build pipeline as the existing 0.927-AUC baseline):**

- **Arm A — content-frozen baseline:** the codebook is frozen at its current build. Measure baseline accuracy on the held-out rule-level task (thematic-fit/argument-role disambiguation, reusing the existing frozen yardstick from `research_plausibility_web_engineering_resources_adoptable_foundation_2026-07-20.md`).
- **Arm B — content-growth-only (self-teaching analog):** run N cycles of gap-detect (existing conformal/Chow abstain) → internal-retrieve-first → external lookup against the already-scoped static resources (VerbNet/PropBank/SemLink role lookup, ATOMIC-2020 outcome tuples) → reliability/common-mode-gated verify → bind into the codebook. Measure (i) codebook word-similarity/analogy generalization AUC (the existing content-only metric, should replicate the self-teaching per-item mechanism: does verified lookup content install a durable, usable trace) and (ii) — the decisive measurement — the SAME held-out rule-level task from Arm A, WITHOUT any direct supervision on that task. This isolates whether content growth alone moves rule-level selection accuracy (the syntactic-bootstrapping-analog signature) or leaves it flat (the fast-mapping/syntactic-bootstrapping SEPARABLE-STAGE finding, in which content acquisition and rule refinement are documented as distinct, non-transferring mechanisms).
- **Arm C — multi-generation loop (model-collapse stress test):** iterate B across several generations (bind → re-derive codebook statistics → gap-detect again on new material → lookup → bind again), tracking both metrics across generations, WITH and WITHOUT the reliability/common-mode gate active. This directly reproduces the Alemohammad/Shumailov self-consuming-loop paradigm in-house.

**Seed-ablation (minimum-seed test, run alongside):** repeat Arm B starting from codebooks of varying pre-existing size/coverage (near-empty vs partial vs full), to test for a Decoding-Threshold-Hypothesis-style (Wang, Sabatini, O'Reilly & Weeks) discontinuity: below some minimum existing content coverage, does the loop fail to bootstrap at all (gap-detect fires on everything, or lookups return content that can't bind onto anything existing), producing a genuine THRESHOLD rather than a smooth improvement curve?

**Must-fail / ablation controls (mandatory, reusing the active-learning note's template plus two new ones specific to this drill):**
1. **Randomized-lookup control** (from the companion note): bind real-but-unrelated text instead of genuine lookups — Arm B's content-AUC gain must vanish; if it doesn't, the win is noise-averaging (the already-refuted "more corpus" pattern), not genuine content acquisition.
2. **Ungated-lookup control:** disable the reliability/common-mode gate — Arm B/C should show WORSE outcomes on a bad-source-injected subset than the gated version (reuses the active-learning note's must-fail control #2).
3. **Content-frozen vs content-growing on the rule-task (the crux control):** Arm A must be run on the IDENTICAL held-out rule-task items as Arm B — if B does not beat A by a pre-registered margin, content growth did not sharpen rules, full stop; this is not an edge case to explain away, per the literature's own prediction that this is the single most likely outcome.
4. **Ungated multi-generation drift (Arm C, gate OFF):** must show measurable degradation (AUC or rule-task accuracy declining, or content diversity narrowing) across generations, reproducing model collapse in-house — if it does NOT degrade even without the gate, the "the gate is load-bearing for loop safety" claim from the active-learning note is undermined and needs re-examination.
5. **Seed-ablation smoothness check:** if the near-empty-codebook condition performs indistinguishably from the full-codebook condition (no threshold), this refutes the minimum-seed hypothesis for THIS substrate — a genuinely informative negative (the substrate's fixed algebra may not need the same from-scratch bootstrapping seed a human syntax-learner needs), not a failure to explain away.

## (c) Falsifiable predictions

**HARD-PASS** (the co-growth loop is a genuine, non-construction-determined reciprocal mechanism — the AND-gate across ALL of the following, matching the calibration-penalty discipline for a claim with a literature-disfavored component):
- Arm B's content-only metric (codebook AUC) improves over Arm A by a non-trivial, seed-consistent margin, and beats the randomized-lookup control (reproduces the self-teaching per-item mechanism — expected to pass; this half of the loop is well-precedented).
- **Arm B's rule-level task (thematic-fit/argument-role disambiguation) improves over Arm A's frozen baseline by a pre-registered margin, with NO direct supervision on that task** — the single decisive result. This is the substrate-analog of syntactic bootstrapping actually working, and per the literature synthesis in (a), this is the LEAST likely of the HARD-PASS conditions to hold.
- Arm C (gate ON) shows no monotonic degradation across generations on either metric; Arm C (gate OFF) DOES degrade — demonstrating the reliability/common-mode gate is the load-bearing anchor keeping the loop virtuous rather than collapsing (the substrate-analog of Gerstgrasser's "accumulate, verify, don't blindly self-consume" fix).
- Seed-ablation shows a genuine threshold: below some minimum codebook coverage, the loop measurably fails to bootstrap — confirming the human-literature-predicted minimum-seed dynamic (Decoding Threshold Hypothesis; Snedeker/Gleitman/Brent's noun-seed requirement) transfers to this substrate.

**HARD-FAIL** (the co-growth loop does not close, or the "rules improve from content" half specifically is refuted for this substrate — pre-registered, and per (a), THIS is the outcome the literature makes most likely):
- **Arm B ties Arm A on the rule-level task (content-frozen and content-growing conditions are statistically indistinguishable)** — the single most damning and, per this drill's synthesis, single most PREDICTED outcome. This would directly confirm, in-house, the human finding that content acquisition (fast mapping, self-teaching, dictionary lookup) and rule-level refinement (syntactic bootstrapping, comprehension-monitoring strategy, grammatical rule revision) are SEPARABLE, non-reciprocal mechanisms — content growth alone does not sharpen selection/combinatorial machinery; a DIFFERENT, explicitly rule-task-supervised mechanism (the reciprocal-teaching analog: direct feedback on the rule-task itself, not passive exposure) would be required instead, and should be the next drill's target if this HARD-FAILs.
- Arm C degrades even WITH the gate active — indicates the reliability/common-mode gate, while independently-derived, is not by itself sufficient to prevent collapse-style drift; would require a stronger anchor (e.g., a permanently-frozen, never-touched-by-the-loop slice of verified content held out and periodically re-anchored to, per Gerstgrasser's accumulate-don't-replace principle) rather than abandoning the loop concept.
- Seed-ablation shows a smooth curve with no threshold — refutes the minimum-seed transfer to this substrate specifically (informative either way, not a failure).
- Randomized-lookup or ungated-lookup controls perform comparably to the genuine gated arms — reproduces the already-refuted "more corpus, not more correct corpus" pattern from the active-learning note; would mean this drill re-derived a known dead end rather than a new result.

## (e) Substrate-product implications

- **Product framing, refined not abandoned:** the honest, evidence-consistent product story is NOT "the reader reads more and everything gets better together" (this framing is the one the Matthew-effect controversy and the correction/rule literature both caution against overselling). The evidence-consistent story is **two separable, both-buildable capabilities**: (1) "the reader genuinely grows its factual/world knowledge through a look-up-verify-bind loop, and can show exactly where each fact came from and why it was trusted" (well-precedented, Arm B's content-metric half); and (2), CONDITIONAL on this drill's decisive Arm B rule-task result, EITHER "growing its knowledge also measurably sharpens its judgment calls" (if HARD-PASS — a genuinely differentiated claim, since this is precisely the mechanism most naive "bigger corpus" approaches assume without testing) OR "sharpening judgment calls requires a separate, explicitly-supervised calibration step distinct from passive reading" (if HARD-FAIL, which is the better-supported prediction) — and the second framing is *still a positive, buildable product story*, it just requires being honest that content-lookup and rule-calibration are two different engineering efforts, not one loop that does both for free.
- **The single most valuable engineering takeaway regardless of outcome:** the model-collapse literature's convergent lesson (accumulate/verify rather than blindly self-consume; keep an independent anchor) is a direct, actionable design constraint for ANY future consolidation/revision mechanism this substrate builds, not just this specific loop — and the substrate already has the right-shaped piece for it (the independently-derived reliability/common-mode gate, CG 29376/29378, which by construction supplies exactly the "not generated by the same closed loop" signal every domain in section (d)/(c) shows is the decisive factor). This is a case where prior-session engineering (built for a different reason — source-trust checking) turns out to be exactly the missing ingredient the bootstrapping-loop literature says is required — a genuine, non-coincidental convergence worth flagging explicitly to the Director.
- **Guard against the specific human failure mode this drill surfaces:** do not market or internally assume a "virtuous compounding" story analogous to the popular (but empirically unsupported at the fan-spread/widening-gap level) version of the Matthew effect. If Arm B rule-task HARD-FAILs as predicted, that is not a setback to the overall learning-layer program — it is a precise, literature-grounded redirection: build the rule-calibration mechanism as its OWN explicitly-supervised component (reciprocal-teaching-style: direct feedback on the selection/disambiguation task itself, using the already-scoped affectedness/thematic-fit signal as the supervision target) rather than continuing to hope passive content growth will eventually produce it.

- **Model-collapse-avoidance-via-existing-reliability/common-mode-gate (Arm C gate-ON prediction):** the ML analogy is strong and convergent (multiple independent papers), and the substrate's gate is architecturally the right SHAPE (independently-derived, not self-generated) → **P=0.50** (kept at the standard cap rather than deflated further, since this is the one component with genuinely strong, convergent, positive precedent AND a good structural match to what's already built).
