## HEADLINE

**USER's claim is TRUE, but narrower than stated, and the verify_v1 cell does not actually test it.** The published literature draws a clean, twice-corroborated regime boundary (compositional-generalization axis: COGS/CFQ/MetaQA hop-scaling; IR-cost axis: cross-encoder/LLM-rerank cost tables) between *where explicit structured/compositional matching decisively wins* (templated/explicit-role queries, multi-hop KG paths with hop-count >= 2, cost/QPS-constrained regimes, interpretability-required regimes) and *where dense/neural methods win or tie* (natural-language queries with implicit structure, world-knowledge-dependent selection, benchmarks with single-hop shortcuts). Crucially, `bge_substrate_compositional_verify_v1` **is not a VSA bind/unbind experiment at all** — reading the cell (`experiments/exp_bge_substrate_compositional_verify_v1.py`) shows it is dense bge-small embeddings + local PCA-whitening over the top-10 candidate set + a Rocchio-style additive query update + greedy non-redundant (MMR-adjacent) 2-pick. No circular convolution, no XOR, no permutation-based role binding is present anywhere in the pipeline. The label "substrate-compositional" in the anchor name is a misnomer relative to true VSA algebra (HRR/FHRR/BSC bind-unbind), which the substrate does have as a primitive (`fhrr_bind`/`fhrr_unbind`, Cycle-#5 ACCEPT atoms) but which this cell never invokes.

## Cheap decisive test

Three tests, cheapest-first, that would directly discriminate the live hypotheses (see "Falsifiable predictions" for exact thresholds):

1. **Benchmark-swap test (cheapest, CPU-only, ~1hr):** re-run the *exact same* verify_v1 pipeline (whitening + additive 2-hop pick vs brute top-10) on `2WikiMultihopQA` instead of the HotpotQA distractor set. 2Wiki was purpose-built (Ho et al. 2020) to defeat the single-hop shortcut that Min et al. (2019, ACL) showed lets a single-hop retriever answer ~2/3 of original HotpotQA questions correctly. If the +0.027 lift reflects genuine multi-hop complementary-selection value, it should be **larger** on 2Wiki, where single-hop-alone reader F1 collapses to ~55.9 (vs ~80 on HotpotQA).
2. **Mechanism-ablation test (cheap, CPU-only, ~1hr):** decompose the pipeline into 3 isolated variants — (a) whitening-only, single-shot top-2 by whitened cosine, no additive update; (b) additive-update-only (no whitening); (c) full pipeline (current). This isolates whether the lift comes from local whitening (known: STS/anisotropy fix, Su et al. 2021 / Mu & Viswanath 2017), from Rocchio-style additive query refinement (known: ANCE-PRF +9.3% NDCG@10, ColBERT-PRF +10-26% MAP — but published PRF results are single-hop ad-hoc IR, not multi-hop), or genuinely requires the combination.
3. **True-binding contrast test (moderate cost, needs entity/relation extraction, ~1 day):** reuse the existing (never-executed) HRR role-binding-chain design from `notes/research_to_exp_dev_MULTIHOP_SELECTOR_DESIGN_HRR_BINDING_CHAIN_2026-06-11.md` (entity-role extraction -> `fhrr_bind` role-filler pairs -> unbind-based selection), applied to an EXPLICIT templated decomposition of HotpotQA bridge questions (bridge_entity, relation1, relation2), instead of the raw-NL-question cosine-similarity proxy verify_v1 actually used. Per the Shaw et al. (2021) regime-boundary finding, predict this shows a much larger lift than +0.027 specifically because it moves the query representation from "implicit NL structure" to "explicit templated structure" — the regime where structured methods are empirically decisive (COGS/CFQ: 60-80pt gaps; MetaQA: 16-46pt hop-scaling gaps).

## Literature review (4 axes, from 4 parallel Sonnet lit-scans)

### Axis 1 -- VSA/HDC binding theory vs dense embeddings on selection tasks
- Plate (1995, HRR) and Frady/Kleyko/Sommer (JAIR 2021, 2018) give rigorous, PROVEN capacity/crosstalk-noise bounds for circular-convolution bind/unbind -- but these are internal-algebra self-consistency results (how much noise unbinding introduces as a function of dimension/superposition count), not comparative benchmarks against dense embeddings on any task.
- Kanerva (2009) and the Kleyko et al. survey pair (arXiv:2111.06077, 2112.15424) -- the most comprehensive HDC references available -- report **no head-to-head accuracy/F1/recall table of VSA-bound representations vs dense neural embeddings on a shared retrieval/selection task.** This is a genuine, notable absence in the public literature, not an oversight of the scan.
- Rachkovskij & Kussul (2001, sparse binary/ternary VSA context-dependent thinning) proves binding/normalization preserves useful similarity structure but **no formal proof was found that bound/compositional representations generalize better than dense distributed ones** on combinatorial/novel-combination tasks specifically.
- The one directly on-point empirical paper found -- **"Holographic Memory for Zero-Shot Compositional Reasoning in Knowledge Graphs" (arXiv:2606.24948)** -- tests HRR/FHRR + modern-Hopfield cleanup on FB15k-237: strong single-hop (filtered MRR ~0.35-0.36, competitive) but **zero-shot two-hop composition fails at chance** across cleanup temperatures, root-caused to superposition interference degrading exactly the intermediate facts needed for chaining (0.26-0.48x atomic accuracy), not a bind/unbind-algebra defect per se. This is a **negative precedent directly on the question asked** and corroborates (does not contradict) the substrate's own known multi-hop cliff pattern.
- Verdict: binding-capacity math is solid; head-to-head "VSA beats dense embeddings at selection" benchmarks essentially do not exist in the public literature, and the one paper testing the closest analog (multi-hop KG composition) shows FAILURE, not success.

### Axis 2 -- LLM/cross-encoder reranker cost, quality, interpretability
- Cross-encoders reliably add **+5 to +15 nDCG@10** over bi-encoder-only baselines (arXiv:2212.06121; Nogueira/Lin survey arXiv:2010.06467) at **50-500ms/query** latency (ColBERT ~50-100ms; mono-duo-T5 at depth 1K ~13s, explicitly called impractical).
- LLM-as-reranker (RankGPT, Sun et al. EMNLP 2023 Outstanding Paper) beats fine-tuned monoT5-3B by a further **+2.3 to +2.7 nDCG**, but at **$0.01-0.03/query, 1-5s latency** -- 10-100x more expensive/slower than cross-encoders for a modest additional quality gain. RankVicuna/RankZephyr exist specifically to distill this expensive behavior into cheaper open models, an explicit cost-driven design choice in the literature itself.
- Known failure modes: **positional bias** (later-window passages systematically under-ranked; arXiv:2604.03642), **degradation on novel/unseen queries** (pairwise -15%, pointwise -12%, listwise -8%; EMNLP Findings 2025 arXiv:2508.16757), and one **unverified single-source claim** (arXiv:2603.28886) of catastrophic failure (78.8% -> 9.1%) applying standard cross-encoder rerank in a multi-hop setting -- flagged explicitly as not independently confirmed from raw text, but structurally consistent with the general argument that passage-INDEPENDENT scoring cannot represent "this passage complements that other passage," which is exactly what multi-hop bridge-evidence selection requires.
- No single academic paper was found plotting all four tiers (no-rerank / lightweight-vector-rerank / cross-encoder / LLM-rerank) on one benchmark with both quality AND cost on the same axis -- closest available data is vendor benchmark roundups (Cohere Rerank 0.735 nDCG@10 / 210ms / $2.40 per 1k queries; bge-reranker-base-v2 0.699 / 92ms / $0.18 per 1k queries).

### Axis 3 -- Whitening, pseudo-relevance feedback, multi-hop query reformulation (the actual mechanism verify_v1 implements)
- Global corpus-level whitening (Mu & Viswanath 2017 "All-but-the-Top"; Su et al. 2021 "Whitening Sentence Representations") is an established, well-cited technique for fixing embedding anisotropy and improving retrieval discriminability.
- **No paper was found that studies whitening computed over a small (tens-to-hundreds) per-query candidate subset** (i.e., what verify_v1 actually does -- PCA-whitening over just the top-10 candidates) as opposed to a global corpus statistic. This specific "local post-retrieval whitening" framing appears to be an unpublished/novel combination, not an established technique -- flagged as a genuine gap, not confirmed prior art.
- Rocchio (1971) and its modern descendants (ANCE-PRF: +9.3% NDCG@10 on TREC DL-HARD; ColBERT-PRF: +10-26% MAP) establish that additive query-vector refinement using retrieved-document embeddings reliably helps -- **but this literature is entirely single-hop ad-hoc IR** (TREC DL/MS MARCO), not multi-hop QA.
- Mainstream multi-hop dense retrieval (MDR, GoldEn Retriever, Beam Retrieval) uses **TEXT-level concatenation or generation** for hop-to-hop query reformulation, not embedding-space vector addition. Verify_v1's specific mechanism (bundle the picked passage's embedding additively into the query vector) is atypical relative to the dominant published multi-hop paradigm.
- The closest real precedent for the SECOND half of verify_v1's mechanism (masking out the first pick, forcing a different second pick) is **Maximal Marginal Relevance / diversity-aware selection** (Carbonell & Goldstein 1998; modern analog Vendi-RAG arXiv:2502.11228, which reports diversity-aware retrieval "consistently outperforms baselines" on HotpotQA specifically for context sizes > 2). This is a well-precedented, modest-but-real effect class -- complementary/non-redundant selection beating naive top-K similarity on multi-hop tasks.
- **Synthesis of axis 3:** verify_v1's mechanism decomposes into (local whitening: unpublished local variant of an established global technique) + (additive query update: established single-hop PRF technique, applied out-of-its-studied-domain to multi-hop) + (non-redundant mask-out: established MMR/diversity-selection technique, WITH direct HotpotQA precedent). None of these three components is "VSA compositional binding" in the Plate/Kanerva/Rachkovskij sense.

### Axis 4 -- Compositional generalization and structured-KG benchmarks (where structure decisively wins)
- COGS (Kim & Linzen 2020): in-distribution 96-99% vs novel-combination generalization-split 16-35% for standard neural seq2seq -- a 60-80 point gap with no domain shift, purely from training-distribution recombination.
- CFQ (Keysers et al. 2020): random-split ~95-97% vs Maximum-Compound-Divergence splits **below 20%** for standard architectures (LSTM+Attn, Transformer, Universal Transformer) -- ~80 point gap.
- MetaQA (EmbedKGQA, Saxena et al. 2020): the gap between a weak-structure text/memory baseline (KV-Mem) and explicit KG-embedding/relational-path methods is **negligible at 1-hop (~1pt)**, grows to **~16pt at 2-hop**, and **~46pt at 3-hop** on the full KG (worse still on sparse/incomplete KGs). This is the single clearest quantitative statement in the whole scan of "where structure starts winning": **hop-count >= 2 with a reasonably complete relational graph.**
- HotpotQA/2WikiMultihopQA: **Min et al. (2019, ACL), "Compositional Questions Do Not Necessitate Multi-hop Reasoning"** -- single-hop retrieval alone answers roughly two-thirds of original HotpotQA questions correctly, because the benchmark leaks shortcuts. 2WikiMultihopQA (Ho et al. 2020) was constructed specifically to patch this (single-hop-alone reader F1 collapses to ~55.9 there vs ~80 on HotpotQA). **This directly explains why verify_v1's own benchmark choice (HotpotQA distractor) structurally caps the achievable lift from any genuine multi-hop mechanism** -- a large fraction of its 120 test questions don't require 2-hop complementary selection at all, so brute top-10 (which already contains the single needed fact, buried among 10) is a strong baseline almost by construction.
- Shaw et al. (2021, ACL), "Compositional Generalization and Natural Language Variation: Can a Semantic Parsing Approach Handle Both?" is the clearest explicit statement of the regime boundary: specialized grammar/structure-biased models (e.g. NQG) achieve near-perfect accuracy on synthetic compositional splits (SCAN/CFQ) but degrade badly on realistic natural-language paraphrase variation, while standard neural seq2seq handles NL variation well but fails compositional splits. **This is the crispest available formal statement of exactly the regime split the USER's claim needs: structured methods win on explicit/templated structure and lose/tie on implicit natural-language structure.**

## Theoretical case for / against USER's claim

**Steel-manned FOR (narrow, scope-qualified form):** "Substrate VSA-style structured/compositional selection is theoretically superior to dense-embedding or cross-encoder/LLM reranking specifically when (a) the query's compositional structure is explicit or can be made explicit (templated slots, extracted relations) rather than left as raw natural language, (b) the retrieval graph/KB has hop-count >= 2 with reasonably complete relational coverage, (c) cost/QPS constraints make cross-encoder (50-500ms) or LLM (1-5s, $0.01-0.03/query) rerank infeasible, or (d) structural/auditable explanation of the selection is a hard requirement (directly relevant to the substrate's M3/M4 conversational architecture, which needs to expose WHY a fact was selected)." This is corroborated by TWO independent literature axes -- compositional-generalization benchmarks (Axis 4: COGS/CFQ/MetaQA hop-scaling) and IR-cost-efficiency literature (Axis 2: cross-encoder/LLM cost tables) -- satisfying the 2x-drill corroboration discipline.

**Steel-manned AGAINST:** the one paper that directly tests true VSA binding on multi-hop KG composition (arXiv:2606.24948) shows it FAILING at chance level on exactly the "hop >= 2" regime where the theory says it should shine, due to superposition interference -- a structural limitation of bundling/superposition memory that is not obviously fixable by better binding math alone (it is a capacity/interference problem, matching the substrate's own known d=25 multi-hop cliff). Additionally, "explicit compositional structure" is not free -- extracting it from natural language (entity-role tagging, relation extraction) reintroduces exactly the semantic-parse step whose loss the task description already flagged as diluting verify_v1's result; a bind/unbind pipeline is only as good as its upstream parse, and errors there propagate multiplicatively through binding chains (this is a known, if less-emphasized, risk in the same literature -- COGS/CFQ structured models trade NL-robustness for compositional accuracy, per Shaw et al. 2021).

**Net assessment:** the USER's broad claim ("substrate should be ideal at selection, even better than LLM") is TRUE only inside a specific, identifiable regime, and FALSE (or at best untested/uncertain, given the one negative precedent) outside it. verify_v1 tested neither the favorable regime (explicit structure) nor genuine VSA binding (bind/unbind) -- it tested a dense-embedding heuristic (whitening + Rocchio-style additive update + MMR-style mask-out) on natural-language HotpotQA questions, which is precisely the UNfavorable regime per Shaw et al.'s boundary. That it still produced a small positive lift (+0.027) is consistent with well-precedented (if modest) IR techniques (PRF, MMR/diversity), not with anything specifically "substrate" or "VSA."

## Efficiency-per-quality curve (estimated from literature figures, not substrate-measured)

| Mechanism | Per-query cost (published figures) | Typical quality lift over dense-retrieval-alone | Quality/cost character |
|---|---|---|---|
| No rerank (bi-encoder top-K only) | ~1-5ms | baseline | -- |
| VSA/vector-only rerank (O(K x N), no attention) | sub-ms to ~1ms | unestablished in public lit for true bind/unbind; verify_v1's dense-heuristic analog measured +0.027 F1 (n=120, one config, MIDDLE_BAND) | near-zero marginal cost; quality ceiling unproven, likely modest per Axis 1/3 findings |
| Cross-encoder rerank (ColBERT / monoBERT-class) | 50-500ms for K~20-100 | +5 to +15 nDCG@10 | well-established, reliable, moderate cost |
| LLM rerank (RankGPT/GPT-4-class) | 1-5s, $0.01-0.03/query | +2 to +3 nDCG over best cross-encoder (incremental) | highest cost for smallest marginal gain over cross-encoder; only clearly worth it when the query is genuinely novel/complex and cost is not binding |

**Reading of the curve:** cross-encoder rerank is the dominant cost/quality operating point for most regimes (biggest jump in quality per added ms). LLM rerank only wins if quality is the sole objective and cost/latency are unconstrained. VSA/vector-only rerank's case rests entirely on regimes where (a) the achievable quality lift, even if modest, is "free" relative to a sub-ms budget that a cross-encoder cannot meet (very high QPS, edge/on-device, or budget-constrained conversational agents), or (b) interpretability is a hard requirement that cross-encoder/LLM cannot satisfy at any cost. Outside those two conditions, the published quality ceiling for a pure vector-only rerank does not yet clear the bar cross-encoders already clear cheaply.

## Interpretability & verifiability

No paper in the scan quantitatively benchmarks decomposability/interpretability of cross-encoder or LLM rerank scores against a simpler linear/structured scorer (Axis 2, item 5) -- this dimension is qualitatively discussed (attention visualization, SPLADE-style sparse lexical matching framed as inherently more interpretable) but not measured. This means the interpretability argument for substrate-native selection is currently a **structural/architectural argument, not an empirically demonstrated one** in the public literature -- it is real (bind/unbind selection can expose which bindings matched; cross-encoder/LLM attention cannot be cleanly attributed to "this fact matched because of relation R"), but should be presented to the substrate-product roadmap as a qualitative/architectural advantage for M3/M4, not as a benchmarked quality metric.

## Substrate-product implications

- Do not market or internally frame verify_v1 as "VSA compositional selection beating dense retrieval" -- it is a whitening + PRF-style + MMR-style dense-embedding pipeline, and its literature precedents (Axis 3) predict exactly the modest, sub-HARD-PASS lift observed. This is a labeling/framing correction, not a capability closure.
- The USER's underlying architectural thesis (substrate-native structured selection as a genuine differentiator vs LLM/cross-encoder rerank) remains OPEN and, per two independent literature axes, plausible -- but only in the identified regime (explicit/templated structure, hop-count >= 2, cost/QPS-constrained, or interpretability-required). The existing-but-unexecuted HRR-binding-chain design (2026-06-11) is the correct next artifact to test this regime directly, not further iteration on the dense-heuristic verify_v1 pipeline.
- For M3/M4 conversational/agentic architecture: the interpretability argument (expose which role-filler binding matched) is a real, literature-unopposed architectural advantage worth carrying forward regardless of the quality-benchmark outcome, since no competing published technique offers the same clean structural attribution.
- Efficiency argument: a genuinely sub-millisecond VSA-native rerank is only product-relevant in high-QPS or edge-constrained deployments; for typical conversational-agent QPS, cross-encoder rerank (50-500ms) is already cheap enough that the efficiency argument alone will not carry the case -- quality parity or a structural/interpretability requirement is needed to justify the architectural choice.
