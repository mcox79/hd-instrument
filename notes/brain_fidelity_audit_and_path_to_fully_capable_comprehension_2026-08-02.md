## PART A: BRAIN-FIDELITY ELEMENT AUDIT

### A1. Coreference = match-or-allocate + Centering-theory salience

**Our implementation:** assign a new mention to an existing entity slot if similarity/compatibility exceeds a threshold, else allocate a new slot; when multiple candidates are compatible, rank by Centering's forward-looking-center order (Grosz, Joshi & Weinstein 1995, *Centering: A Framework for Modeling the Local Coherence of Discourse*, Computational Linguistics 21(2)).

**Brain mechanism + citation:**
- Match-vs-allocate has a real hippocampal analog: dentate gyrus (DG) performs pattern separation (orthogonalizes similar inputs into distinct sparse codes — "new slot"), CA3 performs pattern completion via recurrent attractor dynamics (retrieves an existing stored pattern from a partial/degraded cue — "match to existing slot"). Human fMRI on DG/CA3 with lure stimuli shows CA3/CA1 repetition-suppression for near-duplicate ("lure") items (treated as match) while DG/CA3 activity for lures resembles novel items (treated as separate) — Bakker et al. 2008 *Science* "Pattern Separation in the Human Hippocampal CA3 and Dentate Gyrus"; reviewed in Yassa & Stark 2011 and the DG/CA3 human-imaging synthesis (Frontiers in Cellular Neuroscience 2014, "Human neuroimaging studies on the hippocampal CA3 region").
- Centering Theory itself is a **linguistics-internal discourse-coherence formalism** with no original neural claim in Grosz/Joshi/Weinstein 1995 — it was developed to predict pronoun-form choice and coherence judgments, not as a neural model. It does have real behavioral/psycholinguistic support independent of any neural grounding: Gordon, Grosz & Gilliom 1993 "Pronouns, names, and the centering of attention in discourse" (Cognitive Science) found the "repeated-name penalty" (extra reading time when a full name is used to refer to the current Cb, where a pronoun was expected) via self-paced reading — direct behavioral evidence that something like a ranked backward-looking center is tracked online. There is also ERP work on pronoun resolution broadly (Van Berkum et al. 2007 on discourse-referent ambiguity eliciting a sustained negativity, and N400/P600 effects for gender-mismatch/binding violations), but this is evidence for online referent-tracking and prediction in general, not evidence specifically validating the Cb/Cf ranking mechanism per se.

**Verdict: brain-APPROXIMATING.**
- SHAPE: partially analogous. Allocate-vs-match as separate-vs-complete is a real qualitative correspondence (discrete decision between "new attractor" and "retrieve existing attractor"), but DG/CA3 pattern separation/completion operates on continuous, similarity-graded, sub-symbolic population codes with a soft/graded threshold set by network dynamics (mossy fiber sparsity, inhibition), not a single symbolic scalar threshold compared to one score. Our system's "similarity/compatibility exceeds threshold" is a coarse discretization of what is, in the brain, an emergent property of attractor basin geometry.
- POSITION: correct — pattern separation/completion runs upstream of and interleaved with the same episodic-binding stage where centering-style referent ranking would need to operate (hippocampal-cortical loop, not late "post-comprehension" bookkeeping).
- METRIC: this is the weak axis. Centering's Cb/Cf ranking is judged in the brain (to the extent evidenced) by reading-time cost (repeated-name penalty) and discourse-coherence judgments — a behavioral/relational metric. Our system judges Cb/Cf ranking by a symbolic rank-order rule applied to a fixed list, which is fine as an implementation of the *rule*, but the underlying "compatibility exceeds threshold" allocate/match decision is judged by a similarity score, not by anything like attractor-basin capture, i.e. no actual competitive dynamics between candidate patterns.
- **Fix:** replace the single-scalar-threshold match/allocate decision with a competitive (winner-take-most, soft-max over compatibility scores with a "none wins enough -> allocate" fallback) decision rule — closer to attractor-network completion dynamics than a fixed threshold, and better matches the graded nature of pattern-separation/completion tradeoffs (this is the same mechanism CA3 recurrent collaterals implement via mutual inhibition).
- **ARCHITECTURE DEBT: not flagged here** — the metric is a real discrete decision (allocate/match), not a similarity-proxy substituting for symbolic reasoning; the concern is graded-vs-scalar threshold, a secondary fidelity gap, not a category error.

### A2. strict_cb pronoun resolution (immediately-prior clause only)

**Our implementation:** resolve a pronoun to the backward-looking center of the *immediately preceding clause only* — a literal, narrow reading of Cb.

**Brain mechanism + citation:** Centering Theory's Cb is itself defined utterance-to-utterance (i.e., is inherently a 1-back operator in the original formalism), so "strict Cb" is faithful to Grosz/Joshi/Weinstein's own formal definition. But whether real discourse comprehension is *actually* this narrow is a separate, empirical question, and the evidence says no:
- Garrod & Sanford's scenario-mapping / focus accounts (Garrod & Sanford 1994, "Resolving sentences in a discourse context"; Sanford & Garrod 1981 *Understanding Written Language*) show that referents can remain highly accessible ("in focus") across multiple intervening clauses when they are the scenario's protagonist, not just the immediately prior clause's Cb — e.g. implicit-causality and role-schema effects can reactivate a referent 2+ clauses back with no processing cost, while a strictly-local Cb-only account predicts a cost.
- Working-memory-bound discourse models (Just & Carpenter's capacity theory; McKoon & Ratcliff's minimalist/resonance model 1992 "Inference during reading") argue referent activation is graded and decays with distance/interference rather than hard-cut at one clause — a window, not a point.
- ACT-R-style discourse-buffer accounts (e.g. Budiu & Anderson's construction-integration-in-ACT-R work) likewise use a multi-item working-memory buffer with decay, not a single most-recent-clause pointer.

**Verdict: brain-APPROXIMATING (leaning NOT-faithful on the "strict 1-back only" restriction, though faithful to the *formal* Centering definition it is derived from).**
- SHAPE: the *rule form* (favor most-salient recent referent) is right; the *hard cutoff* (exactly one clause back, nothing further) is wrong-shaped relative to the graded-decay/wider-window evidence.
- POSITION: correct pipeline position (pronoun-resolution stage, post-parse pre-situation-model-update).
- METRIC: judged as a hard boolean (is it the immediately-prior Cb, yes/no) where the brain's analog is graded accessibility/activation that a hard boundary can't represent — this is the core mismatch, not a similarity-proxy issue per se but a categorical-vs-continuous mismatch.
- **Fix:** widen the candidate window to a small working-memory-bound set (e.g. last 3-4 clauses' Cb/Cf entries, or all entities still "in focus" per scenario-mapping/protagonist status) with a recency-weighted (not hard-cutoff) salience decay, falling back to strict-Cb only when the window is empty of compatible candidates. This is exactly the frontier problem noted in Part B (cross-clause discourse coreference) — A2's fix and Part-B-lever-1 are the same build.

### A3. Binding Principle B as a hand-coded disjoint-reference rule

**Our implementation:** a pronoun cannot corefer with a co-argument antecedent in its local clause (Chomsky 1981 *Lectures on Government and Binding*), implemented as a grammatical filter.

**Brain/acquisition mechanism + citation:**
- Chien & Wexler 1990 ("Children's knowledge of locality conditions in binding as evidence for the modularity of syntax and pragmatics", *Language Acquisition*) found young children (~5yo) correctly reject reflexive-antecedent binding violations (Principle A) at adult-like rates far earlier than they correctly reject pronoun-coreference under Principle B — with quantified antecedents ("every bear") children are near-adult, but with referential antecedents ("Mama Bear") children accept the illicit coreferential reading on the order of ~50% of trials. This is the "Delay of Principle B Effect" (DPBE).
- The standard interpretation (Chien & Wexler, and later Reinhart & Grodzinsky) is that the *syntactic* constraint (Principle B proper, ruling out grammatical binding) is present early and modular, but children additionally need a separate *pragmatic* competence (Reinhart's "Rule I", an implicature-like reference-set computation that rules out coreference when a syntactically-bound alternative existed but wasn't used) that matures later — i.e., the DPBE is evidence Principle B is NOT a single unified brain-native rule but a syntax-module constraint plus a separate, later-maturing pragmatic filter.
- Agrammatic-aphasia evidence (Grodzinsky & Reinhart 1993, "The innateness of binding and coreference"; Grodzinsky, Wexler et al. subsequent work) shows Broca's aphasics pattern like young children on Principle B (impaired at ruling out illicit coreference) while remaining intact on Principle A (reflexives) — a double dissociation supporting Principle B specifically (not Principle A, not binding-in-general) as reliant on a pragmatic/processing resource that is selectively vulnerable, consistent with the "Principle B is composite" account. More recent psycholinguistic work (e.g. Conroy, Takahashi, Lidz & Phillips 2009 on children's real-time processing of Principle B) shows children *can* apply Principle B online (eye-tracking) even when they fail offline judgment tasks — suggesting the syntactic constraint is active but gets overridden by response-selection/pragmatic demands at the behavioral level.

**Verdict: brain-APPROXIMATING.**
- SHAPE: partially right. A hard grammatical filter correctly captures the syntactic-module component (which the acquisition/aphasia evidence says IS real and early/modular), but our implementation treats Principle B as a single monolithic rule, whereas the evidence is that mature adult performance is actually the *composite* of (1) an early syntactic constraint plus (2) a separate, later-maturing pragmatic reference-set computation (Reinhart's Rule I) that is selectively fragile (children, some aphasics fail it) — so a single hand-coded rule over-idealizes what is, biologically, a two-component system.
- POSITION: correct — this needs to run at the same local-domain/clause-argument-structure stage where our disjoint-reference filter is applied, consistent with the syntax-module account.
- METRIC: judged as a hard categorical constraint (blocked / not blocked), which does match the *syntactic* component's judgeability (the syntactic module genuinely is binary/rule-based per Chomsky 1981 and the online eye-tracking evidence in Conroy et al. 2009) — this is one of the few places our hand-coded-rule framing is actually well-supported by the "brain does symbolic constraint enforcement here" reading, not a similarity-proxy debt.
- **Fix:** none urgent for the constraint itself (it is defensibly brain-native as a hard rule at the syntactic-module level); the honest addition is to note that the full adult-competence Principle B effect is composite, so if the system starts making residual coreference errors that look like the pragmatic component's failure mode (i.e. cases requiring reasoning about whether a syntactically-legal binding alternative existed and wasn't used), that is evidence for adding Reinhart's Rule I as a second, separate pragmatic-inference stage — not evidence the hard grammatical filter itself is wrong.
- **ARCHITECTURE DEBT:** none flagged — this is the one component of the five that is judged the way the brain plausibly judges it (categorical constraint via a dissociable module), not a proxy standing in for relational reasoning.

### A4. Situation-model register = FHRR bind+bundle (Plate 1995/2003) motivated by Kintsch / Zwaan

**Our implementation:** circular-convolution BIND (role, filler) pairs and elementwise-sum BUNDLE across mentions/clauses to accumulate a persistent entity/event register, motivated by Kintsch's Construction-Integration model (Kintsch & van Dijk 1978; Kintsch 1998 *Comprehension: A Paradigm for Cognition*) and Zwaan & Radvansky 1998's Event-Indexing Model (*Psychological Bulletin*, situation models tracked along space/time/protagonist/causation/intentionality dimensions).

**The central contested question — synchrony vs. conjunctive coding as the analog for convolution-BIND:**
- FHRR/VSA binding is frequently motivated in the VSA literature by an appeal to binding-by-synchrony (theta-gamma phase coding: Lisman & Idiart 1995 "Storage of 7±2 short-term memories in oscillatory subcycles", *Science*; Fries 2005 "A mechanism for cognitive dynamics: neuronal communication through neuronal coherence", *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*).
- But the current standing of binding-by-synchrony is genuinely contested, and the balance of recent evidence leans away from phase-synchrony as the general-purpose binding mechanism and toward conjunctive/mixed-selectivity coding: Rigotti et al. 2013 (*Nature* 497, "The importance of mixed selectivity in complex cognitive tasks") showed PFC populations encode conjunctions of task-relevant variables in single-neuron non-linear mixed selectivity, sufficient to support flexible readout of any variable combination without appeal to phase — this has become a dominant framework (Fusi, Miller & Rigotti 2016, "Why neurons mix: high dimensionality for higher cognition", *Current Opinion in Neurobiology*) for how the brain represents structured/relational conjunctions. On the synchrony side itself, recent work (Schneider et al., and the 2024 "Attentional selection and communication through coherence: scope and limitations" review, PLOS Comp Bio / PMC11326628) argues that observed inter-areal coherence may be a *consequence* of shared drive/connectivity rather than a causal binding mechanism, and that the causal status of CTC for binding specifically (as opposed to selective communication/attention routing) remains unresolved as of the most recent reviews found.
- **Reasoned verdict:** convolution-BIND is better modeled as a **conjunctive-coding analog than a synchrony analog**. Circular convolution produces a single fixed-width vector that is a genuine algebraic function of *both* operands jointly (role AND filler), recoverable only via the inverse/unbinding operation — this is structurally closer to a mixed-selectivity unit's non-linear joint tuning to (variable-A, variable-B) than to a phase-relationship between two separately-coded populations. Synchrony encodes binding as a *relationship between two otherwise-separate codes* (temporal coincidence), which is not what convolution does (convolution produces one merged code, not two synchronized codes) — so the FHRR literature's synchrony framing is, on inspection, a weaker analogy than the conjunctive-coding framing, even though conjunctive coding is rarely cited as FHRR's own motivation.
- Implication for BUNDLE: elementwise-sum bundling across multiple mentions of the same entity is order-insensitive except via the bind roles — this is a reasonable coarse analog of **cortical schema accumulation** under Complementary Learning Systems (McClelland, McNaughton & O'Reilly 1995, *Psychological Review*, "Why there are complementary learning systems in the hippocampus and neocortex") — CLS's slow cortical system integrates statistically across many episodes into an overlapping, blended representation, which elementwise-sum approximates reasonably well. It is a much weaker analog of **hippocampal relational/episodic indexing** (Eichenbaum's relational memory theory; Cohen & Eichenbaum 1993, *Memory, Amnesia, and the Hippocampal System*), which specifically requires each individual episode to remain *separately addressable and flexibly recombinable* (relational, not blended) — elementwise-sum bundling destroys individual-mention addressability (only the aggregate is retained; you cannot ask "what did clause 3 alone say about entity X" without unbinding against noise from all other bundled mentions). This is a real fidelity gap: the situation-model register plays the FAST, single-episode, relationally-flexible role that CLS assigns to the hippocampus, but is implemented with the SLOW, blended, statistical-accumulation math CLS assigns to cortex.

**Verdict: brain-APPROXIMATING, with a specific identified mismatch.**
- SHAPE: BIND (convolution) — reasonably faithful as a conjunctive-coding analog (Rigotti et al. 2013 lineage), NOT well-motivated as a synchrony analog (contested, and structurally a weaker fit) despite that being the field's more common framing. BUNDLE (sum) — faithful to CLS cortical-schema accumulation, NOT faithful to hippocampal relational indexing (Eichenbaum), which the register is currently being asked to do (persistent per-entity role across clauses = an episodic/relational job).
- POSITION: correct pipeline position (situation-model update happens continuously across clauses, matching Kintsch's C-I cyclic integration and Zwaan's event-indexing update-on-each-clause).
- METRIC: the debt is here — success of BUNDLE-accumulate is implicitly judged by dot-product/similarity retrieval of the bundled vector, but the brain's relational-memory analog (hippocampus) is doing something closer to structured pointer/index retrieval (this specific episode, this specific event, not a blended average) — a similarity/cosine-style readout stands in for what should be discrete relational retrieval.
- **Fix:** for entities that need cross-clause relational tracking (the coreference frontier), do NOT rely purely on elementwise-sum bundling of an unbounded number of mentions into one vector; instead keep a small bounded slot-list (a discrete, indexable "episode list" per entity, hippocampal-relational-style) and use bundling only for the CLS-appropriate cortical-schema-style aggregate (e.g. "typical properties of this entity across the whole text"), not for the moment-to-moment tracking job.
- **ARCHITECTURE DEBT: FLAGGED.** BUNDLE-accumulate's implicit similarity-based readout is a proxy for what should be discrete relational (episodic-index) retrieval in the entity-tracking use case specifically — this is the most consequential debt item in Part A because it is directly upstream of the cross-clause coreference frontier named in Part B.

### A5. Flag layer = n_compatible / margin as metacognitive uncertainty signal

**Our implementation:** n_compatible (count of coreference-compatible candidates, for pronouns) and margin (score gap between top-1 and top-2, for names) trigger research/relearning when uncertainty is high.

**Brain mechanism + citation:**
- ACC conflict-monitoring theory (Botvinick, Braver, Barch, Carter & Cohen 2001, "Conflict monitoring and cognitive control", *Psychological Review*) proposes ACC detects *response conflict* — co-activation of competing, mutually incompatible response representations — and signals the need for increased control. This is conceptually close to n_compatible: more co-active competing candidates ~ higher conflict signal. But the ACC signal is a genuine population-level readout of competing neural representations' joint activity (an emergent property of the competition dynamics itself), not an externally-computed symbolic count over a candidate list.
- Fleming & Dolan 2012 ("The neural basis of metacognitive ability", *Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B*) and related work (Fleming, Weil, Nagy, Dolan & Rees 2010, *Science*, individual differences in metacognitive accuracy correlate with anterior PFC gray matter) locate confidence/uncertainty estimation in anterior PFC as a *second-order* readout over first-order decision evidence — i.e., a distinct representation of "how good was my evidence", partially but not perfectly correlated with first-order accuracy, computed from population-level decision-variable distributions (e.g. distance from a decision boundary), which is structurally closer to "margin" (a distance/gap measure) than to "n_compatible" (a raw count).
- Psycholinguistic referential-processing-load evidence: pupillometry and RT studies (e.g. Engelhardt, Ferreira & colleagues on referential ambiguity; the classic visual-world literature on definite-reference resolution, Eberhard, Spivey-Knowlton, Sedivy & Tanenhaus 1995) show measurable processing slowdown/pupil dilation specifically under referential ambiguity (multiple plausible antecedents), which does support "more competing candidates -> measurable brain/behavioral cost" as an ecologically valid phenomenon — supporting the *qualitative* logic of n_compatible even if not its exact symbolic form.

**Verdict: margin is brain-APPROXIMATING (reasonably faithful as a decision-boundary-distance analog); n_compatible is a weaker, more symbolic-proxy fit but still directionally motivated.**
- SHAPE: margin (score-gap) is structurally close to what decision-confidence models actually use (distance from threshold in an evidence-accumulation framework, e.g. drift-diffusion-model confidence — related to Kiani & Shadlen 2009 on confidence as a function of decision-variable state). n_compatible (raw count of compatible candidates) is a cruder discretization — the brain's conflict signal is a continuous population-competition quantity (e.g. Hopfield-energy-like or mutual-inhibition-derived), not literally a tally of how many discrete symbolic candidates passed a threshold; a count is a reasonable behavioral correlate (more plausible referents does correlate with slower RT/more pupil dilation) but is a symbolic simplification of what is, in the brain, an emergent property of the competitive dynamics.
- POSITION: correct — both real-time (per-decision monitoring, ACC/aPFC) and used prospectively to trigger control/learning (matches the "conflict -> increased control allocation" loop in Botvinick et al. 2001).
- METRIC: this is where the debt is concentrated for n_compatible specifically — using a symbolic count as the trigger substitutes for what should be a population-level competition/energy measure; margin is closer to the brain's actual metric (distance-based) so is comparatively more faithful.
- **Fix:** where possible, replace n_compatible with a continuous competition measure derived the same way margin is (e.g. entropy or the compatibility-score distribution's spread/energy, not a hard-threshold count) — this both removes an arbitrary threshold parameter and moves the pronoun-side signal onto the same footing as the more-faithful name-side margin signal.
- **ARCHITECTURE DEBT: FLAGGED (n_compatible only).** A symbolic count-of-candidates is a proxy standing in for what the brain computes as an emergent, continuous population-competition/energy signal (ACC conflict monitoring); margin is judged not to carry the same debt since it already approximates a distance/gap metric.

## PART B: BIOLOGY-LED DRILL — PATH TO FULLY-CAPABLE COMPREHENSION

### B1. Cross-clause / discourse coreference: dialogue-turn tracking + definite-description-to-name bridging

- Centering Theory's transition types (CONTINUE > RETAIN > SHIFT, ranked by discourse-coherence cost) were originally defined utterance-to-utterance within a single speaker's text, but have been extended to dialogue with speaker/addressee role-tracking (Walker, Joshi & Prince 1998, *Centering Theory in Discourse*, the canonical dialogue-extension volume) — the extension explicitly needs a "who is speaking now / who was addressed" index layered on top of Cb/Cf, which our A2 fix (widened, decay-weighted candidate window) does NOT yet include a speaker/addressee dimension for.
- Discourse Representation Theory (Kamp & Reyle 1993, *From Discourse to Logic*) models exactly the definite-description-to-proper-name bridging case ("the girl" -> "Ernestine") via accommodation: a definite NP either binds to an existing discourse referent already in the DRS (discourse representation structure) or, if none matches, a new discourse referent is accommodated and then subsequently identified with a later-introduced proper name through world-knowledge-driven bridging inference (Clark 1975's original "bridging" concept, "Bridging" in *Thinking: Readings in Cognitive Science*). This is precisely the mechanism our stack lacks: definite descriptions currently aren't linked into the same entity slot as a subsequently (or previously) introduced proper name unless surface-similarity/compatibility already catches it.
- Anterior temporal lobe (ATL) as an amodal semantic hub (Lambon Ralph, Jefferies, Patterson & Rogers 2017 Nature Reviews Neuroscience, "The neural and computational bases of semantic cognition") is the plausible substrate for the *world-knowledge* step in "the girl" = "Ernestine": the bridging inference requires knowing that a definite description denoting a category-instance ("a girl") and a proper name are compatible referents when local discourse context makes them coreferential candidates — this is a conceptual/semantic-similarity computation (girl is-a person; Ernestine is-a person, female, previously introduced), not a syntactic one, consistent with ATL's role as the integration hub rather than any syntax-local module.
- Hippocampal relational binding (same Eichenbaum framework as A4) is the plausible substrate for cross-episode linking generally — i.e., once "the girl" and "Ernestine" are both recognized as referring to the same discourse entity, that link itself needs to be stored as a flexible, relationally-addressable association (not just merged/blended), which reinforces the A4 fix (bounded, indexable per-entity episode slots rather than pure elementwise-sum blending).

**NEXT LEVER:** extend the entity-slot compatibility check with a **definite-description bridging pass**: when a definite NP ("the girl") cannot match any existing slot by surface features alone, check semantic-category compatibility (via the existing dictionary, which already supplies category/animacy features — no new grounding needed) against candidate slots introduced nearby in the discourse, and prefer the compatible candidate even without surface-string overlap; layer a lightweight speaker/addressee register on top of the Cb/Cf window for dialogue turns (whose turn is it, who was named as addressee) so "the girl" said mid-dialogue can bridge to the name of whichever entity is currently the speaker's addressee/topic.

### B2. Comprehension beyond who-did-what-per-clause: inference, causal/event relations, prediction

- Kintsch's Construction-Integration model (Kintsch 1988, "The role of knowledge in discourse comprehension: a construction-integration model", *Psychological Review*; Kintsch 1998) draws the textbase (propositional, clause-level who-did-what) vs. situation-model (integrated mental model incorporating inference and world knowledge) distinction directly — our current build is a competent textbase-builder, and the situation-model register (A4) is the right *architectural slot* for the next capability, but currently only accumulates entity/role bindings, not causal/inferential links between events.
- Graesser, Singer & Trabasso 1994 ("Constructing inferences during narrative text comprehension", *Psychological Review*) — the constructionist theory of inference — is the most directly actionable next-step reference: it distinguishes bridging inferences (necessary for local coherence — e.g. resolving "why did X happen" by connecting to a just-stated prior clause) from elaborative inferences (optional, world-knowledge-driven, e.g. predicting plausible consequences). Bridging inferences are drawn online/automatically during comprehension (this is well-replicated); elaborative inferences are drawn more selectively/effortfully and are the ones that support prediction/QA. The minimal, tractable next step per this framework is **causal bridging inference specifically** (the "why"/"because" connective-driven case), not the fuller elaborative/predictive layer, because bridging inferences are the ones with the strongest evidence of being computed online by all readers as a baseline requirement for coherence (vs. elaborative inference, which is reader/goal-dependent and harder to specify a ground truth for).
- Event Segmentation Theory / SEM (Zacks, Speer, Swallow, Braver & Reynolds 2007, "Event perception: a mind-brain perspective", *Psychological Bulletin*; Zacks & Swallow 2007) supplies the mechanism for WHEN to draw a new causal link vs. close out an event: prediction-error-driven event-boundary detection — an event model is maintained and updated only when incoming information becomes hard to predict from the current model, at which point a new event boundary (and a fresh causal-chain anchor) is opened. This gives a principled trigger for when the situation model should attempt a causal bridging inference (at predicted-boundary mismatches) rather than doing it exhaustively at every clause.

**NEXT LEVER:** add a **causal-bridging-inference pass** over the existing situation-model register: when two consecutive events in the register share an entity and a connective (explicit "because"/"so"/"after" or an implicit adjacency that a prediction-error signal — event-model mismatch per Zacks — flags as needing explanation), store a causal-link edge between the two event bindings in the register. This is the minimal Kintsch-style step from textbase to situation-model-with-inference, and reuses A4's fix (bounded per-entity episode slots) as the substrate the causal edges attach to.

### B3. Meaning/grounding: dictionary vs. embodied/ATL-hub semantics — deferrable or load-bearing at the coref step?

- Currently word meaning comes from a supplied dictionary (deferred grounding, allowed as data per project convention). The literature on what a static dictionary is missing, once cross-clause bridging is required: Patterson, Nestor & Rogers 2007 ("Where do you know what you know? The representation of semantic knowledge in the human brain", *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*) and the broader hub-and-spoke model (Lambon Ralph et al. 2017) show conceptual knowledge is not just a symbol-to-definition lookup but a graded, similarity-structured space (category membership is graded, feature overlap matters, e.g. semantic dementia patients lose fine-grained distinctions before coarse category ones) — a flat dictionary entry ("girl: category=person, gender=female, age=child") is a reasonable coarse approximation of exactly the *category-level* features that hub-and-spoke theory says are represented, so for the bridging-inference use case in B1 ("the girl" = category-compatible with "Ernestine" = a previously-introduced female child entity), a dictionary supplying category/animacy/gender features is sufficient — this is squarely the kind of static feature lookup the hub-and-spoke model itself treats as a legitimate input to the hub's integration, not a shortcut around it.
- Where grounding becomes load-bearing (per Barsalou's perceptual symbol systems, Barsalou 1999 "Perceptual symbol systems", *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*): world-knowledge bridging that requires relational/affordance inference beyond category membership — the example given in the prompt, "a house has a door", is a *relational/part-whole* fact, not a category fact, and a flat category dictionary does not supply relational facts unless they are separately enumerated. This becomes necessary specifically when a bridging inference depends on part-whole/functional relations rather than category/kind identity (e.g. "she opened the door" -> "the door" bridges to "the house" mentioned earlier via a has-part relation, not a kind-of relation).

**NEXT LEVER (partially deferrable, with an explicit boundary):** grounding CAN stay deferred through the cross-clause coreference/bridging step described in B1 as long as bridging is driven by category/kind compatibility (dictionary-sufficient, per hub-and-spoke) — that covers "the girl" -> "Ernestine". It becomes load-bearing the moment bridging needs part-whole/relational facts ("the door" -> "the house") rather than category facts; at that point the minimal addition is not full embodied grounding but a small supplementary **relational-fact table** (part-whole / has-a / typical-location relations, the same kind of static supplied data as the existing dictionary, per the project's own "supplying data is allowed, supplying the mechanism is not" convention) — defer full perceptual/embodied grounding indefinitely; this narrow relational-fact extension is the actual next threshold, and only trip it when B1's dictionary-only bridging demonstrably fails on relational cases.

### B4. Self-improving loop at scale: beyond 2 hand-driven flag-fix cycles

- McClelland, McNaughton & O'Reilly 1995 (Complementary Learning Systems, *Psychological Review* 102(3)) — the fast hippocampal encoding / slow interleaved cortical consolidation split — is the standard account for how the brain avoids catastrophic interference while continuing to learn from ongoing experience without a supervisor: novel/flagged episodes are rapidly encoded in the hippocampal system, then replayed (during offline periods — sleep, quiet wakefulness) and interleaved into the slower cortical system at a rate that respects existing structure.
- Wilson & McNaughton 1994 ("Reactivation of hippocampal ensemble memories during sleep", *Science*) is the direct empirical anchor for offline replay: hippocampal place-cell sequences experienced during waking behavior are replayed (compressed) during subsequent sleep, providing exactly the "batch consolidation without a human in the loop" mechanism needed.
- Schema-consistency effects on consolidation speed (Tse et al. 2007, "Schemas and memory consolidation", *Science*; van Kesteren, Ruiter, Fernández & Henson 2012, "How schema and novelty augment memory formation", *Trends in Neurosciences*) show that new information consistent with an existing schema is assimilated (consolidated) much faster — sometimes near-immediately, bypassing slow hippocampal-to-cortical transfer — while schema-inconsistent (surprising/flagged) information still requires the slow replay-based route. This maps directly onto our flag layer: flagged high-uncertainty cases ARE the schema-inconsistent cases, and the brain's answer is not "hand-fix them one at a time" but "batch them, replay/consolidate offline, and importantly weight schema-consistent corrections faster than schema-breaking ones."

**NEXT LEVER:** implement a minimal **replay buffer + offline consolidation pass**: instead of fixing flagged cases one at a time in the main read loop, accumulate flagged low-confidence cases (from A5's margin/n_compatible signal) into a buffer during the "awake" read pass, then run a separate offline pass that (a) re-attempts resolution on the buffered cases now with the benefit of the full document's later-arriving context (a cheap proxy for hippocampal replay providing retrospective context), (b) checks each fix against existing rule/dictionary consistency (schema-consistency gate, per Tse/van Kesteren — consistent fixes get folded in fast, inconsistent ones get flagged for a slower, more deliberate review) before committing it back into the standing rule set. This is the concrete, buildable analog of CLS replay-based consolidation and directly removes the "2 hand-driven cycles" bottleneck without requiring a human to review each flagged case individually.

## PRIORITIZED NEXT-STEPS (build-ordered, cheapest/highest-leverage first)

1. **(A5 fix, cheapest)** Replace `n_compatible`'s hard-threshold candidate count with a continuous compatibility-score-spread/entropy measure, matching `margin`'s already-more-faithful distance-based form. This is the single highest-leverage change against the named ~53% cross-clause frontier because it directly targets both the width-of-context failure (A2) and the dialogue-turn-tracking failure (B1) with one shared data structure.

Directly closes the "the girl" -> "Ernestine" bridging gap named as the frontier.

5. **(B2 lever, causal bridging)** Add a causal-bridging-inference pass over the (now-slotted, per #4) situation-model register: link two event bindings sharing an entity when an explicit connective or a Zacks-style prediction-error/event-boundary mismatch signals a needed explanation. First genuine step from textbase to situation-model-with-inference.
6. **(B4 lever, self-improving loop)** Build a flagged-case replay buffer + offline consolidation pass (schema-consistency-gated: fast-fold-in for rule-consistent fixes, slower review for rule-breaking ones), replacing the current 2-hand-driven-cycle bottleneck. Real but secondary fidelity gap (graded vs. scalar threshold); do after the higher-leverage items above since it doesn't gate the named frontier directly.
8. **(B3 lever, conditional/deferred)** Only build a supplementary relational-fact table (part-whole/has-a/typical-location) if and when #3's dictionary-only category bridging demonstrably fails on relational cases (e.g. "the door" -> "the house"). Explicitly held pending evidence of need, per the project's grounding-deferral convention.
9. **(A3)** No fix needed now; hold as a watch item — if residual coreference errors start showing the Reinhart-Rule-I pragmatic-override signature (system fails to rule out a coreference where a syntactically legal bound alternative existed and wasn't chosen), that is the trigger to add a second pragmatic-inference stage alongside the existing hard Principle-B filter.
