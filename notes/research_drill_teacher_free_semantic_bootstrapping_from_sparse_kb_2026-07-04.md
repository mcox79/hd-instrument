# Research drill: teacher-free semantic bootstrapping from a sparse relational KB (foundational, non-blocking)

Tag: 2x_RESEARCH_DRILL (broad lit-scan + narrow operational-test synthesis)
Date: 2026-07-04
Scope: literature review only. No code touched, no experiments run, no other notes files modified.

## HEADLINE

Teacher-free ("R3") semantic bootstrapping from the substrate's own sparse relational KB is NOT viable now, at the KB's current density of ~1.6 atoms/entity. This is not a soft "probably not ready" judgment call - it is the single most quantitatively over-determined finding in this drill: every method family surveyed (classical co-occurrence semantics, KG embeddings, random-walk embeddings, GNN self-supervision, graph-native self-distillation) either has a published, tested floor sitting well above 1.6/entity, or has never been tested anywhere near that sparse a regime at all. The gap to the nearest literature-grounded viability floor spans two very different size classes: roughly 2-5x density growth to clear the field's OWN operational definition of "low-degree/tail" node (degree <= 5), versus roughly 60-250x density growth to clear the empirically-measured noise floor for classical distributional semantics (~100-400 co-occurrences per entity). R3 should be treated as a grow-into-it foundational bet, gated behind an explicit, measurable corpus-density milestone ladder, not as an alternative available to try now. This REINFORCES rather than weakens the prior sequencing decision (BGE distillation now via D1, R3 sequenced behind corpus maturation) - it converts a qualitative "not ready yet" into a quantified, and larger-than-expected, gap.

## Viability verdict (decisive call)

**NOW (at ~1.6 atoms/entity): NOT VIABLE.** No method family surveyed has literature precedent at this density or anywhere close to it. The nearest tested floor for ANY of the six method families is Citeseer's average degree of ~2.84 (graph self-supervised learning benchmarks never go sparser than that) - itself only 1.8x denser than the KB - and the best-characterized quantitative floor (classical distributional semantics) sits at 60-250x current density.

**GATED, not abandoned.** The brain-grounding argument for R3 (hippocampus as fast internal teacher, no external oracle, dense-first-then-sparsify) remains sound as a long-term architectural target. What this drill adds is a concrete milestone ladder to sequence it behind, replacing the previous qualitative "gated on corpus maturity that does not exist today" with numbers:

- **Stage 0 (now, ~1.6/entity):** below every tested floor in every adjacent literature. Do not attempt any R3 pilot.
- **Stage 1 (~5-10 atoms/entity):** clears the field's own operational "tail-node" cutoff (Tail-GNN / meta-tail2vec convention: degree <= 5 = "tail") and approaches WN18RR's own average degree (~4.24-4.5). Worth a CHEAP pilot of KG-embedding or graph-SSL-style methods at this point, but expectations should stay modest: even WN18RR's best published KGE result (RotatE, MRR 0.476) is mediocre, not "0.85-semantic-cosine-quality" geometry, and there is an object-mismatch caveat below that likely caps this family's ceiling regardless of density.
- **Stage 2 (~100-400 atoms/entity):** clears the empirically-measured classical-distributional-semantics noise floor (Antoniak & Mimno 2018; Wendlandt et al. 2018). This is where teacher-free bootstrapping has its strongest literature support of any method family surveyed - real precedent (word2vec/GloVe/PPMI-SVD) for producing non-degenerate, semantically meaningful geometry from co-occurrence alone. This is the density regime worth targeting for a genuine "wean off BGE" attempt.

The size of the gap between Stage 1 and Stage 2 (2-5x vs 60-250x) matters for planning: a cheap corpus-enrichment pass (2-hop KB neighborhoods, gloss expansion - already flagged as option 4 in the design-correctness drill) might plausibly reach Stage 1 relatively soon, but Stage 2 is a much larger, likely multi-month, corpus-growth program. Do not let a Stage-1 pilot's mediocre-but-nonzero result be read as "R3 is working" - Stage 1's own literature ceiling (MRR ~0.48-class results) is far below what the encoder needs.

The two methods ranked highest for "viable now" (temporal-contiguity, self-distillation) are ranked there only because nobody has tested them at this sparsity in either direction - that is absence of evidence, not evidence of viability, and the calibration discipline requires treating it as such rather than as an implicit endorsement.

## Corrections to the pre-supplied research lead

The task brief asked me to verify, not assume, a specific number: "WN18RR has a notably low average entity degree (reported ~2.19 in some KGE literature)." **This number did not check out.** Computed from primary entity/triple counts (40,943 entities, 86,835 train triples) and cross-checked against two independent secondary sources, WN18RR's average degree is **~4.24-4.5** (2 x edges / nodes; also independently reported as 4.54), with a MODE degree of 2 (i.e. many entities have exactly 1-2 edges, which is likely the source of the ~2.19-ish folk number - it may be conflating mode/median with mean, or citing an unverified secondary source). FB15k-237's average degree computes to ~42.66 (also independently reported), confirming the qualitative claim (WN18RR is much sparser than FB15k-237) even though the specific ~2.19 figure is not the right number. This is exactly the kind of "verify the actual number, do not just assert it" discipline the task asked for, and it changes the comparison: the KB (1.6/entity) is not close to WN18RR's degree regime as the ~2.19 lead would have implied - it is meaningfully sparser than even WN18RR, which is itself already the sparse end of published KGE benchmarks.

## Cheap decisive test (zero-cost, disk-only, no experiment dispatch needed)

Compute the KB's actual entity-degree HISTOGRAM (not just the mean 1.6/entity), binned per the KG-Mixup convention: [0,1), [1,10), [10,50), [50,inf). This is a single query over already-materialized KB structure - no code changes, no GPU, no new experiment cell.

- **HARD-PASS (would upgrade the Stage-1 pilot from "worth trying" to "reasonably promising"):** >= 30% of entities have degree >= 5 AND median atoms/entity >= 3. This would mean the mean of 1.6 is being dragged down by a long tail of near-isolated entities while a meaningful subset already clears the field's own tail-cutoff - a materially different picture than "everything is uniformly starved."
- **HARD-FAIL (confirms the NOW verdict with no ambiguity; matches what the known mean of 1.6 predicts):** >= 70% of entities have degree <= 2. This is the expected outcome given the mean, and if confirmed, closes any residual "maybe the average is misleading" hope - the KB is uniformly in the worst-performing bin of every degree-stratified study surveyed, not just on average.

Run this before any future R3 pilot decision, and re-run it after any corpus-enrichment pass (2-hop neighborhoods, gloss expansion) lands, to track progress against the Stage 1 / Stage 2 milestone ladder above.

## Falsifiable predictions (HARD-PASS / HARD-FAIL)

**P1 (classical-distributional transfer to relational-KB co-occurrence).** IF/WHEN KB density reaches the literature stabilization zone (~100-400 atoms/entity), PPMI-SVD or word2vec-style embedding over the KB's own relation-path co-occurrence structure will produce non-trivial semantic-neighbor structure.
HARD-PASS: cosine-similarity gap between true concept pairs and shuffled-label controls, measured at that density, is >= 2x the gap measured at CURRENT density on the identical metric.
HARD-FAIL: no measurable gap over the shuffled baseline even at that density - this would mean relational-KB co-occurrence is not a valid token-corpus analog at all, refuting the entire classical-distributional-transfer premise (not just a density problem, a category problem).

**P2 (degree-stratification literature transfers to this KB).** Entities in the KG-Mixup-style [0,1) and [1,10) bins will show materially worse neighbor / retrieval-mass availability than entities in higher bins, mirroring the published within-benchmark degree-bias finding.
HARD-PASS: bin-conditional atom-richness differs in the same direction KG-Mixup found (low bins strictly worse).
HARD-FAIL: no bin effect found - would suggest this KB's structure is atypical relative to every degree-bias study surveyed, which would itself be a notable (and separately worth investigating) finding.

**P3 (self-distillation / temporal-contiguity remain genuinely uncharted, not proven-safe).** Neither method family has ANY literature precedent below degree ~2.84 (Citeseer, graph-SSL) or average degree 13.77 (BGRL/ogbn-arxiv, graph-native self-distillation) respectively.
HARD-PASS (for the eventual-viability claim in the milestone ladder): once corpus density clears those regimes AND a pilot is run, non-degenerate geometry emerges (no dimensional/complete collapse, measurable distinct-cluster structure).
HARD-FAIL: a pilot at that density still collapses - this would falsify the density-gating hypothesis itself and point to relational STRUCTURE TYPE (not raw count) as the actual bottleneck, a materially different and more expensive problem to solve.

## Substrate-product implications

The "substrate stands alone / no external LLM" long-term principle is NOT refuted by this drill - it remains the correct architectural north star, and the brain-grounding argument for R3 (internal teacher, no external oracle) is unchanged. What changes is the TIMELINE and the MILESTONE STRUCTURE: this drill converts an open-ended "someday, once the corpus matures" into a measurable ladder (Stage 0 -> Stage 1 at ~5-10/entity -> Stage 2 at ~100-400/entity), with a free, disk-only test (the degree histogram above) to track progress against it. Practically, this means:

1. Keep BGE distillation as the pragmatic bridge for LONGER than a purely qualitative reading of "gated on corpus maturity" would have suggested - the size of the Stage 2 gap (60-250x) is a genuinely large lift, likely a multi-month corpus-growth program, not a near-term pivot. This reinforces, rather than second-guesses, the D1 rescue (decouple sparsify, fix the current FULL failure with the dense-teacher path) as the correct immediate move, with R3 firmly sequenced behind it.
2. Corpus-enrichment work (2-hop KB neighborhoods, gloss expansion) should be reframed from a "nice-to-have densification" into an explicit prerequisite workstream for the no-external-LLM roadmap, with the degree-histogram test above as its own measurable checkpoint, independent of and cheaper than any encoder experiment.
3. When a Stage 1 pilot eventually becomes worth running, calibrate expectations honestly: even the best literature comparator at that density (WN18RR RotatE, MRR 0.476) is a mediocre result by the standards of what the encoder needs (0.85 semantic cosine), and KG-embedding-class objectives target relational-pattern prediction, not open-domain semantic similarity - a likely objective-family mismatch independent of density. The Stage 2 target (classical distributional semantics) is the method family with the correct OBJECTIVE (similarity geometry from co-occurrence) as well as the correct literature-precedent shape, and is the one worth actually building toward for a genuine wean-off-BGE attempt.

## Intuitive summary

What we asked: can the substrate teach itself the meaning of concepts using only its own sparse knowledge base (about 1.6 known facts per concept, on average), with no help from an outside AI model - and if not yet, how much more knowledge would it need before that becomes possible?

What we found: no. Not because the idea is wrong in principle - the brain itself does something like this (the hippocampus teaches the slower "thinking" part of the brain, with no outside teacher) - but because every method the field of AI research has ever tried this with, in any domain, has needed noticeably more raw material per concept than we currently have. Some methods (the classical "count how often things appear together" family, which is the best-understood and most mature) have an actual published number: real research shows you need on the order of 100 to 400 facts per concept before the resulting picture of "what means what" stops looking like random noise. We currently have 1.6 - somewhere between 60 and 250 times too little for that specific method. Other, newer methods (like a system that gradually teaches a slowly-improving copy of itself) have never even been tested at anything close to this thin a knowledge base in any published research anywhere, in any field - so we genuinely do not know if they would work here, we only know nobody has checked at this thinness before.

Why it matters: this doesn't kill the long-term goal of a substrate that doesn't depend on an outside AI model for its sense of meaning - that goal is still the right one, and still grounded in how the brain actually works. What it does is turn a vague "we're not ready yet" into a concrete checklist: grow the knowledge base to roughly 5-10 facts per concept before even a cheap first experiment is worth running, and to roughly 100-400 facts per concept before a real, full attempt to drop the outside AI model entirely has a fair chance of working. That second number is a much bigger lift than it might have sounded like before this drill - likely a sustained, multi-month effort of enriching the knowledge base, not something to attempt in the next work cycle.

Where this leaves the project: the existing plan - keep using the outside AI model as a temporary stand-in for now, fix the immediate technical problem with that approach separately, and treat "teach itself" as a long-term target to grow into - was already the right call, and this research makes that call MORE clearly correct, not less. The one new, concrete, zero-cost thing worth doing soon is a simple count: check how many concepts in the knowledge base currently have five or more known facts versus two or fewer. That single check, which requires no new experiments and no new code, tells us exactly where we stand on the path toward the first (cheap, modest-expectations) milestone, and should be re-checked every time the knowledge base grows, so progress toward self-teaching becomes something we can actually track rather than something we just hope for.
