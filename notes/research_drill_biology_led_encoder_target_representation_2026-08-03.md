## 1. How cortex earns conceptual/lexical meaning, and what structure results

**Lead with evidence, three families, adjudicated:**

**(i) Error-driven / predictive accounts — robust as MECHANISM, less direct as semantic-content theory.** Friston's free-energy principle (Friston 2010, *Nat Rev Neurosci*, "The free-energy principle: a unified brain theory?") frames cortical learning generally as hierarchical prediction-error minimization; its extension to lexical semantics specifically is more generalization-by-analogy than a directly-tested theory of word meaning. The concrete, directly-tested case for language is the N400 ERP component, now well-documented as indexing semantic prediction error during comprehension (recent unification: Michaelov et al., 2025, *PLOS Biology*, showing sharpening and genuine prediction-error signals operate at different hierarchical levels in real speech comprehension). The clearest model of meaning ITSELF being built by error-driven learning (not stored similarity) is the discriminative-learning line: Naive/Linear Discriminative Learning (Baayen, Ramscar and colleagues), built directly on the Rescorla-Wagner rule — word meaning as cue-outcome associations shaped purely by prediction error, which generalizes to novel combinations by construction. Rogers & McClelland (2004, *Semantic Cognition: A Parallel Distributed Processing Approach*, MIT Press) is the classic PDP account: graded conceptual structure emerges from error-driven weight adjustment over experience, and — importantly for (D) below — this model treats category-specific deficit patterns as an EMERGENT consequence of training statistics, not evidence for hard-wired category modules.

**(ii) Experiential/componential vs. sensorimotor-grounding accounts — componential wins, strong embodiment is contested.** Binder et al. (2016, *Cognitive Neuropsychology*, 33(3-4), 130-174, DOI 10.1080/02643294.2016.1147426 — venue verified as Cognitive Neuropsychology, NOT Cerebral Cortex) proposes ~65 experiential attributes (sensory, motor, spatial, temporal, affective, social) each grounded in a known cortical processing system, rated by human participants per concept. Barsalou's Perceptual Symbol Systems (1999, *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*, 22, 577-660) is the theoretically-richer embodied-simulation account (concepts as reactivated sensorimotor states). The REPLICATION CONTROVERSY is real and should be weighted: Arévalo et al. (2019, *Scientific Reports*, "Neurosurgical lesions to sensorimotor cortex do not impair action verb processing") found focal sensorimotor lesion patients perform normally on action-verb semantic tasks; a 2026 three-level meta-analysis (*Behavioral Sciences*) and a 2017 review both report semantic processing of action words does not reliably depend on motor-cortex activation, with a 2026 commentary explicitly calling this an active "replication crisis for embodied action semantics." Net: STRONG embodiment (motor cortex NECESSARY for verb meaning) is not well supported; WEAK/graded embodiment (motor regions engaged, modulated, but not necessary) is defensible. Binder-style componential features are distinct from strong embodiment claims — they are multi-system (not motor-cortex-only) and empirically survive where strong embodiment does not.

**(iii) Pure distributional/relational accounts — excellent for similarity, weaker for inference.** Firth's "you shall know a word by the company it keeps" is a real quote, but its standard retelling as a clean distributional-semantics manifesto is itself contested by historians of linguistics (Firth's actual framework was pragmatic/situational). Huth, de Heer, Griffiths, Theunissen, Gallant (2016, *Nature*, 532, 453-458, "Natural speech reveals the semantic maps that tile human cerebral cortex" — DOI verified) shows voxel-wise, cross-subject-consistent distributed semantic topography spanning temporal/parietal/prefrontal cortex — strong evidence meaning is distributed/graded, not localized-symbolic, but this is a finding about SIMILARITY-STRUCTURE topography, not about inference/composition.

**(D) Dissociation evidence bearing directly on inference vs. mere similarity:** Patterson, Nestor & Rogers (2007, *Nat Rev Neurosci*, 8, 976-987, "Where do you know what you know?") — semantic dementia shows graded, feature-type-linked degradation (losing specific/subordinate attributes before superordinate category information), directly supporting a componential/experiential-feature account over a monolithic similarity space. Classic property-induction literature (Sloman 1993 feature-based induction model vs. Osherson et al. 1990 similarity-coverage model) shows taxonomic/conceptual-relation structure predicts inductive-argument strength better than raw featural/surface similarity — i.e., INFERENCE draws on structured conceptual relations, not surface similarity. Representational-similarity-analysis work comparing experiential (Binder/Fernandino-style) vs. distributional vectors against fMRI patterns (2017, *Cerebral Cortex*) found experiential features uniquely predict fusiform/angular-gyrus similarity patterns where pure distributional vectors do not — direct neural evidence that experiential/componential content carries information distributional co-occurrence alone lacks. On the modeling side, static/distributional embeddings are independently documented (ACL/arXiv compositional-generalization literature) to specifically struggle with compositional, multi-way relational generalization.

**Bottom-line read (flagged as synthesis/interpretation, moderate confidence, deflated):** No single family is sufficient alone. Distributional statistics (Huth-style) capture broad similarity/relatedness robustly and are cortex-wide, but are demonstrably weaker at supporting compositional/relational INFERENCE. A componential/experiential-feature layer (Binder/Fernandino) carries additional, brain-verified information that pure co-occurrence lacks, and this is the layer that predicts fusiform/AG neural patterns and supports property induction. The ACQUISITION MECHANISM with the strongest direct evidence is error-driven/predictive learning (discriminative learning, PDP weight adjustment, N400-indexed prediction error). Sensorimotor grounding as a NECESSARY substrate is the weakest-supported strong claim and is actively challenged by lesion/replication-failure data — treat it as a real but graded, non-necessary contributor, not the load-bearing mechanism.

## 2. How the brain represents and infers event relations

**(A) Event Segmentation Theory (Zacks & Swallow).** Verified citations: Zacks & Swallow (2007, *Current Directions in Psychological Science*, 16, 80-84); Zacks, Speer, Swallow, Braver, Reynolds (2007, *Psychological Bulletin*, 133(2), 273-293); Kurby & Zacks (2008, *TICS*, 12(2), 72-79); Speer, Zacks & Reynolds (2007, *Psychological Science*, 18, 449-455, "Human Brain Activity Time-Locked to Narrative Event Boundaries"); Reynolds, Zacks & Braver (2007, *Cognitive Science*, 31(4), 613-643, "A Computational Model of Event Segmentation From Perceptual Prediction"). Mechanism (robust, well-replicated): comprehension maintains a working event model that predicts what happens next; when prediction error transiently spikes, the model updates — an event boundary. **Critically for narrative specifically**, Speer et al. (2007) found reader-identified boundaries in brief narratives were time-locked to transient BOLD increases in posterior medial cortex (precuneus, posterior cingulate, retrosplenial), superior/anterior temporal cortex, and right middle frontal gyrus — and this activity tracked SITUATIONAL changes (character goals, causes, spatial/temporal shifts), not surface lexical/sentence boundaries. The Reynolds-Zacks-Braver (2007) computational model formalizes this: an RNN over perceptual/event features, with boundaries defined by transient increases in sequential-prediction error — i.e., the predictive-coding mechanism from Q1 operates natively at this grain in the brain's own model of event cognition.

**(B) Trabasso & van den Broek causal networks.** Verified: Trabasso & van den Broek (1985, *Journal of Memory and Language*, 24, 612-630, "Causal thinking and the representation of narrative events"). Stories are represented as causal networks of goal-action-outcome nodes linked by antecedent-consequent relations. Central, heavily-replicated result: whether an event lies on the main causal chain, and its number of causal connections, predicts recall probability, summarization inclusion, and importance ratings — and does so OVER AND ABOVE serial position or surface story-grammar category. This is the single strongest piece of evidence that causal-relation effects are computed over a structured event/proposition graph, not raw text order.

**(C) Zwaan & Radvansky situation models / event-indexing.** Verified: Zwaan & Radvansky (1998, *Psychological Bulletin*, 123, 162-185); Zwaan, Langston & Graesser (1995, *Psychological Science*, 6, 292-297). Readers track five indices: space, time, causation, intentionality/goal, and protagonist/entity identity. Reading-time studies show discontinuities on temporal, causal, goal, and protagonist dimensions reliably slow reading (an index-update cost); spatial-discontinuity effects are weaker/less consistent than originally predicted — a genuine, flagged nuance in the model's original claim.

**(D) Mentalizing/ToM circuitry for unstated goals.** Saxe & Kanwisher (2003, *NeuroImage*) — bilateral TPJ responds selectively to reasoning about others' mental content; CONTESTED by later work (e.g. a 2008 *Cerebral Cortex* critique) arguing TPJ activity may partly reflect attentional reorienting rather than pure ToM — flag as unresolved, not settled. Mar (2011, *Annual Review of Psychology*, 62, 103-134, "The Neural Bases of Social Cognition and Story Comprehension") — ALE meta-analysis finds a core mentalizing network (mPFC/dmPFC, bilateral TPJ, precuneus/PCC, temporal poles) consistently engaged specifically by STORY-based ToM tasks, substantially overlapping the default-mode network. Ferstl & von Cramon's narrative-coherence fMRI series shows DMN/dmPFC tracking discourse coherence even without explicit mental-state content — an open question (contested) whether "coherence-building" and "mentalizing" are the same mechanism or two overlapping ones.

**Bottom-line read on representational grain (confidence: high for "not word-level"; moderate-high for "discrete bound-predicate structure" specifically):** The evidence converges on EVENT/PROPOSITION-LEVEL bound structure (agent-action-goal-outcome roles), not word-level lexical co-occurrence, and not undifferentiated whole-episode gist. **Strongest single piece of evidence:** Trabasso & van den Broek (1985) — causal-connectivity in the goal/action/outcome graph predicts recall/importance over and above serial/surface position, meaning the causal-relatedness computation operates on a structured event representation, not token order. This triangulates with Speer et al. (2007): neural event-boundary signals are time-locked to SITUATIONAL feature changes (goal shifts, causal shifts) embedded in continuous prose, not to lexical surprisal — the boundary-detection substrate is reading the situation model, not the token stream. Mentalizing regions (mPFC/TPJ/precuneus) provide the additional unstated-goal/intention-inference layer operating on top of that event structure. Open/contested points (report honestly, do not oversell): whether TPJ engagement is ToM-specific vs. partly attentional; whether coherence-processing and mentalizing are one mechanism or two.

### Lock-compatibility question — both sides, then a flagged recommendation

**This is Director's call, not mine to decide unilaterally. I give both arguments and my own lean, clearly flagged as advisory.**

**Argument FOR allowing Binder-style norms as supplied data (same category as a dictionary):** The Binder table is a fixed lookup of human behavioral RATINGS (collected via Mechanical Turk), structurally analogous to WordNet synsets or a dictionary — a static resource, not a trained mechanism. Using it as a SUPERVISION SIGNAL still requires the substrate's OWN error-driven learning process to do the work of encoding, and — critically for this drill's actual frontier — the substrate's OWN binding/composition machinery must still do all the work of composing lexical content into event structures and inferring SATISFY/THWART/CAUSE relations from them. The norms supply nothing about events, goals, or causal structure; they only supply a richer per-concept feature target than raw co-occurrence. This is arguably no different in kind from supplying a dictionary definition to supervise word-sense disambiguation, which the charter already treats as allowed "knowledge."

**Argument AGAINST (counts as the forbidden shortcut):** The entire point of the "no borrowed embeddings" lock is that a borrowed vector encoding "meaning" pre-solves exactly the problem the substrate is meant to earn: WHICH dimensions of variation matter for a concept, and how much. A Binder profile IS a semantic representation — 65 real numbers encoding direct human semantic judgments per concept. Training the encoder to regress onto this table means the encoder reproduces a pre-existing human-rated meaning vector rather than discovering the relevant dimensions from its own predictive errors on its own experience stream. By this argument Binder norms are arguably a MORE direct shortcut than GloVe/BGE, not less: GloVe/BGE at least derive from raw co-occurrence statistics (a description of distributional structure, not a direct meaning judgment), whereas Binder norms are literal human semantic ratings — closer to "meaning itself" than a distributional embedding is. Brain-faithful error-driven acquisition (per Q1's own strongest-evidenced mechanism) means concepts should be learned from the substrate's own predictive failures on its own experience, not supervised against an external human-rating oracle the substrate never itself generated prediction error against.

**My recommendation (advisory, flagged as Director's call):** Lean AGAINST using the raw Binder numeric table as a direct regression target for the core encoder — the "against" argument is stronger on the substrate's own stated first-principles (error-driven, own-experience-derived meaning), and using literal human semantic ratings as ground truth is a harder violation of that principle than a distributional embedding, not a softer one. A middle path that may satisfy both concerns: adopt the Binder-style FEATURE CATEGORIES (sensory/motor/affective/spatial/temporal/social distinctions) as an architectural/inductive-bias prior — i.e., structurally dedicate substrate sub-populations or channels to these known-cortical-system distinctions, mirroring what the brain's own known processing systems already partition — while leaving the VALUES entirely to the substrate's own error-driven process operating on its own experience stream. This supplies "knowledge of what dimensions exist" (arguably closer to the allowed dictionary/knowledge category) without supplying "what the values are" (the disallowed shortcut). The raw norms remain usable in a DIAGNOSTIC role only (as already used in `preregs/2026-07-28_scale_meaning_learn_arc_heldout_v3_grounding.md`'s existing `L_ground` framing) — to CHECK whether the substrate's own emergent representation aligns with brain-based dimensions post hoc, never as a training target.
