## 1. Brain: is inference generative recombination + (soft) test, not a smooth read-off?

**Yes, with an important asymmetry between the two dominant frameworks.** The hippocampal associative-inference / transitive-inference literature (Bunsey & Eichenbaum 1996 paradigm; Zeithamova, Schlichting & Preston program) splits into exactly two mechanisms, neither of which is a continuous geometric read-off:

- **Encoding-based integration**: when a new pair (BC) overlaps with a prior pair (AB) through the shared item B, the overlap triggers hippocampal **pattern completion** — a discrete, attractor-basin event, not a smooth interpolation — that reactivates the AB trace *during* BC encoding. The reactivated AB is then encoded together with BC into a single integrated ABC trace. Right-hippocampal activation during BC encoding is elevated specifically on trials where the later AC inference is correct, and this reactivation (measured via multivoxel pattern analysis) predicts inference success [Zeithamova/Preston line; Schlichting & Preston, *Nat. Commun.* 2015, "Learning-related representational changes reveal dissociable integration and separation signatures"; Frontiers review "The hippocampus and inferential reasoning: building memories to navigate future decisions" (Zeithamova, Dominick, Preston 2012, *Front. Hum. Neurosci.*)].
- **Retrieval-based integration**: separated AB and BC representations are kept apart at encoding and are instead retrieved **in parallel at decision time**, flexibly recombined on-the-fly to answer the novel AC query. This is a generative act performed at test time, not baked into storage.

Both are **generative recombination of discrete stored elements**, gated by an event (pattern completion / parallel retrieval), not a continuous surface being smoothly interpolated. This directly supports the session's unifying principle: geometric smoothing cannot manufacture new discrete structure, but discrete recombination of *already-bound* elements can.

**Generative replay** extends this to an explicitly generative, propose-then-select account: "Generative replay underlies compositional inference in the hippocampal-prefrontal circuit" (Cell, 2023) reports that hippocampal replay assembles elements into novel compounds never directly experienced (e.g., "shortcuts" between separately learned trajectories), and frames each replay sequence as **a hypothesis about a possible configuration of elements** — i.e., propose-a-candidate is literally how the paper's authors describe replay's function, with the hippocampal-PFC circuit doing compositional inference and hypothesis testing. (Note: full text was not fetchable — 403 — so this is corroborated at the abstract/press-summary level only, not verified against primary figures. Treat as directionally strong, not certain in mechanistic detail.)

**Is there a "test"/verification step in biology, or is it pure generation?** This is the honest gap. The classic transitive-inference/associative-inference literature does not describe an internal verifier that checks a candidate AC link against ground truth before it is "accepted" — correctness is measured behaviorally (did the animal/human answer right), not computed internally by the hippocampus. The closest thing to an internal verifier is:
- **Novelty and reward-prediction-error (RPE) signaling** via VTA dopamine, which modulates which replay content gets reinforced/consolidated and even where replay is spatially localized (Duvelle et al., "Spatial localization of hippocampal replay requires dopamine signaling," *eLife* 2024/2025 preprint; chemogenetic silencing of VTA dopamine neurons produces aberrant, reverse-ordered replay when reward is unchanged — i.e., dopamine gates which generated sequences are treated as "real"/consolidated).
- **Prediction-error-driven memory updating**: "Prediction errors disrupt hippocampal representations and update episodic memories" (*PNAS* 2021) — mismatch between a generated/expected trace and actual experience triggers representational change, functioning as an implicit correctness signal.

**Conclusion for Q1**: biology's mechanism is generate (pattern-completion-triggered or parallel-retrieval recombination) + a **soft, scalar, implicit** verifier (novelty/RPE gating), not a hard binary oracle check. This matters for Q3/BUILD RECOMMENDATION below — biology under-specifies the verifier the substrate actually needs.

## 2. Abduction / generate-and-test: concrete mechanism, and how it differs from smooth read-off

**Formal logic side.** Abduction is standardly defined in contrast to induction and deduction as: given an observation O and a background theory T, find a hypothesis H such that T ∪ H ⊨ O (H explains O), then verify H is consistent (T ∪ H is satisfiable) and preferably minimal/best among competing explanations (Denecker & Kakas, "Abduction in Logic Programming"). Abductive Logic Programming (ALP) operationalizes this as: enumerate/propose candidate abducible facts, check consistency against the logic program's integrity constraints, and rank by minimality or probability.

**Zhou Zhi-Hua's Abductive Learning (ABL)** (NeurIPS 2019, "Bridging Machine Learning and Logical Reasoning by Abductive Learning") gives the cleanest generate-and-test **loop** structurally: (1) a neural perception model predicts primitive symbolic facts from raw input; (2) **abduction** revises the predicted facts using a symbolic domain knowledge base so the revised fact-set becomes logically consistent/explains the target; (3) the revised (corrected) facts are fed back as training labels to retrain the perception model. The load-bearing move is that abduction is genuinely a **search-and-check** step (search over a space of fact-revisions, check logical consistency against the KB) — not a differentiable/smooth operation. Known failure mode: the abduction search space can explode ("nondeterminism of abduction... prohibitively large abduction space," motivating follow-up work like "Curriculum Abductive Learning," arXiv 2505.12275, which tames the search by curriculum-ordering the abduction difficulty).

**The most directly relevant concrete precedent — vector-symbolic abductive reasoning ("Rel-SAR", arXiv 2501.11896, fetched in full).** This is a VSA system (same algebraic family as the substrate's own bind/unbind) solving Raven's-matrices-style abstract relational reasoning by abduction:
1. Perception maps raw input to an estimated hyperdimensional representation (SHDR).
2. **Unbind** operations decompose the estimated representation into constituent attribute vectors.
3. A learned rule-generator proposes candidate **relation-function parameters** (operator powers) — this is literally "propose a candidate relation."
4. **Verification**: the candidate rule is forward-applied across all context pairs/triples; the rule is scored by how consistently its predicted output vector matches the *actual* observed pattern across the whole context (discrete similarity/consistency check, not a smooth loss).
5. The rule with the best consistency score is selected and its **inverse** (unbind again) is used to generate/infer the missing element.
6. Selection among final candidates uses cosine similarity against candidate answer panels.

The authors explicitly frame this as **closer to symbolic constraint satisfaction than to gradient-based/smooth scoring** — i.e., the verification step is a discrete accept/reject (or rank) operation over candidate structured hypotheses, structurally different from a continuous decoder reading a point off a manifold. This is a near-identical shape to what Q5 (fair-test design) below proposes for the substrate: bind/unbind to propose, then a discrete consistency check to verify.

**How this differs from the failed smooth read-off, concretely**: a smooth/geometric method (in KG-completion terms, an embedding method like TransE/DistMult/ComplEx) computes a fixed vector-space transform and reads off a plausibility SCORE for a query triple directly from geometry (distance/inner-product) — there is no explicit candidate-hypothesis object being generated and separately checked; the "hypothesis" and its "score" are the same computation, entangled, and interpolate smoothly between seen configurations. A generate-and-test method separates these into two decoupled stages: (a) **propose** a discrete symbolic object (a rule, a relation-composition, an abduced fact) from a combinatorial space, and (b) **verify** that object against an independent criterion (logical consistency, statistical support/confidence on the actual graph, or an external oracle). This decoupling is exactly what lets it manufacture genuinely new discrete structure instead of only interpolating within the span of what geometry already encodes — which is the diagnosis the session already reached for why smoothing failed.

## 3. The verifier / active intervention: is it the load-bearing piece?

**Strong yes, and there is now a very close literature match to the substrate's own finding.** The knowledge-graph-completion field has independently rediscovered the exact same fork as this session:

- **Embedding-based (geometric/continuous) KG completion** — "provide reasonable accuracy and scalability for transductive reasoning but do NOT provide good results for inductive reasoning involving entities/patterns not previously seen" (survey synthesis, corroborated across multiple 2023-2024 sources on rule- vs. embedding-based KG completion). This is the KG-lit twin of the substrate's additive-geometric-codes / structure-aware-encoder / low-rank-spectral-routing HFs all failing at inductive relational inference.
- **Rule-based / rule-induction methods** — AMIE (association rule mining with **support and confidence** scores over the existing graph), Neural LP (differentiable rule learning), RNNLogic (a rule **generator** + a reasoning **predictor**, trained via an EM loop: E-step selects high-quality rules by scoring them against the KG with the predictor; M-step retrains the generator on the selected rules) — these ARE generate-and-test mechanisms, and the literature credits them specifically with supporting **inductive** reasoning to entities/relation patterns absent from training, which pure embeddings cannot do.

The verifier in these systems is not exotic: it's literally "does this candidate composed-relation, when checked against the actual graph (support = how often it holds; confidence = fraction of instantiations where it holds), clear a threshold." That's an internal, cheap, fully automatable exogenous check — no need for a human-in-the-loop oracle. This is important: **the verifier does not have to be as expensive as a math/code/sim oracle** to be load-bearing; for a first cell, "does the composed candidate relation actually appear elsewhere in the stored graph, scored by frequency/consistency" is a legitimate, substrate-internal verifier, distinct from (and cheaper than) the arc's deeper external-oracle target (math/code/sim, dual-number toehold).

**Causal discovery reinforces the theoretical necessity of intervention, one level up.** A body of work (Active Learning of Causal Networks with Intervention Experiments, He & Geng, JMLR 2008; "Active Learning for Optimal Intervention Design in Causal Models," Nature Machine Intelligence 2023; "Interventions, Where and How? Experimental Design for Causal Models at Scale," ICLR 2022) establishes formally that **passive/observational data alone cannot in general identify a causal structure past its Markov-equivalence class** — some edge orientations/relations are provably unresolvable without intervening and observing the outcome. This generalizes (at a more abstract level than KG rule-mining) the same point: there is a class of relational-structure questions that passive/observational (geometric-smoothing-equivalent) methods cannot answer even in principle, and only an active check (an intervention, or its cheaper analogue — an explicit verification query) resolves them. This is the theoretical backbone for why the substrate's failures were not a tuning problem but a **class-of-method** problem.

**Conclusion for Q3**: the verifier is the load-bearing piece. Biology's verifier is soft/implicit (novelty, RPE); the ML precedent that demonstrably solves the exact "infer new relations beyond the stored graph" problem uses a hard, cheap, self-contained verifier (support/confidence against the existing graph, or logical consistency). Build the hard kind first.

## 4. How Candidate B composes with Candidate A (denser knowledge)

**Complementary, with a named literature precedent for the interaction, not just an assertion.** IterE ("Iteratively Learning Embeddings and Rules for Knowledge Graph Reasoning," WWW 2019) and RulE (arXiv 2210.14905) both formalize a bidirectional loop:
- Rules (generate-and-test, Candidate B) are learned/mined **from** the embedding space or the existing graph and are then used to infer new triples.
- Those newly-inferred triples densify the graph, and are fed back to improve the embeddings (Candidate A gets richer) — the paper's own framing: "deductive rules can infer additional triples for sparse entities and help embedding learning methods encode them better," while "embeddings encoded with rich semantics can turn rule learning from discrete graph search into vector-space calculation, reducing the search space significantly."

Concretely, for the substrate: (a) B *needs* A because a generator that composes bind/unbind chains over an empty or sparse graph has almost nothing to recombine — the combinatorial space of 2-hop and 3-hop compositions scales with edge count, and the verifier needs enough existing instances of a candidate relation elsewhere in the graph to compute a meaningful support/confidence score (a support statistic computed from n=1 example is not a statistic). (b) A *needs* B because passively adding more edges (density-subset HF, relation-richness HF — already tried and failed) does not by itself create NEW relation types or NEW inferential reach; it only gives B more raw material once B exists. Put plainly: **A without B still cannot infer beyond the ingested graph (already empirically shown this session); B without A has too little to recombine/verify against (a generalizable prediction from the KG-rule-mining literature: rule-mining performance degrades sharply on sparse KGs — this is a well-known practical failure mode of AMIE-class systems, "iteratively learning embeddings and rules... to address the generalization... limitation" was motivated specifically by rule mining struggling on sparse entities).** They are genuinely complementary, not alternative bets on the same mechanism.

## 5. Fair-test design pointers (not a full cell design — pointers only, per role contract)

A minimal generate-and-test relational-inference cell, informed by the AMIE/RNNLogic/Rel-SAR shape above, would need at minimum:
- **Propose**: enumerate candidate composed relations by chaining the substrate's existing CG bind/unbind operator over 2-hop (and optionally 3-hop) paths through the stored graph — i.e., generate a candidate "A --r_new--> C" hypothesis from observed "A --r1--> B --r2--> C" instances, exactly as AMIE enumerates Horn-rule bodies.
- **Verify**: score each candidate composed relation by support/confidence computed from OTHER instances of that same (r1, r2) -> r_candidate composition pattern already present elsewhere in the graph (does this composition, when it has occurred before, reliably predict a real edge?) — a cheap, substrate-internal, non-oracle verifier, matching the AMIE/RNNLogic verifier design.
- **Measure**: held-out inference accuracy — withhold a subset of true relations from the graph, run propose+verify, check whether the withheld relations are recovered (this is precisely the KG-completion "inductive/transductive split" evaluation protocol used across all the cited rule-mining papers).
- **Must-fail control (non-negotiable, per prior discriminator-must-fire discipline)**: run the identical propose step but replace the verifier with a broken/random one (accept candidates at random, or score by a signal known NOT to reflect real graph structure — e.g., candidate-vector norm or a shuffled-label confidence). This control MUST fail to recover held-out relations above chance; if it does not fail, the mechanism is not actually doing generate-and-test — it has silently degenerated back into a smooth/geometric read-off in disguise (the exact failure class this arc is trying to escape), per [[feedback-saturation-vacuous-smoke]] / [[feedback-discriminator-must-be-telemetry-sensitive]].
- **HARD-PASS** candidate criterion: held-out relation recovery with generator+real-verifier is materially above both (i) the must-fail random-verifier control and (ii) the best prior passive/geometric HF's held-out relational-inference number, AND recovery rate scales monotonically with graph density (support Q4's complementarity claim — thin out the graph and recovery should degrade, richer graph should improve it).
- **HARD-FAIL** candidate criterion: recovery is statistically indistinguishable from the random-verifier control (verifier isn't discriminating), OR recovery does not improve with graph density (breaks the A+B complementarity story and suggests B is not knowledge-hungry the way predicted, which would itself be a valuable falsification), OR recovery is achievable ONLY on relation-compositions already directly observed (i.e., not real induction, just recall of stored 2-hop chains restated — this is the "trivial fan-out" failure mode already flagged elsewhere in this program's audit history).

This is intentionally pointer-level, not a cell spec — exp_dev owns turning this into pre-registered code.

## BUILD RECOMMENDATION

Build a **propose-verify loop on top of the existing CG bind/unbind operator**: propose candidate composed relations by chaining known edges through bind/unbind (exactly as AMIE enumerates rule bodies and Rel-SAR proposes rule-operator candidates via unbind-decomposition), verify each candidate with a cheap **substrate-internal support/confidence statistic** (frequency with which the same composition-pattern holds elsewhere in the graph) rather than attempting to reproduce biology's soft novelty/RPE-style verifier or reaching straight for an expensive external math/code/sim oracle. This is expected to succeed where smooth read-off failed because it is a genuinely different computational class — decoupled propose (discrete, combinatorial, uses bind/unbind, which composition already works on this substrate per Cross-thread synthesis below) + verify (discrete accept/reject against real graph statistics) — rather than a single continuous function computing "plausibility" and "content" as the same entangled operation. The mechanism should be built with Candidate A (denser knowledge) as a co-requisite, not sequenced strictly after it: run the generate-and-test cell on the current graph density AND on an artificially densified variant to get the density-scaling curve in the same cycle (this both tests the mechanism and produces the A-vs-B interaction data point in one shot).

**A vs B call: COMPLEMENTARY, not competing.** Do not treat this as "which one do we pick" — the literature evidence (IterE/RulE loop) plus the substrate's own empirical pattern (denser-knowledge HFs alone didn't unlock inference; composition-of-knowns already works) both point to B being the mechanism and A being both B's fuel and B's downstream beneficiary.

## Substrate-product implications

A working generate-and-test relational-inference cell would be the substrate's first demonstrated capability to produce NEW facts beyond what was ingested — this is the single most product-differentiating claim available if it clears its HARD-PASS bar (verifiably not achievable by pulling harder on any smoothing lever, and not reducible to trivial recall). Framed honestly: this is a discovery/hypothesis-generation primitive (propose candidate facts, self-verify against existing knowledge, surface high-confidence ones for use or for external confirmation) — directly relevant to any product surface built on "what does this knowledge base imply that it doesn't explicitly say," with the built-in caveat (per must-fail-control discipline) that the very same mechanism, without a real verifier, produces confident-looking garbage — so the verifier's integrity is the product's integrity, not an implementation detail.

## Falsifiable predictions (HARD-PASS / HARD-FAIL) — consolidated

See Section 5 for the primary cell-level thresholds. Additional cross-cutting predictions:
- **HARD-PASS**: confidence/support score assigned by the verifier correlates with actual held-out correctness (higher-scored candidates are more often right) — this is the signature that verification is doing real epistemic work, not just window-dressing on a fixed generation process.
- **HARD-FAIL**: confidence/support score is uncorrelated (or anti-correlated) with held-out correctness — indicates the "verifier" is a post-hoc rationalizer, not a real check, and the mechanism should be rejected/rebuilt before further investment.
