## (a) HEADLINE

**The 1-hop cap has a precise, convergent mechanistic diagnosis across all four literatures, and it is NOT one thing — it is two independent, stackable limitations, both of which our one-shot k-NN readout is missing by construction.**

1. **No iterative settling.** The graded, far-reaching structure in biological semantic memory is not a property of a single feedforward/one-shot pass — the specific papers that show deep, graded, multi-hop structure (O'Connor, Cree & McRae 2009; Rogers et al. 2021 *eLife*) are recurrent attractor networks that **relax over many time-steps within a trial** (20-28 ticks in the cited models) before reaching a stable state, and the deep/graded signature only appears in that settled state, not in early-cycle activation. Notably, the *original* Rogers & McClelland (2004) hub-and-spoke model was **feedforward** — its famous graded coarse-to-fine structure is a property that emerges **across training epochs** (a learned weight geometry), not a within-trial recurrent dynamic. Our substrate's readout is a single k-NN propagation step: it is the direct artificial analog of a one-shot feedforward pass, not of either the recurrent-attractor route or the trained-weight-geometry route. This is the load-bearing structural reason for the diagnosis in bullet 2 below: our propagation evaluates only the k=1 term of a diffusion/random-walk expansion. Graph diffusion theory gives the exact reason this is a hard geometric-decay wall, not a soft one: on the graph Laplacian eigenbasis, every mode except the stationary one decays as a power of the spectral gap per step; a single step only ever realizes the first-order term, and reach is bounded by the (distance-increasing) effective resistance between seed and target — this is a mechanical, near-mathematically-certain consequence of using k=1, independent of anything substrate-specific.

2. **No compositional/binding structure.** Independently, the Fodor & Pylyshyn (1988) / Smolensky (1990) / Plate-Kanerva-Gayler VSA literature converges on: **multi-hop, systematic transfer of a property through a relation graph requires an explicit role-filler BINDING operation that can be chained (bind, then unbind, then bind again)** — not just similarity/bundling over a flat distributed code. Near-random, near-orthogonal atomic codes with no binding machinery support only similarity-based 1-hop retrieval; there is no "unbind chain" to traverse a second relational edge compositionally. No paper states this as a formally-named theorem for exactly "near-random codes cap at 1 hop" (flagged medium-confidence, inferred not directly cited) — but the mechanism is unanimous across every source in this literature and matches our finding precisely.

**Child vocabulary-spurt cascade adds a THIRD, complementary mechanism absent from both of the above: a genuine self-reinforcing accelerator, but it is not fast-mapping or cross-situational learning (both are one-shot/parallel, not compounding) — it is specifically the trained, generalizable SHAPE BIAS (Smith, Jones et al. 2002): an attentional/inductive-bias mechanism that, once acquired from a modest number of examples, makes every SUBSEQUENT word easier by supplying a reusable inductive template ("new object, similar shape to a known category -> same name"), not by having more relational edges to walk. This is a "learning to learn" cascade, not a graph-traversal-depth cascade — it changes the LEARNER, not the propagation depth of any single grounding event. Mutual exclusivity is a secondary, weaker, threshold-gated accelerator (onset ~100-200 known words). Syntactic bootstrapping is real but gated by prior grammatical scaffolding, not raw word count. The vocabulary-spurt's population-level explosiveness itself is best modeled as a percolation-like phase transition in a growing lexical network (Stella, Beckage, Brede & De Domenico 2018, *Sci Rep*, "explosive learning"), not smooth propagation depth increasing — i.e., part of what looks like "cascade" in children is a structural/network-growth phenomenon (new connections appearing) rather than existing connections carrying signal further.**

Net diagnosis: **our 1-hop cap is the simultaneous, overdetermined consequence of (i) evaluating diffusion at step k=1 instead of iterating/settling, and (ii) having no compositional binding operator to chain relational edges.** Both are independently well-evidenced fixes in the literature; neither requires abandoning the near-random-code substrate design. The developmental literature's clearest true "cascade" mechanism (trainable shape bias) maps onto a DIFFERENT lever than graph depth: it says the propagation MECHANISM itself (not just one query) can be made to improve with experience, which is a genuinely new buildable direction beyond deepening any single query's reach.

## (b) Cheap decisive test

No new architecture required for the first two directions below — both are readout-time changes over the EXISTING trained encoder and the EXISTING seed-grounding data from the just-landed Prediction-B measurement.

**Test 1 — iterative/recurrent settling readout vs one-shot k-NN (isolates mechanism 1).**
Replace the one-shot k-NN label-propagation readout with an iterated version: repeat propagation for T steps (T = 2, 4, 8, 16...) either as (a) repeated k-NN passes (discrete power-iteration analog of a heat kernel / PPR with decreasing restart probability), or (b) a proper attractor relaxation if a recurrent settling dynamic already exists anywhere in the stack. Measure near_acc(d1), far_acc(d3), and NEW: acc(d5), acc(d7) if the graph has that much diameter available. Compare the **decay curve shape** (not just endpoint accuracy) across T.
- **HARD-PASS**: the decay curve visibly lengthens with increasing T — i.e., acc(d3) and beyond rises measurably (>=10 points) as T increases from 1 to 8-16, while the scrambled-grounding control stays flat at every T (rules out the deepening being "just more noise reaching everywhere" rather than genuine structured spread).
- **HARD-FAIL**: acc(d3+) does not move with T (propagation saturates immediately, meaning the graph's local structure genuinely has no path-based information beyond 1 hop for this relation set), OR acc rises but the scrambled control ALSO rises with T (this would mean iterating just homogenizes everything toward the global mean — the over-smoothing failure mode the diffusion-theory lit-scan explicitly warns about; if this happens, mid-range T with an explicit stopping criterion, not maximal T, is the right target).
- Novel-synthesis P estimate: **P=0.40** (deflated; capped at 0.50) — this is the single highest-confidence, cheapest test given the recurrent-attractor literature is unanimous that this class of mechanism produces the deep/graded signature and no code change to the trained encoder is needed, only the readout.

**Test 2 — compositional/binding-chained propagation vs flat k-NN (isolates mechanism 2).**
For pairs of seeds connected by TWO relation-typed edges in series (e.g. seed -[is-a]-> intermediate -[has-property]-> target), test whether an explicit bind-then-unbind two-step composition (using the existing relational encoder's own bind/bundle primitives, if present, or a simple two-stage typed-edge traversal) recovers grounded-attribute signal at the target atom better than flat k-NN similarity alone reaches it.
- **HARD-PASS**: bind-chained 2-hop accuracy exceeds flat-similarity 2-hop accuracy by a pre-registered margin (e.g. >=15 points), and the gap WIDENS (not narrows) as hop count increases to 3.
- **HARD-FAIL**: no gap, or gap present at 2 hops but collapses by 3 hops (would suggest binding chains also decay, just with a longer but still-finite constant — informative, but not the "structural fix" hoped for).
- P estimate: **P=0.30** (deflated; capped at 0.50) — lower confidence than Test 1 because it requires the encoder to already have (or be retrofitted with) an actual chainable bind/unbind primitive, which may not currently exist in the readout path; this is more implementation-dependent.

## (c) Falsifiable predictions (HARD-PASS / HARD-FAIL)

**Prediction 1 — recurrent/iterative settling deepens reach (mechanism 1, addressed by Test 1 above).** Thresholds as stated. P=0.40 (deflated, capped 0.50).

**Prediction 2 — compositional binding-chaining deepens reach independent of iteration count (mechanism 2, addressed by Test 2 above).** Thresholds as stated. P=0.30 (deflated, capped 0.50).

**Prediction 3 — multi-channel triangulation is superadditive, not additive, for grounding depth.** Per the word-learning literature (cross-situational + social cues jointly, Frank/Yurovsky-style unified models; PubMed 28288392) and multisensory-integration superadditivity literature (redundant-signals effect), attaching a SECOND, structurally-independent grounding channel to the same seed set (e.g. a second real measured attribute, sourced independently of the first, such that the two channels are not derivable from one another) should produce more than the sum of each channel's individual reach.
  - Buildable cell: ground the same ~50-200 seed atoms with TWO independent numeric attributes (e.g. physical size AND weight, sourced from two different measured/logged data streams so they are not simply monotone-redundant), run the existing propagation, and compare combined-channel decay curve against each single-channel curve and against the naive sum of the two single-channel curves.
  - **HARD-PASS**: combined-channel far-hop accuracy (d3+) exceeds the SUM of the two single-channel far-hop accuracies (superadditive), with the gap increasing at farther hops (mirrors the multisensory-integration signature: nonlinear gain that grows, not shrinks, under harder/noisier conditions).
  - **HARD-FAIL**: combined performance is simply additive (equals the sum, no synergy) or subadditive (channels interfere/compete) — still useful information (multi-channel doesn't help beyond redundancy reduction), but refutes the "true cascade accelerant" framing.
  - P estimate: **P=0.35** (deflated, capped 0.50) — good analogical support from two independent literatures (word-learning, multisensory) but zero direct precedent in an HD/relational-graph artificial system.

**Prediction 4 — a trainable, reusable "shape-bias" analog (learned inductive template, not graph depth) accelerates SUBSEQUENT groundings, and this is a DIFFERENT lever than Predictions 1-3.** Per Smith/Jones/Landau's attentional-learning account: once a system has learned that "shared X implies shared name/property" for one small category, that learned regularity should transfer to speed up grounding of the NEXT, unrelated category, without needing deeper propagation on the ORIGINAL seed set at all.
  - Buildable cell: after grounding seed-set-1 and running propagation, extract whatever regularity the propagation mechanism implicitly discovered (e.g. which relation-types were most predictive of the grounded attribute), and test whether applying that discovered relation-type-weighting to a SECOND, disjoint seed-set-2 (never directly grounded together with set 1) grounds set 2 faster / with fewer seeds than set 1 needed.
  - **HARD-PASS**: seed-set-2 reaches the same near-hop accuracy as seed-set-1 did, using fewer seeds or fewer propagation steps (a measurable "learning to learn" transfer).
  - **HARD-FAIL**: no transfer — set 2 needs the same seed density/steps as set 1, meaning the mechanism is not extracting a reusable inductive bias, just running the same computation twice.
  - P estimate: **P=0.25** (deflated, capped 0.50; this is the least tested/most novel direction of the five, by far the most interesting if it works because it explains the "vocabulary EXPLOSION" acceleration signature specifically, not just deeper single-query reach).

**Prediction 5 — curriculum-ordered seeding produces a percolation-like threshold effect, not smooth improvement.** Per Stella et al. (2018) explosive-learning network model and Hills/Maouene/Riordan/Smith's connectivity-driven (not simple rich-get-richer) growth finding: if seeds are added in an order that maximizes new-community-bridging (rather than random order), reach should show a nonlinear jump once enough bridging seeds are present, rather than a smooth linear improvement with seed count.
  - Buildable cell: compare random-order seed addition vs bridging-maximizing-order seed addition (order chosen by, e.g., betweenness-centrality-style heuristic on the existing relation graph) at matched total seed-count checkpoints (10, 25, 50, 100, 200 seeds); track far-hop accuracy at each checkpoint.
  - **HARD-PASS**: bridging-order shows a discontinuous jump in far-hop accuracy at some checkpoint that random-order does not show at the same checkpoint (or shows only much later / more gradually).
  - **HARD-FAIL**: both orders show the same smooth curve — no percolation signature, meaning the effect (if real in children) doesn't transfer to this graph's connectivity structure, OR the graph is already past its percolation threshold at baseline density (a saturation-vacuous-test risk — must check that the RANDOM-order control is capable of showing a discontinuity too, i.e., that the graph is not too dense/sparse for percolation phenomena to be visible at these seed counts, before trusting a flat result).
  - P estimate: **P=0.30** (deflated, capped 0.50) — cheapest of the five to implement (pure reordering of an experiment already run), directly testable against already-collected Prediction-B data by just re-binning the existing seed set by a centrality heuristic post hoc, no new grounding data needed for a first pass.

**Honest structural caveat (per calibration discipline — do not oversell):** it is entirely possible that 1-hop is close to a hard floor for THIS SPECIFIC representational choice (near-random atomic codes with similarity-only readout, no binding operator) absent Predictions 1 and 2's specific fixes — the VSA/compositionality literature is unanimous that binding is what licenses hop-chaining, and our current substrate, by explicit design choice (near-random codes for capacity/collision-avoidance reasons per `[[reference-correlation-hurts-associative-store-capacity]]`), does not have that operator wired into the propagation readout today. If Test 1 AND Test 2 both HARD-FAIL, the honest conclusion is that deepening requires adding compositional binding machinery to the readout path as new construction, not a parameter/readout tweak — a materially bigger lift than either test above, and worth knowing early rather than iterating on T or hop-count hyperparameters indefinitely.

## (e) Substrate-product implications

1. **The shallow-1-hop result should be framed as "correctly diagnosed and quantified, with a known-mechanism fix path" rather than "a limitation"** — per house discipline (deflate our claims, not the ambition), the literature gives a clean, converging, two-part mechanistic explanation (no iteration + no binding), both independently addressable without abandoning the near-random-code design. This is a strong position: a genuine effect, correctly shaped, with textbook next moves.
2. **Prioritize Test 1 (iterative/recurrent readout) as the next buildable cell** — it is the cheapest (readout-time only, reuses existing trained encoder and already-collected Prediction-B seed/grounding data), has the highest P estimate of the five predictions, and the recurrent-attractor neuroscience literature is unusually direct and unanimous that this is the correct mechanism class for graded depth.
3. **Test 2 (binding-chain propagation) is the architecturally important test even if lower P** — a HARD-FAIL there (no chaining benefit) would be informative in itself (means whatever bind/bundle primitives exist in the current stack aren't doing real compositional work), and a HARD-PASS gives a concrete, near-random-code-compatible path to real multi-hop compositional inheritance, directly answering the "striped horse -> zebra-like" compositional-transfer case the drill asked about.
4. **Prediction 4 (trainable inductive-bias transfer / substrate "shape bias" analog) is the most novel and highest-payoff-if-true direction** — it is the one mechanism in the literature that explains an ACCELERATING cascade (not just a deeper single propagation), matching the child-vocabulary-explosion phenomenon the drill named directly. It is also the least tested (P=0.25) and should be treated as a genuine research bet, not a near-term build commitment.
5. **Prediction 5 (curriculum/bridging-order seeding) is nearly free to test first** — it can be evaluated by re-binning already-collected Prediction-B data by a centrality heuristic, with no new grounding pass required, and should be run before any new data-collection cell as a zero-cost first look at whether percolation-threshold framing applies to this graph's actual connectivity.
6. **Do not conflate "iterating more" with "getting deeper AND staying precise"** — the diffusion-theory lit-scan is explicit that over-iteration causes over-smoothing (everything converges to the same global average), which would look superficially like "deeper reach" but is actually a loss of discriminative signal. Any Test-1 implementation MUST monitor the scrambled-grounding control at every T value, not just the true-seed decay curve, exactly as this session's original measurement already did — the control is the thing that catches over-smoothing before it's mistaken for genuine cascade depth.

## Sharpest open question

**Is the 1-hop cap dominated by mechanism 1 (missing iteration) or mechanism 2 (missing binding) — or genuinely overdetermined by both equally?** This matters for build-order: if Test 1 alone (cheap, readout-only, reuses existing data) HARD-PASSes and extends reach substantially, mechanism 2's more invasive binding-chain construction may not be needed for near-term product value. If Test 1 HARD-FAILs (iteration just causes over-smoothing with no real depth gain, per the diffusion-theory lit-scan's explicit warning) while Test 2 HARD-PASSes, that would be the sharper and more interesting result: it would mean depth is NOT a settling/time-constant problem at all, but a representational-structure problem requiring compositional binding — a materially bigger but more architecturally honest fix, and would validate the near-random-code-plus-binding-operator design (rather than near-random-code-plus-more-iteration) as the right direction for the encoder's next capability increment. Both tests are cheap and run against already-collected data or trivial extensions of it — recommend running Test 1 and Prediction 5 (free re-binning) in the same cycle, since both use zero new data collection.
