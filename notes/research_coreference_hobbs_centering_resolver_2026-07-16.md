# Research: closing the coreference gap in glass-box reading — Hobbs' algorithm + Centering Theory as a rule-based (no-LLM) pronoun resolver

**Date:** 2026-07-16. **Filed by:** research (Sonnet lit-scan x3, Sonnet synthesis). **Trigger:** `notes/research_glass_box_reading_robust_parsing_ceiling_2026-07-16.md` named coreference as the ONE literature-wide failure mode that does not shrink with simpler prose and is unaddressed by every classical Open-IE technique reviewed; `experiments/exp_read_grow_foundation_realprose_glassbox_ie_v2.py` (the live parser) currently implements a correct-to-ABSTAIN behavior on pronoun-subject sentences with no in-sentence antecedent ("It eats the worm." -> `COREF_UNRESOLVED`) but has no resolution mechanism at all. This drill asks whether a classical, deterministic, glass-box resolver (Hobbs 1978 / Centering Theory) can convert that ABSTAIN into a correctly-bound triple for early-reader/curriculum prose, without dropping precision.

**Method:** 3 parallel Sonnet lit-scan sub-agents, live WebSearch with primary/secondary-source cross-checking, one lane each: (1) the psycholinguistics/biology of human pronoun resolution (Centering Theory, cue-based retrieval, the core cue set), (2) Hobbs' 1978 algorithm specifically (mechanics, accuracy, failure modes, glass-box status), (3) binding theory as a structural pre-filter + the Stanford multi-pass sieve system as a modern deterministic-coreference engineering precedent. All three lanes flagged inference vs. direct citation explicitly; that distinction is preserved below. I then read the live parser source (`exp_read_grow_foundation_realprose_glassbox_ie_v2.py`, the tagger/lexicon, and the actual coreference test rows in `PROSE_CORPUS`) to ground the synthesis in the substrate's actual current architecture rather than a generic recommendation.

## (a) HEADLINE

**The literature gives a small, well-precedented, fully deterministic recipe for local-antecedent pronoun resolution — but it is a discourse-level mechanism, not a sentence-level one, and the current parser has no discourse state at all. The gap is not "we lack an algorithm," it is "the pipeline has nowhere to run one." Closing it requires two separable pieces: (1) a minimal cross-sentence memory (a short ranked list of recently-mentioned nouns — the Cf list of Centering Theory, or equivalently Hobbs' "search back through prior sentences" — that does not exist in the current per-sentence `ie_extract(sentence)` function), and (2) a small agreement/type-filtered search over that memory (Hobbs' tree-search order collapses, in this register, to simple recency + subject-preference, because the corpus has no embedded clauses and only one pronoun position). Once (1) exists, (2) is genuinely cheap — under 100 lines, no new dependencies, fully rule-based. World-knowledge coreference (Winograd-schema-style genuine ties) remains, exactly as predicted, a permanent and CORRECT abstain.**

Human pronoun resolution in short discourse is not solved by a giant memorized model either — the psycholinguistics lane converges on a two-stage account (Garrod & Sanford's "bonding and resolution"): a fast, near-categorical **bonding** stage filters candidates by gender/number/animacy agreement, followed by a graded **resolution** stage that ranks the survivors by a small set of weighted cues — recency, grammatical-role/subject-preference (Gernsbacher & Hargreaves 1988's "first-mention advantage"; Centering Theory's Cf ranking, Grosz-Joshi-Weinstein 1995 / Brennan-Friedman-Pollard 1987), parallel-function preference, and implicit-causality/verb-semantic bias (Garvey & Caramazza 1974). Lewis & Vasishth's (2005) cue-based-retrieval / ACT-R account formalizes this as content-addressable memory retrieval: a small cue-set (not a full world model) drives resolution, with graded activation rather than a hard rule producing the final pick. This is architecturally exactly the two-stage design this note recommends: a hard agreement filter (bonding) followed by a ranked-preference pick over survivors (resolution) — not a coincidence, the classical NLP algorithms below were themselves built to approximate this account computationally.

Hobbs' 1978 algorithm operationalizes this as a **breadth-first, left-to-right search over a syntactic parse tree**, climbing from the pronoun up through its own sentence and then, if unresolved, into progressively earlier sentences (most recent first), filtered at every step by gender/number/person agreement (and binding-theory-style same-clause exclusions, per Chomsky 1981's Principle B — a pronoun cannot corefer with a c-commanding NP in its own clause — which is a purely syntactic, uncontroversial pre-filter that Hobbs' own traversal order implicitly respects). On his own 300-pronoun, 3-genre corpus, Hobbs reported **88.3% correct using syntax+agreement alone ("naive"), rising to 91.7% when a crude selectional-restriction/plausibility layer was added** — this 3.4-point gap is the literature's own direct measurement of exactly how much of the residual is a world-knowledge problem versus a syntax problem. Independent re-implementations vary a lot by genre/language (Tetreault 2001: 89% on newspaper text vs. 79% for the Centering-based BFP algorithm on the same text, roughly tied on fiction; non-English re-implementations as low as 40-65%), so the oft-repeated "70-80%" folk figure is a rough cross-study summary, not Hobbs' own number — his own English figure is materially higher (88-92%). **The single most important corroborating engineering precedent found in this scan**: a deterministic, fully rule-based cascade descending directly from this same toolkit — the Stanford multi-pass sieve system (Raghunathan et al. 2010; Lee et al. 2011/2013) — did not just perform "acceptably" as a glass-box fallback, it **won the CoNLL-2011 coreference shared task outright**, beating every contemporary statistical/learned competitor, using an ordered cascade of precision-ranked deterministic rules (string-match -> precise syntactic constructs -> head-matching -> Hobbs/Centering-style pronoun resolution as the LAST, lowest-precision sieve). This is strong, direct evidence that rule-based coreference is not merely a defensible fallback under a glass-box constraint — circa 2010-2013 it was state of the art.

## (b) Cheap decisive test

**Don't build the full resolver first — build the smallest possible discourse-memory shim and test it against a HONEST fixture set, because the current coreference test rows in the corpus are not actually testing "local antecedent resolution."**

Reading `PROSE_CORPUS` directly: the two existing coreference rows (`"It eats the worm."` gold=`bird`, `"They chase the mouse."` gold=`dog`) are **isolated single sentences with no antecedent-establishing sentence immediately before them in the corpus's processing order** — the nearest textual mention of "bird" is 6 rows earlier, with dog/cat/frog/cow all mentioned in between. This is not a "local antecedent" test case at all under any definition in the literature reviewed (Hobbs, Centering, or cue-based retrieval all require a genuine 1-2-sentence discourse window with a clean, agreement-compatible candidate); it is closer to a long-distance, high-distractor-count case that even a correctly-implemented Hobbs resolver should probably NOT resolve confidently. Testing a new resolver against these exact rows would be a mis-specified test, not a real localization of what the resolver can/cannot do.

**Procedure:**
1. Build the minimal discourse-memory shim: thread a short rolling list (window = 2-3 prior sentences) of `(sentence_index, lemma, grammatical_role, number)` tuples through the read-loop, populated from every noun the parser already extracts a role for (subject/object) in `ie_extract`. This is new state, not a new algorithm — the tagger/role-assignment logic already exists and does not change.
2. Implement the minimal Hobbs/Centering search over that memory: when `ie_extract` hits its existing `COREF_UNRESOLVED` branch, instead of returning immediately, rank memory candidates by (most-recent-sentence-first, then subject-role-before-object-role within a sentence — Centering's Cf ranking collapsed to this register's flat SVO/SVOA clause shapes), filter by number agreement (`it`->singular, `they`->plural; this register's closed animal-noun lexicon has no grammatical-gender cue at all, so number is the ONLY hard categorical filter available here — see the deflation note in Prediction A below), and optionally break remaining ties using the schema's own verb-argument type constraint (see Prediction B). If exactly one candidate survives, bind and emit a triple tagged `HOBBS_COREF` with provenance = (current sentence, antecedent sentence, rule). If zero or 2+ candidates survive, ABSTAIN — unchanged, still correct.
3. **Before running this against the resolver**, REBUILD the coreference fixture rows into genuine discourse PAIRS/TRIPLES: an antecedent-establishing sentence immediately followed by a pronoun sentence with a single, unambiguous, agreement-compatible referent (e.g. `"The bird eats a seed. It eats the worm."` -> gold second-sentence triple `(bird, eats, worm)`), separately from a small set of deliberately AMBIGUOUS discourse fixtures (two same-number candidates in the window) that should still correctly ABSTAIN. This is a corpus-construction step, not a resolver-design step, and it is a prerequisite — testing against the current mis-specified rows would not be decisive either way.

**HARD-PASS:** on the rebuilt local-antecedent fixture set (>=10 discourse pairs/triples, single unambiguous candidate), the resolver achieves >=80% correct antecedent-binding AND every existing guard-class correct-rate stays at 1.00 (no regression) AND on a separate deliberately-ambiguous fixture set (>=5 discourses, 2+ equally agreement-compatible candidates) the resolver correctly ABSTAINS >=90% of the time rather than guessing.

**HARD-FAIL:** <40% correct-binding on the local-antecedent set, OR any guard-class regression, OR the resolver GUESSES WRONG on >=30% of the deliberately-ambiguous set (a false-confidence failure — worse than the current abstain-everything behavior, because it would silently admit an incorrect fact into the store rather than correctly declining).

MIDDLE otherwise — partial lift; report which failure class dominates (agreement-insufficient ties vs. discourse-window-too-narrow vs. role-ranking-wrong).

## (c) Falsifiable predictions (HARD-PASS / HARD-FAIL)

**Prediction A — a minimal discourse-memory + Hobbs/Centering-style resolver (recency + subject-role-preference ranking, number-agreement filter) correctly resolves pronoun-subject sentences whose gold antecedent is the immediately-preceding sentence's subject or object, in this closed-vocabulary curriculum register.**
P estimate: **P=0.45** (deflated from an estimated raw ~0.65-0.70; Hobbs' own naive accuracy is 88.3% on general text and this register is structurally SIMPLER — no embedded clauses beyond one relative-clause type, no gender ambiguity to worry about at all, small fixed vocabulary — which argues for higher, not lower, accuracy than Hobbs' general-text number. But this register is also uniquely AGREEMENT-POOR relative to general English: the closed animal-noun lexicon carries no grammatical-gender cue (all animals default to "it"/"they"), so number agreement is the ONLY hard categorical filter available, versus gender+number+person for general text — every same-number candidate in the discourse window is a live tie-risk that gender resolves for free in general text but cannot here. This specific register property is NOT covered by any of the three lit-scan lanes and is this note's own inference; hence capped below the 0.50 novel-synthesis ceiling despite the otherwise-favorable simplicity of the register.)
HARD-PASS: >=80% correct-antecedent-binding on the rebuilt local-antecedent fixture set (section b), zero guard-class regression.
HARD-FAIL: <40% correct-binding — would indicate the single-cue (number-only) agreement filter is insufficiently discriminating even in a closed 6-8-noun vocabulary, and a genuinely wider disambiguation signal (verb-selectional typing, Prediction B; or a narrower discourse window, e.g. N=1 sentence back only) is required before this approach is viable at all.

**Prediction B — the substrate's existing closed, type-checked animal/verb schema (the same schema the ingest gate already uses to reject type-violating facts) functions as a zero-additional-cost analog of Hobbs' own selectional-restriction augmentation (which took his own accuracy from 88.3% -> 91.7%), breaking ties that survive number-agreement filtering alone.**
P estimate: **P=0.35** (deflated; the mechanism is a direct, low-risk structural analogy to Hobbs' own documented SR layer, and the substrate ALREADY HAS the schema machinery built for an unrelated purpose (the ingest gate) — but the schema in this closed micro-register is likely too PERMISSIVE to add much value: if most nouns in the lexicon satisfy most verbs' type constraints (a small closed set of animals, most of which can plausibly "eat," "chase," or "live in" most things in this schema), the selectional-restriction layer may break few or no ties in practice, unlike Hobbs' general-text corpus where semantic implausibility is a much stronger signal. This uncertainty about the schema's actual selectivity, not the mechanism's soundness, is why P is capped well below Prediction A.)
HARD-PASS: adding verb-argument-type tie-breaking (as a SECOND filter, applied only when 2+ candidates survive number-agreement) resolves >=50% of the ties that number-agreement alone leaves unresolved, on the fixture set.
HARD-FAIL: <10% of ties broken — would confirm the schema is too small/permissive in this register to carry selectional-restriction value, and any further tie-breaking must come from a genuinely different signal (e.g. widening the agreement cue set, or accepting the tie as a correct abstain rather than trying to resolve it).

**Prediction C (the honest expectation, restated as a falsifiable bound, not a novel claim) — genuinely ambiguous coreference (2+ candidates that survive both number-agreement AND schema-type filtering) remains a permanent, correctly-abstaining gap; the resolver must not silently guess on these cases.**
P estimate: **P=0.55** (this is closer to a literature-confirmed claim than a novel synthesis — every lane independently converges on world-knowledge-dependent/genuinely-tied cases as the residual naive-Hobbs and even the SOTA deterministic sieve system cannot resolve; the Winograd Schema Challenge was explicitly DESIGNED to defeat exactly this class of syntactic/statistical cue. Deflated from ~0.75 to 0.55 only because this register's actual TIE FREQUENCY is unmeasured — it is plausible ties are rare enough in a small closed-schema curriculum corpus that this prediction is rarely exercised either way, which would make the HARD-PASS/HARD-FAIL bar below hard to evaluate with a small fixture set.)
HARD-PASS: on a deliberately-constructed tied-candidate fixture set (>=5 discourses, 2+ equally agreement-and-type-compatible candidates), the resolver correctly ABSTAINS (empty triple list, not a guess) on >=90% of them.
HARD-FAIL: the resolver guesses WRONG on >=30% of genuinely-tied cases — this would be a worse regression than the status quo, because it would convert a correct "no fact" into an incorrect "wrong fact," undermining the substrate's core zero-hallucination invariant, and would mean the ranking mechanism resolves ties non-randomly-badly (e.g. always picks the closer wrong answer) rather than correctly detecting the tie in the first place.

## (d) Cross-thread synthesis

- **Directly extends, does not restart, `research_glass_box_reading_robust_parsing_ceiling_2026-07-16.md`'s roadmap.** That note's Prediction D (P=0.42, deflated) predicted coreference would be a load-bearing, non-shrinking gap by curriculum Rung 3+ and recommended budgeting a Hobbs/centering pass as a first-class pipeline stage — this drill is the requested follow-up depth-drill on exactly that recommendation, and confirms the mechanism choice (Hobbs' tree-search collapsed to recency+role-ranking; Centering's Cb/Cf formalism as the underlying theory) while surfacing a build-order finding the prior note could not have known without reading the live parser: **the blocking prerequisite is discourse STATE, not a resolution ALGORITHM.** `ie_extract(sentence)` is currently a pure, stateless, single-sentence function — no version of Hobbs, Centering, or cue-based retrieval operates on an isolated sentence with no prior discourse, because all three are explicitly discourse-level mechanisms (Hobbs searches "previous sentences in reverse chronological order"; Centering's Cb is defined as "whichever Cf(Uᵢ₋₁) entity is realized in Uᵢ" — undefined without a Uᵢ₋₁). This reframes the engineering task: it is not "add an algorithm," it is "add a small piece of state and then a small algorithm."
- **The existing `PROSE_CORPUS` coreference rows are a mis-specified test, not a validated gap-confirmation.** The two current rows (`"It eats the worm."` / `"They chase the mouse."`) sit many rows and several intervening animal-mentions away from their gold antecedent in the corpus's own processing order, with no discourse-context mechanism to reach back that far even in principle at the current window sizes the literature supports (Hobbs' search is in principle unbounded but in practice nearly all true antecedents resolve within 1-2 sentences — Hobbs 1978's own corpus evaluation; this specific "nearly all" figure is this drill's own synthesis of the general finding, not a single quoted statistic). This is not a criticism of the prior cell — those rows correctly exercise the ABSTAIN behavior for an out-of-window pronoun, which is itself a correct and useful test — but a NEW, cleanly-specified local-antecedent fixture set (section b, step 3) is required before Prediction A can be tested at all, and this note treats that corpus-construction step as a first-class, non-optional part of the build, not a formality.
- **Binding theory (Principle B) is a free, near-zero-cost pre-filter that is largely a non-issue in this register specifically, which simplifies the recommended design relative to general-text Hobbs implementations.** Principle B excludes a pronoun from coreferring with a c-commanding NP in its OWN clause — but in this substrate's register, every coreference-triggering case is, by construction, a pronoun-SUBJECT sentence with NO other noun present in the same clause (that is exactly the `pron_idx before v0, no noun before v0` condition that currently triggers `COREF_UNRESOLVED`). There is no same-clause candidate to exclude. This means the general-purpose binding-theory pre-filter that Hobbs' own traversal order and the Stanford sieve system both build in as a first-stage filter is, in THIS register, vacuous — one less piece of machinery to build, not a missing piece to add. (Flagged as this drill's own register-specific inference, not a literature claim.)
- **The Stanford sieve system's own error analysis (Lee et al. 2011) surfaces a finding worth carrying into the pre-reg: of THEIR pronoun-resolution errors specifically, roughly 40% were pure search-order/ranking artifacts (the system picked a closer WRONG antecedent because of its fixed traversal order), not world-knowledge gaps** — distinct from nominal-mention errors, which were dominated by genuine semantic/category-membership gaps (matching Hobbs' classic "needs plausibility knowledge" residual). This means that even after a Hobbs/Centering resolver is built and even in a world-knowledge-free closed register, some fraction of residual errors should be EXPECTED to be fixable by re-tuning the ranking order (e.g., discourse-window size, subject-vs-object weighting) rather than being a hard structural wall — worth distinguishing explicitly in any post-hoc error analysis of this cell's own misses, per the "log every miss with a reason-code" discipline the prior note already established.

## (e) Substrate-product implications

1. **Immediate next build is the discourse-memory shim, not the resolver logic.** A rolling `(sentence_index, lemma, grammatical_role, number)` list, window size 2-3 sentences, threaded through the existing read-loop. This is genuinely small (the role-tagging logic already exists in `ie_extract`; this only persists it across calls) and is the true blocking dependency — building resolver logic against a stateless function is not possible even in principle.
2. **The resolver itself, once state exists, is a small addition (recommend under 100 lines): rank memory by recency-then-subject-role, filter by number agreement, optionally break ties with the existing schema type-check (Prediction B), bind on exactly one survivor, ABSTAIN on zero or 2+.** No new external dependency, no learned parameters, fully deterministic, fully provenance-traceable (every bound triple carries both the current-sentence pointer and the antecedent-sentence pointer as its rule-firing record) — satisfies the substrate's glass-box invariant exactly as cleanly as the existing passive/coordination/relative-clause rules do.
3. **Corpus-construction is a first-class, non-optional prerequisite, not an afterthought.** Rebuild the coreference fixture rows as genuine antecedent-establishing-sentence + pronoun-sentence PAIRS (unambiguous case) and a SEPARATE small set of deliberately-tied discourse fixtures (ambiguous case, correct-abstain target). Testing against the current isolated-sentence rows would not be decisive in either direction.
4. **Number agreement is this register's only hard categorical filter (no grammatical-gender cue exists in a closed animal-noun lexicon) — this is a genuine, register-specific weakness relative to general-text Hobbs/Centering implementations, and argues for keeping the discourse window DELIBERATELY NARROW (favor N=1-2 sentences back over Hobbs' in-principle-unbounded search) to minimize same-number distractor accumulation**, at least until the curriculum's vocabulary grows enough to diversify referent types. This is this note's own design recommendation, not a literature-sourced number.
5. **The existing ingest-gate type-check schema is a candidate free selectional-restriction layer (Prediction B) — wire it as a SECOND-STAGE tie-breaker in the resolver (only invoked when number-agreement alone leaves 2+ survivors), not a replacement for agreement filtering.** Its value is uncertain (P=0.35, deflated) because the current schema may be too permissive to break many ties in a small closed vocabulary — this should be measured directly rather than assumed, per Prediction B's HARD-PASS/HARD-FAIL bars.
6. **The zero-hallucination invariant is the load-bearing design constraint for this specific gap more than for any other reviewed failure mode**, because coreference is the one place where a WRONG GUESS (not just a missed extraction) becomes newly possible — passive/coordination/relative-clause rule failures produce either no triple or a differently-structured but still textually-grounded triple, whereas a bad pronoun resolution binds a fact to the WRONG ENTITY entirely. Prediction C's HARD-FAIL bar (resolver guesses wrong on >=30% of genuinely-tied cases) is accordingly the single most important guardrail in this whole roadmap — it should gate ship/no-ship more strictly than either Prediction A or B.
7. **Do not extend this resolver to "he/she/him/her" or first/second-person pronouns yet** — the corpus's actual pronoun usage (`it`/`they` referring to animals) is narrower than the full `PRONS` set already recognized by the tagger; building gender-agreement handling now would be speculative engineering against a cue this register does not currently exercise. Scope the first build strictly to the number-agreement case actually present in the corpus.

## Register-specific finding not in any cited source (this drill's own synthesis, flagged as such)

This substrate's curriculum register is **simpler than general text in grammar (no embedded clauses beyond one relative-clause type) but AGREEMENT-POORER than general text (a closed animal-noun lexicon carries no grammatical-gender cue, so number is the only hard categorical filter)**. These two properties point in OPPOSITE directions for expected resolver accuracy relative to Hobbs' own 88-92% general-text figures, and neither the biology lane, the Hobbs lane, nor the binding-theory/sieve lane measured or discussed a register with this specific combination. This is the single largest source of uncertainty behind Prediction A's P=0.45 (deflated well below what the simplicity of the grammar alone would suggest) and is exactly what the cheap decisive test (section b) is designed to measure directly rather than estimate further from the literature.
