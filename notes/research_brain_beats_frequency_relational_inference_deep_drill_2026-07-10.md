## HEADLINE (5-line)

1. The brain does not beat frequency with a *smarter frequency-weighting formula* — it beats frequency because it maintains a **second, structurally separate data type**: a geometric/relational **map** (grid-cell-like combinatorial basis; a rank/order axis for transitive relations) that is **factorized apart from item identity** (Tolman-Eichenbaum Machine, Whittington et al. 2020, *Cell*) and evaluated by **algebraic composition** (vector addition/distance), not by tally. Composition is *defined for every point in the space*, including a pairing never jointly observed — a lookup/frequency table's output is simply undefined there.
2. That map is not innate per-fact — it is **built offline**, by hippocampal replay during rest/sleep extracting shared structure across many individually-stored episodes into a compact generative code (systems consolidation / Complementary Learning Systems, McClelland-McNaughton-O'Reilly 1995; schema formation, Tse et al. 2007, *Science*). Replay demonstrably generates **novel, never-traversed combinations** (shortcut trajectories: Gupta et al. 2010, *Neuron*; never-visited-arm sequences: Ólafsdóttir et al. 2015, *eLife*; never-seen compound hypotheses: Schwartenbeck et al. 2023, *Cell*) — proof the map is queried generatively, not played back as a tape of stored footage.
3. Even where a frequency/familiarity signal legitimately exists (perirhinal cortex), the brain never lets it conflate with the structural/evidence signal — it is architecturally confined to a **starting-point/bias offset** in an evidence-accumulation race, while structural evidence sets the **drift rate** (Mulder et al. 2012, *J Neurosci*: manipulating prior shifts starting-point *z*, leaves drift rate *v* statistically unaffected — a genuine double-dissociation, both algorithmic and anatomical). This is a **strictly stronger** claim than "subtract out popularity": frequency's influence is capped to a bounded intercept, so strong structural evidence can always eventually win given enough accumulation, which a naive additive score does not guarantee.
4. Prefrontal cortex supplies a third, independent lever: **abstract, item-general rule/schema representations** (Wallis, Anderson & Miller 2001, *Nature*: PFC neurons encode which rule is in force, independent of the specific stimulus pair) that let a *relation-type property* (transitivity, symmetry, functional/cardinality-1-ness) be applied to a brand-new pair with **zero co-occurrence frequency**, because the rule is defined over the relation-type, not the instance.
5. Substrate-mapping: our compose/bind-unbind CG layer is the hippocampal fast-episode-storage analog (proven, CG tier) and our ranker is the perirhinal familiarity analog (all we currently have) — we are **missing all three of**: (a) a factorized structural/geometric relation-code separate from item identity, (b) an offline replay/consolidation process that BUILDS that code from raw stored episodes, and (c) an architectural (not additive) separation enforcing frequency-as-bounded-offset rather than frequency-as-conflated-feature. Sections 6-7 below turn this into 4 ranked, deflated-P courses.

## Part 1 — Brain-ground, in depth

### 1.1 Cognitive maps and transitive inference (hippocampal-entorhinal)

Behrens et al. 2018 (*Neuron*, "What Is a Cognitive Map?") redefines "map" away from a literal picture of space and toward a **structural/relational code**: a representation of the transition/similarity structure between elements of a task, factored apart from which specific elements currently occupy those slots. Grid cells are treated as one instance of a general class of basis functions over relational structure; place cells are the conjunction of that structural basis with sensory/item content.

**The Tolman-Eichenbaum Machine** (Whittington et al. 2020, *Cell*) formalizes this: it factorizes representation into (i) a structural/grid-like code generated by path-integrating over abstract "actions" (which need not be physical — they can be any task transition), shared across all environments/tasks with the same relational structure, and (ii) a sensory/memory code, learned per-environment via Hopfield-like pattern completion, that binds the current structural state to whatever content occupies it. Because structure and content are stored **separately**, entering a brand-new environment with a familiar transition structure but entirely novel items lets the model zero-shot-predict relations among the new items — the structural code, not the item associations, carries the relational information. TEM's units spontaneously develop grid/band/border/object-vector tuning and place-cell-like remapping, and the paper shows hippocampal "random" remapping across environments actually hides the same reused structural code.

**Neural circuit for A>B, B>C ⟹ A>C.** Rodent lesion work (Dusek & Eichenbaum 1997, *PNAS*; Bunsey & Eichenbaum 1996, *Nature*) shows hippocampal(-cortical) lesions spare learning of the trained premise pairs but abolish correct inference on the untrained non-adjacent probe (a clean causal dissociation between memorizing-trained-pairs and inferring-untrained-ones). mPFC lesions (DeVito et al. 2010, *Learn Mem*) impair acquisition of the inference itself. Human fMRI (Wendelken & Bunge 2010, *J Cogn Neurosci*) dissociates hippocampus (relational *encoding*) from rostrolateral PFC (relational *integration* — combining two premises into a novel inference). Single-unit primate work (Jensen et al. 2015-16; parietal LIP "implied serial order"; premotor "geometric mental line," 2024 preprint) finds PFC/parietal/premotor neurons encoding item **rank/position along an abstract ordinal axis** — independent of whether that specific pair was ever directly compared. This rank-axis account is the leading geometric hypothesis for why TI generalizes: once items sit on a shared ordinal dimension, distance between ANY two positions is computable, trained or not.

**Vicarious trial-and-error** (Johnson & Redish 2007, *J Neurosci*): at high-uncertainty choice points, decoded CA3 ensembles transiently sweep forward down each optional path **before** the animal commits — an internal simulation of a route not yet (and possibly never) physically traveled, evaluated at the decision point. This is the map being queried offline, prospectively, not merely retrieved.

**Confidence grading**: the lesion double-dissociations are causal and solid. The ensemble-sweep and rank-axis geometric accounts are correlational (decoded activity, not manipulated). TEM is an explicit computational model that reproduces known cell types and gives a principled account of *why* factorization enables generalization — a compelling theoretical proposal, not a directly observed biological algorithm.

### 1.2 Grid codes and why additive/vector codes generalize where lookup tables cannot

Grid cells (Hafting et al. 2005, *Nature*; Moser, Kropff & Moser 2008 review) fire in a periodic hexagonal lattice; multiple modules at different spatial scales jointly specify location. Sreenivasan & Fiete (2011, *Nat Neurosci*) formalize this as a **residue number system**: each module reports position modulo its own period, and because moduli are near-coprime, the *combination* of module phases specifies position over a range that scales as the **product** of module periods — combinatorial/exponential capacity from a handful of periodic "digit" populations. A novel location is a NEW COMBINATION of existing basis units, never a new dedicated cell.

Banino et al. 2018 (*Nature*, "Vector-based navigation using grid-like representations in artificial agents") is the sharpest computational demonstration: an RNN trained only to path-integrate self-motion spontaneously grows grid-like units, and an RL agent given these units takes direct novel shortcuts through never-traversed space. (Caveat: the *biological* version of literal animal shortcut-taking is more contested — a 2023-24 meta-analysis of Tolman's "sunburst maze" paradigm found poor replicability of genuine shortcutting in rodents; the cellular/computational capacity for vector-addition is well-established even where the clean behavioral demonstration is weaker.)

Reuse of grid-like codes for **non-spatial conceptual domains**: Constantinescu, O'Reilly & Behrens 2016 (*Science*, "Organizing conceptual knowledge in humans with a gridlike code") find hexagonal fMRI modulation in entorhinal cortex during navigation of a 2D non-spatial conceptual space (birds varying in neck/leg length), replicated a week later. Bao et al. 2019 (*Neuron*) extend this to a 2D odor-mixture space. Both are macroscopic fMRI signatures (not single-unit proof) but are treated by the field as strong converging evidence for domain-general reuse of the same code.

**Why additive codes generalize systematically.** Fodor & Pylyshyn (1988) argue systematicity requires compositional constituent structure; Lake & Baroni (2018, ICML, SCAN benchmark) empirically show plain RNNs achieve only "mix-and-match" (lookup-flavored) generalization, failing on genuinely novel combinations — a direct illustration of the lookup-table failure mode. Vector-symbolic architectures (Plate 1995, HRR) formalize "relation = a consistent offset/transform applied to any filler," so the same relation vector transfers zero-shot to any new operand — word2vec's king−man+woman≈queen (Mikolov et al. 2013) is the canonical (if brittle/curated — Linzen 2016, Rogers et al. later showed inconsistency outside curated relation sets) toy illustration of exactly this principle. Dayan's Successor Representation (1993, *Neural Computation*) shows a predictive vector-state code lets value updates for a NEW reward function generalize instantly via linear recombination across all states, where tabular Q-learning must relearn every state-action cell independently.

**The core mathematical contrast**: a lookup/frequency-count code allocates one unit/tally per instance — coverage is strictly bounded by the number of entries, and any never-seen combination is by construction absent, however much data accumulates. A grid/vector code represents each instance as a coordinate in a shared low-dimensional basis; the SAME small basis is reused additively/combinatorially, so N dimensions (or M periodic modules) span a combinatorial space where any point — visited or not — already has a well-formed representation reachable by algebra. Generalization becomes "evaluate an algebraic combination" (defined everywhere), not "retrieve a stored case" (defined only where trained).

### 1.3 Replay/consolidation — the map-BUILDER

Wilson & McNaughton 1994 (*Science*) established hippocampal reactivation during sleep as real. Foster & Wilson 2006 (*Nature*) showed reverse replay; Diba & Buzsáki 2007 showed bidirectional forward/reverse sequences. Critically, **Gupta, van der Meer, Touretzky & Redish 2010** (*Neuron*, "Hippocampal Replay Is Not a Simple Function of Experience") directly falsified simple recency/frequency accounts: replay depicted **shortcut trajectories the rat had never physically traveled** — the strongest direct evidence that replay content is generated from the underlying structural map, not indexed off a table of literal episodes.

**Complementary Learning Systems** (McClelland, McNaughton & O'Reilly 1995; updated Kumaran, Hassabis & McClelland 2016, *TICS*): neocortex must learn via slow, interleaved weight changes to extract shared statistical structure across many episodes without catastrophic interference; hippocampus does the opposite (sparse, one-shot, pattern-separated storage) precisely so it does NOT force premature blending. Replay is the mechanism presenting hippocampal episodes to cortex in interleaved order, slowly reweighting cortical synapses toward the shared regularities — i.e. **replay trains a generative/statistical model**, it does not just move copies of episodes around. **Tse et al. 2007** (*Science*, "Schemas and Memory Consolidation") is the concrete payoff: once a flavor-place "schema" is built over many trials (slow, hippocampus-dependent), a SINGLE new fact fitting that schema consolidates almost instantly, hippocampus-independent — direct evidence that once the shared relational structure exists, individual new facts need no further slow interleaving.

**Prospective/preplay use of the map**: Pfeiffer & Foster 2013 (*Nature*) show hippocampal sequences depict paths to remembered goals **including novel start-goal combinations never previously traveled together**, predicting the animal's immediate subsequent path. Ólafsdóttir et al. 2015 (*eLife*) show rest-period sequences construct trajectories through space the animal has never physically occupied, driven by inferred value.

**Generative replay as compositional inference**: Schwartenbeck et al. 2023 (*Cell*, "Generative replay underlies compositional inference in the hippocampal-prefrontal circuit") — human MEG-decoded replay literally assembles elements into never-before-seen compound configurations, each replayed sequence functioning as a testable hypothesis. Spens & Burgess 2024 (*Nature Human Behaviour*) propose a computational bridge: replay trains a cortical generative (VAE-like) network whose latent code supports relational inference via **vector arithmetic** ("A is to B as C is to D") — directly convergent with the additive/vector-code argument in 1.2, from a completely independent (consolidation) angle. The explicit ML analog is Shin et al. 2017 (NeurIPS, "Continual Learning with Deep Generative Replay") — a generator+solver architecture that trains on generated pseudo-samples instead of stored raw examples, explicitly drawn by CLS-theory papers as the isomorphic machine-learning implementation.

**Confidence grading**: SWR replay phenomenology, the shortcut/novel-sequence findings, and the Tse et al. schema result are established, direct (behavioral/electrophysiological/lesion) evidence. CLS theory and the Spens & Burgess generative-model framing are explicitly theoretical/computational-model interpretations that unify the empirical findings but are not themselves direct observations.

### 1.4 Familiarity vs. recollection, deepened with the DDM math

Yonelinas 2002 (*J Mem Lang*) and Diana, Yonelinas & Ranganath 2007 (*TICS*) establish the dual-process dissociation: perirhinal/anterior-parahippocampal cortex gives a fast, graded, **item-only** familiarity signal (a unitized strength value carrying no structure about co-occurrence); hippocampus gives a slower, threshold-like **recollection** signal built from conjunctive/relational binding. Their explicit rationale for why one conflated signal is insufficient: familiarity cannot support source attribution or arbitrary new relational judgments — it can only tell you an item recurred, not what it recurred WITH. Intracranial recordings (Kahana-lab line, *Nat Neurosci*) show perirhinal and hippocampal signals run on genuinely different temporal profiles, not just different anatomical loci for the same computation.

**DDM formalization, deepened.** The accumulator obeys `dx = v·dt + s·dW`, starting at bias point `z` (0 < z < a, boundary separation a); decision triggers when x hits 0 or a. Ratcliff & McKoon (2008, *Neural Computation*) and Bogacz et al. (2006, *Psych Rev*) show empirically that manipulating the *proportion* of a stimulus category (a base-rate/"popularity" analog) shifts `z`, while manipulating discriminability shifts `v` — two independently-recoverable parameters from the same RT+accuracy data via likelihood/hierarchical-Bayes fitting (Wiecki, Sofer & Frank 2013, HDDM tool). **Mulder et al. 2012** (*J Neurosci*, "Bias in the Brain") is the sharpest result: manipulating an alternative's prior probability shifted `z` and left `v` statistically **unaffected** — a genuine double-dissociation, both algorithmic (separate free parameters recoverable from behavior) and anatomical (fMRI localizes the bias signal to striatum/pre-SMA baseline activity BEFORE stimulus onset, while evidence-quality tracks visual/parietal drift). Summerfield & de Lange (2014, *Nat Rev Neurosci*) review corroborate prior-probability and stimulus-relevance loading onto distinct computational stages.

**The arbiter**: Lisman & Grace 2005 (*Neuron*, "Hippocampal-VTA Loop") propose hippocampal mismatch/novelty detection routes via subiculum→accumbens→ventral pallidum→VTA, whose novelty-gated dopamine feeds back to gate LTP/consolidation — i.e. a **surprise/violation signal recruits deeper (recollection-grade) processing** rather than accepting a cheap familiarity match. Bunzeck & Düzel 2006 (*Neuron*) show SN/VTA fMRI codes absolute (not just relative) stimulus novelty.

**Honest failure mode** (both sub-agents flagged this independently): unitization can make relational conjunctions "feel" familiarity-like, and some tasks show prior information leaking partially into drift rate rather than staying purely in starting point. The z/v separation is a strong architectural default, not an absolute partition — this matters directly for course design below (Section 6, course A refinement).

### 1.5 PFC schemas — frequency-independent rule application

Wallis, Anderson & Miller 2001 (*Nature*): monkey PFC neurons encode which abstract rule is in force ("same"/"different"), independent of the specific stimulus pair, and monkeys correctly apply the rule to entirely novel pictures never seen in training. Badre & D'Esposito establish a caudal→rostral PFC abstraction gradient (concrete stimulus-response mappings → abstract task/policy rules). Preston & Eichenbaum 2013 (*Curr Biol*) and Kumaran, Summerfield, Hassabis & Maguire 2009 (*Neuron*) show PFC-hippocampal interaction extracts a schema across episodes and lets schema-consistent new information transfer at accelerated speed — the transferred component is structural, not perceptual-frequency-based. A 2024 *PNAS* theoretical paper on transitive-inference generalization formalizes the rank-axis idea: once items are embedded on a low-dimensional geometric axis learned across training pairs, comparison between ANY two items — including brand-new pairs with **zero co-occurrence frequency** — reduces to a geometric comparison, independent of how many times that specific pair appeared.

## Part 3 — Ranked courses (deflated P, biggest risk each)

### Course A — Residualization ranker, ARCHITECTURALLY HARDENED [immediate, already in flight]

Brain analog: Mulder et al. 2012 DDM starting-point/drift-rate double-dissociation; perirhinal/hippocampal dual-process.

This is the RESID_CONF arm from `research_frontier_ranker_glassbox_fb15k237_rankfair_2026-07-10.md`, **deepened** by today's drill with one concrete architectural refinement the prior note did not have: Mulder et al.'s finding is not merely "compute frequency and structural terms separately, then add them" — it is that manipulating the prior population-level base rate leaves drift rate **statistically unaffected**, i.e. the two terms are not just computed separately but the frequency term's influence on the final decision is **bounded** (an additive offset to a starting point inside a fixed race, not an unbounded multiplicative or linear-regression coefficient that could in principle dominate for large enough frequency counts). Refinement to the existing design: cap the popularity-residual term's maximum magnitude (e.g. clip to the observed spread of the structural/confidence term, or normalize both to comparable scale before summing) so that, architecturally, no amount of raw frequency can out-vote a candidate with strong structural support — matching the "starting point can be overridden by enough evidence, evidence quality is never capped by prior" asymmetry found in the brain data.

**New falsifiable addition (beyond the existing must-fail controls)**: a **frequency-dominance-bound check** — verify that for candidates in the top decile of structural/rule-confidence score, POP rank never overrides them in the final RESID_CONF ranking (i.e., audit that the cap is actually load-bearing, not just present in the formula but numerically inert).

Deflated P: carries the existing **P ≈ 0.28** from the prior note (novel-synthesis cap 0.50, deflated for no direct degree-stratified precedent + empirical-Bayes-shrinkage-reverts-to-POP risk + corpus-compositionality risk). The architectural-cap refinement does not change P materially (it is a robustness improvement, not a new mechanism), but it closes a real gap: without the cap, a "pass" could still be a fragile additive-coefficient accident rather than an architecturally-guaranteed structural win.

**Biggest risk**: identical to the prior note's risk #2/#3 — the FAIR low+mid stratum is precisely where support counts are thinnest, so a well-calibrated smoothed estimator may correctly converge toward POP (this would be the RIGHT Bayesian behavior, not a bug) rather than beat it; and FB15k-237's fair stratum may simply be non-compositional (a corpus problem, not a ranker problem).

### Course D — Explicit relation-type meta-property closure layer [cheap, complementary, narrow scope]

Brain analog: PFC abstract-rule cells (Wallis et al. 2001) + hippocampal-mPFC transitive-inference integration (Wendelken & Bunge 2010) — a rule ("this relation is transitive/symmetric/functional") applied to a brand-new pair with zero co-occurrence frequency, entirely independent of any geometric embedding.

Concrete build: classify each relation TYPE in the corpus for algebraic meta-properties (transitivity, symmetry, anti-symmetry, functional/cardinality-1-ness — cheap to estimate empirically from the existing graph: sample triples, check closure violation rate). For relation types that pass a transitivity/symmetry check at high confidence, apply the corresponding **closure rule directly** (if A>B and B>C are known, assert A>C as a candidate with a distinct, non-frequency provenance tag) as an ADDITIONAL candidate-generation/re-ranking signal alongside course A's confidence score — this is the multi-path/multi-rule-agreement signal already named in the prior ranker note (Section 1, bullet 4) but sharpened to be **type-driven and unconditional on instance frequency** rather than support-count-driven.

This is the course most directly and cleanly evidenced by today's cognitive-map/TI lit-scan (Section 1.1) and is architecturally the cheapest of the four — no new embedding geometry, no offline consolidation pass, just relation-type classification (a one-time, auditable, glass-box computation) plus closure-rule application.

Deflated P ≈ **0.30-0.35** — reasonably well-precedented (transitive-inference neural circuit is one of the best-established relational-inference findings in the literature; DRUM/AnyBURL per-relation-type confidence is an existing, working ML precedent) but narrow in scope: it only helps for relation types that genuinely have clean algebraic closure properties, and many real-world KG relations (the messy majority) are neither strictly transitive nor symmetric.

**Biggest risk**: scope limitation — even a perfect closure-rule layer only moves the needle on the subset of relation types with clean algebraic structure; it does not touch the harder, non-compositional relation types that may dominate the FAIR stratum (same corpus-compositionality risk as course A). Secondary risk: closure-derived candidates may just re-derive facts already inside the existing reach=0.424 ceiling rather than adding new true positives, in which case it helps ranking (moves already-reached candidates up) but not reach itself.

### Course B — Compositional corpus density (fuel for the map) [durable, necessary-not-sufficient]

Brain analog: schema formation requires enough exemplars fitting a common pattern before consolidation can extract it (Tse et al. 2007 — the schema itself took many trials to build before the one-shot-transfer benefit appeared); a grid code still needs a training history with the relevant transition structure represented somewhere before it can be reused.

This is the existing Candidate-A density-floor thread (`research_knowledge_density_information_ceiling_relational_inference_2026-07-10.md`). Today's drill reinforces it from a NEW angle: even a perfect map-builder (course C) or a perfect closure layer (course D) cannot manufacture structure that was never present anywhere in the ingested corpus — replay/consolidation compresses and generalizes EXISTING structure, it does not invent structure ex nihilo. Corpus density is therefore a genuinely independent, necessary input to any of the other three courses, not a competing alternative to them.

Deflated P (this alone flips a fair-stratum ranker to beat frequency) ≈ **0.15-0.20** — density alone, without a structural/map mechanism (course C or D) or an architectural frequency-decoupling (course A), plausibly just makes the SAME frequency-correlated signals more confident, not more structurally correct (more data feeding the same conflated statistic). P(density is a necessary complement to whichever of A/C/D is pursued) is much higher, but that is a different, non-standalone claim.

**Biggest risk**: density work could absorb significant investment while leaving the core conflation problem (frequency and structure computed as one signal) completely unaddressed — this is precisely the trap the prior ranker note already flagged ("iterate the ranker again" vs. "the corpus is the real lever").

### Course C — Replay/consolidation map-builder producing a geometric relation-basis [deepest, highest ceiling, highest risk]

Brain analog: the FULL stack — CLS/systems-consolidation (McClelland et al. 1995) extracting shared structure via slow interleaved replay, into a factorized geometric code (TEM) that supports algebraic composition (Spens & Burgess 2024's explicit "replay trains a generative network with vector-arithmetic relational structure" proposal is the closest published bridge).

Concrete build: an offline process that (1) takes the discrete (entity, relation, entity) triple corpus as "episodes," (2) fits, per relation TYPE, a small shared geometric transform (a low-dimensional offset/rotation — structurally identical to what TransE/RotatE-class KG embedding models already do, and independently convergent with the word2vec-analogy/grid-code additive-code argument in Section 1.2 and 1.3) via a replay-like process that presents many triples interleaved rather than fitting end-to-end in one pass, (3) keeps this structural code factorized/separate from any entity-specific content representation (the TEM factorization), and (4) gates acceptance of any GENERATED (never-observed) inference through an explicit hard verifier — per the prior generate-and-test note, biology's own verifier (novelty/RPE gating) is soft/implicit and NOT what the substrate should copy; the working ML precedent (DRUM/AnyBURL support-confidence, or Abductive-Learning-style logical consistency check) is a hard, auditable check and should be the substrate's verifier instead.

This is the course that most directly answers "the last piece" — it is the only one of the four that builds something structurally NEW (a reusable geometric relation-basis + its offline builder) rather than re-weighting or re-ranking what compose/bind already produces.

Deflated P ≈ **0.20-0.25** (below the generic novel-synthesis cap of 0.50, deflated further than courses A/D for two substrate-specific reasons that are NOT generic hedging): (1) our own cert ledger already contains a DIRECT on-substrate negative for the naive version of this claim — `stage3_hrr_involutive_systematic_generalization_v1` HARD_FAIL (hrr_inv=0.0067=chance) proves "algebraic-transform generalization" is a mechanism null for at least one prior instantiation on this substrate; any course-C proposal must explain concretely what differs (candidate difference: TEM/TransE-style relation-specific LOW-RANK transforms fit via many interleaved triples, vs. the failed cell's single global involutive operator — a materially different hypothesis, but untested); (2) the specific combination "replay-BUILT (not end-to-end-trained) geometric code" has weak external precedent — Spens & Burgess 2024 is the closest paper and is explicitly a theoretical/computational proposal, not a demonstrated-at-scale result.

**Biggest risk**: reproducing the SAME failure class already found — TransE-style relation-as-single-transform is well-known in the external KG literature to fail on symmetric and many-to-many relations (a documented limitation of the whole embedding-model family, independent of anything substrate-specific), which is uncomfortably close to what the on-substrate HARD_FAIL already showed. If course C is pursued, it should be scoped FIRST to relation types where course D's meta-property classification confirms clean algebraic structure (transitive/functional), where TransE-class methods are known to work best — i.e., course C should be sequenced AFTER course D's classification, not attempted as a blanket geometric embedding over all relation types at once.

**Ranking summary**: A (ready now, moderate P, already in flight) > D (cheap, complementary, good P, narrow scope) > B (durable necessary input, not standalone) > C (highest ceiling, but must be sequenced after D and must explicitly confront the on-substrate HRR-involutive HARD_FAIL before being attempted).

## Analogies (for explaining to the user)

- **Cognitive map / TEM (structure vs. content factorization)**: like carrying a folding subway map that shows only which stations connect to which — the shape of the tracks — completely separate from a phrasebook of station names. Drop the same folding map into a new city with unfamiliar station names and you can still work out a route between two stations you've never personally ridden between, because the map's shape doesn't care what the stations are called.
- **Grid code / geometric generalization**: a ruler vs. a photo album. A photo album (frequency/lookup table) only has a picture for the exact combination it was shown. A ruler (grid/vector code) can measure ANY new distance you ask it to, because it's built from a small, reusable set of tick-marks that combine — you never need a new ruler for a new measurement.
- **Replay/consolidation as map-builder**: a student who, instead of just re-reading the same practice problems the night before an exam (frequency), spends the night dreaming up new combinations of the concepts and testing whether they hold together — waking up with a compressed "formula sheet" (schema) that solves exam questions never explicitly practiced.
- **Familiarity vs. recollection / DDM starting-point vs. drift-rate**: a police lineup. The witness's gut "that face looks familiar" (familiarity, fast, frequency-like) is a separate process from actually recalling the specific event where they saw that face (recollection, slow, structural). In racing terms: popularity should only be allowed to nudge the STARTING LINE of a race, never speed up the runner (the actual evidence) — a slow-but-true runner can still win if given enough track.
- **PFC schema / rule-based inference**: a judge who has internalized the general rule "taller than is transitive" doesn't need to have seen every possible pair of people stood back-to-back — they apply the rule. How often any particular pairing has occurred is irrelevant to a judge who understands the rule itself.
