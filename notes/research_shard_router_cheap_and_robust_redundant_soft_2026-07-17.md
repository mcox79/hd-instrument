## HEADLINE

**Neither the brain nor classical coding/ANN theory gives a free lunch -- but both give the SAME concrete recipe for getting close: replace "one narrow index, hope it's right" (cheap+fragile) and "check everything" (robust+expensive) with a SMALL, FIXED number of REDUNDANT, INCOMMENSURATE probes, combined by a SOFT/graded rule (not majority-of-hard-decisions, not brute-force union). The grid-cell modular code (CRT-like, exponential range from a handful of small-period modules, disambiguated by coarse-anchors-fine population-vector decode) and the LDPC/expander-code/compressed-sensing triad (sparse redundancy + iterative soft/belief-propagation combining = linear-time near-capacity error tolerance, provably NOT achievable by hard-decision decoding of the same code) are the same theorem-shape from two different fields. Multi-probe LSH and top-K (K small, K>1) mixture-of-experts routing are the direct ANN/ML analogs, and they work for the same reason. Critically: (1) the DG->CA3 pathway is NOT actually the mechanism that does this -- DG's job is decorrelation-for-storage-capacity, not narrowing a search space, and CA3's completion is a parallel attractor relaxation bounded by CA3's OWN capacity, not a cheap search over an externally huge store (a correction to the prior 09:31 fuzzy-relational note's framing); (2) classical Sparse Distributed Memory, often cited as the "robust and cheap" existence proof, is actually O(store-size) per query by construction -- DeepMind's own "Sparse Reads and Writes" (2016) and the "Neural Distributed Autoassociative Memories" survey explicitly flag sub-linear SDM as an OPEN, only-partially-solved problem, not a solved one. So the on-platform tension is a genuine, live, unsolved-in-general-form problem -- not a design oversight we can trivially patch.**

P_deflated (novel-synthesis architecture recombining well-established but NOT directly-transplanted math): **0.38** (below the 0.50 novel-synthesis cap; deflated further because two of the three lit-scans explicitly found this exact cheap-and-robust-simultaneously question is an ACKNOWLEDGED OPEN GAP in the ANN literature -- no clean phase-transition-style bound relating redundancy level to corruption-tolerance was found anywhere. We are proposing a specific, testable design point in an area the literature itself has not closed.)

## (a) THE BIOLOGY -- and a correction to the prior framing

### A1. DG -> CA3 is decorrelation-for-capacity, NOT narrow-then-search (category correction)

The standard model (Marr 1971; McNaughton & Morris 1987; Treves & Rolls 1992; O'Reilly & McClelland 1994; Leutgeb et al. 2007, *Science*) treats DG and CA3 as solving genuinely DIFFERENT problems. DG's sparse expansion recoding (~1M granule cells at ~2-4% activity via strong feedforward inhibition) decorrelates overlapping EC inputs so they become SEPARABLE attractors once Hebbian-stored on CA3's recurrent collaterals -- this raises CA3's effective storage CAPACITY (Treves & Rolls 1992 closed-form), it does not shrink a search space at query time. CA3's completion-from-partial-cue is an entirely CA3-internal, PARALLEL attractor relaxation (fixed recurrent connectivity, Hopfield-like), not a serial or staged search over an externally huge store. **This means our prior note's "DG narrows, CA3 completes" framing conflated two different jobs.** The genuine narrowing-of-a-huge-external-store function belongs to hippocampal INDEXING theory (Teyler & DiScenna 1986; Gershman/Fiete/Irie 2025 key-value reframing) -- but that store is NEOCORTEX (a vastly larger, structurally different substrate), and the "narrowing" there works because the INDEX itself is small and compact (a compressed key), not because CA3 performs a cheap search over cortex. Confidence: high (well-established CLS/dual-mechanism consensus).

### A2. Grid cells: the actual existence-proof of cheap-AND-robust via redundancy

Grid-cell modules at different, INCOMMENSURATE spatial scales form a population code that is structurally a neural CRT/residue-number-system (Fiete, Burak & Brody 2008, *Neural Computation*): capacity (unambiguous range) scales EXPONENTIALLY with the number of modules while neuron count scales only LINEARLY -- a handful of small, cheap modules jointly cover an enormous range. Individually each module is periodic and locally ambiguous, but Sreenivasan & Fiete (2011, *Nat. Neurosci.*) show the population code behaves as an **analog error-correcting code**: noise in one module's phase is resolved by cross-module consistency-checking (soft, graded combination), giving graceful degradation, not single-module-error catastrophe. Mathis, Herz & Stemmler (2012, *Neural Computation*) derive an optimal ~3:2 scale ratio between adjacent modules that lets a coarse module ANCHOR/disambiguate a fine module (coarse-then-fine population-vector decode, not hard-per-module-then-combine). A 2017 belief-propagation decoder for exactly this population code exists in the literature. **This is the clean, well-established biological existence proof that a handful of cheap, redundant, incommensurate channels + a soft/graded combining rule buys BOTH compactness and noise-robustness at once** -- directly analogous to the CRT-residue idea our own RC2 cell already tried and HARD_FAILED, but critically RC2 used a HARD per-modulus decode with no soft combination step; grid cells (and LDPC, below) use a SOFT one. Confidence: high.

### A3. Theta/gamma coarse-then-fine staging (medium confidence)

Theta-phase precession orders assembly firing across a cycle; slow-gamma sub-cycles further partition finer content within it (nested theta-gamma multiplexing). This is a real, temporally-staged coarse-then-fine mechanism, but the specific claim that MULTIPLE theta cycles are used redundantly for robustness (as opposed to grid modules' spatial redundancy) is **medium/low confidence -- inferred, not directly quoted** in the literature surfaced. Treat as a plausible but unconfirmed secondary mechanism, not load-bearing for the design below.

### A4. General redundant-population-coding principle

Grid cells and DG/CA3-capacity are specific instances of a broader "redundant population coding" principle in systems neuroscience (population-coding robustness literature): many overlapping, individually low-resolution/noisy channels combine so no single channel's corruption is catastrophic. Robustness is emergent from COMBINING several cheap channels, not from hardening any one channel. Confidence: high for the general principle; its specific fit to a two-STAGE (as opposed to parallel-committee) architecture is the more inferential part.

## (b) CLASSICAL/ENGINEERING MIRROR

| Construction | Sub-linear cost? | Robust to corruption? | Mechanism | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Multi-probe LSH** (Lv et al. 2007, VLDB) | Yes -- O(T x polylog N), T small & N-independent | Empirically yes for random perturbation; an adaptive/adversarial corruption source can still defeat it in O(log) queries regardless of redundancy (arXiv 2402.09707) | Probe T nearby buckets (not just the hashed one); redundancy substitutes for exact hashing | High on mechanism; no clean T-vs-corruption-severity bound found (gap) |
| **IVF+PQ, nprobe** | Yes, while nprobe << nlist | Steep recall gain for first several probed clusters (65%->98% recall going nprobe 1->20 in typical benchmarks), then plateaus | Corrupted query more easily crosses a Voronoi-cell boundary; probing >1 cluster recovers it | High for clean-query curve shape; noisy-query generalization is a literature gap (inferred, not directly sourced) |
| **HNSW** | Yes, O(log N) hops | Robustness under corrupted QUERY vectors specifically is an unstudied gap in the literature surfaced; known fragilities are scale-degradation and graph-structural damage, not query-corruption | Hierarchical greedy graph search | Medium (negative claim from non-exhaustive search) |
| **Classical SDM** (Kanerva 1988) | **No** -- O(hard-locations) per query by construction | Yes, by radius-pooling construction | Checks every hard location within a Hamming radius | Medium-high that classical SDM does NOT solve "cheap and robust" -- it is the "robust but O(N)" corner, not a counterexample to the tension. Sub-linear/hashed SDM variants are explicitly flagged as an OPEN problem by DeepMind's "Sparse Reads and Writes" (arXiv 1610.09027) and the "Neural Distributed Autoassociative Memories" survey (arXiv 1709.00848) |
| **MoE top-K routing** (K small, K>1) | Yes, linear in K << num_experts | Top-2 gives real "inherent smoothing" (shared-expert continuity under router perturbation) vs unstable top-1; caveat: soft top-2 weights often COLLAPSE toward top-1 (~0.71 dominant-expert weight) unless actively counteracted by load-balancing losses | Redundant expert consultation + graded (softmax) combination | High -- best-evidenced ML analog of small-K redundancy buying disproportionate robustness cheaply, WITH the caveat that hard-collapse is a real failure mode requiring active counter-pressure |

**Coding theory (the cleanest theorem-shape):**

- **LDPC + belief propagation:** density evolution gives a provable decoding threshold; BP achieves capacity on the binary erasure channel, near-Shannon-capacity generally (~0.0045 dB from capacity for optimized irregular ensembles). Decode cost is LINEAR in blocklength per iteration (sparse graph, constant iterations) -- vs. hard bounded-distance/ML decoding of the SAME redundant code, which is either far weaker or NP-hard. This is the rigorous, textbook-canonical version of "same redundancy, hard-vs-soft decode changes everything." (Richardson & Urbanke; Gallager; MacKay-Neal.)
- **Expander codes** (Sipser & Spielman 1996): expander-graph structure + the "Flip" algorithm correct a CONSTANT FRACTION of errors in LINEAR time via purely LOCAL majority-flip decisions that aggregate into global correctness because of the graph's expansion/spectral-gap property -- no combinatorial search. 40-year-old, textbook-canonical.
- **Compressed sensing / AMP** (Donoho-Tanner phase transition; Donoho-Maleki-Montanari 2009): modest oversampling (m modestly above the phase-transition curve, not m~N) + iterative SOFT thresholding (AMP) gives Bayes-optimal recovery at O(N)-per-iteration, few-iteration cost -- vs. combinatorial L0 search.
- **RRNS with soft/list decoding** (Mandelbaum; Goldreich-Ron-Sudan-style extensions): a SMALL number of extra moduli + majority/ML combination across moduli tolerates a bounded fraction of per-modulus errors at near-linear (in modulus count) decode cost. This is the direct generalization of our own RC2 CRT-router idea -- RC2 failed specifically because it used a HARD single-modulus-error-is-fatal decode; a soft/ML RRNS decoder is the fix RC2 never tried.
- **Bloom filters (k hash functions):** genuine but WEAKER analogy -- optimal k*=(m/n)ln2 gives a convex interior optimum (diminishing returns past a small k), but combination is AND-of-hard-bits, not graded soft-evidence combination. Useful as a "small redundancy captures most of the benefit" data point, not as a load-bearing mechanism match.

**General principle (high confidence across LDPC/expander/compressed-sensing, medium-high extending to RRNS):** sparse/modest redundancy + an iterative or population-level SOFT (graded-evidence) combining rule that exploits a structural regularity (expansion, sparse-graph density evolution, restricted-isometry-like structure, or grid-module incommensurability) converts a fragile-cheap OR expensive-robust construction into simultaneously cheap (near-linear/sub-linear decode) and robust (constant-fraction/near-capacity error tolerance). **The stage12 cell's own self-test already independently rediscovered half of this principle**: it found "majority vote over R independent narrow decodes" was WORSE than a single wide/holistic decode (0.984 vs 0.997) -- this is EXACTLY the well-established coding-theory fact that hard-decision combination of redundant sub-decodes is provably suboptimal to a single soft/linear-combined decision under i.i.d. noise (the same fact LDPC's BP-vs-hard-decision comparison rests on). That negative result is not evidence against redundancy; it is evidence against HARD combination of redundant channels specifically -- a different, narrower claim.

## (c) SUBSTRATE MAPPING -- a genuinely untested third arm

The stage12 cell tested exactly two points on this design space, both at an extreme:
- **DECOMPOSED** (HARD): zero redundancy (place-value bits, no overlap) + hard per-bit decode + hard place-value combine -> cheap (O(log n_shards)) but fragile (route-acc 0.710 at f=0.7).
- **HOLISTIC** (SOFT-but-exhaustive): maximal redundancy in effect (every shard's own codeword compared) + soft nearest-neighbor combine -> robust (0.973 at f=0.7) but O(n_shards x dims), the exact full-sweep the architecture exists to avoid.

Neither is the construction the biology/coding-theory synthesis actually predicts. The untested design (**REDUNDANT_SOFT**): a SMALL, FIXED number R of independent, INCOMMENSURATE small-modulus/hash channels (R ~ 3-5, grid-module analog / RRNS-with-a-few-extra-moduli analog -- NOT one channel per bit-place, NOT one channel per shard), each channel individually cheap and locally ambiguous, combined via a SOFT/confidence-weighted accumulation (belief-propagation-style graded vote over the small factor-graph of channels x shards, or a population-vector-style soft combine -- explicitly NOT majority-of-hard-decisions, which the cell's own self-test already showed loses) to produce a SHORTLIST of M << n_shards candidate shards (M ~ 2-4, the multi-probe-LSH / top-K-MoE / CA3-attractor-top-M analog already validated on-platform in `exp_addressing_learned_noise_robust_page_router_v1`). Stage 2 then runs attractor completion over the M-shard shortlist, not 1 shard and not all n_shards. Cost ~ O(R + M x dims) -- independent of n_shards by construction (matching the grid-module property that module count doesn't grow with the position range), unlike HOLISTIC's O(n_shards x dims).

This composes 3 already-existing on-platform pieces (stage12's decomposed-channel machinery, a33c634c's top-M candidate-page attractor search, and the cell's own cleanup() as the soft per-channel scorer) with exactly ONE genuinely new piece: the soft cross-channel combining rule (belief-propagation-style or population-vector-style, not majority-vote, not place-value-hard-combine).

## Cheap decisive test

One CPU cell, reusing the stage12 cell's harness and grids (adds a third arm, no new primitives beyond the combine rule): build **REDUNDANT_SOFT** — R (try R=3, R=5) independent small-dim channels, each a random projection + `cleanup()`-style soft score against a SMALL set of shard-coset codewords (not the full n_shards, not decomposed bit-planes); combine channel scores by SUM (not vote) into a per-shard confidence; take top-M (M=2, M=4) shardlist. Sweep: (i) corruption f in the SAME grid as stage12 Part 3 ({0.0,0.3,0.5,0.6,0.7,0.8,0.9}), report route-acc = P(true shard in shortlist) at each f, head-to-head against HARD (0.710 at f=0.7) and HOLISTIC (0.973 at f=0.7); (ii) n_shards in the SAME pair as stage12 Part 2 ({32, 320}), report analytic cost = R (channel decodes) + M x dims (shortlist scoring), confirm growth ratio at 10x n_shards. Est. cost: <1 day, mostly reuse of stage12 + a33c634c code paths.

## Falsifiable predictions (HARD-PASS / HARD-FAIL)

1. **Joint cheap-and-robust point exists.** **HARD-PASS:** REDUNDANT_SOFT (at some R in {3,5}, M in {2,4}) achieves route-acc at f=0.7 within 10% of HOLISTIC's 0.973 (i.e. >= 0.876) AND its cost-growth ratio at 10x n_shards stays < 2.0 (matching stage12 Part 2's own HARD-PASS bar for "cheap"). This would be a genuine existence proof that redundancy + soft combine, at small fixed R/M, closes the tension — matching the grid-cell/LDPC/expander-code theorem-shape. **HARD-FAIL:** no (R,M) setting in the tested range achieves BOTH bars simultaneously — i.e., pushing R/M up enough to approach HOLISTIC's robustness also pushes cost up toward O(n_shards) (redundancy becomes disguised brute force, no free lunch on THIS substrate's encoding), while small R/M stays cheap but no more robust than HARD. This would be informative: it would mean the substrate's current phasor/cleanup encoding lacks the structural regularity (expansion/spectral-gap, grid-like incommensurability) that LDPC/expander-codes/grid-cells rely on to make soft combination pay off cheaply — a real, localized encoding-level gap, not a design-effort gap.

2. **Soft combine beats hard combine at MATCHED redundancy.** Isolate the combine-rule variable specifically: build a HARD_VOTE variant of REDUNDANT_SOFT (same R channels, same M shortlist size, but majority/threshold-vote combine instead of soft-sum combine). **HARD-PASS:** soft-combine route-acc at f=0.7 beats hard-vote-combine route-acc by >= 0.10 at the SAME R, M (isolating the LDPC-style "soft beats hard at matched redundancy" claim, distinct from and not to be conflated with the stage12 self-test's already-found "majority vote of narrow decodes < single wide decode" result, since here redundancy R is held FIXED and only the combine rule varies). **HARD-FAIL:** soft and hard combine perform equivalently (within noise) at matched R, M — meaning the soft-vs-hard lever the LDPC/grid-cell literature predicts does not transfer to this substrate's specific encoding, and the earlier stage12 self-test finding generalizes further than expected (redundancy's benefit, if any, would then have to come from elsewhere, e.g. from M alone, not from soft combination of R channels).

3. **Failure localizes to encoding, not composition, if HARD-FAIL.** If prediction 1 HARD_FAILs, brain-check discriminator: compare against `exp_addressing_learned_noise_robust_page_router_v1`'s own top-M attractor search result (LEARNED_ROUTER's noise-robustness gates, P3/F2) — if a33c634c's top-M mechanism ALSO shows the same cheap-vs-robust wall at comparable corruption levels, the wall is a property of THIS substrate's phasor-cleanup primitive generally (a structural bound, brain-check: accept and pivot to a different encoding primitive, e.g. the block-sparse capacity work in flight); if a33c634c does NOT show the wall at comparable settings, the wall is specific to shard-routing's combine-rule design (fixable, iterate on the combine rule, not the primitive).

## Substrate-product implications

If Prediction 1 HARD-PASSes: fuzzy/partial-cue lookups over an arbitrarily large sharded store become genuinely cheap (sub-linear, not O(store-size)) at the SAME robustness band already validated for the expensive HOLISTIC arm — this is a direct cost/latency win for any user-facing query with an imprecise or corrupted cue (a typo'd name, a partially-remembered fact, a noisy sensor read), letting the store scale without a linear query-cost tax. If it HARD-FAILs, that is equally valuable product information: it means this substrate's current shard-routing encoding has a genuine structural ceiling on the cheap-and-robust tradeoff (matching the field's own unsolved status), and the fix is a different encoding primitive (block-sparse / grid-cell-incommensurate-style codes, next in the queue), not more engineering effort on the combine rule.
