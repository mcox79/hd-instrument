# Research: a sparse-compatible binding operator so DG-expansion and binding-consistency COMPOSE instead of conflict

**Date:** 2026-07-09
**Type:** Brain-first deep drill (4 parallel Sonnet lit-scan sub-agents: brain synchrony/dendritic binding, hippocampal-entorhinal sparse binding, sparse VSA binding-scheme menu, sparsity-vs-chaining tradeoff frontier), synthesized into one buildable proposal.
**Trigger:** retrain VET (MIDDLE_BAND): binding-consistency training objective lifts role-recovery 0.28->0.42 and Pareto-dominates baseline, but reach stays 1 at scale AND dentate-gyrus sparse-expansion HURTS (-0.212 recall; FULL_STACK 0.42->0.25). Diagnosed root cause: dense HRR circular-convolution binding produces a dense bound product; a hard per-block k-WTA argmax sparsifier then destructively collapses it. ## HEADLINE

**The brain never binds directly on its sparsest code either — and the literature already contains a directly-buildable operator that fixes our exact mismatch.** Across all three neural mechanisms that could plausibly "bind" sparse codes (synchrony, dendritic coincidence-detection, expansion+sparsification), **none of them bind the ultra-sparse code with a single reversible algebraic operator and then query it directly.** The brain's actual two-stage solution — modular/block-structured composition (entorhinal grid modules, combined via a residue-number-system-like conjunctive code) followed by a SEPARATE, denser downstream associative stage (CA3 recurrent collaterals) that does the actual query/retrieval/pattern-completion — has a near-exact, already-published, already-measured VSA analog: **sparse BLOCK-CODE binding via local circular convolution (LCC)** (Frady, Kleyko & Sommer, arXiv:2009.06734) for the bind/compose step, paired with an **iterative resonator/factorizer-style cleanup** (Frady et al. Resonator Networks; Hersche et al., arXiv:2303.13957) for the retrieval/chaining step — functioning as the CA3-pattern-completion analog. This is not speculative synthesis: LCC on sparse block codes is measured at ~0.95 unbind fidelity (vs ~0.80 for naive dense-convolution-on-sparse, vs ~0.30 for plain Hadamard-on-sparse) and demonstrated on an explicit 2-hop chaining task in the source paper. It is a direct, structural fix for the diagnosed mismatch: LCC's bound output is, by construction, exactly as sparse as its input (one active index per block in -> one active index per block out) — sparsity is a structural invariant of the operator, not something a hard argmax must re-impose on a dense mess after the fact.

## S1 — How the brain binds sparse codes, and which mechanism is load-bearing

Three candidate neural binding mechanisms were surveyed. None is a clean "algebra": each has a specific, well-documented representational job, and only one comes with a genuine retrieval/query story.

| Mechanism | Format | Supports unbind/query? | Multi-item chaining? | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Binding-by-synchrony** (von der Malsburg 1981; Gray/Singer late-1980s; ~40-60 Hz phase-locking) | timing-tagged rate code | **No** — a grouping/detection signal, not a content-addressable readout | Unclear, unresolved | Medium, and **contested**: Shadlen & Movshon, "Synchrony Unbound," *Neuron* 1999, is the canonical rebuttal — no evidence of millisecond-precision cortical coincidence detection at the needed resolution, and even granting synchrony exists, "the theory does not adequately address how those combinations are computed." Reynolds & Desimone-style attention/normalization (Reynolds & Heeger, *Neuron* 2009) is a better-evidenced partial alternative, but it solves *selection*, not compositional binding with retrieval. |
| **Dendritic/coincidence-detection binding** (Mel, Poirazi-Mel; NMDA-spike branch nonlinearities; apical-basal coupling in L5 pyramidal cells) | branch-local nonlinear AND/threshold | **No** — a detector, not a retrieval mechanism; reports "these co-occurred," nothing lets a downstream reader query one bound element to recover the other | Pairwise/local only (within one branch, or apical-basal within one cell); no evidence for algebraic composability across many items | Medium — biophysics well-replicated, "binding" interpretation more inferential than the raw NMDA-spike/coincidence data itself |
| **Sparse conjunctive coding via combinatorial expansion + WTA-style inhibition** (cerebellar granule cells, Marr/Albus; fly mushroom-body Kenyon cells, Lin et al. 2014; PFC mixed selectivity, Rigotti et al. 2013) | high-D sparse, near-orthogonal conjunctive vector | **Yes, partially** — the one mechanism with a genuine retrieval story: near-orthogonality lets a downstream associative memory (Purkinje/Kenyon-cell weights, or Hamming-distance lookup) content-address and pattern-complete | Mostly **one-shot/single-episode conjunctions**, not native multi-hop chaining; composing further would need re-encoding through another expansion layer | Medium-high for mushroom body (well-replicated); medium and contested for pure cerebellar codon theory (recent recordings show task-dependent dense granule-cell activity, not universally sparse) |

**The load-bearing mechanism is expansion+sparsification — but critically, it is not a "bind two vectors, get a bound vector, then unbind" algebra. It is a one-shot combinatorial *encoding* step, with retrieval delegated to a separate, denser associative stage.** This is confirmed directly by the hippocampal-specific sub-scan: the DG->CA3 pathway is architecturally two stages, not one. DG performs pattern separation (mossy-fiber "detonator" synapses onto CA3; Vyleta, Borges-Merjane & Jonas, eLife 2016), producing a highly sparse, decorrelated code — but **binding/associative retrieval happens downstream, in CA3's recurrent-collateral autoassociator, on a denser ensemble code, not on the raw sparse DG code itself** (Marr 1971; O'Reilly & McClelland 1994; Rolls 2013 lineage). Multi-hop/transitive relational binding specifically requires an additional structural-factorization layer: the best mechanistic model found, the Tolman-Eichenbaum Machine (Whittington et al. 2020, *Cell*/PMC7707106), explicitly factorizes relational structure from sensory content in a separate layer to get genuine multi-step/transitive inference, rather than doing it on the sparse code directly.

The entorhinal grid-cell system supplies the closest thing to a genuine sparse **compositional** operator: grid modules of different spatial periods combine via what is, mathematically, a residue-number-system-style construction — Kymn, Mazelet, Thomas, Kleyko, Frady, Sommer & Olshausen ("Binding in hippocampal-entorhinal circuits enables compositionality in cognitive maps," arXiv:2406.18808, 2024) model this explicitly as a similarity-preserving conjunctive *vector-binding* operator across grid modules, self-consistency enforced by a modular attractor network mapping onto MEC grid modules. This is a **modular/block-structured composition mechanism, not a single global dense bind** — each grid module is effectively an independent "block," and the high-resolution place code is the conjunction (residue-number combination) across blocks, not a full-dimension multiplicative bind.

**Synthesis of S1 (the structural lesson to port):** the brain's real solution to "compose sparse codes without destroying them" is a **two-stage architecture** — (1) modular/block-local composition (grid-module RNS-style conjunction) builds the sparse code, sparsity preserved by construction because each module/block only ever touches its own local support; (2) a separate, denser recurrent/attractor stage (CA3, or TEM's relational-factorization layer) does the actual query/retrieval/multi-hop chaining, rather than a single-shot algebraic inversion of the sparse code itself. This maps directly onto S2 below.

## S2 — The concrete buildable sparse-compatible binding operator

### The technical menu (what's out there)

- **MAP/BSC (bipolar multiply / XOR)** preserve exact self-inverse algebra regardless of sparsity, but do so by touching every dimension — they don't preserve *sparsity* under bind, they just don't corrupt invertibility. Not the fix (Schlegel, Neubert & Protzel, "A Comparison of Vector Symbolic Architectures," *Artif. Intell. Rev.* 2022, arXiv:2001.11797).
- **Dense HRR circular convolution on sparse inputs** (our current failure mode): fidelity measured at ~0.80 correlation, vs ~0.95 for dense-vector HRR — already degraded before a hard argmax further destroys it. Plain Hadamard product on sparse vectors collapses to ~0.30 correlation (Frady, Kleyko & Sommer, arXiv:2009.06734).
- **BSDC-S (plain disjunction-based sparse binding, no thinning):** ~44% dimensionality blow-up under bind+bundle — the worst option.
- **BSDC-SEG (segmented/block-local shift binding):** ~22% blow-up — better than plain disjunction, still lossy relative to block codes proper.
- **Context-dependent thinning (Rachkovskij & Kussul 2001, *Neural Computation* 13(2):411-452):** superpose then resample down to target sparsity via a context-dependent selection rule; a legitimate fallback, but multi-hop chaining fidelity is less studied than the block-code family.
- **Sparse BLOCK-CODE binding via Local Circular Convolution (LCC)** (Frady, Kleyko & Sommer, "Variable Binding for Sparse Distributed Representations: Theory and Applications," arXiv:2009.06734, later IEEE TNNLS): vectors partitioned into K blocks, each block a one-hot (or few-hot) local code. **Bind = within-block circular convolution (index addition mod block-length); unbind = within-block circular correlation (index subtraction).** Measured ~0.95 unbind fidelity — the best of everything tested. **The bound output is, by construction, exactly as sparse as the input**: one active index per block in, one active index per block out — sparsity never has to be re-imposed by a downstream argmax because the operator cannot produce a denser result in the first place. The paper's own abstract states the mechanism directly: *"variable binding for block-codes has ideal properties, whereas binding for general sparse vectors also works, but is lossy."* Crosstalk scales as SNR ~ N/R^2 for R bound role-filler pairs (90% accuracy at R=2, degrading to ~60% at R=8) — a standard superposition-capacity tradeoff, not a binding-fidelity failure. The paper explicitly demonstrates 2-hop chaining (an analogical-reasoning task, `dollar : USA :: ? : Mexico`, computed via two chained unbind operations, correctly recovers "peso").
- **Resonator-network / factorizer iterative unbind** (Frady, Kleyko, Kymn, Olshausen & Sommer, "Resonator Networks," *Neural Computation* 32, 2020; Hersche, Terzic, Karunaratne, Langenegger, Pouget, Cherubini, Benini & Sebastian, "Factorizers for Distributed Sparse Block Codes," arXiv:2303.13957, IBM Research-Zurich/ETH, 2023): extends the exact same block-code family with an iterative, attractor-dynamics unbind that factorizes multiple superposed bound codewords via repeated cleanup against a stored codebook — reported up to 6x fewer iterations than prior stochastic factorizers, and cites arXiv:2009.06734 directly as its foundational binding theory. **This is the direct, already-published analog of CA3's role in the brain's two-stage architecture** (S1's synthesis): block-local binding builds the sparse composite code (grid-module analog); iterative resonator cleanup does the retrieval/multi-hop query (CA3-attractor analog), rather than a single noisy algebraic subtraction.
- **Model-based compressed sensing** (Baraniuk, Cevher, Duarte & Hegde, IEEE Trans. Info. Theory 56(4), 2010) supplies the formal reason structured sparsity beats unstructured: imposing a block/tree/group-sparse model yields a strictly weaker "model-RIP" recovery condition than plain unstructured-k-sparse RIP — a proven bound, not a heuristic, for why block structure specifically (not sparsity alone) is what buys clean invertibility.

### The proposal, ranked

**RANK 1 (primary fix — bind operator): swap dense HRR circular convolution for block-local circular convolution (LCC), matched 1:1 to the existing k-WTA-per-block sparse code structure.** Each block's active index is treated as an element of Z_m (m = block size); bind = modular index addition within the block (keyed by the "relation"/role code's local index applied to the "filler"/value code's local index); unbind = modular index subtraction. Because the operator acts entirely within the same block partition the DG-style argmax code already lives in, **DG-expansion (more blocks and/or larger blocks -> higher resolution and lower fraction-active) and the binding operator stop fighting**: expansion helps capacity/decorrelation exactly as the 07-04 DG drill predicted (per the correlation-hurts-capacity principle, more/larger blocks reduce collision probability), with zero representational mismatch, because the bind op's output support is identical in shape to its input support by construction (k active blocks in -> k active blocks out, always — never denser, never needing a destructive re-sparsify step).

**RANK 2 (retrieval/chaining mechanism): add a resonator/factorizer-style iterative cleanup as the multi-hop unbind step**, rather than a single-shot subtract-then-argmax. This is the direct analog of the brain's CA3 stage (S1): the brain does not do single-shot algebraic unbind on its sparsest code either — it delegates retrieval to a denser, iterative attractor process. Given how consistently the literature separates "build the sparse composite code" from "iteratively clean it up to retrieve," it is the most literature-grounded candidate specifically for restoring reach>=2, because each hop's noise gets cleaned against a stored codebook via attractor dynamics rather than compounding raw through successive raw subtractions.

**RANK 3 (fallback/hedge): context-dependent thinning binding** if block-local LCC underperforms for any reason specific to our training objective — a structurally different sparsity-preservation mechanism (union+thin vs. block-local permutation/convolution) worth a fallback arm, though less literature-characterized for multi-hop chaining than the block-code family.

### The measurable test

Implement RANK 1 (block-local LCC bind/unbind) on the SAME k-WTA sparse block code already used for DG-expansion, keep the binding-consistency training objective unchanged, and rerun the exact reach/role-recovery chain-grade cert cell that produced reach=1 with dense-HRR-then-argmax. Optionally add RANK 2 (iterative resonator-style unbind) as a second arm if RANK 1 alone under-performs at hop >= 2.

**HARD-PASS:** reach >= 2 on sparse learned codes, AND recall/precision/F1 (role-recovery) at least matches the 0.42 dense-code binding-consistency result, AND the DG-expansion arm is now net-positive (not the -0.212 regression) versus no-expansion, at matched sparsity (~2%).

**HARD-FAIL:** reach stays at 1 even with block-local LCC binding (would mean the chaining ceiling is NOT a binding-operator/representational mismatch after all, but an unrelated optimization- or noise-accumulation-over-hops problem — route to a different mechanism, e.g. escalate straight to RANK 2's iterative cleanup layer as the primary lever rather than a secondary one), OR block-local binding fails to preserve algebra fidelity even at hop 1 (unbind similarity to the original filler drops below the current dense-HRR baseline, e.g. < 0.90 cosine/index-match), meaning block-locality itself destroys enough cross-block information to not be worth the swap.

**MIDDLE-BAND:** reach improves to 2 but role-recovery quality degrades relative to the dense-code 0.42 baseline, or DG-expansion becomes only mildly positive rather than clearly net-positive — worth one iteration adding RANK 2's iterative cleanup before declaring PASS or FAIL, matching this project's own established falsifiable-prediction discipline.

## S3 — Sharpest question + deflated P

**Sharpest open question:** does a single-shot block-local LCC unbind (RANK 1 alone, no iterative cleanup) suffice to restore reach>=2, or is the resonator/factorizer-style ITERATIVE cleanup step (RANK 2) structurally REQUIRED — mirroring the brain's own requirement for a separate CA3 attractor stage rather than a single-shot readout directly off the sparse DG code? This determines whether the fix is a **1-component swap** (replace the bind operator only) or a **2-component fix** (replace the bind operator AND add an iterative attractor/cleanup retrieval stage). S1's brain evidence leans toward 2-component being necessary (the brain never skips the CA3-equivalent stage), but the VSA literature's own 2-hop chaining demonstration (Frady et al.) used single-shot LCC unbind successfully at R=2, suggesting RANK 1 alone might clear our specific reach>=2 bar before the R-dependent crosstalk (SNR ~ N/R^2) becomes the limiting factor.

**P_deflated:**
- Narrow claim ("block-local LCC preserves both sparsity and unbind fidelity, structurally resolving the diagnosed dense-bind/sparse-code mismatch"): **P ~0.55** — this is close to directly measured in the source literature (0.95 vs 0.80 vs 0.30 fidelity comparison already run by Frady et al.), not really novel synthesis, only modestly deflated for porting to our specific k-WTA-per-block code format (which may differ in block size/count from their setup).
- Compound claim ("RANK 1 (+ RANK 2 if needed) restores reach>=2 on OUR sparse learned codes AND keeps the DG-expansion arm net-positive AND composes cleanly with the existing binding-consistency training objective," the HARD-PASS bar above): **P_deflated = 0.40** (raw ~0.60-0.65 given the near-direct precedent; deflated ~0.20-0.25 for three specific untested-combination risks: (i) whether RANK 1 alone suffices or RANK 2 is structurally required per the S3 question above; (ii) whether our block sizes/counts match the regime where LCC's ~0.95 fidelity holds, versus a regime closer to their more-lossy general-sparse-vector case; (iii) whether the binding-consistency training objective, designed and tuned against dense HRR, transfers cleanly to a block-local LCC target without re-tuning. ## Substrate-product implications

This is a **structural swap in the binding operator, not a new sub-system** — RANK 1 (block-local LCC) is a drop-in replacement for the current dense circular-convolution bind/unbind, matched to the block structure the k-WTA sparsifier already produces; it requires no new architecture, only a different index-arithmetic rule applied within the same block layout. If the cheap decisive test clears HARD-PASS, this directly unblocks BOTH open items from the retrain VET in one move: it should let the DG-expansion lever (07-04 drill) go from net-negative to net-positive without any change to the sparsifier itself, and it should restore multi-hop reach past 1, which is the load-bearing capability for chain-grade relational retrieval broadly (not just this cell). If RANK 1 alone HARD-FAILs, RANK 2 (resonator/factorizer iterative cleanup) is a well-precedented, moderate-effort follow-up rather than a dead end — the literature explicitly builds RANK 2 as an extension of RANK 1's exact codebase family, so a RANK 1 FAIL does not waste the RANK 1 implementation work; it becomes the substrate RANK 2 iterates against.

## Intuitive summary

**What we asked:** our encoder learned a training trick (binding-consistency) that clearly helps it recognize "who did what to whom" in one relationship — but it still can't chain two or more relationships together, and when we tried to also make the codes sparser (fewer active units, which should help capacity and reduce interference, the way the brain's hippocampus does it), that actually made things WORSE, not better. We asked: how does the brain itself combine sparse signals without this kind of breakdown, and is there an already-known engineering trick that does the same thing?

**What we found:** the brain does not, anywhere we could find, take two sparse signals and cleanly "multiply" them together the way our current code does. Every candidate brain mechanism either (a) doesn't support looking information back up once it's combined (it's a one-way detector, not a two-way memory), or (b) works in small independent local pieces (one dendritic branch, one grid-cell module) that get combined WITHOUT ever needing a single all-at-once dense multiplication, with the actual "look it back up" step handed off to a separate, denser memory stage downstream (the hippocampus's CA3 region). That two-stage pattern — compose in small local pieces, then retrieve with a separate cleanup step — turns out to already exist as a concrete, tested engineering recipe in the sparse-computing literature, built by other researchers, with measured numbers showing it works far better than the naive approach we're currently using, and it was even shown chaining two relationships together successfully in their own tests.

**Why it matters:** this isn't a vague research direction — it's a specific, drop-in fix to try next. Swap the way our sparse codes get combined so that combining them never produces anything denser than what went in (so nothing needs to be forcibly squeezed back down and mangled afterward), and consider adding a small "clean it up" retrieval step for chaining multiple relationships, matching what the hippocampus's downstream stage does. If it works, both stuck problems from the last experiment — chaining stops at one step, and adding more sparsity hurts instead of helps — should resolve together, because they were actually the same underlying mismatch.

**Where this leaves the project:** the next concrete action is a build-and-measure step, not further research: implement the block-local binding swap on top of the existing sparse code structure (no new sub-system, no architecture change, just a different math rule for combining values), rerun the same test that showed the chaining problem, and see whether chaining now reaches two steps instead of one while the sparse-code benefits stop backfiring. A clear pass/fail bar is specified above so this can be resolved in one clean measurement cycle.
