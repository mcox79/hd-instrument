## (a) HEADLINE

**The brain's own answer to "how does a word-form connect to meaning" is not sensorimotor-or-relational — it is a small directly-grounded base plus grounding TRANSFER through relations/composition onto everything else, and the word-form itself is treated in the neuroscience literature as just one more spoke feeding an amodal hub, not a privileged channel.** This is directly good news for a body-less, instrument/relational-grounded substrate: the literature does not resolve whether pure relational/graph-structured grounding "counts" as full grounding on its own (genuinely open question, not a refutation), but it explicitly recognizes structural/relational grounding-transfer from a small grounded kernel as a legitimate, established mechanism (Cangelosi & Riga; Taddeo & Floridi) — which is exactly the shape of "small measured/relational foundation grounds the rest via composition" already adopted in the 07-15 grounding-subsumed thread. Separately, and independently convergent: the **exact architecture already built for the syntactic scaffold — role-filler binding (Smolensky tensor-product representations; Plate's Holographic Reduced Representations; Eliasmith's Semantic Pointer Architecture) — is precisely the published mechanism for uniting compositional STRUCTURE with grounded CONTENT**, and the Fodor-Pylyshyn/SCAN/Lake-Baroni literature independently confirms that this class of explicit structured-binding architecture (not plain sequence models) is what is required for genuine compositional generalization — so the scaffold's proven parse/generate/generalize result is not incidental, it is the textbook precondition for content to compose correctly once real fillers replace random ones. The one clean gap all three lit-scans converge on, independently: **no published account exists of the actual LEARNING RULE that attaches a word-form's pointer/phasor to its correct target meaning** (SPA lit-scan explicitly flags this as thin/unverified even within Eliasmith's own program) — this is real novel-synthesis territory, but child-language literature (cross-situational learning, mutual exclusivity, syntactic bootstrapping, fast-mapping) gives a converging, testable multi-cue recipe for exactly this learning rule, glass-box-transcribable as a co-occurrence-weighted lookup table rather than a hidden embedding.

## (b) Cheap decisive test

Build the smallest possible closed loop and check whether the parse literally YIELDS a foundation fact:

1. Pick ~8-12 Dolch words (a handful of nouns: cat, dog, boy, girl; a handful of verbs: sees, runs, likes; function words: the, a) that also exist, or can be trivially mapped, as concept-nodes in the existing small foundation graph.
2. Build a tiny paired corpus: N SVO sentences ("cat sees dog," "dog runs," "boy likes girl") each paired with the corresponding foundation triple already present (or directly instantiable) in the foundation graph (subject-concept, predicate-relation, object-concept).
3. **Arm LEXICON-LEARNED:** run the glass-box co-occurrence lexicon-learning procedure (section 3 below) over the paired corpus to build a word-form -> foundation-concept-phasor table. Parse held-out (never-co-occurring) SVO recombinations using this table to fill the proven role-filler scaffold. Unbind the OBJECT (or SUBJECT) role from the resulting parse structure and take nearest-neighbor against the foundation's own concept-vector space.
4. **Arm RANDOM (control, already-run baseline):** identical scaffold, random phasors — the existing negative result.
5. **Arm LEXICON-MEMORIZED (overfit control):** lexicon table built the same way, but evaluated ONLY on SVO combinations seen during lexicon training (not held out) — this isolates whether any success in arm 1 is genuine compositional recovery vs. rote pair memorization.

**HARD-PASS:** LEXICON-LEARNED's nearest-neighbor foundation-concept-retrieval accuracy on HELD-OUT novel role-filler combinations exceeds a frequency/unigram baseline (most-common-concept-for-that-role) by >=20 points, AND is not statistically distinguishable from LEXICON-MEMORIZED's accuracy (i.e., held-out generalization is not meaningfully worse than the memorized-pairs case — the signature of genuine compositional recovery, not overfit lookup).

**HARD-FAIL:** LEXICON-LEARNED's held-out accuracy is indistinguishable from RANDOM control, OR held-out accuracy is far below LEXICON-MEMORIZED's accuracy (>=20-point gap) — the latter would mean the lexicon+binding pipeline memorizes seen pairs rather than compositionally generalizing, i.e. a construction-proof, not a capability win (same discipline already applied to the additive_map enrichment result).

## (e) Substrate-product implications

1. **Do not treat "grounding the lexicon" as a new module.** Per the ATL lexical-spoke finding and the 07-10 hub-node architecture option already scoped, the lexicon table is one more spoke-to-hub edge type. Building it reuses the existing foundation hub machinery rather than adding a parallel content system — lowest-friction integration path.
2. **The word-form <-> foundation-concept mapping should be a literal, inspectable lookup table (word_form -> ranked candidate foundation-concept-ids + association weights + provenance/exemplar-count), not a learned dense embedding.** This is both the glass-box requirement and the direct computational analog of the co-occurrence-tracking + mutual-exclusivity + syntactic-role-gating recipe the child-language literature converges on — every one of those three mechanisms is expressible as an update rule on a sparse countable table, not a black-box optimization.
3. **Bootstrap order, mirroring the biology directly:** (i) semantic-bootstrapping-first for the smallest content words (nouns whose foundation-concept is unambiguous from co-occurrence alone — concrete Dolch nouns are the natural starting set, matching this cycle's Prediction-3 role-restriction logic and the sibling curriculum note's Rung 1b "grounding-seed" nouns); (ii) syntactic-bootstrapping for verbs and relational words once the scaffold's role structure is available to narrow their candidate foundation-relation type (matches Pinker's semantic-bootstrapping-then-syntactic-bootstrapping developmental handoff, found independently in lit-scan lane 2).
4. **Use the SAME foundation-concept vectors as both (a) the reasoning-architecture's fillers (additive_map composition) and (b) the language-scaffold's fillers (role-filler binding).** This is the literal mechanism realizing "the language module's structure composes the knowledge foundation's content" — no translation layer needed if the vector space is shared, which the existing shared-API decision (`project_additive_map_builder_integration_endgame_functional_plus_strict_via_shared_api_2026-07-13`) already puts within reach.
5. **Fast-mapping + mutual-exclusivity give a cheap incremental-vocabulary-growth rule for free**, once the smallest closed loop (section b) passes: a novel word encountered in a sentence with otherwise-known words and an otherwise-unlabeled foundation concept in the appropriate role-slot should be tentatively bound to that concept on a SINGLE exposure (elimination-based fast-mapping), then confirmed/revised via cross-situational tracking on subsequent exposures (propose-but-verify) — directly reusable for scaling the lexicon past the initial 8-12-word closed loop without redesigning the mechanism.
6. **Flag honestly: this is genuine novel-synthesis for the LEARNING RULE specifically** (word-form-to-pointer attachment), even though the BINDING algebra and the GROUNDING-transfer architecture are both well-precedented. Do not overclaim the whole pipeline as "brain-validated" — only the structural shape is; the specific lexicon-learning update rule is this drill's own construction, built by combining four independently-precedented child-language mechanisms, and must clear its own HARD-PASS/HARD-FAIL bar (Prediction 2/3) rather than inherit confidence from the binding-algebra claim (Prediction 1).

## 1. The brain's word-grounding (full detail)

**Symbol grounding is not "sensorimotor or relational" — it is sensorimotor-seed-plus-relational-transfer.** Harnad (1990) grounds a base vocabulary bottom-up via iconic (analog sensory projection) and categorical (learned invariant-feature) representations. Cangelosi & Riga (2006) show empirically (robotic/simulated agents) that a small directly-grounded base of "basic" words/actions supports meaning for a much larger set of higher-order/abstract words through **grounding transfer** — combining already-grounded components via linguistic definition/composition, with no further sensorimotor exposure needed for the transferred words. Taddeo & Floridi's review of post-Harnad approaches explicitly names **relational/structural grounding** — meaning derived from a symbol's logical/relational position among already-grounded symbols — as a recognized (non-representationalist-adjacent) strategy, distinct from but not incompatible with sensorimotor grounding.

**The hub-and-spoke model treats word-FORM as just one more spoke.** Patterson, Nestor & Rogers (2007) and Lambon Ralph et al.'s hub-and-spoke hypothesis put an amodal transmodal hub in ventrolateral ATL, integrating modality-specific spokes (vision, audition, action/motor, emotion) into a single concept. This cycle's lit-scan confirms explicitly that a **lexical/phonological spoke** (word-form / language-area input) is one of those converging spokes, architecturally on par with the perceptual ones, not a privileged or separate channel. A related but distinct literature — the "triangle model" of reading (Seidenberg & McClelland 1989; Plaut et al. 1996) — separately models the orthography-phonology-semantics interface WITHIN the word-form-processing system itself; no paper found in this cycle explicitly reconciles the two literatures, which is itself a useful gap to note (they are complementary, not contradictory, but haven't been formally unified).

**Distributional-vs-embodied is a live, unresolved debate, and the specific question we care about ("does relational/graph-structured grounding count?") is genuinely open, not settled against us.** Bender & Koller's "octopus test" argues pure text co-occurrence (form with zero external referent) cannot bootstrap meaning. But this cycle's lit-scan finds active pushback (arXiv 2407.21070; Michael's "To Dissect an Octopus") arguing the real requirement may be a small grounding "toehold," not full sensorimotor immersion — and no paper was found that adjudicates whether a knowledge-graph-only (relational, instrument-measured) representation counts as that toehold or remains within Bender & Koller's ungrounded "form" category. **This is the load-bearing takeaway for a body-less substrate: the literature does not say relational grounding is insufficient — it says the question hasn't been settled, which means our own cheap decisive test (section b) is genuinely informative science, not just an engineering convenience.**

**Cross-situational word learning is the child's actual mechanism for resolving Quine's gavagai problem across ambiguous exposures**, and the field (per a 2023 15-year review) has NOT converged on one winning mechanism — associative/statistical tracking, hypothesis-testing/"propose-but-verify," and full joint Bayesian inference over the lexicon are all empirically supported in different regimes, treated as complementary rather than competing. This directly licenses building our own glass-box hybrid (section 3) rather than needing to pick a single "correct" biological mechanism — the biology itself runs a mixed-mechanism system.

**Which path for a substrate with instrument-measured/relational grounding, no body:** relational-transfer-from-a-small-grounded-kernel, per Cangelosi & Riga and Taddeo & Floridi, is the literature-matched path — not a fallback or a compromise, but the SAME structural solution the brain uses for its own vast majority of abstract/non-directly-perceived vocabulary (most adult words were never individually sensorimotor-grounded; they were acquired via definition and relation to an earlier-grounded base). Our foundation's small set of instrument/relational-measured "seed" concepts plays the role of Harnad's/Cangelosi's directly-grounded base; the lexicon-learning mechanism (section 3) is the transfer step.

## 2. Structure-content unification — is role-filler binding the right glass-box architecture

**Yes, and this is close to a mechanical consequence of two independently-converging literatures, not a novel proposal.** Smolensky's tensor-product representations (1990) define exactly this: a role vector (structural position — "subject-of," "first-argument-of") bound via tensor product to a filler vector (content); structures are superpositions of bindings; because the algebra is bilinear and filler-agnostic, the SAME operations that bind/compose known fillers work identically on NOVEL fillers substituted into the same role — this is the literal algebraic guarantee that "parsing binds the foundation's concepts into role-filler meaning" rather than needing to have seen that specific combination before. Plate's HRR (1995) and the wider VSA family implement the identical binding/bundling/unbinding trio in fixed dimensionality via circular convolution/correlation — precisely the operation class the already-proven syntactic scaffold uses. Eliasmith's Semantic Pointer Architecture explicitly frames a "pointer" as a compressed, non-arbitrary summary of a grounded lower-level representation, i.e. content-inheritance-by-construction — directly matching "the word-phasor is a pointer/label INTO a foundation-concept, not an arbitrary random vector."

**The Fodor-Pylyshyn/SCAN thread supplies the reason this matters empirically, not just architecturally.** Fodor & Pylyshyn (1988) argued only classical (role-filler, combinatorial) architectures can explain systematicity; Lake & Baroni's SCAN (2018) empirically showed plain sequence models fail exactly the kind of held-out compositional-recombination test our decisive test proposes, while explicit structured-binding / meta-learned-compositional architectures reach near-100% on the same benchmarks. **This retroactively validates why the already-proven syntactic scaffold's compositional-generalization result is the right kind of result to have gotten** — it is the documented empirical signature of having the correct architectural class, not an incidental pass.

**The one honest gap:** none of the three published architectures (TPR, HRR/VSA, SPA) supplies a general, worked-out account of HOW a pointer/filler gets attached to its target meaning during learning — Eliasmith's own program explains why a pointer's content ISN'T arbitrary once assigned (compression relationship) but not the assignment/learning process for individual lexical items. That gap is precisely section 3's job, and precisely why Prediction 2/3 in section (c) are capped at the novel-synthesis ceiling rather than inheriting Prediction 1's higher confidence.

**Answer to the framing question:** yes, "language = compositional STRUCTURE over foundation-GROUNDED fillers" is the right glass-box architecture — it is what TPR/HRR/SPA are FOR, it is what the systematicity literature says is required for genuine compositional generalization, and it directly realizes the recreate-structure-content-factorization thrust as a concrete build (word-phasor := pointer to foundation-concept-phasor; parse := bind role-vectors to those pointers; unbind := retrieve a foundation fact; generation := run the same binding backward from a chosen foundation fact).

## 3. Glass-box lexicon-learning (no LLM) — the update rule, built from four converging child-language mechanisms

None of these four mechanisms is individually sufficient in the child-language literature either — the field treats them as a **multi-cue system whose relative weighting shifts with development**, which licenses combining them glass-box rather than picking one:

1. **Cross-situational co-occurrence tracking (Yu & Smith; Frank/Goodman/Tenenbaum Bayesian framework)** — for each word-form encountered in a curriculum sentence, increment an association weight toward every foundation-concept present in that sentence's paired grounded context (if the curriculum ships sentence+fact pairs, as scoped in the sibling curriculum note); across many sentences, the TRUE word-concept pairing accumulates weight faster than any single spurious co-occurrence, because it recurs across varying contexts while distractor pairings don't. This is directly implementable as a countable co-occurrence table, fully inspectable, no hidden state.
2. **Mutual exclusivity (Markman & Wachtel)** — when resolving a word's top candidate, down-weight foundation-concepts that are already the top-weighted target of a DIFFERENT, already-stable word-mapping; this breaks ties and accelerates convergence exactly as it does for children (with the literature's own caveat that the constraint is not absolute — the update rule should soften rather than hard-forbid a re-mapping if co-occurrence evidence strongly contradicts it later, mirroring the "flexible use of mutual exclusivity" findings in the bilingual/taxonomic-hierarchy literature).
3. **Syntactic bootstrapping (Gleitman/Naigles/Fisher)** — the ROLE a word occupies in the proven scaffold's parse (subject-slot, object-slot, verb-slot) restricts its candidate foundation-concepts to the role-appropriate class before co-occurrence statistics even run, exactly mirroring Naigles' transitive/intransitive-frame experiment; this is Prediction 3's mechanism and requires the foundation's concepts to carry (or be trivially assigned) a role-eligibility tag (agent/patient/relation-type), which the foundation's existing schema likely already encodes implicitly via edge-type.
4. **Fast-mapping via elimination (Carey & Bartlett, tempered by the "propose-but-verify" and forgetting-without-reinforcement critiques found this cycle)** — on encountering a genuinely novel word in a sentence where every OTHER word and foundation-concept is already known/mapped, tentatively bind the novel word to whichever foundation-concept in the sentence's role-appropriate slot is not yet claimed by any other word; per the literature's own robustness critique, treat this as a PROVISIONAL single-exposure guess requiring later cross-situational confirmation (mechanism 1) before being trusted, not a one-shot final answer — this directly avoids the "fast-mapped words are quickly forgotten without reinforcement" failure mode found in this cycle's lit-scan.

**Net update rule (fully glass-box, inspectable as a table):** `lexicon[word_form] = ranked list of (foundation_concept_id, weight, exemplar_count)`, updated by (a) co-occurrence increment restricted to (b) role-eligible candidates, (c) softened-mutual-exclusivity re-weighting against concurrently-stable competing mappings, with (d) single-exposure provisional entries for words satisfying the elimination criterion, confirmed or revised on subsequent exposures. This is a lookup table with a well-specified update rule, not a learned embedding — satisfies the glass-box requirement by construction, and every individual update term traces to a named, cited child-language mechanism.
