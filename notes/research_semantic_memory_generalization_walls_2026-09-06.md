## HEADLINE

Of the four walls, **two are FIDELITY GAPS with a named brain mechanism to build next** (Wall A: part-whole generalization used the wrong inheritance mechanism — should be similarity/analogical nearest-exemplar transfer, not transitive closure; Wall B: the gate's superordinate abstention is a real Rosch basic-level effect, but the brain's *alternate* validation channel for superordinates — aggregation/consensus across many known children — was never built). **One wall is a PINNED, well-supported architecture as a first-order account with a known future refinement** (Wall C: recency/salience-primary + type-as-filter is confirmed by Lappin & Leass 1994's exact two-stage algorithm and Centering's prominence-based ranking, but cue-based-retrieval and coherence-driven literature show this is the majority case of a more general graded multi-cue system, not an absolute law). **One wall is PINNED for the measured finding and a low-priority FIDELITY GAP for future consumers** (Wall D: exhaustive sense activation with small cost on low-polysemy items is textbook-expected; the brain resolves to a single sense within ~0.2–1.5s via a construction→integration step before long-range inference, which our raw-string store does not yet replicate, but this should stay deferred until a high-polysemy/long-range-sense-dependent consumer is targeted).

## Wall-by-wall

### Wall A — part-whole generalization

**Brain mechanism.** Even is-a inheritance is not literal transitive closure: Collins & Quillian's (1969) "cognitive economy" hierarchy was empirically falsified by its own authors (Collins & Loftus 1975, *Psych Review*) once typicality data showed "robin is a bird" verified faster than "chicken is a bird" at equal hierarchy distance — is-a generalization is similarity/typicality-weighted, not pure logical inheritance. Meronymy is worse off structurally: Winston, Chaffin & Herrmann (1987, *Cognitive Science*) identify six distinct part-whole subtypes (component-integral, member-collection, portion-mass, stuff-object, feature-activity, place-area) that compose transitively only *within* a subtype — mixed-subtype chains are not reliably transitive ("merological syllogisms" often fail). Tversky & Hemenway (1984, *JEP:General*) show parts concentrate at the basic level (~5.8 listed parts/category) and nearly vanish at the superordinate level (~0.4), i.e., partonomies are shallow and exemplar-specific compared to taxonomies. The induction literature (Gentner 1983 structure-mapping; Osherson, Smith, Wilkie, Lopez & Shafir 1990 similarity-coverage model; Sloman 1993 feature-based induction — his "inclusion fallacy" shows similarity routinely beats class-inclusion) converges on: novel-item property generalization is driven by similarity/analogical transfer from the nearest known exemplar, not by transitive class inheritance. Hampton (1987) shows even successful inheritance is exception-gated, not blind.

**Verdict.** FIDELITY GAP. Our attempted mechanism (is-a-mediated property-inheritance closure: car is-a vehicle, vehicles have wheels → car has wheels) is not the brain's mechanism for either relation, but the mismatch is more forgiving for is-a (homogeneous relation, high within-category similarity) and punishing for meronymy (six-subtype heterogeneous relation, low cross-exemplar similarity). The brain-faithful replacement is **k-nearest-known-exemplar prototype transfer**: for a candidate whole, retrieve the most distributionally-similar already-known wholes and score candidate parts by similarity-weighted vote over their known part-lists, instead of by is-a closure. Expect a real gain over the measured +0.012 (CI incl. 0) closure result, but calibrate below is-a's ceiling — this is a partially-pinned residual, not a full close.

**PINNED-vs-FIDELITY-GAP: FIDELITY GAP — wrong generalization mechanism; replace transitive is-a closure with similarity-weighted nearest-known-exemplar (analogical) part-list transfer.**

### Wall B — the basic-level effect in the admission gate

**Brain mechanism.** Rosch, Mervis, Gray, Johnson & Boyes-Braem (1976, *Cognitive Psychology*) and Rosch & Mervis (1975) show the basic level maximizes "cue validity" (P(category | feature)) precisely because basic-level members share the most attributes/shape/motor-program with each other and the fewest with contrast categories; superordinates (furniture, vehicle) structurally fail this test — members share almost no concrete features. Markman & Wisniewski (and the broader Murphy & Medin 1985 "theories" line) show superordinate coherence instead comes from an abstract relational/functional/causal "theory" (e.g., "mechanism for movement"), not shared perceptual features. Developmentally, Mervis & Crisafi (1982) show children master basic-level categorization by ~2.5 years but superordinate categorization requires more explicit category-differentiation/language input. Neuropsychologically, Warrington (1975) and the PDP hub-and-spoke model (Rogers, Lambon Ralph, Garrard, Bozeat, McClelland, Hodges & Patterson 2004, *Psych Review*) show superordinate/general knowledge survives semantic-dementia degradation *longer* than subordinate/specific knowledge, because superordinate representations are coarse-coded — a statistical residue distilled redundantly across many exemplars — while fine features rest on few, fragile connections.

**Verdict.** The gate's AUC-0.94-on-basic-level / abstain-on-superordinate behavior is not a bug — it is exactly the Rosch differentiation effect reproduced by a distributional-coherence proxy. PINNED. But the brain does not leave superordinates unvalidated; it validates them by a *different* channel the gate never built: **aggregation/consensus across many already-admitted basic-level children** (does the candidate child's signature fall inside the distribution formed by the parent's other known children, not "does the parent's own vector resemble this one child"), reinforced by theory/functional coherence and explicit taxonomic/linguistic scaffolding (which the curated-KB provenance path already partially supplies).

**PINNED-vs-FIDELITY-GAP: PINNED effect (real basic-level asymmetry) + FIDELITY GAP in mechanism — build a many-child consensus/aggregation check for superordinate admission, not a pairwise cosine test.**

### Wall C — recency dominates, type licenses (coreference)

**Brain mechanism.** Lappin & Leass (1994, *Computational Linguistics*) confirm exactly the measured architecture: hard agreement/selectional filters eliminate candidates first, then a salience score (recency, grammatical role, mention frequency) ranks survivors — 86% accuracy from this two-stage design. Centering Theory (Grosz, Joshi & Weinstein 1995, extended by Brennan, Friedman & Pollard 1987) ranks forward-looking centers primarily by grammatical role (SUBJ > OBJ > ...) with recency as a secondary tie-break, not raw recency alone, but the shape (salience/prominence primary, semantics not an independent ranking axis) matches. However, Lewis & Vasishth's (2005) ACT-R cue-based retrieval model treats semantic/type features as retrieval cues combined *in parallel* with activation/recency (graded interference, not a categorical filter), and Kehler, Kertz, Rohde & Elman (2008, *J. Semantics*) show coherence relations and verb-semantics (implicit causality) can *override* recency/role-based salience outright — so "recency selects, type filters" is the dominant/default case, not an exceptionless law.

**Verdict.** PINNED as a first-order, well-supported account — it is exactly what the measured result shows (type-as-selector loses to recency 0.641 vs 0.688; type-as-filter-on-recency wins 0.700). The literature flags a known refinement path (coherence-relation/verb-semantics as an additional weighted cue) for future headroom on harder residual cases, not a correction to the current build.

**PINNED-vs-FIDELITY-GAP: PINNED (first-order architecture confirmed) — future refinement (graded coherence-relation cue) is a headroom item, not a current defect.**

### Wall D — the raw-string / sense-resolution guard

**Brain mechanism.** Swinney (1979) and Onifer & Swinney (1981), cross-modal priming, show *both* senses of an ambiguous word activate immediately regardless of biasing context (exhaustive/frequency-independent access), with the contextually-irrelevant sense's priming decaying to non-significance only after a delay (published estimates span roughly 200ms to ~1.5s post-offset depending on probe design). Duffy, Morris & Rayner (1988) show this cost is small/absent specifically when one sense strongly dominates and context agrees with it (reordered access) — exactly the low-polysemy, low-ambiguity-cost regime our test items sit in. Kintsch (1988, *Psych Review*) formalizes the resolution as two stages: "construction" (promiscuous, union-like overgeneration) then "integration" (constraint-satisfaction pruning) before the stable textbase/situation-model forms. Rodd, Gaskell & Marslen-Wilson (2002) show the cost of unresolved competition scales with polysemy count and especially with meaning-*un*relatedness (homonymy costs more than related polysemy).

**Verdict.** The measured near-zero consumer cost is exactly what this literature predicts for low-polysemy, dominance-skewed test items — PINNED, not a fortunate accident. But the brain never *persists* the union: by the time content supports cross-clause/cross-sentence inference, integration has already pruned it to one sense. Our store performs construction (stage 1) but not integration (stage 2) at the point a fact is committed to the frozen semantic-memory store — a genuine fidelity gap that the current low-polysemy consumers happen not to expose, but that should bite harder on high-polysemy or homonymy-heavy material or any consumer needing long-range specific-sense fidelity.

**PINNED-vs-FIDELITY-GAP: PINNED (current small-cost finding is textbook-expected) + deferred FIDELITY GAP — build a consolidation-time context-integration/pruning step before targeting a high-polysemy consumer, not now.**

## Substrate-product implications

In plain terms: two of the four rough edges we hit are not walls at all — they are the AI correctly behaving the way real human memory behaves (a "furniture" concept genuinely doesn't look like any one item of furniture; a person's memory of a rare word-meaning that never gets used again fades within about a second and doesn't cause problems, exactly as it does for people). The other two rough edges are places where the AI is currently trying to solve a problem with the wrong tool, and we now know which tool to swap in: for "does a car have wheels because vehicles have wheels," instead of following a strict family-tree rule, it should ask "what do the other cars I already know about have," which is closer to how a person actually reasons about parts of things. For "is this general category real," instead of checking one example against the category, it should check whether many different known examples all agree, the way a person builds an idea of "furniture" from having seen many chairs, tables, and lamps rather than from any single one of them. Cost: each of the two buildable fixes is a small, contained addition to code that already exists (a similarity lookup and a consensus check), not a new training run or a new data source — this fits the "the brain does not do long training runs, learning is online" discipline. Risk: my recommendation to defer Wall D's fix is itself a bet that stays right only as long as no near-term consumer needs long-range sense fidelity on high-polysemy text; if that assumption changes, the deferred fix becomes urgent, not optional.
