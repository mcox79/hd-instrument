## HEADLINE

1. **The brain does NOT have a "relational" representation for a zero-anchor concept -- it has only an intrinsic-content signature, held by a non-relational hippocampal index, and this is a structural fact, not an engineering gap.** Three independent traditions converge: fast-mapping research describes the first-exposure trace as explicitly "sketchy"/skeletal (Carey & Bartlett 1978; Carey 2010, "Beyond Fast Mapping"); hippocampal indexing theory (Teyler & DiScenna 1986; Teyler & Rudy 2007) states the hippocampus provides an address/pointer to distributed cortical content, not semantic/relational structure itself, built via pattern separation (Marr 1971; O'Reilly & McClelland 1994; Yassa & Stark 2011) that DELIBERATELY minimizes overlap/association with everything already stored; and Complementary Learning Systems theory (McClelland, McNaughton & O'Reilly 1995; Kumaran, Hassabis & McClelland 2016) explicitly frames this as an "instance" code, with relational/schematic structure being a product of SLOW, LATER, interleaved neocortical learning that has by definition not yet happened. Barsalou's Perceptual Symbol Systems (1999) and classical exemplar theory (Smith & Medin 1981) independently agree: the only content available is the perceptual/situational gestalt present at first exposure -- a "simulator"/relational apparatus is built only after multiple instances accumulate. **This directly validates the substrate's own finding**: `AdditiveKGMap`/SR-compose/grounding-bound-onto-neighbors are all built to compose FROM relational structure that a cold entity, by construction, does not have yet -- the brain does not solve this problem either; it uses a categorically different mechanism (content-only indexing) for exactly this regime.

2. **A single relational anchor is not "one more feature" -- it is the minimal sufficient condition for the brain's relational-inference machinery to engage at all, and this is a qualitative (not merely quantitative) threshold.** Structure-mapping theory (Gentner 1983) shows one relational correspondence licenses "structural completion" (projecting further unstated relational content from a base domain onto the target). Schema-based rapid consolidation (Tse et al. 2007 *Science*; Tse et al. 2011 *Science*) shows a single new paired-associate, IF it lands inside a rich pre-existing schema, consolidates to neocortex in as little as 48 hours (vs. the normal multi-week systems-consolidation timescale) via direct immediate-early-gene-driven cortical encoding -- the richness requirement is on the PRE-EXISTING network, not on the number of edges attached to the new item. Most decisively, the acquired-equivalence/transitive-inference literature (Shohamy & Wagner 2008, *Neuron*, "Integrating Memories in the Human Brain") shows a SINGLE shared/overlapping edge between two otherwise-unrelated associations (A-B, B-C) is sufficient to produce above-chance NOVEL inference (A-C, never directly observed) -- with unpaired/no-overlap controls sitting at chance. **This is the mechanistic explanation for why the substrate's own d1 bucket clears d2_3plus's relational floor at all while cold sits at zero: one anchor is qualitatively, not just incrementally, different from zero.**

3. **The honest Q4 verdict (this is the load-bearing finding for the substrate): "zero-anchor + rich intrinsic content" is NEVER shown, in either the cognitive-science or the ML literature, to be sufficient for genuine relation-level inference by itself.** Every apparent counter-example collapses into the same underlying mechanism: content/feature similarity is used to perform a FAST, IMPLICIT one-shot anchor assignment (a soft nearest-known-neighbor retrieval), and it is THAT manufactured pseudo-anchor -- not the raw content -- that then licenses relational inference. In cognitive science: essentialism and label-dominance findings (Medin & Ortony 1989; Gelman & Markman 1986) show that once a category label/anchor is available it OVERRIDES raw perceptual similarity, and perceptual similarity is used only as a fallback to find that anchor, not to bypass needing one. In ML: DKRL (Xie et al. 2016) and BLP (Daza, Cochez & Groth 2021) handle zero-training-edge ("cold") entities not by avoiding an anchor but by manufacturing one -- embedding the entity's text/attributes into the SAME manifold as edge-rich entities and literally borrowing the nearest neighbor's structure. Jambor, Teru, Pineau & Hamilton (2021, arXiv:2102.03419) show the gap between a genuine zero-support baseline and a real 1-shot-anchored model is real but MODEST (NELL-One Hits@10: 34.2% zero-support vs. 46.4% 1-shot; Wiki-One: 36.1% vs. 44.8%) -- even a real anchor mostly buys coarse positional/popularity signal, not deep relational semantics, so expectations for lift magnitude from any anchor (real or manufactured) should be calibrated modest, not spectacular.

4. **Given this substrate's specific cold-tail structure (59% degree-1, majority name-transparent single-synonym/hypernym children of common parents), cold IS winnable for relation-inference -- but ONLY for the name-transparent, taxonomically-typed subset, and ONLY via manufacturing a pseudo-anchor from intrinsic content (the entity's own name string / WordNet gloss), not via composing from neighbors (which by definition don't exist) and not via passive content-similarity retrieval alone (which never converts into relational inference without the anchor-manufacturing step).** Name-opaque cold entities with no reconstructable anchor are the true structural floor: no evidence in either literature supports relation-level inference for that population from intrinsic content alone; they are placeable only by content-similarity RETRIEVAL (nearest-neighbor-by-gloss), not by relational inference, and this is expected to remain a genuine, non-fixable floor (a capacity to plan around, not a bug to chase).

## Cheap decisive test (pre-registered, remote-CPU-runnable, zero training, reuses on-disk data + Test3 harness)

**"Borrowed-parent content-placement" test.** Population: the SAME cold-bucket held-out entities used by `exp_anchor_compose_bottleneck_pinpoint_cskg_v2`'s Test3 (single anchor edge removed to create the cold condition), plus a larger cold sample if available at full scale.

1. **Name-transparency classifier (deterministic, zero training, cheap string op).** For each cold entity `c` with underscore-tokenized name `s_c`: check whether any token or token-suffix of `s_c` is ITSELF the name of another node already present in the training graph (candidate parent `p`). `cathode_ray_oscilloscope` -> tokens include `oscilloscope`, which is a known node -> name-transparent, candidate parent = `oscilloscope`. If no such match exists (`pseud`, `averment`), fall through to step 2b.
2. **Placement rule (this IS the cold mechanism being tested -- content-only, not neighbor-composition):**
   (a) **Name-transparent:** PLACEMENT(c) = borrow candidate parent `p`'s own training-graph relation edges wholesale as `c`'s predicted relation set (zero-training; this literally re-derives the held-out `SYNONYM`/`IS_A`/`HYPERNYM` anchor from content, then inherits via category-based induction -- the mechanism Osherson et al. 1990 and the essentialism literature describe, and the mechanism DKRL/BLP implement computationally).
   (b) **Name-opaque:** use the already-on-disk WordNet gloss field (`description` on `WN_*` atoms, e.g. `WN_definition.n.01` -> "a concise explanation of the meaning of a word...") -- token-overlap or cheap embedding similarity between `c`'s gloss and candidate parents' glosses; if no match clears a pre-registered threshold, PLACEMENT = abstain (scores at the current below-random cold baseline, honestly).
3. **Score** using the SAME reach@1/2/3 BFS metric Test3 already computes (`reachability_by_bucket`), for: (i) borrowed-parent placement, (ii) the current `ANCHOR_COMPOSE`/`ADDITIVE_TRANSE` zero-anchor scores (already on disk, ~0 reach), (iii) `RANDOM_CODES` baseline (0.013 MRR at selftest scale), (iv) a **scrambled-name control** -- randomly permute name strings across the cold-entity set BEFORE running step 1's substring match, which must destroy the string-content signal while leaving the graph itself untouched (must-fail control: if this control does NOT collapse the effect, the apparent lift is a confound -- e.g., the heuristic is secretly favoring high-degree/popular candidate parents regardless of actual name match -- not genuine content-driven placement).
4. **Stratify results** by (a) name-transparent vs. name-opaque and (b) relation-type predictability: taxonomic (`IS_A`/`HYPERNYM`/`SYNONYM`, directly inheritable from the borrowed parent) vs. arbitrary (other edge types on the same held-out entity, which the borrowed-parent profile may or may not also predict) -- this directly tests whether the mechanism generalizes beyond the exact edge type it reconstructs.

This reuses `reachability_by_bucket` and `build_heldout_entity_split_ac` (already-exercised entrypoints per the selftest's own `exercised_entrypoints` list) plus a pure string-match/gloss-lookup pass over already-downloaded `relations.jsonl`/`atoms.jsonl` -- no new data acquisition, no GPU, no training.

## Falsifiable predictions (HARD-PASS / HARD-FAIL)

**Prediction 1 -- content-based pseudo-anchor manufacturing lifts name-transparent cold entities meaningfully above the current floor.**
HARD-PASS: borrowed-parent placement on name-transparent cold entities reaches `reach@3` clearly above `RANDOM_CODES` (>0.013) and clearly above the current `ANCHOR_COMPOSE`/`ADDITIVE_TRANSE` zero-anchor score (~0.0), AND the scrambled-name control collapses back to at-or-below `RANDOM_CODES` level (confirms the lift is genuinely content-driven, not a confound).
HARD-FAIL: name-transparent borrowed-parent reach is statistically indistinguishable from `RANDOM_CODES`/current cold baseline (~0.0) -- would mean even a LITERAL content-based re-derivation of the missing anchor does not help on this substrate's specific graph, a materially stronger negative than currently known, and would falsify the whole "content-similarity manufactures a usable pseudo-anchor" mechanism for this graph/relation-type mix -- OR the scrambled-name control does NOT collapse (stays comparably high), meaning the apparent lift was never about name content at all (e.g., a degree/popularity confound in candidate-parent selection).

**Prediction 2 -- name-opaque cold entities do NOT benefit from string-based placement (expected, must-fail control, not a hoped-for result).**
HARD-PASS (of the honest-floor claim): name-opaque cold entities without a gloss-match above threshold stay at or near the current ~0.0 cold baseline under this mechanism -- confirms the mechanism is genuinely content-gated, not universally lucky, and correctly predicts where the floor actually sits.
HARD-FAIL: name-opaque entities ALSO show a large, unexplained lift under this mechanism -- would mean something other than name/gloss content is driving any observed Prediction-1 lift (a leak or confound to hunt down before trusting the result).

**Prediction 3 -- the mechanism generalizes beyond the exact reconstructed edge type only partially.**
HARD-PASS (informative either way): borrowed-parent profiles predict OTHER (non-taxonomic) edges on the same name-transparent cold entities at a rate above random but below the taxonomic-edge recovery rate -- consistent with category-based induction's own graded-generalization signature (properties inherited from a category are probabilistic, not certain).
HARD-FAIL: borrowed-parent profiles predict non-taxonomic edges at chance -- would mean the mechanism only ever reconstructs the ONE edge type it was built to reconstruct (SYNONYM/IS_A/HYPERNYM) and provides zero relational lift beyond that -- still a real, useful, but much narrower capability than hoped, and should be reported as such rather than generalized.

## Substrate-product implications

1. **Do not expect any tuning of `AdditiveKGMap`, SR-compose, or grounding-bound-onto-neighbors to fix the cold bucket** -- per both the on-disk selftest verdict and the brain literature, this is a structural mismatch (composition mechanism applied to a zero-input regime), not an undertrained or under-tuned one. Effort spent tuning composition hyperparameters against the cold bucket specifically is effort spent against a wall the brain itself does not climb this way either.
2. **Build a SEPARATE, content-first placement path for the cold bucket** (the cheap decisive test above is the minimal version): string/gloss-based candidate-parent retrieval -> borrow the parent's relational profile as a provisional placement. This is a small, cheap, mechanistically well-motivated addition (backed by both DKRL/BLP in ML and category-based induction/essentialism in cognitive science), NOT a redesign of the existing composition machinery -- it is a coexisting mode, exactly analogous to how `AdditiveKGMap` already coexists with the multiplicative `KGStore` without disturbing it.
3. **Expect this to close only the name-transparent, taxonomically-typed majority of the cold bucket (plausibly a large majority, given 59% degree-1 and the concrete examples surveyed are overwhelmingly synonym/hypernym children)** -- not all of it. Name-opaque cold entities (no reconstructable anchor from content) are a genuine, literature-confirmed floor: they are retrievable by gloss-similarity but not relationally inferable, and should be reported and planned around as such rather than chased with more mechanism.
4. **Do not conflate "content-similarity retrieval" with "relational inference" when reporting future cold-bucket results** -- per the Q4 verdict, passing a content-similarity/nearest-neighbor check is NOT evidence of relational-inference capability; only the borrowed-parent's relation-edges actually landing on the held-out BFS targets counts as relational-inference evidence. This distinction is the single most likely place a future overclaim could occur and should be gated explicitly in any cell built from this drill.
5. **The one-anchor (d1) bucket's own `REPRESENTATION_INFERENCE_WALL` verdict (per the selftest) is a SEPARATE, already-flagged problem** from the cold bucket -- per Headline point 2, one real anchor should be qualitatively sufficient for above-chance relational inference (Shohamy & Wagner's A-C transitive-inference paradigm), so d1 sitting at a "wall" rather than a clean pass is itself informative: it suggests the substrate's composition mechanism is not yet extracting the full inferential leverage a single anchor buys in the brain, a DIFFERENT (and likely higher-priority, since it's the mechanism family that's supposed to already work) gap than the cold bucket's complete absence of applicable mechanism.
