# Research note: Working memory integration into reading/conversation pipeline + upper-limit characterization

**Date:** 2026-07-16
**Trigger:** Director task -- design how to integrate working memory (WM) into the substrate's reading + conversation pipeline; characterize the upper limit and how to exceed it. Mid-task course correction from Director added a load-bearing design axis: DECOUPLED DIMENSIONALITY PER SUBSYSTEM (store vs. working buffer need not share one global N).
**Method:** 5 parallel Sonnet lit-scan sub-agents (public cognitive-science/neuroscience literature only, generic terms, no substrate specifics off-platform) covering: (1) Baddeley-Hitch model + episodic buffer, (2) Cowan embedded-processes + chunking/expertise, (3) sentence-parsing memory limits (center-embedding, DLT, cue-based retrieval), (4) discourse/situation-model comprehension, (5) regional dimensionality/sparsity specialization (hippocampus/cortex/PFC + EC interface). ## HEADLINE

The brain does not have ONE working-memory number -- it has a **small (~4-chunk) attentional focus nested inside a much larger activated-LTM pool** (Cowan), fed by **hierarchical chunking** that lets each of those ~4 slots carry arbitrarily compressed content (Miller, Gobet, Ericsson-Kintsch), bounded in recursive depth at **~2-3 levels** by interference, not a hard stack limit (Miller & Chomsky; Christiansen & Chater), and its memory subsystems run at **structurally different effective dimensionality/sparsity** (sparse hippocampus vs. dense cortex vs. tiny PFC buffer) connected by dedicated anatomical translation circuits (entorhinal cortex), not one uniform code. The substrate already has two of these numbers **measured on disk**: the flat-bundle crosstalk cliff (n_slots collapses between N/16 and N/32, confirmed directly from `data/exp_nativelang_svo_vsa_probe_v1/stress_metrics.json`) and the resonator factorization sweet spot (F=3-4 simultaneous factors, previously verified in `notes/research_brain_capacity_efficient_language_mechanisms_vsa_analogs_2026-07-16.md`). The KEY FINDING of this note is that **these two substrate numbers map to two DIFFERENT brain quantities, not one**: the N/16-32 bundle ceiling is the analog of raw passive-store capacity (how much can be superposed before destructive interference), while F=3-4 is the analog of Cowan's ~4-chunk focus of attention (how much can be jointly, faithfully bound-and-decoded in one reasoning step). Conflating them (as the task's framing initially suggested) would misattribute the substrate's real functional WM ceiling. Given hierarchical chunking (nest a clause into one outer-level filler) plus decoupled dimensionality per subsystem (a chunk encoded at its own N, translated across a fixed projection into the outer buffer's N -- directly mirroring the brain's EC-mediated hippocampus<->cortex translation) plus exact external paging (a substrate-native affordance the brain lacks), the honest ceiling for reading sophisticated text and holding long conversations is **not a single number** -- it decomposes into four separately-bounded quantities (active-buffer capacity, chunk-decode fidelity, nesting depth, paging bandwidth), three of which the substrate can plausibly exceed the brain on, and one (chunk-decode fidelity, F=3-4) which is a hard, brain-matched wall that should NOT be fought, only worked around via architecture (more/smaller chunks) rather than "just add more iterations."

## (a) The brain's working-memory architecture

### A1. Baddeley-Hitch multi-component model + episodic buffer (7 citations)

Four components (Baddeley & Hitch 1974; Baddeley 2000 *Trends Cogn Sci* 4:417-423): **phonological loop** (passive acoustic store ~1.5-2s + subvocal rehearsal; evidenced by the word-length effect and its abolition under articulatory suppression -- a real, dissociable mechanism, not just noise), **visuospatial sketchpad** (visual/spatial store; evidenced by domain-selective dual-task interference -- two verbal tasks or two spatial tasks interfere severely, verbal+spatial barely interferes), **central executive** (attentional-control supervisor, fractionated by Miyake et al. 2000 into correlated-but-separable Inhibition/Shifting/Updating factors -- "unity and diversity," not a unitary capacity-limited store; Baddeley 1996), and the **episodic buffer** (added 2000 specifically to explain cross-domain binding, and immediate memory for prose vastly exceeding raw loop capacity by drawing on long-term/semantic knowledge). The episodic buffer's capacity is **~4 chunks**, where a chunk is a multimodal package bound by strong within-chunk / weak between-chunk associative links, retrieved via conscious access, and interfacing bidirectionally with LTM (schemas support buffer maintenance; buffer contents consolidate to LTM). Overload manifests in the adjacent visual-WM precision literature as **binding/misattribution (swap) errors**, not pure item loss -- features from the wrong encoded item get reported as if from the probed one (illusory conjunctions), accounting for over a third of errors in some load conditions. Flag: the strong "buffer = literally the activated state of LTM" framing is a more recent, contested extension of Baddeley's 2000 position, not itself Baddeley's original claim.

### A2. Cowan's embedded-processes model + chunking (11 citations)

Cowan (2001, *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*, "The magical number 4 in short-term memory") reframes WM as **the currently-activated subset of long-term memory**, with a further-nested **focus of attention holding ~4 chunks** when strategic recoding/rehearsal is blocked (concurrent articulatory suppression, fast running-span, change-detection). This is explicitly distinguished from Miller's 7±2 (1956), argued to reflect capacity AFTER recoding inflated the effective count -- i.e., 7±2 measures chunked material, ~4 measures the raw attentional-focus limit underneath the chunking. This is a fixed discrete-slot (chunk-count) view, contrasted with more recent visual-WM precision models (Zhang & Luck 2008 discrete-but-flexibly-allocated slots; Bays & Husain 2008 continuous shared resource with no strict item ceiling; Ma, Husain & Bays 2014 *Nat Neurosci* review synthesizing item-count/precision tradeoffs) -- an open, unresolved debate for continuous perceptual features, less contested for symbolic/verbal chunk capacity.

**Chunking mechanism** (Miller 1956): capacity is limited in chunk-COUNT, not raw bits, so recoding low-information items into higher-order units (dits/dahs -> letters -> words) raises effective throughput within the same fixed slot budget. A chunk is operationally a unit bound by pre-existing LTM associations/schemas, not raw perceptual content. **Expertise chunking** (Chase & Simon 1973: chess masters reproduce real positions far better than novices, but the advantage vanishes for randomized non-game positions -- proving pattern/schema recognition, not superior raw STM) elaborated into **template theory** (Gobet & Simon 1996, 1998): recurring chunks evolve into templates -- higher-order chunks with fixed core slots plus variable slots rapidly filled via a discrimination network, letting an expert's single WM "slot" point to a rich pre-structured object instead of one raw item. **Ericsson & Kintsch (1995, *Psychol Rev*, "Long-term working memory")** propose skilled performance relies on practiced retrieval STRUCTURES that keep LTM-stored content rapidly cue-accessible from ordinary short-term WM -- a second bottleneck (retrieval-cue reliability) layered on top of the ~4-chunk focus limit; if cues are lost/interfered-with, expert performance collapses toward novice-like limits.

**Bottleneck consensus**: attentional focus / chunk-count (~4) is primary; chunking and templates do not raise this ceiling, they raise how much content rides on each unit under it; retrieval-cue capacity/reliability is Ericsson-Kintsch's secondary bottleneck for expert-scale content.

### A3. How the brain holds multi-clause sentences (13 citations)

**Center-embedding depth limit**: Miller & Chomsky (1963) documented that doubly center-embedded relative clauses ("The rat the cat the dog chased bit died") become nearly unparseable at 2-3 levels, though the grammar permits unlimited recursion and single embedding is trivial. The classic account (Yngve) was a bounded push-down stack (~3 items); Gibson's Dependency Locality Theory (DLT; Gibson 1998, 2000) reframes this quantitatively as **storage cost** (number of open/predicted syntactic dependencies held simultaneously) plus **integration cost** (cost of connecting a word to an earlier head, growing with intervening discourse referents) -- doubly-embedded sentences maximize both terms at once, giving a principled account of why depth 2-3 is the empirical wall rather than an arbitrary limit. A directly-cited alternative quantification is the **"magical number two/three"** interference account (Gibson & Thomas-style, *J. Psycholinguistic Research*): syntactic WM can index no more than ~2 constituents under the same relation before similarity-based interference dominates.

**Garden-path sentences** ("The horse raced past the barn fell") demonstrate an incremental, largely-serial parser that commits early and must reanalyze -- direct evidence for a limited-capacity buffer holding partial syntactic structure (the "parser's stack"), with higher-WM-span readers reanalyzing faster/cheaper.

**Currently favored account**: Lewis & Vasishth (2005, ACT-R) model comprehension as **content-addressable cue-based retrieval** from general associative memory, not traversal of a dedicated syntactic stack -- subject to activation decay and similarity-based interference (partially-matching cues slow/misdirect retrieval, explaining agreement-attraction errors like "The key to the cabinets *are* on the table" that a pure stack-depth account does not predict). Jäger, Engelmann & Vasishth's 2017 meta-analysis treats interference/cue-based retrieval as currently ahead of a rigid depth-counter, though DLT-style storage/integration cost and cue-based interference are increasingly treated as complementary rather than rival. Just & Carpenter (1992, "A Capacity Theory of Comprehension") ties the depth limit to domain-general WM span (reading-span individual differences: high-span readers tolerate deeper embedding); their claim of a single undifferentiated storage+computation pool is contested (Caplan & Waters 1999 -- flag as unsettled).

**Christiansen & Chater's "Now-or-Never bottleneck"** (2016, *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* -- already surfaced in the prior capacity-fix-menu note) argues the brain does NOT implement a true symbolic stack at all: it survives apparent recursion via aggressive **chunk-and-pass compression** -- each constituent is compressed into a chunk and discarded from raw WM as soon as possible, so recursion is *approximated* and the approximation's error compounds with depth, consistent with the sharp depth-2-3 ceiling. This is the single most important brain-mechanism finding for the substrate mapping below: **the brain's recursion mechanism is itself finite and leaky, not a template for unbounded recursion.**

### A4. How the brain holds discourse/conversation context (10 citations)

Kintsch's **Construction-Integration (CI) model** (Kintsch 1988; van Dijk & Kintsch 1983) posits three representational levels: **surface form** (literal wording, decays fastest), **textbase** (propositions + local bridging inferences), and **situation model** (the state of the world described, integrated with prior knowledge -- persists longest). Zwaan & Radvansky's **event-indexing model** (Zwaan, Langston & Graesser 1995; Zwaan & Radvansky 1998) specifies the situation model tracks **protagonist, time, space, causality, intentionality** -- discontinuity on any dimension produces measurable reading-time cost, i.e., an active, monitored updating process, not a passive accumulation.

**Fuzzy-Trace Theory** (Brainerd & Reyna): verbatim and gist traces are encoded in PARALLEL, independently; verbatim decays fast, gist persists -- comprehenders retain wording only transiently and rely on durable gist for anything beyond a sentence or two.

**Centering Theory** (Grosz, Joshi & Weinstein 1986/1995) models local coherence via a backward-looking center (Cb, the current focus, typically pronominalized) and a ranked list of forward-looking centers (Cf) -- keeping only a small, salience-ranked set of entities "in the spotlight" at once; less-salient referents fall out of the Cf list and must be explicitly re-introduced (full NP, not pronoun) rather than staying actively held. This is the direct discourse-level analog of a tiny WM buffer.

**Event Segmentation Theory** (Zacks & Tversky 2001; Zacks, Speer, Swallow, Braver & Reynolds 2007): ongoing narrative is chunked into discrete event models that generate predictions; a spike in prediction error (recently linked to Bayesian surprise) triggers an event boundary and the current event model is updated/replaced rather than all prior models being held simultaneously -- the discourse-level analog of chunking.

**Mechanism for exceeding the flat ~4-chunk limit across a whole conversation** -- literature converges on a COMBINATION, not one trick: (a) hierarchical compression (CI's surface->textbase->situation-model discard-at-each-level, with incremental vs. global updating), (b) long-term working memory (Ericsson & Kintsch retrieval structures linking current focus to a large subset of text held in LTM), (c) early verbatim discard + gist retention (Fuzzy-Trace + CI surface decay). Flag: one study found situation-model updating showed NO relation to WM span, suggesting updating may be sequential/incremental rather than WM-taxing -- a genuine open contest, not settled.

### B2. The corrected two-number mapping (the key finding)

The task framing proposed "the N/16 flat bundle = the analog of the ~4-chunk focus." **This is not the sharpest mapping and should be corrected before it gets load-bearing.** Cowan's own model already distinguishes two nested quantities: a large **activated-LTM pool** and a small **focus of attention** nested inside it. The substrate has a directly analogous two-number structure already measured:

- **N/16-N/32 bundle crosstalk ceiling** (32-64 slots at N=1024) is the analog of the **large, raw, passive-store pool** -- how much CAN be superposed/held active before destructive interference, independent of whether it is being actively reasoned over right now. This is closer to Baddeley's combined loop+sketchpad raw capacity or Cowan's "activated LTM," NOT the ~4-chunk focus.
- **Resonator factorization sweet spot F=3-4** (verified in the prior capacity-fix-menu note: capacity vs. number-of-jointly-decoded-factors peaks at F=3-4 and falls off sharply beyond it) is the analog of **Cowan's ~4-chunk focus of attention / Baddeley's episodic-buffer capacity** -- how much can be JOINTLY, FAITHFULLY bound-and-decoded in a single reasoning/query step. The numeric convergence (F=3-4 vs. Cowan's ~4) is a genuine, non-forced match between an independently-derived VSA capacity result and an independently-derived psychology result -- worth treating as a real structural analogy, not coincidence, though it should be held to the novel-synthesis P cap below since no paper directly derives one from the other.

This distinction matters operationally: **raising N/16-32 (bigger N, or sparser coding) does not raise F=3-4** -- they are different bottlenecks in different parts of the pipeline (the bundle is the store; the resonator/unbind-cleanup step is the reasoning operation performed on it), exactly mirroring how Cowan's activated-LTM pool and focus-of-attention are not the same number and do not move together.

### B3. Chunking / hierarchical binding = the depth lever

Miller/Gobet chunking maps directly onto **nesting a clause as one filler**: instead of flat-binding every word into one outer bundle (consuming one of the N/16-32 raw slots per word, and immediately hitting the crosstalk cliff on anything past a short sentence), encode a clause's own SVO meaning as its own bundle `M_clause`, then bind `M_clause` into the SENTENCE-level bundle as a single filler under a role like `SUBORD_CLAUSE`. This multiplies effective content per outer slot by whatever the clause itself can hold (up to ITS OWN crosstalk cliff), exactly matching Miller's recoding insight (chunk = compressed unit riding under a fixed slot budget) and Gobet's template theory (a slot pointing to a rich pre-structured sub-object). Decoding requires unbind + cleanup at the OUTER level to recover `M_clause`, then a SECOND unbind + cleanup pass at the inner level to recover the clause's own SVO triple -- each level is a resonator-style decode pass bounded by the SAME F=3-4 sweet spot and by cleanup noise that compounds with depth. This is precisely the mechanism Christiansen & Chater describe (chunk-and-pass, not a true stack) and precisely why the brain's own depth ceiling sits at 2-3: **the substrate should target the SAME 2-3 nesting-depth ceiling, not fight to exceed it via more cleanup iterations** (the prior capacity-fix-menu note already established that near a resonator ceiling, more iterations do not rescue basin-narrowing failure -- the fix is architectural: fewer/wider chunks, not more iterations).

### B4. The substrate-native affordance the brain lacks: exact external paging (Frontier-2)

The brain's WM is small BECAUSE synapses are lossy, capacity-bound, and interference-prone even in "long-term" cortical storage (dense overlapping cortical codes are explicitly, per CLS theory, good for generalization but bad for one-shot non-interfering storage -- this is WHY the hippocampal fast-route + slow-consolidation architecture exists at all: cortex's own "disk" is not exact). The substrate's disk is different in kind, not degree: an addressable, exact-record external store (arbitrary schema, unbounded by any FHRR/VSA dimensionality-capacity law) can hold WM items OUT of the lossy active bundle entirely, paged IN only when actively reasoned over -- a genuine RAM (VSA working buffer at N_wm, lossy, interference-bound, small) + disk (exact records, unbounded, zero interference by construction) split. Ericsson & Kintsch's "long-term working memory" is the brain's best attempt at this same idea, but it REQUIRES years of practiced schema/retrieval-structure building to make cue-based access reliable (that reliability is what "expertise" purchases). The substrate's equivalent retrieval structure -- a hash-addressable exact store -- is reliable BY CONSTRUCTION, with no practice requirement. This is the one place the design should explicitly NOT replicate the brain (the brain's small-WM-because-lossy-store constraint is a limitation to beat, not a target to match), while still replicating the STRUCTURAL insight that a working buffer and a long-term store are functionally separate systems connected by a translation interface.

### B5. Decoupled dimensionality per subsystem (course-correction addition)

**(1) Brain precedent -- this is CLS regional specialization, not an ad hoc design choice.** The lit-scan on regional dimensionality confirms a well-established, directly-cited principle, not three isolated facts:
- **Hippocampal dentate gyrus**: entorhinal cortex (EC) layer II projects to DG granule cells with roughly a **5-11x expansion in cell count** (estimates range by whether raw cell counts or perforant-path connectivity ratios are used), and DG activity is extremely sparse (**~2-4% of granule cells active** per Arc immediate-early-gene studies, vs. ~40% in CA1 for the same exposure). This expansion+sparsification is the physical substrate of Marr's (1971) pattern-separation proposal (modern reviews: Yassa & Stark 2011).
- **Entorhinal cortex as a bidirectional TRANSLATION interface, not just a relay**: EC-II -> DG/CA3 (perforant path) is the expand+sparsify direction; CA1/subiculum -> EC-V/VI -> neocortex is the compress+densify direction (van Strien, Cappaert & Witter 2009, *Nat Rev Neurosci*, "The anatomy of memory"). A directly-on-point 2026 *Nature* finding on "sparse-to-dense coding transformation between hippocampal areas CA3 and CA1" shows this transformation happening progressively WITHIN the hippocampal output pathway itself (CA3 near-single-place-field ultrasparse -> CA1 denser multi-place-field), before the signal even reaches EC/cortex -- i.e. dimensionality/sparsity translation is not a single interface but a **graded pipeline**.
- **Neocortex**: dense, distributed, overlapping population codes (McClelland, McNaughton & O'Reilly 1995 CLS theory) -- explicitly good for generalization/statistical structure extraction, explicitly bad for one-shot non-interfering storage, which is the CLS rationale for needing hippocampus as a SEPARATE fast route in the first place.
- **Prefrontal cortex working buffer -- a third, separately capacity-limited regime**: small populations of sustained-firing PFC neurons (Goldman-Rakic delayed-response studies) hold actively-maintained content, tightly capacity-limited (consistent with the behavioral ~4-chunk number), gated by a DEDICATED subcortical interface -- the basal-ganglia gating model (PBWM: O'Reilly & Frank 2006) proposes dopaminergic RPE-trained Go/NoGo gating controls what enters/exits the small PFC buffer from the much larger associative-cortex/LTM pool. This third-regime linkage to the CLS framing is less canonically unified in the primary literature than points 1-3 (flag as well-supported synthesis, not a single co-cited source) but the STRUCTURAL claim -- three regimes, three different effective dimensionalities/sparsities, dedicated translating/gating interfaces between them -- is squarely brain-backed.

**(2) Interface cost, quantified as the price of decoupling.** The substrate already has a directly-costed precedent for this exact operation: the design note for a hippocampal-analog sparse index (`notes/design_stage2_concept_encoder_spoke3_sparse_hippocampal_pattern_separation_one_shot_2026-07-02.md`) specifies a DG-analog expansion projection `N -> 2N` costing `O(N x 2N)` per input as "the largest per-token op" in that pipeline. Generalizing: a fixed linear projection between two spaces of dimension `N_a` and `N_b` costs `O(N_a x N_b)` per paging event (or cheaper, `O(max(N_a,N_b))`, with a structured/hashed projection instead of a dense random matrix). This is a ONE-TIME cost per item paged in or out, not a standing cost on the active buffer -- i.e. decoupling N per subsystem is cheap PER SWAP and does not itself consume active-bundle crosstalk budget. The brain's homolog (EC's fixed, largely non-learned perforant-path projection, per the Spoke-3 design note's own read of the anatomy) supports treating this as a FIXED, not learned, projection as the default design -- learned projections are a v2 lever, not a v1 requirement.

**(3) The Frontier-2 extreme.** Nothing requires the long-term store to be a fixed-N VSA vector at all. The store can be **exact addressable records** (arbitrary form -- token sequences, structured triples, whatever the content is), with NO FHRR/VSA capacity law applying to it, projected into the working VSA buffer at a chosen `N_wm` ONLY at the moment an item is actively bound/reasoned over. This is the RAM+disk split stated precisely: disk = exact records (unbounded, zero interference by construction), registers = VSA at `N_wm` (lossy, bounded, but that's fine because it's a transient, respawnable working state, not the system of record). This is where the substrate can structurally exceed the brain: the brain's cortical "disk" is ITSELF a lossy, dense, capacity-bound analog store (subject to the very interference CLS exists to manage) -- ours need not be.

**(4) The reframe -- separate the CONSOLIDATION ROUTE from the DIMENSIONALITY SPECIALIZATION.** A prior finding this session (`notes/research_cls_stc_currency_wrong_regime_2026-07-16.md`) found the CLS fast/slow ROUTE (replay-then-consolidate, tested via a single-shot decision-accuracy arena) tied rather than beat a monolithic baseline -- diagnosed as WRONG-CURRENCY, not a same-limitation refutation, because CLS/STC's entire biological justification is retention-under-continual-interference measured at a LATER test, never single-shot accuracy. That finding is about whether REPLAY-BASED CONSOLIDATION TIMING earns its keep on an accuracy metric. It says nothing about, and does not refute, the SEPARATE, purely architectural claim being made here: that using **different effective dimensionality/sparsity for different subsystems, connected by a fixed translation projection**, is a valid design independent of whether replay-consolidation "wins." The dimensionality-per-subsystem claim is tested by different metrics entirely (crosstalk/interference rate at a given N and sparsity, paging round-trip fidelity) -- it is a standing architecture decision, not an empirical route needing the single-shot-accuracy test that already produced a wrong-currency result for the ROUTE. Treat these as two independently falsifiable claims: (i) decoupled-N-per-subsystem architecture [this note, untested], (ii) replay-consolidation timing [prior note, wrong-currency, awaiting the corrected continual-ingestion re-test].

**(5) Effective WM upper limit -- per subsystem, not one number.**

| Subsystem | What bounds it | Substrate-measured or brain-predicted value |
|---|---|---|
| Active working buffer (`N_wm`) | Superposition crosstalk cliff | N_wm/16 to N_wm/32 RAW slots (measured; a THRESHOLD not a gradual curve) |
| Joint reasoning / chunk-decode step | Resonator factorization sweet spot | F=3-4 simultaneously-decoded factors (measured in prior note; convergent with Cowan's ~4) |
| Hierarchical nesting depth | Compounding cleanup noise per level (Christiansen & Chater chunk-and-pass) | ~2-3 levels (brain-matched target; do not chase deeper via more iterations) |
| Long-term store | None, if exact-record (Frontier-2); FHRR capacity law if VSA-native | Effectively unbounded (disk/DB-limited, not VSA-limited) if built as exact records |
| Store <-> buffer interface | Fixed-projection cost per paging event | O(N_wm x N_store) per swap (or O(max) with structured projection) -- a THROUGHPUT bound, not a capacity bound |
| Discourse-referent binding | Same resonator/focus bottleneck (Centering Theory's small Cb/Cf list) UNLESS given its own dedicated sub-bundle | F=3-4 "in the spotlight" referents by default; more only if referents get a dedicated, separately-dimensioned sub-buffer |

**Honest ceiling stated once**: for READING SOPHISTICATED TEXT, the wall is NOT store size (solved, if exact-record paging is built) -- it is (a) how many chunks fit in the active buffer without crosstalk (N_wm/16-32), (b) how many of those can be jointly, faithfully decoded per reasoning step (F=3-4, the real analog of the human ~4-chunk wall), and (c) how deep a chunk-of-chunks nesting can go before compounding decode error blows up (~2-3, matching center-embedding). For CONVERSATION LENGTH specifically, since the store can be exact and paging is cheap per-swap, conversation length is **not fundamentally capacity-bounded by WM at all** the way it is for the brain (which must compress everything to gist because its cortical store is itself lossy) -- the substrate's real conversation-length ceiling becomes a RETRIEVAL-PRECISION problem (does the right exact record get paged back in when a later reference/pronoun points to it), not a capacity problem. This is a genuinely different, and better, failure mode than the brain's -- but it is a NEW failure mode (referent-to-record matching) that must itself be built and tested, not assumed free.

## (c) Recommended first cell -- exp_dev-actionable (pointer-only, per USER-locked no-routing-files discipline: this section IS the hand-off, no separate `exp_dev_handoff_*.md` file is filed)

**Anchor candidate:** `exp_wm_hierarchical_chunk_crossN_projection_v1` (name is a suggestion, not binding on exp_dev).

**Why this cell, why now:** it is the cheapest cell that operationalizes BOTH load-bearing levers from this note in one shot -- (i) hierarchical chunking as the depth/capacity multiplier, and (ii) decoupled dimensionality across the chunk/sentence interface (the course-correction axis) -- reusing the EXACT mechanism and harness already proven in `experiments/exp_nativelang_svo_vsa_probe_v1.py` (role-filler bind/bundle/unbind/cleanup, arms-with-nulls pattern, held-out generalization split). No new primitives are required beyond what that file already implements plus one fixed projection matrix.

**Mechanism sketch (glass-box, no learned params beyond what the base cell already has):**
1. Encode each CLAUSE the same way the base cell encodes an SVO sentence today: `M_clause = sum bind(role_i, word_i)` at dimension `N_chunk`.
2. Project `M_clause` through a FIXED random linear map `P: N_chunk -> N_wm` (the EC-analog interface) to obtain a chunk-filler vector at the OUTER buffer's dimension.
3. Bind the projected chunk-filler into the OUTER sentence-level bundle under a clause-role (e.g. `MAIN_CLAUSE`, `SUBORD_CLAUSE`): `M_sentence = bind(ROLE_i, project(M_clause_i))` for each clause, superposed as today.
4. Decode: unbind + cleanup at the outer level recovers (an approximation of) `project(M_clause)`; apply the (known, fixed) pseudo-inverse/transpose of `P` to recover an estimate of `M_clause`; unbind + cleanup AGAIN at the inner level recovers the clause's own SVO triple.
5. Sweep: `N_chunk` vs. `N_wm` (equal and unequal, to test whether decoupling costs accuracy vs. matched-N), number of clauses per sentence (1, 2, 3, 4 -- to locate the nesting-depth wall), and whether it lands at ~2-3 per the brain-matched prediction.

**Arms:** `hierarchical_chunk_crossN` (mechanism above); `flat_no_chunk` (today's base-cell flat encoding, as the capacity-ceiling control -- should crater once total word count exceeds its own N_wm/16-32 budget while the chunked arm should not); `chunk_same_N_no_projection` (ablation: chunking without the cross-dimensionality projection, i.e. `N_chunk == N_wm` and `P` = identity -- isolates whether the PROJECTION step itself costs accuracy); `memorization_lookup` and null arms reused from the base cell (must fail on held-out combinations, same discipline as today).
