## HEADLINE

**The dominant win-lever is CROSSING A CAPACITY-THRESHOLD PHASE BOUNDARY, not smoothly tuning any single knob.** Three independent literatures converge on the exact same shape our own data shows: fixed-form associative/VSA memories (Frady/Kleyko/Sommer superposition theory, arXiv:1707.01429; classical/modern Hopfield capacity, arXiv:2503.00241, arXiv:2301.10352) have a **sharp phase transition** — below a critical load/noise threshold, retrieval is near-perfect; above it, retrieval **collapses catastrophically**, not gracefully. Repetition-code/majority-vote theory shows the identical shape: voting suppresses error exponentially **only if per-trial error rate is already below 50%**; above that threshold no amount of redundancy helps. Concatenated-code / quantum-threshold-theorem theory shows per-stage error correction turns exponential-in-depth error growth into polynomial/exponentially-suppressed growth **only below a per-stage error threshold** — above it, correction still helps a little (a constant offset) but the decay *slope* stays the same, which is EXACTLY what our own Stage-4 data shows: PLAIN_CLEANUP's decay_slope (-0.1289) is statistically indistinguishable from NO_CLEANUP's decay_slope (-0.1283) — cleanup added a flat boost at each hop but did **not** change the exponential collapse rate. That is the signature of operating **above the correction threshold**, not evidence that cleanup-as-a-strategy is wrong.

This reframes the 4 angles from "pick one" to "the win-lever is whichever combination of (1) architecture and (2) resource-scale actually buys the threshold crossing" — and because we are not energy-constrained, the answer is to combine an **exponential-capacity cleanup architecture** (dense/modern-Hopfield-style attractor readout, NOT classical linear-VSA-bundle cleanup, which the lit confirms has only ~linear/log-bounded capacity in N) with a **qualitatively different chaining primitive** (bind the whole 2-3-hop path as ONE object and decode once, rather than recomposing hop-by-hop) — because path-as-object has a fundamentally different, more favorable noise law (SNR ∝ D/path_length, a LINEAR budget you can buy down with dimension) versus iterative hop-by-hop composition (which the data shows collapses far faster than naive squaring: fid1=0.424 -> fid1^2=0.180 would be the naive-independent-hops prediction, but NO_CLEANUP actually lands at 0.025, a ~7x worse collapse than simple compounding predicts — meaning something beyond simple noise-multiplication is happening at the hop1->hop2 transition, consistent with crossing a capacity cliff, not a smooth SNR decay).

## Cheap decisive test

Before building the full Stage-5 cell, run ONE cheap diagnostic on the EXISTING Stage-4 data/config: sweep `code_dim` (currently 256) up by 4x and 8x with PLAIN_CLEANUP held fixed, on a SMALL matched subgraph (few hundred nodes, 1 seed, CPU-feasible), and check whether fid2 and the decay_slope move in a THRESHOLD-CROSSING way (a sharp jump to a much higher fid2 plus a visibly flatter slope) rather than a smooth linear creep. If a 4-8x `code_dim` bump produces a qualitative jump (not just +0.02-0.05), that confirms we are near a threshold and the resource-throw lever is the right one to scale hard on Stage-5. If it produces only a smooth, small linear creep even at 8x, that argues the correlation-structure ceiling (see Honest risk below) is already binding and Stage-5 should weight the path-as-object primitive change more heavily than raw N-scaling.

## Falsifiable predictions (HARD-PASS + HARD-FAIL)

**Prediction 1 (threshold-crossing, not smooth scaling):** Reach-2 fidelity as a function of `code_dim`/N shows a visible knee/cliff shape (small gains below some N*, then a jump), not a smooth curve, when cleanup architecture is held fixed and only N is scaled 1x/4x/8x.
- HARD-PASS: the 8x-N arm's fid2 is >=2x the 4x-N arm's fid2 gain-over-baseline (super-linear jump = threshold signature), AND decay_slope between hop2-4 flattens by >=40% relative to the 1x-N arm.
- HARD-FAIL: fid2 scales roughly linearly with N with no knee (smooth diminishing-returns curve, consistent with the Johnson-Lindenstrauss-style linear capacity bound arXiv:2301.10352 already being the operative regime, not a threshold cliff) — in that case, resource-throwing N further is provably NOT the dominant lever and the win must come from angle 4 (path-as-object) or angle 2 architecture (dense-Hopfield) instead.

**Prediction 2 (dense/modern-Hopfield exponential-capacity cleanup beats classical-bundle cleanup at matched N):** Swapping PLAIN_CLEANUP's cleanup readout for a dense/modern-Hopfield-style softmax/energy-descent attractor pass (same N, same compute budget class) materially outperforms PLAIN_CLEANUP, because classical VSA-bundle cleanup capacity is bounded ~linearly (with log factors) in N (arXiv:2301.10352 Thm 6/16) while dense Hopfield capacity is exponential in N (Krotov-Hopfield / Demircigil-family results cited in that same capacity-analysis literature).
- HARD-PASS: DENSE_HOPFIELD_CLEANUP arm's fid2 >= PLAIN_CLEANUP's fid2 + 0.15 absolute at MATCHED N (a decisive, not marginal, architecture-alone gain).
- HARD-FAIL: DENSE_HOPFIELD_CLEANUP indistinguishable from PLAIN_CLEANUP at matched N (paired, matched seeds) -- would mean the bottleneck is not cleanup-rule capacity but the correlation structure of the codes themselves (see Honest risk).

**Prediction 3 (path-as-object sidesteps compounding, doesn't just relocate it losslessly, but the tradeoff is favorable):** Encoding a candidate 2-hop or 3-hop path as ONE bound object (HRR/TPR-style multi-role binding, dimension budget D_path scaled ~linearly with path length k, e.g. D_path = k * D_single) and decoding it ONCE materially outperforms k sequential hop-by-hop decodes at the SAME total dimension budget (i.e. D_path total dims vs k * D_single dims spent across k sequential steps -- a fair total-resource comparison).
- HARD-PASS: PATH_AS_OBJECT reach-2 fidelity >= 0.50 at matched total-dimension budget (vs the iterative arms), confirming the SNR ∝ D/path_length law (linear-in-length noise budget) beats the observed super-exponential iterative collapse.
- HARD-FAIL: PATH_AS_OBJECT fidelity is no better than (or worse than) iterative hop-by-hop at matched total budget -- would mean the "relocate, don't escape" reading is correct for our correlated-code regime specifically (Smolensky-style rank-explosion or SNR-vs-*m* crosstalk from binding many roles into one vector dominates just as badly as sequential compounding does).

**Prediction 4 (redundant parallel voting is a cheap multiplier ONLY once single-hop per-trial error is already sub-50%):** Running the whole reach-2 pipeline K=5-10 times with independent random sub-codebook projections and majority-voting the winner gives an exponential-in-K error reduction, IF (and only if) the best single-configuration reach-2 arm (from Predictions 1-3) already clears >50% per-trial correctness on some operational notion of "correct top-1" (not raw cosine fidelity).
- HARD-PASS: adding K=5 parallel voted chains on top of the best Stage-5 arm improves fid2 by >=0.10 absolute over that arm alone.
- HARD-FAIL: voting gives <0.03 absolute gain (matches the "majority vote is provably powerless past the 50%-error threshold" result -- would mean the base arm still hasn't crossed that threshold and voting is being spent for nothing).

P_deflated (the COMBINED win-cell, Predictions 1+2+3 all landing, clears a REAL win bar of reach-2>=0.60 material fidelity): **0.38** (base intuition ~0.55-0.60 given three independent literatures converging on the identical threshold-crossing shape our own data already shows; deflated 0.20 per lit-scan calibration penalty for uncharted-regime risk -- no direct precedent tests this exact combination on real correlated ConceptNet-derived codes; further discounted below the 0.50 novel-synthesis cap because this is a 3-4-way STACKED architecture change, which raises variance and the chance any one component silently doesn't transfer to our regime). Individual-prediction P's (1-4 above, tested independently before the full stack) are each higher, ~0.45-0.55, since they are narrower and more directly grounded.

- Query-embedding methods (GQE/Query2Box/BetaE) are a real, established middle ground between full path-as-single-vector and step-by-step: they decode ONLY the final answer region, never materializing intermediate hop entities, while still applying one geometric operator per hop internally. This is a lower-risk fallback if the strict "one bound path-object" arm (Prediction 3) HARD-FAILs due to rank-explosion/binding-crosstalk -- worth keeping as a Stage-5b contingency arm rather than treating Prediction 3's failure as terminal.

## Honest risk (stated without deflating the ambition)

The lit-scans found a REAL, load-bearing caveat that resource-throwing cannot buy past: the VSA/resonator capacity literature explicitly treats the CORRELATED-codebook regime (our actual regime -- real ConceptNet relations genuinely overlapping across a mean-degree-6.65 graph with 16 relation types) as an OPEN problem, not a solved one, in EITHER direction -- some sources flag it as possibly even capacity-INCREASING under certain reweighting schemes, others as strictly worse. None of the four lit-scans found a paper that directly tests dense-Hopfield-style or path-as-object encoding against GENUINELY semantically-correlated (not i.i.d./random) codebooks at scale. The predictive-coding literature (Chalk/Marre/Tkacik PNAS 2018, carried over from the prior drill) proves a principled floor: you cannot decorrelate away genuinely-correlated semantic content without destroying the semantics itself -- so if the residual crosstalk at hop2 is dominated by REAL semantic overlap (e.g. multiple true relations connecting the same entity pair) rather than incidental/statistical noise, no amount of N, architecture, or voting redundancy escapes it, because it isn't noise to be cleaned up -- it's information that genuinely aliases. This is exactly what Prediction 2/3's HARD-FAIL branches are designed to detect rather than paper over: if DENSE_HOPFIELD_CLEANUP and PATH_AS_OBJECT both fail to move the needle at matched or even 8x resource, that is the signature of hitting the real semantic-aliasing floor, not a signal to throw yet more resource at the same lever.

## Intuitive summary

**What we did:** instead of asking "why didn't Stage-4's cleanup work well enough," we asked "what would it take to make it WIN decisively, given we have compute the brain doesn't." Four parallel searches went out: one on what raises single-step retrieval quality, one on whether combining fixes multiplies rather than just adds, one on what happens when you spend way more compute than a stingy brain would, and one on whether there's a fundamentally different way to chain memories that skips the step-by-step buildup of errors entirely.

**What we found:** the real story isn't "which one knob to turn" -- it's that our system is currently sitting on the WRONG SIDE of a cliff. Three completely separate fields of math (memory-capacity theory, error-correcting-code theory, and majority-vote theory) all say the exact same thing: below a certain noise threshold, a cleanup step works beautifully and errors barely pile up across steps; above that threshold, the same cleanup step only helps a little and errors still pile up almost as badly as if it weren't there. Our own numbers show precisely that signature -- Stage-4's cleanup gave the first hop a fixed boost, but the RATE at which fidelity was crashing hop over hop barely changed at all. That means Stage-4 wasn't "almost there" with a weak cleanup gate; it was doing the right kind of thing while still standing on the wrong side of the cliff.

**Why it matters:** since we're not stuck with the brain's 20-watt budget, the win-move is to spend real resource to shove the system decisively across that cliff, not tune the same cleanup gate harder. Concretely: use a much richer "attractor" cleanup style that mathematically has room for exponentially more memories rather than the current straightforward style that's capacity-limited; make everything about 8x bigger and see if that alone produces a sudden jump rather than a slow crawl; and, most interestingly, try encoding an entire 2-3-step chain as ONE object up front and reading it out in a single decisive pass, instead of doing three separate noisy hops in a row that each add their own error. We built a concrete next experiment with a real win bar (not just "better than before," but genuinely usable multi-hop fidelity) and an honest, named risk: if the overlap between memories is because the underlying knowledge genuinely, truly overlaps (not because our encoding is sloppy), then no amount of extra compute can clean that up -- and we built a specific check into the experiment that will tell us, honestly, if that's what we're actually facing.
