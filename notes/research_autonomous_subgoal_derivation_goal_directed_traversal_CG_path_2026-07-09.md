# Research: how does a system DERIVE an intermediate waypoint autonomously (MM -> CG for goal-directed multi-hop traversal)?

**Filed by:** research sub-agent. **Trigger:** the just-banked goal-conditioning cert — supplying a waypoint to the query lifts reach@2 4.1x (memoryless 0.121 -> goal-waypoint 0.500) and hop-1 to 0.756, MM-tier because the waypoint is SUPPLIED. The banked CG criterion needs the SAME lift when the substrate derives the waypoint itself from (query, final-goal) alone. **Method:** 3 parallel Sonnet lit-scan sub-agents, one per angle: (1) hippocampal forward-sweep/replay generative mechanism (what actually GENERATES a candidate waypoint, one level deeper than "goal biases the sweep," already established by the same-day `[[research-multihop-test-fairness-brain-goal-directed-traversal]]` drill); (2) PFC/HRL subgoal decomposition (backward-from-goal vs forward-search vs landmark/bottleneck discovery); (3) VSA/HDC substrate realization (unbind-as-equation-solving, landmark/hub retrieval, eigenoptions, generate-and-verify). Generic neuro/RL/math terms only, no substrate-specific mechanism names or numbers off-platform, per query-privacy discipline.

## HEADLINE

**No literature — biological or computational — shows a system deriving a waypoint from nothing. Every domain surveyed either (a) recombines/searches over an already-known local structure to propose a candidate, or (b) wraps that proposal in a verify/select step. There is no closed-form "solve for the missing waypoint" operation with direct empirical precedent anywhere.** The two mechanisms that DO have real precedent, across at least 2 of the 3 angles each, are: **(1) generate-and-verify / propose-then-check** (angles 2 and 3: HRL subgoal-optimization's sample-evaluate-resample loop, and kSubS/AdaSubS's explicit generator+verifier search) as the necessary WRAPPER regardless of what generates the candidate; and **(2) landmark/bottleneck-state identification** (angles 2 and 3: betweenness-centrality subgoal discovery in HRL, graph-Laplacian eigenoptions) as the best-precedented CANDIDATE-GENERATION mechanism in the pure computational literature — but this is **not cleanly confirmed biologically**: Duvelle, Grieves, Poulter et al. (Current Biology 2021) directly tested and found hippocampal place cells do NOT preferentially encode doorway/bottleneck states despite behaviorally-demonstrated connectivity sensitivity, an explicit disconfirmation sitting alongside older positive doorway-activity reports. The cleanest DIRECT biological evidence for an actual generative act (not mere biasing) is Gupta et al. (Neuron 2010): hippocampal replay demonstrably splices never-before-experienced shortcut trajectories from fragments of separately-experienced paths — i.e., **recombination over locally-known real structure**, not free generation and not bottleneck-biased.

**The convergent, honest picture across all 3 angles: nobody generates a waypoint out of thin air. Everybody either recombines real local structure (brain: replay splicing) or searches real local structure (ML: candidate-then-verify). This directly licenses the cheapest possible substrate mechanism: don't invent a new "waypoint-generation operator" — reuse the substrate's REAL, already-retrievable local-neighborhood structure, and pick among those real candidates using the exact same goal-cosine bias already certified as MM.** This is a substantially cheaper and more brain-aligned proposal than the "unbind-and-solve-for-X" framing in the original task prompt, and the lit-scan actively refutes that framing as the primary mechanism: the one paper found doing exactly that (Kumar 2026, arXiv 2606.24948, unverified peer review) reports a NEGATIVE result — accuracy collapses with chain length due to superposition contention, which is the same failure mode already documented for this substrate's own long-chain composition work.

## The brain mechanism (convergence, honest about disagreement)

1. **Hippocampal replay is a splicing/recombination operation over experienced fragments, not free generation.** Gupta, van der Meer, Touretzky & Redish, *Neuron* 65:695-705 (2010): rats generate replay of shortcut routes never physically traversed, built by splicing forward fragments of one experienced path with reverse fragments of another at the point they intersect. This is the field's clearest DIRECT (not model-inferred) evidence of a generative act, and it is graph-traversal-shaped: recombine real, already-encoded local segments at a shared node, not conjure a novel point.
2. **Successor-representation (SR) theory supplies the computational substrate the recombination could ride on, but has not been caught generating a waypoint in vivo.** Stachenfeld, Botvinick & Gershman (*Nat Neurosci* 2017) show place/grid fields fit an SR eigenbasis; Momennejad et al. (*Nat Hum Behav* 2017) show human revaluation behavior fits SR-plus-replay best. Both are model-fit/behavioral, not a direct neural "generation event" — an honest gap between the theory's elegance and what has actually been observed.
3. **Bottleneck/doorway-biased hippocampal encoding is DISPUTED, not confirmed** — Duvelle et al. (2021) is an explicit null result on the doorway-cell hypothesis in rats despite clear behavioral sensitivity to connectivity, sitting against older positive doorway-activity reports. The ML analog (betweenness-centrality/eigenoption subgoal discovery, angle 2/3) is strong computationally but should NOT be over-claimed as brain-confirmed.
4. **Goal-vector-arithmetic-on-cognitive-map (Boccara goal-locality distortion, Sarel goal-vector cells, Whittington/Behrens TEM) remains a modeling proposal** — plausible, architecturally elegant, but no cited study shows the brain computing a genuine intermediate waypoint via additive/interpolative vector arithmetic mid-path. Flagged as unconfirmed, not dismissed (per [[feedback-dont-dismiss-adjacent-methods]]).

## The PFC/HRL decomposition (the two-stage pipeline the literature implies but never states as one theory)

- **Stage A — candidate discovery:** McGovern & Barto (2001, ICML, diverse density), Şimşek & Barto (2004/2005, ICML, relative-novelty and betweenness-centrality "access states"), and the pure-ML analog Machado, Bellemare & Bowling (2017, ICML, "A Laplacian Framework for Option Discovery," graph-Laplacian eigenoptions) all independently converge on: identify recurring bottleneck/hub states across experienced trajectories as candidate subgoals. Sutton, Precup & Singh's original options framework (1999) explicitly does NOT specify subgoal derivation — this later work exists specifically to fill that gap.
- **Stage B — goal-directed selection/valuation among candidates:** Newell & Simon's General Problem Solver (1959/61) means-ends analysis and Florensa et al.'s Reverse Curriculum Generation (2017, CoRL) are the clearest backward-from-goal precedent — reason/grow outward FROM the goal to value which candidate reduces goal-distance. Forward rollout/MCTS is used to score/refine among candidates once proposed, not as primary derivation.
- **No PFC neuroscience paper found supplies an explicit derivation ALGORITHM** — Badre & D'Esposito's rostro-caudal hierarchy is about abstraction-level control, and Wilson/Takahashi/Schoenbaum/Niv and Basu & Ito's OFC "goal pointer" work describe REPRESENTING/maintaining a goal, not computing a derived subgoal from it. This is a genuine, honestly-reported gap between HRL computational theory and PFC neuroscience — the computational pipeline (discover candidates -> value/select) is well precedented; its neural implementation in PFC specifically is not.

## Substrate realization: reuse real local structure + the already-certified goal-cosine bias (no new primitive)

Given final-goal code **G** and current-node code **C**, do NOT attempt closed-form unbind-and-solve-for-the-missing-term as the primary mechanism (the lit-scan actively disfavors this: it is the least-precedented option and the one directly-on-point paper found reports a negative chain-length result consistent with this substrate's own known superposition-contention failure mode). Instead:

**Landmark-seeded greedy hop-selection ("self-generated waypoint via real local neighbors + goal-cosine argmax"):**
1. At the current node C, retrieve C's TRUE local hop-1 neighbor set via the substrate's existing local-neighborhood-scoping operation (already built, already used for the multi-hop retrieval work) — this is the recombination-over-real-structure step licensed by Gupta et al.'s replay-splicing finding: the candidate set is never invented, only ever real, already-encoded local structure.
2. Score each real candidate neighbor by cosine-similarity to G — this is EXACTLY the same bind/cosine operation already certified MM for supplied-waypoint goal-conditioning; nothing new is added, it is applied to the substrate's own generated candidate set instead of an externally-handed one.
3. Select argmax as the derived next-hop waypoint W; repeat from W until goal reached or hop budget exhausted. This is a one-hop-at-a-time greedy best-first search — the direct algorithmic analog of Stage-A-candidates (real local neighbors standing in for "bottleneck/access states," cheaper than precomputing betweenness centrality) + Stage-B-valuation (cosine-to-G standing in for backward-goal-value), fused into a single existing-primitive loop.
4. **Generate-and-verify wrapper (angle 2 + angle 3 convergence):** at each step, if the top-scoring candidate's cosine-to-G does NOT exceed C's own cosine-to-G (i.e., no available real neighbor moves closer to the goal), reject and fall back to the next-highest candidate, or terminate the derivation as failed rather than take a wrong-direction step — this is the well-precedented generate-and-verify safety check, cheap to add, and produces a clean, honest failure mode instead of silently drifting.

This proposal is deliberately the CHEAPEST buildable option consistent with the lit-scan: it invents no new operator, reuses local-neighborhood scoping (already exists) and goal-cosine biasing (already MM-certified), and its failure modes are the SAME failure modes the brain/ML literature honestly documents (myopic greedy traps, branch-point ties) rather than a novel unverified mechanism.

## Cheap decisive test

Zero new architecture, one new harness pass reusing the already-built local-neighborhood-scoping op and the already-certified goal-cosine bind: run the SAME subgraph/harness used for the supplied-waypoint MM cert, but instead of handing the query a ground-truth waypoint, have it self-select the next hop at each step via the greedy landmark-seeded loop above. Compare reach@2 and hop-1 accuracy against the two already-measured anchors (memoryless floor 0.121 / hop-1 baseline; supplied-waypoint ceiling 0.500 / hop-1 0.756).

## Falsifiable predictions (HARD-PASS + HARD-FAIL)

**Prediction 1 (autonomous greedy-hop derivation recovers most of the goal-conditioning lift — this IS the CG discriminator):**
- HARD-PASS: autonomous reach@2 >= 0.40 (recovers >=80% of the supplied-waypoint ceiling's 4.1x lift over memoryless, decisively clear of the 0.121 floor) AND autonomous hop-1 accuracy within 15pp of the supplied ceiling's 0.756.
- HARD-FAIL: autonomous reach@2 < 0.20 (collapses to within noise of the memoryless 0.121 floor) — the derivation step itself fails; autonomous CG is refuted for this mechanism and MM-with-supplied-waypoint remains the only certified tier.

**Prediction 2 (the SAME branch-point underdetermination diagnosed in the sibling-test drill recurs one level down, honestly extending that finding):**
- HARD-PASS: accuracy at "tied" branch points (top-2 real-neighbor cosine-to-G gap < 0.05) is measurably lower (>=15pp) than at "clear-winner" branch points (gap >= 0.15) — confirms the underdetermination mechanism from `[[research-multihop-test-fairness-brain-goal-directed-traversal]]` is the SAME mechanism limiting autonomous derivation, not a separate defect.
- HARD-FAIL: no measurable accuracy difference between tied and clear-winner branch points — would mean greedy selection is not bottlenecked by ambiguity in the way predicted; good news for robustness, but refutes this specific risk mechanism.

**Prediction 3 (myopic one-hop-at-a-time greedy underperforms a full-chain resonator/backward-chain alternative at longer depths — tests whether the cheap mechanism is sufficient or whether the costlier alternative is needed):**
- HARD-PASS: a resonator-network-style full-chain factorization (solve the whole goal = C (x) R1 (x) ... (x) Rk equation iteratively, in the spirit of Frady/Kent/Olshausen/Sommer 2020) outperforms greedy-hop reach@2 by >=10pp specifically at hop-depth >= 3, where one-step myopia should show most clearly.
- HARD-FAIL: no improvement, or resonator underperforms greedy at all depths — the cheap greedy mechanism is sufficient; do not invest in the costlier resonator alternative.

**P_deflated (autonomous derivation recovers most of the certified lift, Prediction 1): 0.38** (base intuition ~0.55 — the mechanism reuses two already-certified/already-built primitives, which is unusually favorable; deflated 0.17 per lit-scan calibration penalty because no source in any of the 3 scans directly tests THIS exact "greedy real-neighbor + goal-cosine argmax" loop — the convergence is structural/pipeline-level (candidate-discovery + goal-valuation as two well-precedented but never-jointly-tested stages), not an empirical precedent on anything resembling this substrate's codes). Prediction 2 sits higher (~0.55, close to a direct re-application of the already-strong sibling-test finding to a new context) and Prediction 3 is capped at the 0.50 novel-synthesis ceiling (genuinely new comparison, no direct precedent for either side winning).

## Substrate-product implications

- If Prediction 1 HARD-PASSes: this closes the MM -> CG gap with essentially free reuse of two already-shipped primitives (local-neighborhood scoping + goal-cosine bind), turning "the substrate can navigate to a supplied waypoint" into "the substrate autonomously plans its own path toward a stated goal one real hop at a time" — a materially stronger, genuinely autonomous-reasoning product story, and one that is honestly brain-aligned (splicing over real local structure, per Gupta et al.) rather than over-claiming a "generates novel waypoints from nothing" capability the literature does not support for ANY system, biological or artificial.
- If Prediction 1 HARD-FAILs but Prediction 2 HARD-PASSes: still valuable — confirms the SAME underdetermination mechanism (not a new defect) is the blocker, meaning the fix is the same fix already proposed for the sibling test (carry more explicit path-context / accept near-ties as a set) rather than a wholly new investigation, and rules out "the greedy mechanism is fundamentally the wrong shape" as the explanation.
- If Prediction 3 HARD-PASSes: signals the cheap greedy mechanism should be treated as a first-tier product feature with a documented depth ceiling, and the costlier resonator-based full-chain factorization becomes the natural Tier-2 upgrade path for longer-horizon autonomous planning — a legitimate escalation ladder (greedy for short-horizon, resonator for long-horizon) rather than a single monolithic mechanism, and this ladder itself mirrors the brain's own apparent division of labor (fast local bias vs. slower deliberative VTE/forward-sweep for harder cases) per the companion sibling-test drill.

## Intuitive summary

**What we did:** we already proved that if you TELL the substrate a specific waypoint on the way to a goal, it uses that waypoint dramatically better than searching blind (a 4x jump in success). The open question was the harder, more valuable one: can the substrate figure out its OWN waypoint, without anyone telling it, just from knowing where it is and where it ultimately wants to end up? We looked at how real brains do this (hippocampal "replay" that previews possible routes, prefrontal cortex holding a goal in mind), how AI planning systems do it (subgoal discovery, backward reasoning from a target), and what's directly buildable in our own kind of memory system.

**What we found:** nobody, brain or machine, invents a waypoint out of nowhere. Every real example either recombines pieces of ALREADY-KNOWN local structure (the brain literally splices together fragments of routes it has actually experienced before, never a route from thin air), or proposes a real candidate and then checks whether it actually helps before committing to it. That is genuinely reassuring: it means the fix is not some exotic new mechanism we'd have to invent, it is a small, cheap reuse of two things we already have working — the ability to look up a memory's real, true local neighbors, and the same "which option looks most like my goal" comparison we already proved works when handed the answer. The one fancier idea from the original question (solving an algebra equation for the missing middle step) turned out to be the WEAKEST-supported option in the literature, and the one direct example of someone trying it got worse results the longer the chain got — the same kind of "too much crosstalk over long chains" problem we've already run into elsewhere. So the literature actively steers us away from the fancy idea and toward the cheap, honest one.

**The plan we're proposing:** at each step, have the substrate look at its own real, true nearby neighbors (not invented ones), pick whichever one looks most like the final goal, move there, and repeat — checking at each step that it's actually getting closer, not drifting. This is basically the substrate doing its own turn-by-turn navigation using tools it already has.

**Why it matters, honestly:** this could genuinely close the gap between "give it a hint and it navigates well" (already proven) and "it plans its own route" (the real prize) using nothing new. But there's a real risk, and it's the same risk we just found in a sibling test: when there are several nearby neighbors that all look roughly equally close to the goal, the substrate has no better way to break the tie than a person would at a genuine fork in the road with no extra information — so we expect this to work well on clear paths and to struggle exactly at ambiguous forks, which is a fair, brain-like limitation rather than a failure, and we've written a specific test to check that this is really what happens before calling it either a win or a wall.
