## (a) HEADLINE

**The brain does not run one gate at constant stringency — it runs a SCHEDULE: wide-open/coverage-first while the foundation is empty, tightening as structure accumulates, with active "brake" machinery that only engages once there is something worth protecting. Live lit-scan confirms this convergence AND sharpens the mechanism: closure is not simply time-gated or interference-count-gated — it is SIGNAL-DETECTION-gated. Chang & Merzenich (2003, *Science*) show a critical period stays OPEN under sustained pure noise (uninformative rearing) rather than closing on schedule, and Toyoizumi et al. (2013, *Neuron*) formalize critical-period transition as an explicit signal-to-noise-ratio (SNR) shift in the underlying learning rule. Combined with Elman's starting-small computational demonstration (contested but directly relevant, see Section 1) and Achille et al.'s (2019, ICLR) deep-network Fisher-information "lock-in" account, three independent literatures converge: PERMISSIVE-EARLY / SELECTIVE-LATE is real, but the trigger for tightening is "has the system found extractable structure," not "how much time has passed" or "how much noise has accumulated." This directly refines the builder recommendation: gate stringency should ramp as a function of the substrate's OWN measured schema-fit/surprise-resolution success rate (an internal SNR proxy, already computable from existing `additive_map.score_all` + `reachability_audit.py` signals), not a fixed time-or-volume schedule — and it explains why a system fed pure noise should NOT prematurely lock in (mirroring Chang & Merzenich) while a system that has found real structure should tighten to protect it. This is also the most parsimonious resolution of "gate-selectivity only pays under high interference/noise": under low-noise/high-signal input, SNR rises fast and tightening happens early and cheaply; under high-noise/low-signal input, SNR never crosses threshold, tightening is (correctly) withheld, and the FIXED-schedule alternative would tighten anyway on a noise-heavy stream and needlessly reject/starve — this is the mechanism, not just an analogy, and is now testable directly (Section b).**

## 1. ORDER / CURRICULUM — is there a core -> periphery, concrete -> abstract sequence?

**Core knowledge (Spelke & Carey) is PARALLEL/modular, not itself strictly sequenced — but it is uniformly EARLIEST. Live-verified.** The proposed innate/early-maturing core systems (object representation/object permanence precursors — Baillargeon's violation-of-expectation work; approximate number system, ANS — Wynn 1992's *Nature* infant-arithmetic looking-time study; core geometry/spatial layout — Hermer & Spelke 1994/1996 reorientation studies; core agency/goal-attribution — Woodward 1998, Kuhlmeier/Wynn/Bloom 2003) are each independently evidenced very early in life via looking-time paradigms (Spelke & Kinzler 2007, *Developmental Science*, is the canonical review), and the literature does not establish a strong serial order AMONG them — they look more like parallel, domain-specific starter modules than a chain. What IS a robust, repeatedly-confirmed sequence is the BROADER one: these perceptual/sensorimotor-grounded core systems reliably precede and causally enable later symbolic/language-mediated abstract concepts (Section 2) — concrete-perceptual-first, abstract-symbolic-later is well-supported as a macro-order even though the core systems themselves are roughly parallel at the micro-level. **Actively contested, must flag (new, sharper than first draft):** whether this early competence reflects genuine innate DOMAIN-SPECIFIC conceptual systems at all, versus general-purpose perceptual/statistical-learning mechanisms misread as "core knowledge," is a live, unresolved fault line — Bremner's developmental-psychology critique explicitly notes a dissociation between infants' looking-time "knowledge" and their much-later ability to use that knowledge to guide action (echoing the classic A-not-B search literature); a 2024 *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* target article (Kaicher, Conti, Dedhe, Aulet & Cantlon, "Is core knowledge a natural subdivision of infant cognition?") directly questions whether Spelke's taxonomy is a mechanistically coherent natural kind or a convenient grouping; and a 2025 BBS commentary ("Core Perception: Re-imagining Precocious Reasoning as Sophisticated Perceiving") argues the competencies reflect sophisticated PERCEPTION, not conceptual "core knowledge" per se. Confidence: high for "core systems are earliest and parallel-not-serial among themselves" and for "concrete precedes abstract" as a macro-trend; high that the nativist-vs-perceptual-learning debate over WHY infants show this competence is genuinely unresolved as of 2024-2025, not a settled matter.

**Piagetian strict staging is LARGELY NOT the modern consensus — flag honestly.** The classic sensorimotor -> preoperational -> concrete-operational -> formal-operational sequence, as a domain-general, universally-staged, discrete-transition account, has been substantially superseded by neo-Piagetian (Case's central conceptual structures, Fischer's dynamic skill theory, Karmiloff-Smith's representational-redescription) and dynamic-systems (Thelen & Smith) accounts, which show development is more continuous, domain-specific, and individually variable than Piaget's discrete universal stages implied. What survives: the general DIRECTIONAL claim (sensorimotor/perceptual scaffolding before abstract symbolic operations) — the strict stage BOUNDARIES do not survive. Confidence: high that strict Piagetian staging is outdated; medium that a weaker "rough directional sequence" survives.

**Starting small / less-is-more — live-verified, and MORE HEAVILY CONTESTED than the director's first-draft synthesis stated. This correction matters and is carried forward honestly.** Elman 1993 ("Learning and development in neural networks: the importance of starting small," *Cognition* 48:71-99) showed a simple recurrent network given full-complexity recursive-grammar input and full memory capacity from the start FAILED to learn long-distance embedded dependencies, while networks that started with EITHER (i) simple sentences with complexity ramped up over training, or (ii) limited memory/capacity gradually increased, SUCCEEDED. Newport (1990, "Maturational constraints on language learning," *Cognitive Science* 14:11-28) proposed the human-developmental analog: children's smaller working-memory/analytic capacity may HELP (not merely fail to hinder) language acquisition by forcing attention onto smaller, local units, whereas adults' full capacity favors whole-form/rote analysis and produces worse ultimate L2 attainment. **But the live scan surfaced a DIRECT, same-paradigm failed replication and a systematic review siding against the hypothesis, which the director's recalled-knowledge draft understated:** Rohde & Plaut (1999, *Cognition* 72:67-109), using Elman's own architecture/parameters/training input, FAILED to replicate the starting-small advantage — full-capacity-from-the-start networks learned successfully, and restricting capacity/input complexity did not help and sometimes hurt, especially as the language was made more naturalistic. Rohde & Plaut (2003, in Quinlan ed., *Connectionist Models of Development*) escalated this into an explicit rebuttal titled "Less is Less in Language Acquisition." Marcus (1999, *Cognition*) published a direct commentary continuing the exchange. Most importantly, **Brooks (2019, *Language Learning* 69(S1), "More Is More in Language Learning: Reconsidering the Less-Is-More Hypothesis")** — a systematic re-examination of all three components of Newport's hypothesis (capacity limits, constrained hypothesis space, simplified input) — found "little to no evidence of benefits of limited cognitive capacity," reduced capacity instead associating with childhood LANGUAGE DISORDERS, and child-directed-speech simplicity helping only at the very earliest stage while later input complexity actually benefits attainment. **Revised confidence: high that the controversy is real, multi-decade, and NOT resolved in the "less-is-more" direction by the current review literature — this claim should be presented as contested, with the critique literature currently carrying more systematic-review weight, not as an established mechanism to design around uncritically.** This task/paradigm-dependency (works on Elman's specific toy grammar, fails to generalize under Rohde & Plaut's more naturalistic conditions) is itself useful evidence for Section 4's task-dependency argument, but the clean "less-is-more" story cannot be taken as settled science.

**Statistical-learning order effects (frequency-first, structure-second) — live-verified, and the director's first-draft claim of "directionally supportive" was TOO GENEROUS; corrected here.** Saffran, Aslin & Newport (1996, *Science*) and Fiser & Aslin (2002, *Cognition*) establish that infant statistical learning (transitional-probability extraction from continuous streams, extending to non-linguistic visual domains) is real and domain-general, with a genuine developmental ONSET threshold (a 2-vs-5-month-old visual-sequence-segmentation comparison found only 5-month-olds succeeded at extracting statistical structure at all — an onset-threshold finding, not a frequency-before-structure ORDERING finding). Critically, one directly relevant study found first-order TRANSITIONAL PROBABILITY dominates over simple co-occurrence FREQUENCY in driving infant predictions even early on — the OPPOSITE of a clean "frequency is learned first/is more basic" story. **Revised verdict: no clean, well-replicated evidence for a general "simple statistics before complex/higher-order statistics" curriculum effect was found in the human infant literature** — the strongest related finding is an age-related capacity-onset threshold (statistical learning of any kind becomes possible above some maturational floor), not a frequency-before-structure sequencing law. Confidence: medium on the onset-threshold claim; this section should NOT be cited as support for a frequency-first curriculum design — that specific claim is now a confirmed EVIDENCE GAP, not a weak positive.

## 2. BOOTSTRAPPING — does existing structure gate and scaffold what gets added next?

**Carey's Quinian bootstrapping (*The Origin of Concepts*, 2009) is the canonical, precisely-worked-out mechanism, and it is EXPLICITLY order-dependent.** Canonical case: exact integer concepts. Children first memorize the count list ("one, two, three...") as a rote sequence with NO numerical meaning — the "one-knower" stage (Wynn 1990, 1992): a child can correctly hand over "one" object on request but treats all higher number words as an undifferentiated "some" (asked for "three," grabs a random handful). Over roughly 12-18 months, children progress one-knower -> two-knower -> three-knower -> (sometimes four-knower) — the documented "subset-knower" sequence (Le Corre & Carey 2007) — before making a qualitatively DISCONTINUOUS leap to cardinal-principle (CP) knower status: understanding that the LAST word reached in counting gives the whole set's cardinality, a principle that generalizes immediately to numbers far beyond any individually memorized so far (Sarnecka & Carey 2008 probed exactly what CP-knowers understand about the count list's successor structure). Carey's mechanism: the count list is a borrowed PLACEHOLDER structure (initially meaningless symbols in a fixed relational order) that the child maps, one small number at a time, onto the core object-tracking system's exact-small-quantity (subitizing, 1-to-1 correspondence) capacity — gradually inducing the successor-function pattern implicit in the list's order, and generalizing it to produce a genuinely NEW concept (exact natural number) not reducible to either core-knowledge primitive alone (the object-tracking system is exact but small-capacity-limited; the ANS is unlimited-range but only approximate).

**This IS a hard sequencing/gating requirement, not a soft correlation.** The CP-knower transition CANNOT happen without BOTH prerequisites already in place: (i) the rote-memorized count list itself (an external, borrowed symbolic structure), and (ii) the core object-tracking system's small-exact-number capacity to anchor the first few mappings. Bootstrapping a genuinely new abstraction, on this account, structurally REQUIRES admitting and holding a placeholder structure the system cannot yet semantically evaluate — which is directly informative for Section 4: **a strict, meaning-requiring admission gate applied from the very start would prevent this exact, textbook-canonical bootstrapping mechanism from ever getting off the ground.**

**Contested, must flag: the "bootstrapping problem" itself.** Critics (Rips, Asmuth & Bloomfield 2006/2008, among others) have argued bootstrapping-as-described risks circularity: if the child is manipulating placeholder symbols by their POSITIONAL/syntactic relations before grasping their meaning, how does genuine new semantic content ever non-circularly attach — is this just symbol-shuffling relabeled as concept acquisition? This is an actively debated, unresolved philosophical objection, not settled in Carey's favor by consensus. Confidence: high that the empirical subset-knower timeline is real and well-replicated; medium-only on whether "bootstrapping" is the correct THEORETICAL account of what's happening mechanistically (the phenomenon is solid; the explanatory mechanism is contested).

**Adjacent scaffolding literatures, same shape:** syntactic bootstrapping (Gleitman 1990 — known sentence structure scaffolds inference of a new verb's meaning) and semantic bootstrapping (Pinker 1984 — known semantic categories scaffold inference of syntactic category) run in opposite directions but share the core logic: EXISTING structure in one domain is used as a lever to acquire NEW structure in an adjacent domain. Gentner's structure-mapping theory, and specifically the "relational shift" (Gentner & Rattermann 1991), documents a developmental ORDER within analogical reasoning itself: children rely on surface/object similarity FIRST, shifting to relational/structural similarity only LATER in development — a second, independent instance of concrete-before-abstract sequencing, this time within a single cognitive mechanism rather than across the whole trajectory.

## 3. SHIFTING PLASTICITY / CRITICAL PERIODS — permissive early, selective/protected later, and is it reversible?

**Classic biology and the OPEN mechanism — live-verified.** Hubel & Wiesel (1963, 1965, *J. Neurophysiology*) established that monocular deprivation during a bounded developmental window in kittens (as little as 3-4 days during the ~4-week peak in cat) causes a permanent ocular-dominance shift/amblyopia, with little effect if the same deprivation occurs outside the window; effects persist for years after restored normal vision. Hensch's mechanistic account (Hensch et al., *Science* 1998; Hensch, *Curr Top Dev Biol* 2005; *Ann Rev Neurosci* 2004) identifies the OPENING trigger as maturation of GABAergic inhibitory circuits — specifically parvalbumin-positive (PV+) fast-spiking basket interneurons crossing an inhibitory-tone threshold. Critically, this can be directly MANIPULATED, not just observed: GAD65-knockout mice (impaired GABA synthesis) fail to open the critical period on schedule; dark-rearing delays PV+ maturation and correspondingly delays onset; benzodiazepines (boosting GABA-A transmission) can trigger the critical period EARLY, before its normal developmental time. This is BOTH intrinsically-timed and experience-gated at once — not a pure clock, not a pure experience trigger, but their interaction, and a PNAS 2020 paper ("Critical period regulation across multiple timescales") frames this explicitly as a fast experience-driven layer riding atop a slower maturational layer, not a single-timescale process.

**The CLOSE mechanism is an active, multi-layered, partially-redundant "brake," not passive decay.** Perineuronal nets (PNNs — chondroitin-sulfate-proteoglycan lattices condensing around PV+ neurons at closure, physically stabilizing existing synapses against further rearrangement), the Otx2 homeoprotein (transferred non-cell-autonomously into PV+ cells, where it is BOTH necessary and sufficient for opening AND, at a different dose/timing, for closing the window — the same molecule drives both phases, a striking mechanistic unification — Sugiyama et al. 2008, *Cell*; Beurdeley et al. 2012, *J. Neurosci*, showing Otx2 binds directly to PNNs to persistently maintain the mature/closed state), myelin/Nogo-receptor and PirB signaling (a structurally separate brake tied to oligodendrocyte maturation), and Lynx1 (an endogenous prototoxin, structurally related to α-bungarotoxin, that dampens nicotinic-acetylcholine-receptor signaling and tunes excitatory/inhibitory balance — a FUNCTIONAL, not structural, brake acting on a distinct axis) all actively CONSOLIDATE and lock in existing structure once the window closes.

**Reversibility — the single most important nuance for the builder, and now confirmed at the molecular-mechanism level, not just phenomenologically.** Critical-period closure is a maintained STATE, not a permanent architectural loss: chondroitinase ABC (chABC) enzymatic digestion of PNNs reopens ocular-dominance plasticity in adult rodents; mechanistically, chABC (and independently fluoxetine) works by releasing TrkB receptor from suppression by a PTPσ-CSPG complex specifically WITHIN THE SAME PV+ cells that opened the window originally (Carulli et al./J. Neurosci 2021) — an elegant closure of the loop: the brake targets the identical cell type as the original trigger. Fluoxetine (chronic SSRI) plus environmental enrichment reopens adult visual-cortex plasticity and promotes amblyopia recovery via reduced intracortical inhibition plus increased BDNF (Vetencourt et al. 2008, *Science* 320:385-388) — and co-administering diazepam (which boosts GABA-A signaling, re-raising inhibition) BLOCKS the fluoxetine reopening effect, directly implicating disinhibition, not some independent mechanism, as causal. Steinzeig et al. (2019, *Eur J Neurosci*) show fluoxetine-induced plasticity can even outlast the natural critical period's own duration. **The brain retains the underlying CAPACITY to be permissive again — "selectivity" is a dial actively held in place by ongoing brake machinery, not a one-way door.** This directly supports a builder design where stringency is a reversible, region-scoped setting rather than a one-time architectural commitment.

**Generality beyond vision — real, but multiple-mechanism, not one transplantable rule; and hierarchically STAGGERED across brain regions (new, sharper finding than the director's first-draft version).** Language sensitive/critical periods (Lenneberg 1967; Johnson & Newport 1989's classic quantitative L2 age-of-arrival study, 46 native Chinese/Korean speakers showing a strong negative age-performance relationship for ages 3-15 with no significant correlation for 17-39 — a decline-then-plateau shape, though **contested**: some replications find continued decline past puberty rather than a clean plateau, so the "hard offset" claim is weaker than "earlier is generally better") and synaptic-density trajectories (Huttenlocher 1979 *Brain Research*, 1987 *J Comp Neurol*: human cortical synaptic density overshoots adult levels in childhood by roughly 2x then is pruned ~40% by early adolescence) both instantiate the same broad SHAPE. **New, live-verified refinement:** Petanjek et al. (2011, *PNAS*, "Extraordinary neoteny of synaptic spines in the human prefrontal cortex") shows different cortical regions close their windows on markedly DIFFERENT schedules, hierarchically — sensory cortex prunes/closes relatively early, while prefrontal/association cortex retains elevated spine density into the third decade of life. **Honest flag, sharpened:** the underlying molecular mechanism differs meaningfully by system — vision has the most detailed, directly-manipulated molecular account (GABA/PV/PNN/Otx2/Lynx1); language's "closing" mechanism remains comparatively INFERRED from behavioral age-curves rather than directly demonstrated at the cellular level; general synaptic pruning is activity-dependent elimination of a genetically overproduced substrate, likely PARTLY overlapping with the vision-specific PV/PNN machinery in sensory cortex but not proven identical elsewhere. Scholarpedia's "Critical period" article explicitly flags domain-general-vs-domain-specific mechanistic homology as an open question. This is convergent evolution of a SHAPE via at-least-partially-distinct implementations, not one universal mechanism — should not be oversold as a single transplantable rule, but the STAGGERED-BY-REGION finding (Petanjek) is itself a useful, directly transferable design idea (Section 4/e).

**Computational demonstration that this shape is substrate-general, not biology-specific.** Achille, Rovere & Soatto (2019, "Critical Learning Periods in Deep Neural Networks," ICLR, arXiv:1711.08856) showed deep networks given an early-training deficit (blurred inputs, or in later variants label noise, for the first N epochs) suffer a PERMANENT performance deficit that later training on clean data cannot recover — even though a network trained clean throughout shows no such deficit — tracking a measured Fisher-information/"information plasticity" curve that rises then decays early in training, largely independent of the deficit's specific content. **A telling, live-verified control result sharpens this further:** deficits that do NOT corrupt the input's low-level statistics (e.g. simple vertical image flips) have NO lasting effect — only corruptions that genuinely interfere with the statistics the network is trying to learn cause the permanent CP-like damage. This demonstrates permissive-early/selective-late is a property of gradient-based learning systems generally, AND that the mechanism is specifically sensitive to distribution-relevant interference, not just any early perturbation — a materially more precise, transferable precedent than the director's first-draft version stated.

## 4. THE PAYOFF — does permissive-early/selective-late match "gate-selectivity only pays under high interference/noise"? — MAJOR UPDATE: live scan found DIRECT supporting citations, not just inference

**The director's first-draft version of this section (below the line) said no literature directly answers this and it must be treated as pure inference. The live-verified critical-periods lit-scan found two directly on-point citations that upgrade this from inference to a real, if still narrow, empirical/theoretical base — and they sharpen the mechanism in an important way: closure is triggered by SIGNAL-DETECTION SUCCESS, not by accumulated interference/noise as such.**

1. **Chang & Merzenich (2003, *Science* 300:498-502, "Environmental Noise Retards Auditory Cortical Development") — the single most directly on-point citation found.** Rat pups reared in continuous, unstructured (uninformative) noise show DELAYED maturation of tonotopic organization in auditory cortex — and when later exposed to clean, structured tones as ADULTS, their auditory cortex STILL reorganized (remained in a plastic, critical-period-like state) long after normally-reared animals' plasticity had already closed. **This directly answers the crux question in the specific direction hypothesized:** when the environment offers no reliably extractable structure (pure noise/interference with no signal), the system does NOT close its plasticity window on a fixed schedule — it stays open, permissive, and waiting for structure to lock onto, rather than closing prematurely on a noise-only diet and getting stuck. Closure is gated on HAVING EXTRACTED something worth protecting, not on elapsed time or accumulated exposure alone.

2. **Toyoizumi et al. (2013, *Neuron*, "A theory of the transition to critical period plasticity: inhibition selectively suppresses spontaneous activity") — a formal theoretical bridge, not just an empirical correlate.** This model frames the GABA-maturation-driven critical-period transition explicitly as a shift in the learning rule's SIGNAL-TO-NOISE RATIO — increasing sensitivity to visually-evoked (structured, external) activity relative to spontaneous (internal, noise) activity. The brain's own theoretical account of why critical-period timing exists is framed in exactly SNR terms, not as an arbitrary maturational clock.

3. **Achille et al.'s flip-vs-blur control (Section 3, restated here for the payoff argument)** — early corruption that does NOT touch the input's real statistics (flips) causes no lasting damage, while corruption of the actual statistical structure does — directly consistent with "the lock-in mechanism is tracking real signal-relevant interference, not generic perturbation."

4. **Noisy-label machine-learning literature (live-verified, directly relevant, previously not covered at all):** networks reliably fit CLEAN/consistent data first and only begin memorizing noisy/corrupted labels after a measurable delay — a well-documented "early-learning phase" (e.g. "Early-Learning Regularization Prevents Memorization of Noisy Labels," arXiv:2007.00151; co-teaching / dual-network mutual-filtering methods explicitly exploit the early-training loss-divergence between clean and noisy examples as a selectivity signal). This is directly exploitable engineering precedent: corruption introduced EARLY causes irrevocable damage; the same corruption introduced LATER (after the network has locked onto clean structure) does not — the timing of when noise arrives relative to the network's own progress matters more than the noise's raw quantity.

5. **Elman/Rohde & Plaut's task-dependency (Section 1) remains a real, if now more heavily contested, secondary precedent** — the effect is task/structure-dependent rather than a universal law, consistent with (but weaker than) the Chang & Merzenich / Toyoizumi direct evidence above.

6. **Bootstrapping's logic (Section 2, director's own knowledge, not live-verified this cycle) implies the same failure mode from the opposite direction:** a strict, meaning-requiring gate applied while the foundation is empty would block Carey's bootstrapping mechanism outright — nothing yet exists to be interfered with, so a selective gate at this stage has all of the cost (blocks scaffold formation) and none of the benefit.

**Revised verdict: the "selectivity pays off contingent on found-signal, not on elapsed time or raw noise-count" mechanism is now supported by a genuine, if narrow, direct empirical citation (Chang & Merzenich) plus a formal theoretical account (Toyoizumi) plus a converging computational literature (Achille et al.'s control experiment; noisy-label early-learning). This is meaningfully stronger than "pure novel synthesis with zero direct precedent" — it should be treated as a real, transferable mechanism candidate, though still capped: none of these sources measure the SPECIFIC claim on OUR substrate, and the live-scanning sub-agent itself flagged that no single paper states this as a formal "always vs. only-under-interference" theorem — it is a synthesis bridging Toyoizumi's SNR framing, Chang & Merzenich's empirical result, and the computational lock-in literature, which the sub-agent independently arrived at using the SAME "medium confidence, interpretive bridge" caveat this note's calibration discipline already requires. That convergence (director's synthesis and independent live sub-agent synthesis landing on the same structure) is itself a modest confidence-raising signal, not a substitute for a direct substrate-level test.**

**Design implication, revised:** the gate-tightening trigger in the builder (Section b) should be keyed to the substrate's OWN measured SIGNAL-EXTRACTION SUCCESS RATE (an internal SNR proxy — e.g., the rate at which candidates pass schema-fit/resolve low surprise, using the already-existing `additive_map.score_all` + `reachability_audit.py` signals) rather than a fixed time-or-volume-based ramp. This is a direct, concrete, cheaply-testable refinement of the schedule design that the live lit-scan specifically motivated — see revised Test (b) below.

## (b) Cheap decisive test

**Schedule x noise-level interaction test, reusing the existing 07-15 pilot-cell design and all already-built machinery (`additive_map.score_all` for surprise, `reachability_audit.py` for schema-fit, per-candidate recurrence tally) — no new scoring mechanism needed.**

Construct TWO candidate-stream conditions at matched total data budget:
- **LOW-NOISE stream:** candidate batches per the existing 07-15 design (REDUNDANT / GENUINE-NOVEL-RELIABLE / ONE-OFF-NOISE / INTERFERENCE-CONTROL), with the ONE-OFF-NOISE fraction held SMALL (e.g., <=5% of the stream).
- **HIGH-NOISE stream:** identical composition except the ONE-OFF-NOISE (scrambled/corrupted, non-recurring) fraction is substantially larger (e.g., >=30% of the stream).

Under each noise condition, run TWO gate-schedule arms:
- **FIXED-STRICT:** the full 3-signal gate (schema-fit/surprise/recurrence) at its already-specified mature thresholds (`SCHEMA_FIT_MIN=0.5`, `SURPRISE_MIN=0.5`, `RECURRENCE_MIN=3`) applied uniformly from the very first candidate.
- **PERMISSIVE-THEN-SELECTIVE (time-ramp variant):** thresholds start relaxed (`RECURRENCE_MIN` starts at 1, `SCHEMA_FIT_MIN` starts near 0, surprise not used as a rejection criterion) and ramp linearly to the mature FIXED-STRICT thresholds over a pre-registered fraction (e.g., first 20%) of the total ingest budget.
- **PERMISSIVE-THEN-SELECTIVE (SNR-gated variant — added per the live-verified Chang & Merzenich / Toyoizumi finding, Section 4):** thresholds start relaxed identically to the time-ramp variant, but the ramp-to-strict TRIGGER is the substrate's own measured schema-fit/surprise-resolution SUCCESS RATE (a rolling window of what fraction of recent candidates cross a low bar of extractable structure) crossing a pre-registered threshold, rather than elapsed ingest volume. Under a HIGH-NOISE stream this variant should tighten LATE or not at all (mirroring Chang & Merzenich's noise-reared animals staying plastic); under a LOW-NOISE stream it should tighten EARLY (mirroring normal-reared animals' on-schedule closure).

Measure, per (noise-condition x schedule-arm) cell: (i) final held-out MRR on the original 0.1282-baseline eval split (interference-control metric), (ii) foundation COVERAGE (fraction of genuinely novel-reliable candidates successfully consolidated), (iii) noise-admission rate (fraction of one-off-noise candidates wrongly consolidated).
