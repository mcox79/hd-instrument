## (a) HEADLINE

**The brain applies world knowledge to reference IMMEDIATELY, not late, but the mechanism is graded/distributional (an ATL-style connectionist hub), not a discrete symbolic lookup — and the pre-LLM literature's own ceiling on exactly this problem (the Winograd Schema Challenge) shows a glass-box knowledge-graph-plus-reasoning system CAN reach ~80%, but only with per-item hand-built knowledge representations; the one fully-automatic, full-official-set (WSC-273) rule-based number is 57.1%, achieved via live web search rather than a static offline KG, and the field's own retrospective attributes the plateau to KNOWLEDGE COVERAGE/EXTRACTION plus a genuine REASONING-EXPRESSIVENESS limit (categorical symbolic matching cannot combine multiple facts or weigh competing likelihoods) — not to the schemas being designed to specifically defeat knowledge-based reasoning (they were designed to defeat statistical surface cues).** For the substrate's specific residual: the OBJECT-vs-OBJECT slice ("the parson...as he rode" = human vs. animal) is exactly the class a coarse selectional-plausibility feature (McRae/Ferretti-style thematic fit, computationally approximable by ConceptNet/CSKG edge weights) is BUILT to catch, and the substrate's own positive control already proved this class of signal moves the metric (8/8 on constructed pairs) — this is a real, buildable, bounded lever. The PERSON-vs-PERSON slice ("Cheryl...because HE...") is structurally different: selectional plausibility cannot discriminate two humans, implicit-causality is a LEXICAL PRIMITIVE that only sets an EXPECTATION for who supplies the explanation (Bott & Solstad), not a resolver, and in the substrate's own worked example the competing entity ("Simon") is not even a verb argument, so a per-verb bias table is structurally inapplicable — the literature is explicit that this needs multi-fact, likelihood-weighted ABDUCTIVE reasoning (Hobbs 1993; Kehler & Rohde's Bayesian P(referent) term), which is exactly the reasoning-expressiveness gap the pre-LLM WSC literature (Sharma et al.'s ASP system) measured and could NOT close even with hand-built facts (26/51 of their unsolved cases needed multi-fact combination; 25/51 needed probabilistic comparison — both structurally unavailable to categorical logic-program matching).

## (b) Cheap decisive test

Two SEPARABLE builds, tested independently, because the literature says they are different mechanisms with different ceilings:

**Test 1 — selectional-plausibility-from-KG, targeting the object/concrete-noun slice of the residual.** Replace/augment the substrate's 12-dim grounded space with a ConceptNet/CSKG-sourced selectional-fit score (edge types: `CapableOf`, `UsedFor`, `AtLocation`, `HasProperty`, animacy-relevant relations) computed as an edge-weighted or cosine plausibility score for `verb x candidate-referent`, fused into the SAME Bayesian-product architecture already built and refuted for the coherence prior (reuse the fusion machinery, swap the feature source). Recompute the residual's ORACLE ceiling for this channel FIRST (per the standing "ask if it can even succeed" discipline that caught the coherence-prior's near-chance oracle before wasting a full build) — if the KG-selectional oracle is still near-chance on the person-heavy residual, do not proceed to a full fusion build.
HARD-PASS: KG-selectional oracle on the residual clears >=15% (well above the coherence-prior's measured 1.5-2.9% channel oracles, and consistent with this being a genuinely different, richer feature source), AND a fused build beats its own info-free shuffled-edge twin CI-separated on the object/concrete-noun sub-slice specifically (not the whole residual — see Test 2 for the person-heavy slice).
HARD-FAIL: oracle <=5% — would mean ConceptNet/CSKG's coverage of exactly the facts needed (e.g., "ride" implicates an animal-vehicle relation, not the rider) is too sparse or too coarse-grained to help even in principle, and the mechanism should be shelved pending a richer knowledge asset.

**Test 2 — multi-hop, likelihood-weighted plausibility comparison, targeting the person-vs-person slice.** Do NOT attempt this with a single-hop KG-edge lookup (the literature predicts single-fact matching fails here by construction — see Q3/Q5 below). Build a minimal weighted-abduction-style comparator: for a 2-candidate person tie, retrieve CSKG/ConceptNet paths connecting each candidate to the clause's predicate/complement (e.g., "wouldn't have sex" -> candidate X), and choose the candidate whose best-supporting inference chain has the lower cost / higher combined edge-weight (Hobbs 1993's abductive-cost heuristic, radically simplified to depth-2/3 path search over CSKG rather than full theorem-proving).
HARD-PASS: on a CONSTRUCTED person-vs-person minimal-pair fixture set (>=15 pairs modeled on the Cheryl/boyfriend/Simon-style implicit-causality-plus-discourse-relation case), the comparator beats chance (50%) by a CI-separated margin AND beats an info-free twin (randomly-permuted edge weights). This is a POSITIVE-CONTROL test, not yet a claim about the real residual population.
HARD-FAIL: <=55% on the constructed set (no better than chance) — would directly confirm the person-vs-person slice is a genuine no-LLM bound for THIS specific knowledge asset + this shallow-search reasoning depth, consistent with Sharma et al.'s own finding that even hand-built multi-fact cases were their hardest, unsolved residual.
**Do not test Test 2 directly against the real LitBank/GAP residual population before the constructed-pair positive control passes** — per the coherence-prior lesson, a near-chance oracle on the real population would otherwise be indistinguishable from "mechanism doesn't work" vs. "population doesn't contain resolvable cases," and the constructed-pair test isolates the mechanism question first.

## (c) Falsifiable predictions (HARD-PASS / HARD-FAIL)

**Prediction A — a KG-sourced selectional-plausibility score recovers a measurable, non-trivial slice of the OBJECT/concrete-noun residual, beating its info-free twin.**
P estimate: **P=0.40** (deflated from ~0.60; the mechanism class is well-supported — McRae/Ferretti thematic fit and the ATL hub literature both converge on graded plausibility being used immediately and pervasively — but ConceptNet/CSKG's coverage of the SPECIFIC facts needed for 200-year-old literary prose or modern Wikipedia bios is untested here, and the substrate's own prior finding that its 12-dim grounded space was "coarse but directionally correct" on the positive control (8/8) does not guarantee a KG-sourced score will be finer-grained rather than just differently-coarse).
HARD-PASS: KG-selectional oracle >=15% on the residual's object/concrete-noun sub-slice; fused build beats info-free twin CI-separated on that sub-slice.
HARD-FAIL: oracle <=5% — the KG's coverage/grain is not the bottleneck's fix; consistent with a genuinely irreducible slice.

**Prediction B — implicit causality is confirmed as an expectation-setting LEXICAL primitive, not a resolving mechanism, for cases where the competing entity is not a verb argument.**

Not raised higher because this is a logical/structural argument rather than a fresh empirical test.)

HARD-PASS: CI-separated margin over single-hop lookup AND over chance, on the constructed person-vs-person set.
HARD-FAIL: ties single-hop lookup or chance — would directly corroborate Sharma et al.'s own finding that this reasoning class is not easily bought even with correct facts, and would argue the person-vs-person slice is close to a genuine ceiling for this knowledge asset + reasoning depth, not merely an unbuilt lever.

## (d) Detailed answers to the 5 questions (with citations; [PRIMARY]/[SECONDARY] flags preserved from the 4 lit-scan lanes)

### Q1 — Neural/cognitive mechanism, and timing (immediate vs late)

The literature does not pick ONE of (a)/(b)/(c) — it says all three operate, but on DIFFERENT timescales and DIFFERENT grains, and the substrate's practical question ("what can a glass-box mechanism recover") maps onto that same split:

- **Selectional plausibility / thematic fit is graded, statistical, and IMMEDIATE.** McRae, Ferretti, Hare, Elman and colleagues (McRae, Spivey-Knowlton & Tanenhaus 1998, *JML* [SECONDARY]; Matsuki, Chow, Hare, Elman, Scheepers & McRae 2011, *JEP:LMC* [SECONDARY]) show event-based plausibility affects FIRST-PASS reading measures, not late repair. Hagoort, Hald, Bastiaansen & Petersson (2004, *Science* [SECONDARY, paywalled, corroborated via review]) is the landmark ERP result: a factual WORLD-KNOWLEDGE violation ("Dutch trains are white") produces an N400 statistically indistinguishable in onset/latency/amplitude from a plain lexical-semantic violation ("Dutch trains are sour") — world knowledge is integrated as fast as word meaning, not after it. Van Berkum, Zwitserlood, Hagoort & Brown (2003, *Cognitive Brain Research* [SECONDARY]) push this earlier still: a discourse-level world-knowledge anomaly elicits an N400 effect from ~150-200ms post-word-onset, before word recognition is even complete.
- **This immediate signal is coarse-grained and dissociable from a slower, finer-grained event-knowledge signal.** Warren & McConnell (2007, *Psychon. Bull. Rev.* [SECONDARY]) and Paczynski & Kuperberg (2012, *JML* [PRIMARY, fetched]) show selectional/animacy violations disrupt reading EARLIER/harder than equally-implausible-but-selectionally-legal events, and animacy-based N400 effects are immune to semantic-relatedness facilitation in a way real-world event/state-knowledge N400 effects are NOT — i.e., there are (at least) two layers: a fast, coarse "is this even the right KIND of filler" check, and a slower, finer "is this the SPECIFIC right filler" check.
- **The neural substrate for the graded, integrative layer is the anterior temporal lobe (ATL) "semantic hub"** (Patterson, Nestor & Rogers 2007, *Nat. Rev. Neurosci.* [SECONDARY]; causal evidence: Pobric, Jefferies & Lambon Ralph 2007, *PNAS*, rTMS over left ATL transiently mimics semantic-dementia-like impairment [SECONDARY]). Critically for Q4 below, the ATL hub's own computational implementation (Rogers, Lambon Ralph, Garrard, Bozeat, McClelland, Hodges & Patterson 2004, *Psych. Review* [SECONDARY] — the same PDP/parallel-distributed-processing framework as Rogers & McClelland's broader semantic-cognition program) is explicitly a DISTRIBUTED, GRADED connectionist network, not a symbolic lookup table. A genuine rival exists (Binder & Desai 2011, *Trends Cogn. Sci.* [SECONDARY] — multiple convergence zones, not one hub) but nobody in this literature argues for a discrete/symbolic representation.
- **Script/schema and situation-model knowledge operates on a SLOWER timescale and is where "resolution" (as opposed to "bonding") happens.** Garrod & Sanford's two-stage account (Garrod & Sanford 1994 [SECONDARY]; empirical timing: Garrod & Terras 2000, *JML* [SECONDARY]) is explicit: BONDING (fast, first-pass, lexical/structural) happens first, RESOLUTION (situational/world-knowledge-dependent, later, second-pass) happens after. Zwaan & Radvansky's Event Indexing Model (Zwaan, Langston & Graesser 1995, *Psychological Science* [PRIMARY-adjacent, abstract fetched]) formalizes the situation model as tracked along 5 dimensions (space/time/causation/intentionality/protagonist), updated with a measurable reading-time cost at discontinuities — this is a slower, more effortful updating process than the immediate N400 plausibility signal.
- **Whether the SLOW, world-knowledge-dependent resolution stage runs AUTOMATICALLY (constructionist) or only STRATEGICALLY when needed (minimalist) is genuinely contested** (McKoon & Ratcliff 1992, *Psych. Review* [SECONDARY] vs. Graesser, Singer & Trabasso 1994, *Psych. Review* [SECONDARY]) — but a 2024 direct test (Jones & Bergen, *Language and Cognition* [PRIMARY, fetched]) found world-knowledge effects on pronoun choice even with no explicit task demand, arguing world knowledge is deployed "routinely and automatically," which leans constructionist for exactly the Winograd-style case this drill cares about.

**Synthesis for the substrate:** the brain's answer is layered — a fast/immediate, coarse, graded selectional-plausibility signal (ATL-hub-like) handles the "obviously wrong filler" cases quickly; a slower, situation-model-dependent resolution stage handles genuinely tied cases, deployed more routinely than once thought. Both layers are DISTRIBUTIONAL/graded in their neural implementation, not symbolic constraint-checking — this bears directly on Q4.

### Q2 — Implicit causality / coherence-driven cases: lexical primitive or situation-model reasoning?

**Both, but layered, and the substrate's own worked example falls on the situation-model side of the line.** Garvey & Caramazza (1974 [SECONDARY]) and Ferstl, Garnham & Manouilidou's (2011, *Behav. Res. Methods* [SECONDARY]) 300+-verb norming corpus support a genuine per-verb LEXICAL bias component; Hartshorne & Snedeker (2013, *Lang. Cogn. Processes* [SECONDARY]) show a fine-grained VerbNet-style argument-structure taxonomy predicts bias DIRECTION well — a real chunk of "which argument slot is expected to be the cause" IS compositionally recoverable from lexical/argument structure, not arbitrary memorization. Bott & Solstad's "empty-slot" theory (2014/2021 [SECONDARY]) makes this precise: certain verb classes leave a propositional slot for "the cause" structurally unfilled, creating an EXPECTATION that discourse will supply an Explanation relation — but this is a theory of expectation GENERATION (which slot, which relation-type is expected), not of RESOLUTION (which specific entity/proposition fills it).

Kehler & Rohde's Bayesian formalization (2013, *Theoretical Linguistics*; equation confirmed via Zhan, Levy & Kehler 2020, *PLOS ONE* [PRIMARY, fetched]) makes the resolution step explicit: `P(referent|pronoun) ∝ P(pronoun|referent) x P(referent)`, where the prior `P(referent)` is coherence-relation-CONDITIONED — and WHICH coherence relation holds (Occasion vs. Explanation vs. Elaboration) is itself inferred from event/causal structure, not read off the verb alone. Rohde, Kehler & Elman's earlier work shows the SAME verb reverses its referential bias depending on which coherence relation the discourse establishes — direct evidence the lexical bias alone underdetermines the answer. Hobbs, Stickel, Appelt & Martin's "interpretation as abduction" (1993, *Artificial Intelligence* [SECONDARY, primary text access-limited]) is the maximal version of this claim: reference resolution has no dedicated resolver at all, it falls out of finding the cheapest logical explanation of the whole discourse from general-knowledge axioms.

**For "Cheryl dumped her boyfriend...because HE wouldn't have sex with her" specifically:** two things push this decisively toward situation-model reasoning, not lexical lookup. First, van den Hoven & Ferstl (2018, *Frontiers in Communication* [PRIMARY, fetched]) show that with two equally plausible human referents and no selectional asymmetry, resolution flips entirely on discourse context (a genuinely causal/relational judgment) — lexical cues dominate only the earliest instant before world knowledge revises the interpretation. Second, and specific to this exact construction: "Simon" is introduced in an adjunct prepositional phrase ("following Simon's advice"), not as an argument of "dumped" — a per-verb NP1/NP2 lookup table has NO entry that could even in principle discriminate Simon from "boyfriend," because the table only indexes the verb's OWN arguments. Jones & Bergen (2024, *Language and Cognition* [PRIMARY, fetched]) independently confirm the broader point: holding grammatical role, lexical semantics, and coherence-relation type constant, world/physical-plausibility knowledge STILL independently predicts pronoun choice, and even GPT-3 (a large-scale distributional/lexical model) underperforms humans (73% vs. 85%) and fails to fully absorb the effect — their own conclusion, stated directly, is that interpretation is driven by knowledge about the WORLD, not the WORD.

### Q3 — What did pre-LLM symbolic/knowledge-graph systems achieve on WSC, by what mechanism, and what capped them?

Numbers below are reported WITH their exact dataset, because they are not interchangeable (a persistent confusion in secondary sources) — **WSC-273** (the full official adversarial set) is materially harder than **PDP-60** (the precursor competition set) and **DPR** (Rahman & Ng's own, easier, non-adversarially-curated 1,886-sentence pronoun set).

- **Levesque, Davis & Morgenstern (2011/2012, KR 2012 [PRIMARY-adjacent])** designed WSC explicitly to be **"Google-proof"** — defeat statistical/surface-cue methods specifically. The design does NOT target knowledge-graph-plus-reasoning approaches; those are exactly the tool the challenge was inviting.
- **Rahman & Ng (2012, EMNLP, aclanthology.org/D12-1071 [SECONDARY, primary PDF unreadable, number triangulated across 3 independent sources])**: 73.05% on their OWN DPR test set (n=564) — NOT WSC-273 (which didn't exist yet). Mechanism: Google n-gram/search-count semantic association + Chambers & Jurafsky-style narrative event chains + FrameNet + heuristic/learned polarity + discourse-connective features, combined via an SVM ranker.
- **Peng, Khashabi & Roth (2015, NAACL, aclanthology.org/N15-1082 [SECONDARY, single-source number, lower confidence])**: 76.41% on the SAME DPR set, using unsupervised "Predicate Schemas" (world-knowledge patterns mined from unstructured text, not a fixed KB) compiled into ILP constraints. No WSC-273 number found for this system.
- **Sharma, Vo, Baral et al. (arXiv:1907.11112 [PRIMARY, ar5iv text rendered])** — Answer Set Programming (ASP) over hand-built knowledge graphs, on a 291-item WSC pool (elsewhere cited inconsistently as "WSC285" — a real, unresolved dataset-versioning discrepancy in the literature, not a research error here): **82.47%** (240/291) with HUMAN-BUILT knowledge representations per item; only 82.98% of THAT subset survived automatic syntactic parsing (200/240); a FULLY AUTOMATIC pipeline (search-engine knowledge hunting + parsing + ASP) found usable knowledge for only 120/291 problems (coverage collapsed to ~41%), though precision stayed high (120/120) on the items it attempted. **The paper's own error analysis on the 51 unsolved hand-buildable cases is the single most load-bearing number for this drill's Q5**: 26/51 needed combining MULTIPLE knowledge facts (beyond their graph/subgraph-isomorphism matcher's capability), and 25/51 needed PROBABILISTIC/likelihood comparison ("X is more likely true than Y") — something their categorical ASP encoding cannot express AT ALL. This is a genuine, paper-documented REASONING-EXPRESSIVENESS ceiling, separate from knowledge coverage.
- **Emami et al. (2018, EMNLP, aclanthology.org/D18-1220 [SECONDARY, abstract-level])**: **57.1% on the FULL official WSC-273** — the first rule-based (non-neural-LM) system to beat chance on the complete adversarial set. Mechanism is LIVE web search at inference time ("knowledge hunting"), explicitly NOT a static offline knowledge graph — the authors attribute their gain to knowledge-retrieval QUALITY, not reasoning sophistication.
- **PDP-60 (the official IJCAI-16 competition set)**: best 2016 score 58% (Quan Liu et al.); no system cleared the 90% prize threshold (Davis, Morgenstern & Ortiz 2017, *AI Magazine* [PRIMARY-ish, partial render]).
- **The field's own retrospective** (Kocijan, Davis, Lukasiewicz, Marcus & Morgenstern 2022, arXiv:2201.02387, co-authored by original organizer Ernest Davis [SECONDARY survey]) locates the bottleneck at **knowledge extraction/acquisition, not the reasoning layer**: "the extraction of relevant information from the sentence is usually the bottleneck... even the slightest noise in the feature collection can make the problem unsolvable," and states these approaches were largely abandoned "due to the difficulties of developing such systems at scale" — a coverage/scalability verdict. Sharma's own paper is the one place a pure reasoning-mechanism limit is cleanly isolated (multi-fact combination + probabilistic comparison), independent of coverage.

**No source found reports a purely STATIC-KG (ConceptNet/WordNet-only, no live search, no per-item hand-built representation), fully-automatic system evaluated on the complete official WSC-273.** The two "high" numbers (82-84%) required either hand-built per-item knowledge representations or accepted a ~59% coverage collapse under full automation; the one fully-automatic full-set rule-based number (57.1%) used live search, which is explicitly NOT the "static offline-built knowledge asset" this project's no-LLM invariant admits. **This is the honest ceiling bound: a purely-static-KG, fully-automatic, non-neural-LM system's demonstrated ceiling on the hardest official set is unmeasured in the literature searched — the closest comparable numbers (57-58%) come from either live search or an easier precursor set, and the closest STATIC-knowledge number (82.47%) required manual per-item knowledge construction that does not scale to an arbitrary novel/document.**

### Q4 — Is a structured KG a faithful model of the brain's semantic-memory contribution, or is the brain fundamentally distributional?

**The brain's mechanism is distributional/graded, not symbolic — this is not contested in the literature reviewed.** The ATL "semantic hub" is explicitly implemented and empirically evidenced as a PARALLEL-DISTRIBUTED-PROCESSING network (Rogers, Lambon Ralph, Garrard, Bozeat, McClelland, Hodges & Patterson 2004, *Psych. Review* [SECONDARY] — McClelland is a shared author with the broader Rogers & McClelland PDP semantic-cognition program the task named directly), with semantic-dementia patient data showing GRADED, cross-category, cross-modal concept degradation as ATL tissue is lost — exactly the signature a distributed representation predicts and a discrete symbolic store does not (a symbolic KG would predict clean, all-or-nothing loss of specific facts/edges, not graded blurring of category boundaries). McRae/Ferretti-style thematic fit is likewise explicitly a CONTINUOUS, corpus/norming-derived quantity, not a binary constraint-satisfaction check.

**So a symbolic KG (ConceptNet/CSKG) is NOT a faithful implementation-level model of the brain's mechanism — but it can be a legitimate, coarser COMPUTATIONAL-LEVEL approximation**, in the same sense that McRae's own norming-corpus cloze scores (themselves a discretized, sampled proxy for a continuous underlying distribution) are successfully used as inputs to psycholinguistic models without claiming to BE the neural representation. The substrate's own prior work already validates this exact move empirically: a coarse 12-dim grounded space (itself a discretized approximation, not a faithful ATL replica) correctly separated concrete object-vs-object selectional pairs 8/8 in the positive control — i.e., a coarse discretization of the right computational-level idea DOES carry usable signal for the cases it's suited to (concrete, category-separable referents), and DOES NOT for the cases it isn't (person-vs-person, where the graded features collapse to near-identical). This is consistent with, not contradicted by, the brain being "really" distributional: a discretized KG-based selectional score should be expected to work on roughly the SAME sub-population the brain's fast coarse-grained ATL signal works on (Warren & McConnell's dissociation, Q1 above), and fail on the same finer-grained cases the brain needs its SLOWER, richer, situation-model machinery for.

### Q5 — Bottom line

**Not one answer — the residual splits into two mechanistically different slices, and the literature gives a different, calibrated verdict for each:**

1. **Object/concrete-noun selectional-plausibility slice (e.g., parson-vs-mare): a glass-box, no-LLM mechanism plausibly recovers a MEASURABLE fraction.** The brain's own fast, coarse, immediate selectional-plausibility signal (McRae/Ferretti thematic fit, ATL-hub-grounded, N400 evidence for immediacy) is exactly the mechanism class this case needs, and the substrate's own positive control already proves a coarse discretized approximation of it works on this case-type (8/8). **Mechanism to build:** a ConceptNet/CSKG-sourced selectional-plausibility feature (edge-weighted plausibility of `verb x candidate`), fused via the SAME Bayesian-product architecture already built for the (refuted) coherence prior — reuse the fusion machinery, replace the feature source with a richer, KG-backed one. P_deflated = 0.40 (Prediction A above); measure the channel's ORACLE first before building the full fusion, per the standing "ask if it can succeed before building" discipline that already saved wasted effort once this cycle (the coherence-prior's near-chance oracle).

2. **Person-vs-person, discourse-relation-driven slice (e.g., Cheryl/boyfriend/Simon): the PRE-LLM literature's own ceiling is a real, near-term practical bound for a STATIC-KG-only, single-hop system — but not a proven LOGICAL bound.** No pre-LLM system achieved strong accuracy on this class fully automatically with only a static offline knowledge asset; the two routes that reached ~75-84% either used live search (not admissible under a static-asset invariant) or required hand-built per-item knowledge (does not scale). The field's own error analysis (Sharma et al.) pins the remaining gap on two separable causes: **knowledge coverage** (a 1.21M-edge ConceptNet/CSKG general-commonsense graph is known to be weak on exactly the specific interpersonal-behavior/narrative facts this case needs — "not wanting sex is a reason to be dumped" is not a typical ConceptNet edge, though CSKG's ATOMIC-derived component, which encodes event-centered social "if-then" commonsense, is the one sub-resource worth checking specifically before concluding coverage is hopeless) and **reasoning expressiveness** (single-edge/single-hop KG lookup structurally cannot do the multi-fact-combination and likelihood-weighted-comparison that both Hobbs' abduction and Kehler & Rohde's Bayesian formalization say the computation actually requires). **The specific reason for the ceiling, named precisely: it is not that the brain's mechanism is unreplicable in principle (Sharma's ASP system proves symbolic multi-fact reasoning CAN solve some of these when given the right facts) — it is that (a) our specific static KG likely lacks the fine-grained social/behavioral facts this slice needs, and (b) a plausibility computation that is single-hop/single-fact (which is what the substrate's already-refuted coherence prior effectively was) cannot express the multi-fact, likelihood-weighted comparison the literature says this case-type requires.** A genuinely new, small reasoning primitive (a depth-2/3 weighted-path comparator over CSKG, testable cheaply and first on CONSTRUCTED minimal pairs per Test 2 above, never directly on the real residual until the constructed-pair positive control passes) is the correctly-scoped next experiment — not a claim this slice is unrecoverable, and not a claim it will clearly succeed either. P_deflated = 0.35 (Prediction C), explicitly capped below 0.50 because the strongest available precedent (Sharma et al.) shows this exact reasoning class was disproportionately represented among THEIR unsolved cases even with correct hand-built facts.

## (e) Substrate-product implications

1. **Two separate, small builds, not one unified "world-knowledge module."** The object/concrete-noun slice (Test 1, KG-selectional-plausibility) and the person-vs-person slice (Test 2, multi-hop weighted comparison) are different mechanisms with different literature-supported ceilings; conflating them into one build (as the refuted coherence-prior attempt implicitly did, fusing selectional and thematic channels into one score) risks repeating the earlier negative for the reason already diagnosed there (a single fused score cannot separately gate two different-difficulty sub-populations).
2. **Measure the oracle before building the fusion, both times** — this is now a twice-validated discipline this cycle (it caught the coherence-prior's 1.5-2.9% oracle before a wasted full build); apply it identically to the KG-selectional channel (Test 1) before investing in the fusion architecture.
3. **CSKG's ATOMIC-derived component is worth a targeted coverage check before writing off the person-vs-person slice** — ATOMIC specifically encodes event-centered social/interpersonal "if-then" commonsense (e.g., "X wants sex" -> plausible effects on relationship state), which is a closer match to the Cheryl-style case than ConceptNet's more taxonomic/property-based edges. This is a cheap, disk-only lookup check (query CSKG for coverage of a handful of constructed relationship-behavior facts) that should run BEFORE building Test 2's comparator, since a coverage failure there would sink the mechanism regardless of reasoning-depth improvements.
4. **The multi-hop weighted comparator (Test 2) is a genuinely new reasoning primitive, not a re-parametrization of the existing (refuted) coherence-prior fusion** — it needs path search over the KG and a cost/likelihood comparison between competing explanation chains (a simplified Hobbs-style weighted abduction), which is architecturally different from a single Bayesian-product score. 5. **Do not expect either build to close the full residual.** Even the pre-LLM literature's best fully-automatic, full-set numbers (57-58%) are well below human ceiling (~100% by WSC design) and below the substrate's own existing 0.775 baseline — these builds are targeted, bounded levers on SPECIFIC sub-slices of the remaining ~19-22%, not a path to closing it entirely.
