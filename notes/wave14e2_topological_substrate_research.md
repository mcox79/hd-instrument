# Wave 14e Iteration 2 — Topological Invariants for Categorical Noise Immunity in BSC Substrates

Date: 2026-05-19
Substrate context: N=4096 BSC bipolar atoms (each dim in {+1,-1}). Bundling = sign(sum). Binding = element-wise product. Decode via resonator or argmax-dot. Goal: replace *statistical* noise margin ("bigger N → smaller error") with *categorical* noise margin (an integer invariant that small perturbations literally cannot change).

This iteration goes deeper than Wave14e first-pass. First pass identified the longshot ("topological invariants exist; maybe they map"). This pass asks: *which exact construction is implementable on bipolar ±1 atoms with no other machinery, and what does its minimal test look like?*

---

## TL;DR — single most feasible construction

**Build a chiral-symmetric SSH-like 1D substrate from a pair of BSC atoms (`a_A`, `a_B`) representing the two sublattices, with a binary "hopping pattern" word `h ∈ {+1,-1}^N` whose sign-flip pattern defines a Z winding number. The bundle `b = sign(a_A + h ⊙ a_B)` then has a *parity invariant* — the count of sign-domain-walls in `h` mod 2 — that survives any perturbation that does not close the local "gap" (does not flip a domain-wall bit and its symmetric partner simultaneously).**

Concretely:
- Two random atoms `a_A, a_B ∈ {+1,-1}^N`.
- A "modulation pattern" `h ∈ {+1,-1}^N`. Define winding ν = (number of i where h[i] ≠ h[(i+1) mod N]) / 2.
- A *topologically distinct* family is generated by re-shifting `h` to change ν (ν=0: all +1; ν=1: one domain wall; ν=k: k separated walls).
- Encode a "fact" as the bundle `b_ν = sign(a_A + h_ν ⊙ a_B)` plus a "boundary atom" `z_ν` carrying the bulk-boundary witness.
- Decode the winding by counting domain walls in the recovered `h` via convolution with the shift atom. This is **integer-valued**, **bounded by N/2**, and changes by ≥1 only when adjacent bits both flip *with opposite signs* — a measure-zero event under independent Bernoulli noise.

Why this is the simplest viable Z-invariant in BSC: chiral symmetry collapses to "sublattice labelling" (cheap on bipolar), the winding number reduces to *count of bit-pair sign disagreements* (cheap to compute), and bulk-boundary correspondence guarantees a robust witness in a single auxiliary atom.

**Minimal viable test (one afternoon):**
1. Generate two families of bundles for the same payload, one with ν=0 and one with ν=1.
2. Apply Bernoulli(p) bit-flip noise for p ∈ {0.01, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3}.
3. Measure: (a) probability the bundle's payload decodes correctly (continuous), (b) probability the recovered winding number ν is correct (integer).
4. Predicted result: (a) degrades smoothly with p; (b) stays at 1.0 for all p < p_c ≈ 0.25 then drops sharply — the **categorical signature**.

## 1. Topological insulator math primer, applied to a BSC substrate

### 1.1 The core idea in 60 seconds
A *topological invariant* is an integer-valued functional on the parameter space of a Hamiltonian (or more generally, a state-family) that cannot change under continuous deformations that do not close a "gap". Familiar examples:
- Winding number ν ∈ Z of a closed curve in the punctured plane.
- Chern number C ∈ Z of a U(1) line-bundle over a torus (Brillouin zone).
- Z₂ invariant of a time-reversal-symmetric band structure (Kane-Mele 2005).

The protection theorem: if the family of states `|ψ(θ)⟩` is parameterized by θ ∈ S¹ (or T², or any closed manifold) and the spectral gap stays open, then the integer invariant `I[|ψ⟩]` is *literally invariant*. Noise that does not close the gap cannot change `I`. This is the source of `R_K = h/e²` being good to 10⁻⁹ in real materials.

### 1.2 The substrate analogue we want
Replace "Hamiltonian" with "BSC state-family". Replace "Brillouin zone parameter" with a parameter that families of atoms naturally carry. Replace "gap" with "decoding margin" (sign-overlap distance to nearest competitor).

Three natural parameters present in the substrate:
1. **Site index** i ∈ Z_N — the index along the N=4096 hypervector.
2. **Role index** k ∈ {0,...,K-1} — which slot a bound atom occupies in a structured bundle.
3. **Time/training step** t — the trajectory of a Hebbian update.

(1) maps to the SSH model's spatial index. (2) maps to a unitary-orbit parameter (like the Brillouin zone for FHRR roles-as-phase-ramps). (3) maps to Floquet (periodically-driven) topological insulators.

### 1.3 Kane-Mele Z₂ and why it does *not* directly apply
Kane & Mele 2005 (cond-mat/0411737, the precursor to the QSH paper, and cond-mat/0506581 for the Z₂) defined a Z₂ invariant for *time-reversal-symmetric* 2D Hamiltonians. The construction requires:
- A 2D Brillouin zone (a torus T²).
- A time-reversal operator Θ with Θ² = -1 (anti-unitary, fermionic).
- A pair of Kramers-degenerate bands.

The substrate has none of these. ±1 atoms have no anti-unitary structure; they live in (Z₂)^N, a discrete abelian group. There's no Kramers degeneracy unless we *construct* it (pair two atoms and impose a swap symmetry). **Honest verdict: Kane-Mele's Z₂ is the wrong primitive for BSC. The right primitives are the simpler 1D winding number (SSH) and the chiral-symmetry-protected zero modes.**

### 1.4 Hasan-Kane 2010 review classification, mapped
The 10-fold Altland-Zirnbauer classification (reviewed in Hasan & Kane 2010) tabulates topological invariants by *symmetry class*. The relevant rows for BSC:
- **Class A (no symmetry)**: invariants only in even dimension. *Useless for 1D BSC.*
- **Class AIII (chiral symmetry only)**: Z invariant in 1D. **This is the one.** Chiral symmetry on bipolar atoms = a sublattice labelling that anti-commutes with the "Hamiltonian" (= bundling kernel).
- **Class D (particle-hole)**: Z₂ in 1D (Kitaev chain). Requires a notion of "fermion parity" — synthesizable as a parity-of-sum bit, but no obvious advantage over class AIII.

**Decision: target class AIII in 1D**. This is exactly the SSH model and its generalisations, and the Z invariant is the simplest topological winding number that exists.

## 2. Chern number construction for the substrate

### 2.1 Why a literal Chern number is hard
A Chern number C = (1/2π) ∫_BZ Ω d²k requires:
- A continuous parameter manifold (the Brillouin zone, T²).
- A line bundle (an assignment of a 1D subspace at each parameter point).
- A connection (Berry connection) on that bundle.

BSC atoms live in a *discrete* space. There is no continuous parameter. Even with K stored atoms there's only a finite discrete set.

### 2.2 The Fukui-Hatsugai-Suzuki workaround
Fukui-Hatsugai-Suzuki 2005 ("Chern Numbers in Discretized Brillouin Zone") gave a *manifestly integer* discretization. On a discrete lattice of parameter values {θ_ij}, define link variables U_x(θ) = ⟨ψ(θ) | ψ(θ+δ_x)⟩ / |...| and the field strength F = Im log(U_x U_y U_x^{-1} U_y^{-1}). The Chern number is C = (1/2πi) Σ F, **manifestly an integer when the discretization is fine enough**.

**Substrate mapping**: pick a 2D grid of "phase atoms" parameterized by (θ_x, θ_y) ∈ {0, 2π/M, ..., 2π(M-1)/M}², where each atom is `a(θ_x, θ_y)[i] = sign(cos(2π i k_x/N + θ_x) + cos(2π i k_y/N + θ_y))` for some integers k_x, k_y. Compute the FHS link variables U_x = ⟨a(θ) | a(θ + δ_x)⟩ / N. If (k_x, k_y) traces a non-trivial cycle, the resulting Berry phase **is integer-quantized** by the FHS theorem and the substrate carries a Chern number C(k_x, k_y) = ?.

In the **continuum limit** (sufficiently many phase samples), this Chern number is exactly `k_x · sign(k_y) - k_y · sign(k_x)` or similar — i.e., a *parameter chosen at construction* that survives bit-flip noise on the realized atoms.

**Honest cost**: this needs O(M²) phase atoms to define one bundle. For M = 16 that's 256 atoms per "Chern-charged" object. Not free, but the integer survival is real.

### 2.3 Cheaper alternative: discrete 1D winding (the SSH route)
The 1D winding number ν of a chiral-symmetric Hamiltonian `h(k) = (h_x(k), h_y(k))` is the number of times (h_x, h_y) winds around the origin as k goes around the Brillouin zone. *On a discrete lattice this is a sum of arctangent differences*, which is again integer-quantized when the gap (h_x² + h_y² > 0) stays open.

In BSC terms: each atom contributes a pair of bits per "k-point" — call them the A-sublattice and B-sublattice bits at site i. Define `h_x(i) = (a_A[i] + h[i]·a_B[i])`, `h_y(i) = (a_A[i] - h[i]·a_B[i])`. The pair (h_x, h_y) is a 2-vector in {-2, 0, 2}². The winding number is the count of (h_x, h_y) excursions around the origin — which on this discretized circle equals **half the number of sign-flips in the modulation pattern h**.

This is the construction I propose in the TL;DR.

## 3. Edge modes and bulk-boundary correspondence

### 3.1 The general theorem
Bulk-boundary correspondence (proved rigorously for SSH by Asbóth-Oroszlány-Pályi 2016 and generalized SSH by Pérez-Pardo 2017, arxiv:1705.06913) states: *the number of robust zero-energy edge modes equals the bulk winding number*. The edge modes survive any local disorder that preserves chiral symmetry and keeps the bulk gap open. This is the most directly transferable topological theorem.

### 3.2 Substrate analogue
Construct two adjacent bundles, b_left with winding ν_L and b_right with winding ν_R. At their "interface" (a boundary atom z), the bulk-boundary correspondence predicts |ν_L - ν_R| robust modes — specifically, basis vectors that *cannot be removed* from the substrate's null space by perturbation.

Concretely: define an "interface" by concatenating the first N/2 bits of b_left with the last N/2 of b_right. Compute the decoder's response on the interface atom — bulk-boundary correspondence predicts a robust |ν_L - ν_R|-dimensional subspace of "edge eigenvectors" of the substrate's decoding kernel localized near the cut.

**Useful application**: a stored fact's *retention guarantee* equals the winding-number difference between the storing region and the surrounding background. Edge modes are robust by topology; ordinary stored facts are robust only by statistical capacity.

### 3.3 The "edge" in BSC space
In a real material, the edge is a spatial boundary. In BSC, the "edge" is wherever an atom-family's parameter (e.g., the modulation pattern h) changes its winding. Since BSC has no spatial boundary in the literal sense, we *engineer* one by stitching two atom-families at a chosen index. This is similar to "domain wall states" in SSH polyacetylene — and these are exactly the soliton states Su-Schrieffer-Heeger originally studied for polyacetylene's mid-gap states (which carry fractional charge ±1/2).

## 4. Quantum Hall effect analog for the substrate

### 4.1 The QHE robustness mechanism
Hall conductance σ_xy = (e²/h) · C with C ∈ Z (TKNN 1982). Robustness: C is the Chern number of the occupied-band bundle, and C cannot change while the spectral gap is open. Therefore σ_xy is exactly quantized; the quantization is good to 10⁻⁹ in metrology.

### 4.2 What's the substrate's "conductance"?
We need a *quantity computable from the substrate state* that's integer-quantized. Candidates:

(a) **Winding-number readout**. Given a bundle b, compute a "winding response" by sweeping a probe atom q through a parameter family q(θ) and integrating the response. If b carries non-trivial winding, the integrated response is an integer (Berry-phase pumping analog).

(b) **Quantized "polarization"**. In the modern theory of polarization (King-Smith-Vanderbilt 1993), bulk polarization P = (1/2π) ∫ A·dk is integer-quantized mod 1 for an inversion-symmetric crystal — the *Zak phase*. For BSC: define the Zak-phase of an atom family parameterized by θ ∈ S¹ as φ_Zak = arg(Π_θ ⟨a(θ)|a(θ+δθ)⟩). This is 0 or π mod 2π for inversion-symmetric families — a **Z₂ invariant directly computable from bipolar atoms**.

(c) **Spectral flow under twist**. Add a "twist" to the substrate (multiply some atoms by a phase ramp) and count the number of decoder eigenvalues that cross zero. This is a Laughlin-style adiabatic argument, giving an integer flow.

**Recommendation**: (b) is the cheapest. The Zak phase of a 1-parameter atom family `a(θ)[i] = sign(cos(2π i k/N + θ))` for θ ∈ [0, 2π) is exactly **πk mod 2π**, an integer multiple of π. This is the BSC analog of polarization quantization.

## 5. The big bet — facts indexed by topological charge

The aspirational endpoint: each stored "fact" carries a topological charge q ∈ Z. The pool's contents become a *direct sum* over charge sectors: P = ⊕_q P_q. Operations are:
- **Storage** in sector q: fixed-cost insertion.
- **Retrieval** within sector q: standard BSC similarity, restricted.
- **Erasure of sector q**: delete the entire q-sector by orthogonal projection. Categorical erasure — no probabilistic re-emergence.

The GDPR-compliance angle is real. If user data lives in a topologically distinct sector from non-user data, a single projection erases it. The substrate cannot "approximately forget" — the integer charge is gone or it is not.

**Engineering plan**:
1. Construct K topologically distinct atom families F_q for q ∈ {0, 1, ..., Q-1}. Each family has its own winding number / Zak phase / Chern number.
2. Each fact is encoded as a bundle whose role atoms are drawn from F_q for the chosen q.
3. The pool is partitioned at decode-time by first measuring topological charge, then doing similarity within the inferred sector.

**Concrete construction for Q = 2** (the GDPR-minimum: user vs non-user): use SSH winding ν ∈ {0, 1}. Two role atoms a_A, a_B. Modulation `h_q` with ν=q sign-flips. Charge measurement: count domain walls in the recovered `h`, mod 2.

**Costs**:
- Two extra atoms per bundle (sublattice labels). 8192 bits per fact in storage, vs 4096 baseline.
- One extra dot product per retrieval to measure charge.
- Sector imbalance: if 99% of facts are q=0, the q=1 sector has poor SNR. Need to allocate atom families to charge sectors proportionally to expected mass.

**Failure modes to test**:
- Sufficient noise (Bernoulli p > 0.25 on bits) closes the BSC gap and flips winding. Verify experimentally: does the gap close gradually or catastrophically?
- Hebbian training may drift the modulation pattern h, destroying chirality. Verify: under our continual-learning regime, does ν remain quantized?

## 6. Concrete construction attempts

### 6.1 The SSH-BSC construction (full recipe)
Setup:
- N=4096, K-fact pool.
- Two "sublattice" atoms `a_A, a_B ∈ {+1,-1}^N`, fixed once at substrate init.
- For each fact f with topological charge q_f ∈ Z, draw a modulation pattern `h_f ∈ {+1,-1}^N` with ν(h_f) = q_f. Generate by starting from all-+1 and inserting `q_f` non-adjacent sign-flips at random positions.

Storage of payload `p_f` with charge `q_f`:
```
key_f = sign(a_A + h_f * a_B)         # sublattice-modulated key
bundle += key_f * p_f
auxiliary_modulation_pool.add(h_f)    # store the modulation pattern
```

Retrieval of payload from query `q`:
```
charge_estimate = measure_winding(unbind(bundle, q))
key_q = sign(a_A + h_q * a_B)         # with h_q from auxiliary pool of matching ν
recovered_p = unbind(bundle, key_q)
```

Winding measurement on a recovered modulation `h̃`:
```
ν̃ = sum(h̃[i] != h̃[(i+1) % N] for i in range(N)) // 2
```
This is an integer in [0, N/2]. Bit-flip noise on a single bit changes ν̃ by at most 1 (it flips two adjacent comparisons). For ν̃ to change by 2+, we need *coordinated* bit-flips at non-adjacent positions of size matching the modulation. Under i.i.d. Bernoulli(p), the probability of such coordinated change scales like p^2 — a categorical jump in the noise budget.

### 6.2 Phase-ramp / Fourier-basis winding (FHRR-style)
On complex-FHRR atoms (unit-modulus N-vectors), define the "k-th winding atom":
```
a_k[i] = exp(2π i k i / N)
```
This is a phase ramp of slope k, with winding number k around the unit circle. Element-wise products preserve winding additively: `a_k * a_l = a_{k+l}`. Bundling does *not* preserve winding (bundling adds phasors, and the winding of a sum of phasors is not the sum of windings).

**Honest constraint**: this works beautifully in FHRR but **doesn't survive the projection to BSC** (sign of real part). The winding number of `sign(real(a_k))` is *no longer* k after the sign quantization — it's a noisy integer-valued function of k whose expectation is k for small k/N but degrades.

This route is more natural for FHRR than for pure BSC. If we are willing to maintain a complex-valued shadow of the BSC substrate (compute in complex, project to ±1 only at retrieval), we get the full FHRR winding mechanism for free.

### 6.3 Quaternion-bound atoms (Plate HRR's SO(3))
Plate 1995 noted that HRR can use *quaternion-valued atoms* to encode SO(3) rotations, where binding is non-commutative quaternion multiplication. The relevant topological invariant is the Hopf map S³ → S² and its winding (the "Hopf invariant"). For BSC this is **2 bits per slot**: a quaternion has 4 components, each could be sign-quantized.

This direction is interesting because it gives a *non-commutative* binding, which the project's two-bets framework explicitly wants (current FHRR is commutative). The cost: 4x storage per atom. The win: SO(3) rotations are intrinsically 3D, and the Hopf invariant gives a *Z-valued* topological charge per atom.

**Status: worth a separate Wave 14f investigation. Not the minimal viable test.**

### 6.4 Floquet (temporal binding)
Floquet topological insulators (e.g., observed by Rudner-Lindner 2013 and subsequent works) realize non-trivial topology *via the time evolution operator*, not the instantaneous Hamiltonian. The invariant is a winding of U(T) over one period. **Substrate analog**: a Hebbian update rule applied periodically (e.g., a fixed sequence of bind/unbind operations) defines an effective "Floquet operator" U whose winding could be non-trivial.

This is the most ambitious and the most directly relevant to the *big bet* (Hebbian-trained VSA-LM). The Floquet picture lets us define topological charge of a *training trajectory* — different trajectories implement different unitary topological classes.

**Status: speculation. Tractable iff the Hebbian update has a clean periodic structure, which it currently doesn't.**

### 6.5 2D Berry curvature via tensor-product atoms
Use a *pair* of phase parameters (θ_x, θ_y) and assign atoms a(θ_x, θ_y). The Berry curvature Ω = ∂_x A_y - ∂_y A_x is computable via FHS, and ∫Ω = 2πC for C ∈ Z. The construction in §2.2 above. Concrete cost: M² atoms per Chern-sector, scaling fast. Useful only if we genuinely want a 2D parameter (e.g., for 2D image patches).

## 7. Minimal viable test design

Goal: demonstrate that **for one substrate construction (SSH-BSC of §6.1), an integer-valued readout exists that's invariant under bit-flip noise up to a sharp threshold p_c, in contrast to the smooth degradation of payload accuracy**.

### Setup
- N = 4096. Two fixed sublattice atoms a_A, a_B.
- Two test charge sectors: q ∈ {0, 1}. Generate 100 facts per sector.
- Each fact: random payload p_f ∈ {+1,-1}^N, random h_f with ν(h_f) = q_f.
- Bundle B = Σ_f key_f * p_f using the SSH-key construction.

### Trials
For p ∈ {0, 0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.1, 0.15, 0.2, 0.25, 0.3, 0.4}:
  Repeat 1000 times:
    Sample bit-flip noise mask m_i ~ Bernoulli(p) independently per bit.
    B' = B * (1 - 2m) (flipped where m=1).
    For each fact f:
      Unbind: candidate_modulation = unbind(B', payload_f * a_A_assumed)
      Measure ν̃ = winding_count(candidate_modulation)
      Record (true_ν, recovered_ν, payload_accuracy)

### Predictions
1. **Payload accuracy** vs p: smooth sigmoid degradation, hitting 50% at p ≈ 0.5 (standard BSC).
2. **Winding accuracy** vs p: a *plateau* at 100% for p < p_c followed by an avalanche to chance-level. p_c should be in the range 0.15–0.30 (we need at least one calculation to predict it exactly; back-of-envelope: p_c ≈ 1/(2ν_max) where ν_max is the typical winding density).
3. **Categorical signature**: the plot of P(correct ν) vs p has a *visible kink* near p_c. The plot of P(correct payload) vs p has no kink.

### Falsification
If P(correct ν) vs p is also smooth (no kink), the construction does not deliver categorical protection in BSC. This would mean BSC's projection-to-±1 destroys topological quantization at exactly the wrong step. We'd fall back to FHRR or complex-shadow substrate.

### Cost
≈ 1000 trials × 100 facts × 10 noise levels × N=4096 = 4 × 10⁹ bit operations. Order of seconds on GPU. Should land in an afternoon.

## 8. Anyon braiding angle

### 8.1 The exchange-statistics setup
For point particles in 2D, the configuration space has nontrivial π_1 (the braid group B_n, not the symmetric group S_n). Wavefunctions can pick up an arbitrary phase or even a non-trivial unitary under exchange. Abelian anyons: phase e^{iπα}. Non-abelian anyons: unitary matrix acting on a degenerate state space.

### 8.2 VSA analog
- **FHRR binding is commutative**: a ⊙ b = b ⊙ a. This is "abelian, α=0" — bosons.
- **Quaternion binding is non-commutative**: pq ≠ qp in general. This is the simplest non-abelian binding — but the exchange relation pq = sgn·qp doesn't have the rich braid-group structure.
- **Resonator network binding**: explicitly sequential — the order of factor-extraction matters. This is closer to braid-group action, but the "braid" is on the iteration order rather than spatial paths.

A *true* anyon-like VSA would need:
- A bind operation `⊙` such that `(a ⊙ b) ⊙ c ≠ a ⊙ (b ⊙ c)` and the difference encodes a non-trivial topology — i.e., the bind operation is **non-associative**, not merely non-commutative.
- Computable braid generators that map onto unitary gates on the substrate.

Frady & Sommer's 2018 "Robust computation with rhythmic spike patterns" is the closest existing work, but it uses periodic phasor encodings (which are abelian). The non-abelian extension to braids is, to my knowledge, **open in the VSA literature**.

### 8.3 Practical implication for the project
Anyons are over the horizon for this project. They suggest a research direction (non-associative binding for topological protection), but the immediate priority is the SSH-BSC Z-winding construction.

## 9. Honest constraints

What does NOT work, and why:

1. **Pure Kane-Mele Z₂ on BSC.** Requires anti-unitary time-reversal symmetry and Kramers pairs. ±1 atoms don't carry this structure without a heavy artificial construction.

2. **Continuous Brillouin zone integrals.** No continuous parameter is built into BSC. We need *discretized* topological invariants (Fukui-Hatsugai), and even those require a substantial number of phase samples per Chern-charged object.

3. **2D and higher invariants.** A 2D Chern number wants a 2-torus parameter space. We can construct one, but the storage cost is O(M²) atoms per topological object. For 1D facts this is wasteful.

4. **Quantization is gap-dependent.** Every topological invariant is protected only *if the gap doesn't close*. For BSC the "gap" is the decoding margin (similarity to nearest competitor). A heavily loaded pool has small gap → easy to close → topology offers no protection. So the topological-protection regime is exactly the low-load regime where statistical protection is also strong. **The marginal value of topological protection is largest in the medium-load regime where the gap is finite but small.** This is the interesting regime to test.

5. **Topology protects against perturbations, not against design errors.** If we *construct* an atom-family wrong (e.g., assign winding 0 to a fact we wanted to be winding 1), no amount of topology recovers it. Topology is a *passive* guard, not an *active* error corrector.

6. **Hebbian training can break topology.** A Hebbian rule that updates a_A, a_B in response to inputs can drift the sublattice structure. The chiral symmetry must be *imposed as a constraint* on Hebbian updates — e.g., only update on the symmetric combination `(δa_A + δa_B)/2` and `(δa_A - δa_B)/2` separately. This is an additional design constraint on the big-bet's training rule.

7. **FHRR-style phase windings don't survive BSC projection cleanly.** §6.2. If we want phase-based windings, we need a complex-valued shadow substrate.

8. **Non-abelian / anyon binding is research-grade.** No production-ready construction exists for VSA. Worth a Wave 14f, not Wave 14e2.

## 9.5 Compatibility with Hebbian training (the big bet)

The big bet (Hebbian-trained VSA-LM) requires that the topological structure *survive learning*, not just be present at init. The key compatibility questions:

### 9.5.1 Does Hebbian update preserve chiral symmetry?
Chiral symmetry C requires: the bundling/binding kernel `K` anticommutes with a sublattice operator `Γ`, i.e. `Γ K Γ = -K`. For our SSH-BSC construction, Γ swaps `a_A ↔ a_B`. A standard Hebbian rule `δW ∝ x ⊗ y` for input-output pair (x, y) breaks chiral symmetry generically because it doesn't respect the sublattice block structure.

**Constraint**: project Hebbian updates onto the chirally-symmetric subspace. Decompose any update δa = δa^+ + δa^- where δa^± are the symmetric/antisymmetric parts under sublattice swap. Apply only the chiral-respecting piece. This costs a 2x slow-down but preserves the topological structure.

### 9.5.2 Does winding number change under Hebbian dynamics?
The winding ν is a *topological* property of the modulation pattern h. If h is *fixed at init* and only payloads p_f are learned, ν cannot change. If h is *also learned*, then ν can change — but only via a gap-closing event (a moment when the decoding margin vanishes). This is exactly the SSH topological phase transition.

**Plan**: keep modulation patterns h_q frozen post-init. Learn only payloads. This decouples topology from learning and preserves categorical protection unconditionally.

### 9.5.3 Catastrophic forgetting and topology
The conventional CL/replay narrative is that information leaks between tasks because they share representational substrate. With topological sectoring (§5), task-specific facts live in distinct charge sectors and *cannot* leak by any continuous mechanism — only by deliberate cross-sector binding. This is a strong, structural form of task separation that is orthogonal to (and stackable with) the existing replay-based CL machinery.

**Caveat**: requires that the task-classifier (deciding which sector a fact belongs to) is itself reliable. If the classifier mis-routes, the topological guarantee doesn't help. So the topological mechanism shifts the failure mode from "leaky shared substrate" to "wrong sector assignment" — a more localized and inspectable error.

## 9.6 Predictions worth pre-registering before running

If we run the §7 minimal viable test, the following should be pre-registered to avoid post-hoc rationalization:

1. **P(correct ν | p) has a kink**. Operationalize the kink as: the second derivative of P(correct ν) vs p, evaluated at the inflection point, exceeds twice the maximum |d²/dp² P(correct payload)| over the same range. A clean kink should be ≥10× sharper, not 2×; we set the bar low to avoid false-negative.

2. **The critical p_c is in [0.10, 0.30]**. Below 0.10 means topology is too fragile to be useful; above 0.30 means the topology dominates so much that the prediction is suspicious (overshoot of theory).

3. **Two distinct winding sectors are distinguishable post-noise**: P(correct sector classification) > 0.95 for p ≤ 0.20.

4. **Bulk-boundary witness**: at engineered interfaces between ν=0 and ν=1 regions, there exists at least one identifiable "edge atom" with anomalously high stability (sign-flip rate ≤ half the bulk rate). This is the substrate analog of the SSH edge state at a domain wall.

5. **Negative result clauses**:
   - If P(correct ν) is smooth (no kink within 2×), conclude: BSC's bit-quantization destroys topological protection at the projection step. Switch to complex-shadow substrate.
   - If sector classification fails (<0.80 even at p=0), conclude: the modulation patterns are too similar to distinguish; need higher winding-density contrast (e.g., ν=0 vs ν=8 instead of ν=0 vs ν=1).

## Single most viable construction — 250-word summary

The SSH-BSC chiral construction is the only proposal in this iteration that fits all four constraints simultaneously: implementable on pure bipolar atoms with no continuous parameters, delivers a manifestly integer-valued invariant, has a closed-form prediction for the noise threshold, and admits an afternoon-scale falsification test.

Recipe: fix two sublattice atoms `a_A, a_B ∈ {+1,-1}^N`. For each fact carrying topological charge q (target 2 sectors for the GDPR-erasure use case), generate a modulation pattern `h_q ∈ {+1,-1}^N` with q domain walls. Encode keys as `key = sign(a_A + h_q ⊙ a_B)`. The fact's *topological charge* equals half the count of sign-disagreement-pairs in the recovered modulation, an integer in [0, N/2].

Categorical protection arises because i.i.d. Bernoulli(p) bit-flip noise can shift the recovered domain-wall count by ±1 only at sites adjacent to an existing wall, and shifting it by ≥2 requires coordinated multi-bit flips of vanishing probability. The protection holds until p_c ≈ 1/(2ν_density), after which the gap closes and the integer collapses.

Bulk-boundary correspondence gives a free witness: at an interface between two sectors, |ν_L − ν_R| robust modes appear that can be used as substrate-native erasure handles.

The minimal test compares payload-decoding accuracy (a continuous metric) against winding-readout accuracy (an integer metric) under varying bit-flip noise. Topological protection predicts a *kink* in the winding curve and no kink in the payload curve — a directly falsifiable categorical signature.

Honest caveat: every topological invariant requires the spectral gap to stay open. In BSC this is the decoding margin. Topology protects best at medium pool load, exactly where statistical protection is weakest — which is where the marginal value of this approach is largest.
