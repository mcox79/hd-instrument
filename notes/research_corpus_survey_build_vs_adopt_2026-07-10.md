# Research: Public corpus/KG survey -- BUILD-OWN vs ADOPT vs COMPOSE for substrate ingest

Self-executed (no sub-agent fan-out per explicit dispatch constraint). WebSearch/WebFetch only, generic public-domain KG-benchmark terms (no substrate-novel framing off-platform).

## HEADLINE

1. **No single off-the-shelf public corpus clears all 6 of our must-haves.** Every candidate that is dense-enough-to-be-inferable is symbol-only (no measured attributes); every candidate that carries real measured/perceptual attributes is either too sparse, too fragmented (per-image, not global), or not canonicalized into a shared entity vocabulary.
2. **The single best "closest to directly usable" pick is CoDEx (M or L)** -- Wikidata-sourced, purpose-built to fix the FB15k/WN18-style leakage problem, genuinely cross-cutting relation vocabulary, free/CC-licensed -- but its avg degree (~14-22) sits BELOW or AT our established ~37 floor, not clearly above it, so it needs a k-core densification pass before it clears criterion 1.
3. **Verdict: COMPOSE, not pure ADOPT, and not build-fully-from-scratch either.** Take a k-core-filtered dense relational spine (CoDEx-L/M densified, or FB15k-237 as a calibration reference) + graft a grounding layer from Wikidata quantity properties / WebChild sensory-relation triples (NOT built from scratch -- WebChild already ties perceptual attributes to WordNet senses, which overlaps our existing vocabulary) + reuse CoDEx's engineered low-leakage split methodology for the held-out eval. This saves real weeks (no need to re-derive a leakage-safe split or hand-curate cross-domain relations) but does NOT let us skip the k-core filtering + grounding-graft work, which is genuinely substrate-specific.

## Top-3 ranked by fit to our needs

1. **CoDEx-M / CoDEx-L (Wikidata-sourced, low-leakage-engineered).** Best joint score on criteria 1 (near-floor, fixable via k-core), 2 (broad cross-domain relations), 3 (best-in-class leakage engineering, purpose-built to fix exactly the FB15k/WN18 problem our context flagged), 5, and 6. Weakest on 4 (only text descriptions, no measured attributes) -- but that gap is EXACTLY what our existing MM grounding work (concreteness/sensory norms) already targets, so it composes rather than needing to be solved twice.
2. **Hetionet.** Best pure-density (avg-deg ~48, clearly above floor) + best cross-cutting functional/causal relation vocabulary of any candidate -- but narrow biomedical domain only (not general commonsense) and no shipped low-leakage split (would have to build one, partially undoing the "adopt" savings). Valuable primarily as a DESIGN REFERENCE for what a dense, non-taxonomy relation vocabulary should look like, and as a literal ingest option IF we want a biomedical-domain proof-of-concept of "dense inference finally works at avg-deg ~48" before committing resources to densifying a general-domain graph.
3. **FB15k-237.** The exact benchmark our density-floor number (37.4) was calibrated against, purpose-built low-leakage, genuinely cross-domain relations -- but tiny (14.5k entities) and Freebase-derived (frozen, English-only, no attributes). Best used as a CALIBRATION/VALIDATION set (confirm our own densified graph, at matched avg-degree, reproduces FB15k-237-like inferability) rather than as the production ingest corpus itself.

**Single best "directly usable" pick if forced to choose one:** CoDEx-M. It is the only candidate that is simultaneously (a) general-domain (not biomedical-only), (b) purpose-engineered against the exact leakage failure mode already identified in our own prior work, (c) freely available with a shipped train/valid/test split, and (d) large enough to be a real ingest candidate rather than a toy. Its density gap (avg-deg ~22 vs ~37 floor) is a real, honest shortfall -- not disqualifying, but real.

## The COMPOSE option (concrete)

The ideal corpus for us does not exist off-the-shelf; it is a composition of three parts, each independently available:

1. **Relational spine:** CoDEx-L (77,951 entities, 69 relations) or CoDEx-M, k-core filtered (take the k-core with avg-degree pushed from ~22 toward >=37 -- this trims the graph to its densest connected sub-region, exactly the operation our own ConceptNet-slice analysis already showed matters: "no dense k-core, max 5-core avg-deg ~11" was the diagnosed failure). CoDEx's engineered low-leakage split (train/valid/test + hard negatives) is REUSED, not rebuilt -- this is the direct multi-week saving.
2. **Grounding graft:** For entities in the CoDEx spine that resolve to Wikidata QIDs (CoDEx is Wikidata-derived, so this is a near-native join, not a fuzzy cross-source match), pull Wikidata's quantity/typed-literal properties (population, elevation, mass, dates, coordinates) for genuine MEASURED numeric grounding. Where entities map to WordNet senses (via existing ConceptNet/ CoDEx-Wikidata-WordNet overlap), graft WebChild's perceptual-attribute triples (hasShape/hasTaste/evokesEmotion, >80% reported accuracy) for genuine SENSORY grounding. This directly answers "grounding doesn't chain to relational inference" by attaching grounding AT THE SAME NODES that carry the dense relational structure, rather than as a disconnected parallel MM as today.
3. **Cross-cutting relation-vocabulary donor (optional, if CoDEx's 69 Wikidata relations are judged insufficiently causal/functional after audit):** ATOMIC2020's 23 relations (causal/social/physical) or ASER's 15 discourse relations (causal/temporal) as a TEMPLATE for what additional relation types to mine/add into the spine -- not directly mergeable (different entity vocabularies), but a concrete existence-proof of what "cross-cutting, not taxonomy" relations look like in a vetted public resource, useful for auditing whether our own composed spine is taxonomy-heavy again.

This composition is NOT "adopt one thing" and NOT "build everything from scratch" -- it is targeted extraction + join + k-core filtering across 2-3 existing public, freely-licensed resources that already did the hard parts (leakage engineering, perceptual-norm collection, numeric-attribute curation) separately.

## BUILD-OWN vs ADOPT vs COMPOSE verdict

**COMPOSE**, with an honest accounting of what adopting saves and what it doesn't:

- **What ADOPT-of-CoDEx genuinely saves (real, not hand-wavy):** (a) the leakage-engineering work itself -- CoDEx's authors specifically re-derived a hard-negative, type-consistent, low-leakage split from Wikidata, which is a nontrivial multi-week research exercise we would otherwise have to redo from scratch and get right (FB15k/WN18's leakage bug went undetected for years); (b) Wikidata's existing entity resolution (CoDEx entities are already deduplicated Wikidata QIDs, avoiding the "entities fragmented across sources" failure mode flagged in our own criteria); (c) WebChild's existing perceptual-attribute-to-WordNet-sense mapping, which is exactly the "grounding at scale, pre-linked to a symbolic vocabulary we can also use" resource we would otherwise have to build via norm-collection studies.
- **What still requires our own work (the honest gap, not solved by any candidate):** (a) the k-core densification/filtering pass to push any of these graphs comfortably above the ~37 avg-degree floor -- no off-the-shelf resource ships a general-domain graph that clears this bar out of the box; (b) the join/graft step attaching Wikidata-quantity + WebChild-perceptual attributes onto the CoDEx relational spine -- no resource ships this combination pre-built; (c) validating that the composed graph's held-out inference is ACTUALLY latent (not just structurally dense) -- density is necessary but per our own prior finding not sufficient, so the mechanism/decoder side of the problem is untouched by any corpus choice.
- Building the relational spine and leakage-safe split fully from scratch would very likely reproduce CoDEx's own design decisions at real cost (this matches the USER's framing: "building our own can only be as good or better, but worth looking" -- looking confirms that in this specific sub-problem, adopting the leakage-split and entity-resolution work is strictly cheaper than re-deriving it, while the density-filtering and grounding-graft work is substrate-specific and was never going to be off-the-shelf).

## Honest gaps of the best off-the-shelf option (CoDEx-M/L composed)

- Density after k-core filtering is UNVERIFIED to actually clear 37 avg-degree while retaining enough entities to be useful -- k-core filtering shrinks the graph, and CoDEx-L only has 77,951 entities to begin with; an aggressive k-core pass could leave too few nodes to be a meaningful ingest corpus. This is a genuine open empirical question, not assumed to work.
- Wikidata-quantity and WebChild joins will not cover all spine entities -- coverage will be partial (numeric measured attributes exist for a minority of entity types: places, people, some organizations; WebChild covers WordNet-sense-mappable concept entities, not all Wikidata entity types). The grounding graft will be a PARTIAL overlay, not universal.
- No candidate resolves the mechanism-side gap (grounding chaining to relational inference is a decoder/inference-mechanism question, not purely a data-density question) -- this composition changes the INPUT distribution to give any future mechanism a fairer shot, it does not itself constitute the missing mechanism.

## Cross-thread synthesis

Directly extends the 2026-07-10 knowledge-density finding (`notes/research_knowledge_density_information_ceiling_relational_inference_2026-07-10.md`): that note established the ~37 avg-degree floor (FB15k-237) vs our ~2.7-7.7 measured regime (WN18RR-adjacent) as a statistical explanation for flat/negative density experiments. This note asks the natural follow-up -- "given that floor, is there a public corpus already living safely above it that we should adopt instead of manually densifying our own slice" -- and the answer is: not as a single resource, but CoDEx's engineered low-leakage Wikidata subset is the best available STARTING SPINE to densify, and WebChild + Wikidata-quantity are the best available GROUNDING DONORS to graft onto it, both being real time-savers relative to building the leakage-split and perceptual-norm-collection work from scratch.

## Substrate-product implications

If the composed corpus is built and cleared through k-core filtering: the ingest pipeline gets a genuinely denser, more relationally-diverse, better-attributed knowledge substrate without having to invent a leakage-safe evaluation methodology or a perceptual-norm collection study in-house -- both of which are real, multi-week research undertakings that CoDEx and WebChild have already done and published. The product-facing claim this unlocks (if the follow-up density experiment passes) is "the substrate can infer new relations from grounded, cross-domain knowledge," not "the substrate memorized a bigger taxonomy" -- but that claim remains gated on the decoder/mechanism side, which this corpus survey does not touch.

## Cheap decisive test (for the natural follow-up experiment, not run here)

Take CoDEx-M (or L), compute its k-core decomposition, and check: does a k-core exist at k>=18-20 (implying avg-degree >=37-40) that retains >=5,000 entities? HARD-PASS: such a k-core exists with >=5,000 entities -- proceed to grounding-graft + inference test. HARD-FAIL: no such k-core exists (density collapses before reaching the floor at any useful scale) -- CoDEx's own graph is too sparse in its dense core too, and the honest conclusion becomes "no current public general-domain KG has a large enough dense core," strengthening the build-own case specifically for the relational spine (though the grounding-graft lesson from WebChild/Wikidata-quantity still stands independent of that outcome).
