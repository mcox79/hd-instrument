## HEADLINE

**Q1 (competition set):** the evidence rejects both poles. It is not a narrow 4-visible-noun contest, and it is not an undifferentiated open vocabulary. Federmeier (2007) N400-graded within-category-vs-between-category pre-activation is impossible to produce from a 4-item sentence-noun set (the near-miss category-mates, e.g. "pines" vs "palms," are never in the sentence). Kukona et al. (2011) show an entirely unmentioned, off-sentence, role-INAPPROPRIATE concept (a competitor agent-type filler, e.g. "policeman" for "arrest," when the agent role is already filled) is activated nearly as strongly as the true patient target (intercept diff .18 vs .22, difference n.s., t(15)=0.89, p=.39) — direct proof the brain's competition space is not bounded by the sentence's own noun inventory nor by role-appropriateness. A broad, graded, role/event-conditioned candidate pool (concretely: a ~300-item plausible-patient neighborhood scored continuously, not a 4-item argmax) is the more brain-faithful instrument, and it should NOT be pruned to role-appropriate-only, because the brain's own "good-enough" wrong answers are role-adjacent/category-adjacent concepts the sentence never names — exactly the failure mode already observed in the live measurement.

**Q4 (centroid vs situation model):** conditioning the patient prediction on 2 already-bound arguments (agent + one more — instrument, in the closest precedent) is directly, cleanly precedented as a real, controlled-for, independent sharpening effect (Bicknell et al. 2010; Matsuki et al. 2011) — and both papers explicitly describe this as **compositional/bounded-tuple event-knowledge retrieval**, NOT evidence for a full recurrent joint situation-state. This is in scope for a predictor upgrade (representation/composition, not generative model) and directly corroborates the substrate's own prior finding (+0.035 CI-sep from a 2nd bound participant). Chow et al.'s (2015) "bag-of-arguments" mechanism supplies the precedented intermediate computational FORM: pool all currently-bound arguments as an unordered, initially role-blind set feeding one joint conditioning step, with role-structured refinement layered on top only later/given more time. The true generative situation model (Rabovsky et al. 2018 Sentence-Gestalt: single recurrent hidden state, queryable for any role, no per-role slots, maintains graded uncertainty across competing role-assignments) is architecturally distinct and remains out of scope.

## Q1 — THE COMPETITION SET (full evidence)

1. **Altmann & Kamide 1999** (*Cognition* 73): verb selectional restriction rapidly restricts anticipatory saccades to the plausible object in a 4-5-object scene; target-directed saccades launched **before noun onset** (mean –85 ms relative to noun onset for "eat," Exp. 1; F1(1,23)=12.3, p<.002). This establishes rapid, pre-word-onset restriction but says nothing about competition breadth beyond the fixed small scene — an artifact of visual-world method, not evidence the underlying computation is 4-candidate-bounded.
2. **Kamide, Altmann & Haywood 2003** (*JML* 49): Exp. 2 shows the effect is genuinely combinatorial — looks to "motorbike" rise only when BOTH agent ("man") and verb ("ride") are present jointly (man-ride vs girl-ride: 9% vs 6%, F1(1,189)=7.3, p<.008); neither alone produces it. Exp. 3 (Japanese case-marking, no verb yet heard) shows dative case marking alone drives looks to a plausible theme (38.1% vs 23.9%, F1(21)=7.0, p<.02) purely from grammatical role information. Prediction is graded, jointly syntax+semantics-driven, and reaches beyond the immediate lexical item.
3. **Kukona, Fang, Aicher, Chen & Magnuson 2011** (*Cognition* 119) — the single most load-bearing source for this question. A role-INAPPROPRIATE but verb-associated competitor (e.g. "policeman" as a candidate for "arrest" even though the agent slot is already filled by "Toby") is activated at a magnitude statistically indistinguishable from the true patient target in active sentences (target-vs-distractor intercept diff .22 vs competitor-vs-distractor .18, target-vs-competitor diff only .04, n.s.). Their own conclusion: verbs pre-activate "all thematically-related fillers regardless of role-appropriateness," directly conflicting with strict role-slot-restriction accounts. In passive sentences (Exp. 2, more time/cues available) the target advantage becomes reliable but the competitor still shows a measurable residual activation — role-sensitivity strengthens with more processing time but does not eliminate broad thematic competition.
4. **Federmeier 2007** (*Psychophysiology* 44): N400 amplitude is graded — expected < within-category-unexpected < between-category-unexpected — for items matched on offline unexpectedness. Since within/between-category items are equally unexpected but produce different N400s, the brain must be pre-activating a **featural/semantic neighborhood**, not a single item or a small closed set. This graded-neighborhood signature cannot in principle be reproduced by scoring only sentence-internal nouns.
5. **Kutas & Federmeier 2011** (*Annu. Rev. Psychol.* 62): general integrative framing of prediction as graded semantic pre-activation (full-text unobtainable this pass — 4 mirrors blocked; treat as corroborating background, not an independent quantitative anchor).
6. **Chow, Smith, Lau & Phillips 2015** (bag-of-arguments, see Q4): a directly relevant structural precedent — verb prediction pools the lexical identity of ALL clause arguments, role-blind, at fast/standard timing; role-sensitivity is a slower-emerging refinement (Chow Mandarin follow-up; Nakamura et al. 2024, *Cognitive Science*). This is the general architectural principle underwriting the Q1 answer: initial competition is broader and role-blinder than a mature/settled state.

**Direct answer to the coordinator's concrete instrument question:** a broad-candidate-pool forward-prediction MRR (true patient ranked among ~O(100s) plausible fillers) is more brain-faithful than a 4-visible-noun rank, on two independent grounds — (a) Federmeier's graded within/between-category N400 requires candidates that are never in the sentence at all, and (b) Kukona's near-equal activation of an off-sentence, role-inappropriate competitor shows the pool must include event/verb-associated distractors, not just patient-role-typical fillers. This directly explains the observed "~half of residual errors are good-enough plausible wrong noun" phenomenon in the live measurement — the brain's OWN competition set produces exactly this error signature (a near-miss category-mate scores nearly as high as the true target), so it should be measured as a graded rank (MRR/percentile) over a broad conditioned pool, not scored as binary correct/incorrect against 4 sentence-visible candidates. Caveat: no cited study specifies an exact pool size or that the pool should be restricted to role-appropriate items only — Kukona argues explicitly for INCLUDING role-inappropriate-but-thematically-related items, so the pool should be event/verb-conditioned (selectional-preference/GEK-neighborhood scored, not uniform-vocabulary) rather than syntactically pre-filtered to patient-eligible-only. P_deflated=0.45 (well-precedented qualitative shape; exact pool size/composition is engineering inference, not directly measured in any source).

## Q4 — CENTROID vs SITUATION MODEL, the cheap in-scope step

1. **McRae, Ferretti & Amyote 1997**: canonical "option A" — verb-role as a verb-specific concept; a (verb, role) marginal, in principle independent of co-occurring fillers.
2. **Bicknell, Elman, Hare, McRae & Kutas 2010** (*JML* 63): agent+verb JOINTLY (ruled out simple lexical priming via a free-association control) determine patient expectation. RT: congruent sentences read 20 ms faster one word after the patient (F1(1,44)=11.50, p<.002), 18 ms faster two words later — a spillover effect, not at the patient noun itself. ERP is immediate: N400 –1.63 µV incongruent vs 0.18 µV congruent at the patient noun (F(1,11)=4.97, p<.05). Authors frame this explicitly as "dynamically combined event knowledge," compositional, not evidence for a full joint state.
3. **Matsuki, Chow, Hare, Elman, Scheepers & McRae 2011** (*JEP:LMC* 37): instrument+verb sharpens patient prediction with the SAME verb+patient pair held constant across conditions (only the instrument differs) — 29-34 ms self-paced-reading effect (p<.01-.03), 11-23 ms eyetracking first-fixation/gaze-duration effects (p<.05-.01), direct lexical instrument-patient priming ruled out (non-significant, 1-15ms). Explicit conclusion: "selectional restrictions may be best considered as event-based conceptual knowledge rather than lexical-grammatical knowledge" — again framed as compositional retrieval over a bounded [element, verb, patient] tuple, not a full situation model.
4. **Rabovsky, Hansen & McClelland 2018** (*Nat. Hum. Behav.* 2): the actual full joint-situation-model pole. One recurrent hidden "gestalt" state queried for ANY role at any timestep; no discrete per-role slots at all; reproduces 16 N400 findings including a genuinely graded reversal-anomaly response (d=0.66-4.19) that a compositional bounded-tuple lookup does not naturally produce (maintained uncertainty across simultaneously-competing role assignments). Explicitly framed by its authors as categorically different in kind from marginal/compositional accounts, not an extension of them.
5. **Chow, Smith, Lau & Phillips 2015/2018** (bag-of-arguments + Mandarin delayed-impact follow-up): the clearest documented INTERMEDIATE computational form — pool the lexical identity of ALL currently-bound clause arguments as an unordered, role-blind set at first pass; role-structured integration is a measurably later, second stage (confirmed via SOA manipulation: role-sensitivity appears only at ≥1200-1800ms argument-verb gaps, or under a slower production/cloze task per Nakamura et al. 2024).
6. **Zwaan & Radvansky 1998** situation-model 5-dimension (time/space/causation/intentionality/protagonist) framework: reports near-additive/near-linear scaling of coherence judgments with number of shared dimensions, but the authors themselves flag the independence-of-dimensions assumption as likely false, and a direct follow-up found an interaction (protagonist x space), not clean additivity. This literature measures offline discourse coherence, not online argument-slot prediction, and does not bridge cleanly to the Bicknell/Matsuki paradigm — no study tests 3+ bound arguments in an online prediction (N400/RT) design. Genuine, explicit evidentiary gap.

**Direct answer:** yes — conditioning patient prediction on all already-bound arguments (agent + oblique/instrument/other participant) is a real, precedented, in-scope REPRESENTATION/COMPOSITION step, not the situation model. The precedent is exactly 2 elements combined (agent+verb: Bicknell; instrument+verb: Matsuki), both via a design that rules out simpler single-source lookup and both explicitly described as compositional/bounded-tuple retrieval — the same computational family as the existing verb-role centroid, just conditioned on a richer bound-argument set (extend the precision-weighted composition to fold in ANY bound participant, not only the agent). This directly corroborates the substrate's own prior finding (+0.035 CI-sep from a 2nd bound participant on a graded 2AFC) — an independent, unplanned convergence with Bicknell/Matsuki's architecture and effect direction. It is NOT yet the situation model: no cited source shows this style of composition reproducing Rabovsky's graded-uncertainty-under-reversal-anomaly signature, and the 3+-argument regime is untested in this literature. **Pre-registered boundary marker: if adding a 3rd bound argument (beyond agent) shows continuing, roughly-Bicknell/Matsuki-sized gains, that's compositional and in scope; if gains saturate near-zero after 2, that also stays in scope (a bounded/2-tuple retrieval ceiling, cheaply detectable); only a demonstrated need for graded, simultaneously-maintained uncertainty across competing role-fillers (the reversal-anomaly signature) would indicate the compositional route has hit its ceiling and the full generative situation model (Rabovsky-style recurrent state) is required — that is a separate, harder problem (P1's scope), not this predictor's.** P_deflated=0.40 (2-element precedent solid and replicated across two argument types; 3+-element extrapolation and the "approaches but doesn't require" framing are this drill's own inference, capped per novel-synthesis rule).

## Q2/Q5 — condensed (full sub-agent data in hand, condensed for the urgent word budget)

**Q2 (prediction<->role-assignment interaction):** McRae et al. 1998 and Trueswell, Tanenhaus & Garnsey 1994 show thematic fit/plausibility is a real, immediate, graded constraint that CAN fully eliminate garden-path RT costs in reduced-relative ambiguity when fit is strong (Trueswell: strong-fit inanimates statistically identical to unambiguous controls, +7ms vs +44-46ms for weak-fit). MacDonald, Pearlmutter & Seidenberg 1994 formalize the mechanism as interactive-activation/settling (winner-take-all relaxation among mutually-inhibitory constraint units), explicitly "contextually constrained but lexically dominated" — thematic fit only succeeds against a roughly-equibiased structural/frequency prior; a strongly-biased verb frequency dominates regardless of fit. Critically, **none of the four sources report an online RT test of thematic fit's role in fully CANONICAL, unambiguous word-order sentences** — the literature treats this as an acknowledged, untested theoretical prediction, not a demonstrated effect. This directly answers the coordinator's decision: feeding hub thematic-fit prediction into the role-assigner is well-precedented as a real lever specifically on the ambiguous/non-canonical subset (reduced relatives, passives, fronted patients), with no clean evidence it should be expected to do independent work in the canonical-active case where the substrate already matches the brain via word order.

**Q3 (ATL sense selection):** PENDING — a sub-agent covering Lambon Ralph et al. 2017 CSC, Jefferies 2013 SA/SD double dissociation, Noonan/Jefferies control mechanism, Desimone & Duncan 1995 biased competition, and polysemy-vs-homonymy evidence completed but its detailed findings are still being retrieved; will be appended in a follow-up note rather than delaying this urgent delivery.

**Q5 (ranked divergence):** provisional ranking given Q1+Q4 answers now in hand: (1) **competition-set breadth** — HIGHEST expected fidelity gain, in-scope (representation/composition: widen live scoring from ~4 sentence nouns to a broad graded role/event-conditioned pool); (2) **bound-argument conditioning** — in-scope (composition: extend precision-weighted composition to all bound participants, not agent-only; substrate's own +0.035 CI-sep data plus Bicknell/Matsuki convergence make this the second-highest-confidence lever); (3) **thematic-fit-into-role-assignment gating restricted to the non-canonical subset** — in-scope but conditional (Q2: only apply where structural cues are genuinely ambiguous; do not expect gains on canonical actives); (4) **sense-selection read from the hub** (Q3) — status TBD pending full retrieval; (5) **full generative situation model** (Rabovsky-style recurrent joint state) — explicitly OUT of this predictor's scope, a separate problem, only indicated if the in-scope compositional extensions (item 2) hit a measured ceiling on the graded-uncertainty/reversal-anomaly signature.

## Substrate-product implications

Widening live scoring to a broad, graded, event/verb-conditioned candidate pool (Q1) and extending precision-weighted composition to all bound arguments not just the agent (Q4) are both REPRESENTATION/COMPOSITION-tier upgrades — buildable without a generative situation model, both with clean cross-thread corroboration from the substrate's own prior measurements (the +0.035 CI-sep finding), and both directly targeted at the two named live failure modes (agent/oblique competition inflating the denominator; "good-enough" wrong-noun errors reflecting genuine brain-like graded near-misses that should be credited partially via rank, not penalized as flat wrong).
