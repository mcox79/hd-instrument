## SECTION 1: WHAT IS SYNTHETIC IN EACH PP ROW

### 1.1 PP-315: Boredom / Novelty-Salience Signal
Test generation: a stream of 4200 events was generated by drawing repeated patterns from a
fixed synthetic pool and novel patterns from a separate pool, by construction. The
repeated-vs-novel label is guaranteed from the generation procedure. The cleanup-margin
discriminator was tested against this known labeling.

Favorable properties of synthetic inputs:
- Repeated items are EXACTLY identical to previously stored items. Real repeated experiences
  differ with noise, viewpoint, context, paraphrase. Cleanup margin drops as repeat-fidelity
  drops.
- Novel items are drawn from a distribution with the same structure (random hypervectors).
  Real novel items may be similar in some dimension to prior items while being genuinely novel
  in another. This creates false-negative cases the synthetic test cannot see.
- The novelty-density correlation (0.815) was measured against a known ground-truth density
  schedule. Real attention streams do not have a ground-truth novelty schedule accessible for
  correlation.

What "synthetic" buys: clean discriminability with zero encoding noise. The AUC=1.000 is
expected under these conditions because the problem is well-posed by construction.

### 1.2 PP-316: Image-Schema Grounding
Test generation: 200 abstract concepts were ASSIGNED to image-schema categories
(CONTAINER, SOURCE-PATH-GOAL, FORCE-DYNAMICS, etc.) by the researcher. The cluster-purity
metric measures whether items from the same assigned category cluster together. Because the
category assignment was done by the researcher (not recovered from real concept distributions),
the test is measuring whether the encoding is consistent with the assigned labels, not whether
the labels capture real-world concept structure.

Favorable properties of synthetic inputs:
- Clean one-to-one schema assignment per concept. Real abstract concepts are polysemous across
  image-schema categories (e.g., "justice" is simultaneously CONTAINER [justice contains
  norms], BALANCE [scales], FORCE-DYNAMICS [power vs. victim], PATH-GOAL [path to justice]).
  Multi-schema concepts will split into separate clusters, reducing purity.
- 200 concepts is a small vocabulary. At 200 concepts with ~10 image-schema categories, each
  category has ~20 members. The statistical power to detect purity is high. Real vocabulary
  evaluations use thousands of concepts.
- Synthetic concepts were chosen to be prototypical examples of each schema. Real abstract
  concepts from legal texts, philosophy, or social science are the hard cases.

What "synthetic" buys: single-schema assignment eliminates polysemy, which is the dominant
failure mode for real abstract concepts.

### 1.3 PP-317: Tool-Extended Body Schema
Test generation: simulated proprioceptive + visual vectors for "body parts" and "tools"
were generated such that used tools were superimposed with body-part features by construction.
The AUC=1.000 measures whether used-tool vectors cluster with body-part vectors.

Favorable properties of synthetic inputs:
- The tool-use association is ENCODED DIRECTLY into the test vectors by generation. In a real
  sensorimotor system, the association must be LEARNED from repeated co-activation patterns
  across sensory modalities. The synthetic test bypasses the learning problem.
- Tool delta (+0.180) was defined from the generation procedure itself. In real robotic data,
  the delta depends on signal-to-noise in proprioceptive channels, tool weight/compliance
  variability, and motor command variation during use.
- The test is essentially "do bundled vectors retrieve as intended?" -- a storage/retrieval
  test, not a grounding test.

What "synthetic" buys: bypasses the transduction chain (raw sensor -> embedding -> substrate
representation) entirely. PP-317 demonstrates the algebra works; it does not demonstrate the
transduction chain works.

### 1.4 PP-313: KB-Shard Retrieval at 40K Atoms
Test generation: 40 shards of 1000 atoms each were generated by constructing random HD
vectors for all entities and binding them with role-filler structure. Retrieval was tested
by exact role-filler unbinding.

Favorable properties of synthetic inputs:
- All atom vectors are EXACTLY orthogonal in expectation (random HD). Real knowledge base
  entities represented via KGEs (TransE, RotatE, ComplEx) are NOT random: they are
  trained to have geometric structure that encodes relational inferences. Near-neighbor
  entities will have high dot-product similarity, creating cleanup-margin interference.
- No aliasing between shards. Real KBs have cross-shard entity overlap (the same entity
  appears in multiple shards), which creates interference in bundle-based storage.
- 40K atoms is a controlled scale. The system has already demonstrated synthetic recall=1.000
  at 50K (story shards). Real KGE embeddings at 40K entities face the correlated-input
  capacity problem documented in McEliece et al. (1987): capacity drops as (1-rho^2)^-1 where
  rho is mean inter-vector cosine similarity.

What "synthetic" buys: near-orthogonality of all stored vectors, which is the ideal operating
condition for HDC associative memory. Real KGEs break this assumption.

## SECTION 2: SYNTHETIC-TO-REAL GAP LITERATURE

### 2.1 OOD Generalization -- Hendrycks-Dietterich Pattern
Hendrycks & Dietterich (2019) showed that models performing well on synthetic corruptions
(ImageNet-C) fail to generalize to natural distribution shifts (ImageNet-R, ObjectNet) at
significantly higher rates than benchmark performance suggests. The pattern: synthetic test
performance systematically overstates real-world robustness when the synthetic distribution
is "favorable" (i.e., generated from the same family as training data).

Applied here: Sprint 1 synthetic tests are generated from the same family as the test (random
HDC vectors). Real-world inputs (real KB embeddings, real text, real sensor data) belong to
a different distribution. The OOD literature consistently finds this gap is not small: 20-40
percentage points on classification accuracy is typical for domain shift from synthetic to
naturalistic.

### 2.2 Shortcut Learning -- Geirhos et al. (2020)
Shortcuts arise when the test distribution makes a spurious feature predictive of the label.
In the Sprint 1 tests:
- PP-316: schema label is perfectly correlated with the encoding because the researcher
  assigned both. The "shortcut" is that the discriminative feature is also the generation
  feature. A real-world test would require the schema assignment to be inferred, not given.
- PP-315: novelty label IS the generation flag. There is no way for the system to fail if it
  correctly implements cleanup-margin, because the repeat-vs-novel signal is structurally
  clean in the generation procedure.

Shortcut-learning lesson: these are not shortcuts in the pejorative sense (the mechanism IS
doing the right thing). But the test cannot distinguish "correct mechanism" from "correct
mechanism + favorable test construction" because both produce AUC=1.000.

### 2.3 VSA Brittleness to Encoding Quality
The capacity of a Hopfield/HDC associative memory is well-characterized for random vectors
(McEliece et al. 1987: capacity approximately 0.14 * N for N-dim vectors). For correlated
vectors with mean pairwise cosine similarity rho, the effective capacity drops as (1-rho^2)^-1
relative to random. KGE embeddings from TransE/RotatE routinely have rho approximately
0.05-0.20 for entity vectors in the same relation neighborhood. At rho=0.15, capacity is
approximately 0.12 * N (15% reduction). At rho=0.30, capacity drops to 0.09 * N (36% drop).

For PP-313 at N=1024 default, random-vector capacity is approximately 143 atoms. At rho=0.20
with real KGEs, the effective capacity is approximately 114 atoms. PP-313 stores 40K atoms
across 40 shards (1000 per shard). The per-shard load factor is 1000/N. This far exceeds
single-shard capacity, which means the shard architecture is doing necessary work. The
question is: does it continue to work when the 1000 stored vectors per shard are not random?

The key test is how correlated real KB entity vectors are WITHIN a shard. If LSH/spectral
shard assignment places similar entities together (which is likely for a semantic KB), then
INTRA-SHARD correlation rho could be substantially higher than the KB-wide average. This
is the real-data risk for PP-313.

### 2.4 Compositional Generalization Gap -- Lake-Baroni SCAN
Lake and Baroni (2018) showed that sequence-to-sequence models achieving >99% on SCAN
in-distribution fail to < 20% on the SCAN length-split and MCD-split, where the distribution
shifts require genuine compositional use. More recent work (2024-2025, "Systematic
Generalization in Language Models Scales") found this gap is narrowed by scale but not
eliminated. The mechanism: synthetic benchmarks by design have clean compositional structure
that real language lacks.

Applied to PP-313/310/311/312 shard series: the compositional structure is EXACT in the
synthetic tests (atoms bind with exactly the role vectors they were given). Real KB relations
are noisy (Freebase/Wikidata have missing links, approximate labels, type errors). The
binding mechanism needs to tolerate ~5-15% noise in role-filler assignments for real KB data.

### 2.5 Probe Task Evaluation -- Synthetic vs Natural Distribution
Research on probing tasks (Ettinger et al. 2016; Hewitt & Liang 2019; Pimentel et al. 2020)
established that probe task accuracy on synthetic data systematically overestimates the
representation quality for natural language tasks. The probe-task literature distinguishes
"what CAN the representation encode" (synthetic test) from "what DOES the representation
encode from real inputs" (natural test). This is exactly the PP-316/PP-317 situation: the
synthetic test shows the CAPACITY of the representation; it does not show that real input
text or sensor data will produce representations with the required structure.

## SECTION 3: PER-PRIMITIVE EXPECTED DROP

### 3.1 PP-315 Boredom -- Real Attention Streams

Expected real-data test: eye-tracking data (OSIE, MIT1003 datasets), web browsing dwell
times, reading fixation corpora (reading time benchmarks from cognitive science).

Specific failure modes:
(a) Repeat-fidelity degradation. Real repeated stimuli are rarely exactly identical.
    A repeated web page has new ads, timestamps, user state. The cleanup-margin discriminator
    requires the repeated item to be sufficiently similar to the stored version. If cosine
    similarity drops to 0.85 on repeated items vs 1.00 on synthetic repeats, false-negative
    rate increases.
(b) Temporal decay of the buffer. The synthetic buffer has an idealized decay schedule.
    Real boredom operates on irregular inter-stimulus intervals. Buffer decay calibrated on
    uniform synthetic intervals will be mis-calibrated on real bursty/irregular exposure.
(c) Semantic novelty without perceptual novelty. Reading new content on a familiar topic is
    perceptually novel (new words/tokens) but semantically boring. The cleanup-margin
    discriminator operates on the HD representation; if the real encoder produces similar
    representations for semantically-similar-but-lexically-different content, the boredom
    signal will correctly fire. But if the encoder is insensitive to semantic similarity
    (which is common for shallow encoders), the signal will not track semantic boredom.

Expected drop: AUC 1.000 (synthetic) -> AUC 0.70-0.85 (real attention streams).
Dominant mechanism: repeat-fidelity degradation (a) + irregular temporal sampling (b).
P_deflated (mechanism holds at AUC >= 0.75 on real streams): 0.45 (deflated 0.20 from 0.65).
Hard-fail if AUC < 0.65 on held-out real attention data with ground-truth novelty labels.

### 3.2 PP-316 Image-Schema Grounding -- Real Abstract Concepts

Expected real-data test: ConceptNet abstract terms (3000-5000 abstract nodes), philosophy
text corpora (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: justice, freedom, causation), legal text
(statutes: contract, duty, liability). Ground truth: expert schema labeling or multi-schema
annotation allowing polysemy.

Specific failure modes:
(a) Polysemy collapse. Abstract concepts in real use are systematically polysemous across
    image-schema categories. "Justice" clusters under BALANCE, FORCE-DYNAMICS, PATH-GOAL,
    and CONTAINER simultaneously depending on context. A single cluster assignment will fail
    for polysemous concepts. Expected: cluster purity drops from 1.000 to 0.45-0.65 at scale.
(b) Schema boundary blurring. Lakoff/Johnson identify approximately 27 primary image-schema
    categories. These categories are NOT cleanly separable in real use: CONTAINMENT and
    FORCE-DYNAMICS share many abstract concept members (e.g., "constraint"). The synthetic
    test uses prototypical examples; real evaluation uses the tail of the distribution.
(c) Domain-specific schema patterns. Legal/medical text develops domain-specific schema
    usages that deviate from Lakoff/Johnson prototypes. "Breach" in contract law combines
    FORCE-DYNAMICS (obligation pressure) + CONTAINER (duty scope) + BLOCKAGE in ways that
    resist clean prototype assignment.
(d) Scale: at 200 concepts with 10 schema categories, random chance purity is 0.10. Real
    evaluation should include 2000+ concepts across 27 schema categories (base rate 0.037).
    The 200-concept test is a best-case statistical setting.

Expected drop: grounding_acc 1.000 (synthetic) -> 0.50-0.65 (real abstract corpus);
cluster_purity 1.000 (synthetic) -> 0.35-0.55 (real, allowing polysemy).
P_deflated (mechanism holds at grounding_acc >= 0.70, cluster_purity >= 0.50): 0.25-0.35.
This is the LOWEST confidence primitive. Polysemy is a qualitative challenge, not just noise.
Hard-fail if cluster_purity < 0.40 or grounding_acc < 0.55 on ConceptNet abstract terms.

### 3.3 PP-317 Tool-Extended Body Schema -- Real Robotic Data

Expected real-data test: simulated proprioceptive data from a physics engine (PyBullet, MuJoCo)
with a gripper and tool objects; or publicly available robot manipulation datasets (DROID, RT-1,
or RoboMimic). The test measures whether the substrate-encoded tool representation develops
membership with body-part encodings after use episodes.

Specific failure modes:
(a) Transduction chain. PP-317 bypasses the transduction chain entirely by constructing tool
    vectors with body-part features inserted. Real robotic data requires: raw sensor -> feature
    extractor -> HD encoding -> substrate storage. At each step, signal fidelity decreases.
    If the feature extractor does not produce sufficiently similar representations for
    used-tool and body-part signals, the substrate has no material to work with.
(b) Modality integration. Maravita & Iriki (2004) showed tool incorporation requires temporal
    coincidence of visual and somatosensory/proprioceptive signals during use. The substrate
    needs temporal co-activation signals, not just post-hoc vector construction.
(c) Tool variability. Multiple tools, varied grip orientations, and compliance differences
    each produce different proprioceptive signatures. A body-schema representation learned
    from hammering may not generalize to using a rake without re-training.
(d) AUC=1.000 with tool_delta=+0.180 comes from a clean generated pairing. Real robot data
    will have delta values reflecting sensor noise; the delta may be positive on average but
    with high variance, reducing AUC.

Expected drop: membership_AUC 1.000 (synthetic) -> 0.65-0.80 (simulated robot data);
tool_delta may remain positive but shrink to +0.05-0.10.
P_deflated (mechanism holds at AUC >= 0.75 on simulated proprioceptive data): 0.40-0.50.
Note: the claim is weaker than PP-316 (one modality, cleaner signal) but the transduction
gap is real.
Hard-fail if AUC < 0.65 or tool_delta < 0.02 on simulated proprioceptive data.

### 3.4 PP-313 KB-Shard Retrieval -- Real KGEs

Expected real-data test: FB15K-237 entities (14,541 entities, 237 relations, 272K triples)
or ConceptNet-Lite (100K relation triples) encoded via a standard KGE (TransE, RotatE,
or ComplEx pretrained embeddings publicly available from DGL-KE / PyKEEN). Test: encode
entities as HD atoms using the KGE vectors as seed, construct shards by relation type,
test top-tier retrieval recall.

Specific failure modes:
(a) Intra-shard correlation. If shards are formed by semantic/relational proximity (which is
    the natural thing to do for a KB), entities within a shard will have higher pairwise
    cosine similarity than random. At rho=0.15-0.30 within a shard, the effective retrieval
    capacity of the shard drops by 15-36% relative to random. For 1000-atom shards at N=1024,
    this means some shards will experience retrieval failure.
(b) Entity polysemy / multi-type entities. FB15K-237 entities appear in multiple relation
    types simultaneously. An entity shard is not a clean partition; entities have membership
    in multiple shards with different role-filler bindings. This creates inter-shard
    interference absent from the synthetic test.
(c) KGE geometry. KGE vectors are trained to place related entities near each other in
    embedding space (that is the objective). This creates the exact correlated-input regime
    the synthetic test avoids. TransE embeddings have entity-entity cosines of 0.10-0.30 for
    same-type entities. The McEliece capacity correction applies.
(d) Missing and noisy triples. Freebase/Wikidata have ~30-40% missing relations (the standard
    KB completion benchmark). The substrate storage must tolerate missing bindings without
    treating them as incorrect retrievals.

Expected drop: recall 1.000 (synthetic) -> 0.70-0.90 (real KGE at similar scale).
The recall drop is expected to be moderate because the shard architecture provides
structural insulation from cross-shard noise. The dominant failure mode is intra-shard
correlated inputs, which is the most tractable to address (increase N, or increase whitening
before HD encoding).
P_deflated (mechanism holds at recall >= 0.80 on real KGE, 40-shard config): 0.45-0.55.
This is the most tractable primitive for real-data validation.
Hard-fail if recall < 0.70 on FB15K-237 at 40-shard configuration with TransE embeddings.

## SECTION 4: SPRINT 2 TEST DESIGNS

### 4.1 BOREDOM-REAL: Real Attention/Reading Stream Test
Resource: MIT LowFix corpus (reading fixation times, publicly available) or OSIE eye-tracking
dataset (943 natural images with gaze data). The substrate test processes sequences ordered by
fixation event, where repeated fixation on the same scene segment is ground-truth "repeated"
and first-visit fixation is ground-truth "novel."

Protocol:
(a) Encode scene segments as HD atoms using a real image encoder (CLIP ViT-B or ResNet50
    last-layer, dimension-reduced to substrate N via PCA-whitening per production protocol).
(b) Build recent-experience buffer with temporal decay calibrated to fixation inter-event
    intervals (not uniform synthetic intervals).
(c) Run cleanup-margin novelty classifier on the real fixation stream.
(d) Evaluate AUC against ground-truth repeat labels from the gaze protocol.

Pre-registered gates:
HARD-PASS: AUC >= 0.75 on held-out gaze stream with known repeat labels.
HARD-FAIL: AUC < 0.65; if this triggers, the mechanism is real but insufficient without
  encoder calibration or buffer-decay recalibration.
MIDDLE-BAND: AUC 0.65-0.75; mechanism holds but needs parameter adjustment.

Cheapest variant: web browsing dwell-time logs (URL hash as atom identity; revisit = repeat).
No image encoder needed; pure session-log test. Cost: ~4 hours CPU. This is the cheapest
real-data test in the entire Sprint 2 battery.

### 4.2 IMAGE-SCHEMA-REAL: ConceptNet Abstract Terms Test
Resource: ConceptNet 5.7 (downloaded; already in pipeline per Testbed overnight chain note
-- 458K facts). Extract abstract noun nodes with no clear physical referent (filter by
IsA -> AbstractThing, or by absence of HasPhysicalProperties relation). Target: 2000-5000
abstract concepts.

Protocol:
(a) Encode each abstract concept node as an HD atom using a real text encoder (Llama-1B
    BASE last-token pooling, as per production encoder).
(b) For each atom, record the ConceptNet schema-neighbor relations (RelatedTo, Causes,
    PartOf, etc.) as a proxy for the dominant image-schema category.
(c) Cluster HD atoms with k-means (k=10-20 schema categories).
(d) Evaluate cluster purity and adjusted Rand index against schema-neighbor proxy labels.
(e) Allow multi-schema assignment (each concept can have top-2 schema membership); measure
    purity with soft labels.

Pre-registered gates:
HARD-PASS: cluster_purity >= 0.50 (hard single-label) OR >= 0.65 (soft top-2 label).
HARD-FAIL: cluster_purity < 0.35 with either labeling scheme; this would indicate that the
  image-schema grounding does not transfer to real abstract vocabulary.
MIDDLE-BAND: 0.35-0.50; mechanism partially holds but polysemy is limiting.

ConceptNet data is already available locally (458K facts, ~10 CPU hrs to process abstraction
filter). Cost: ~2 CPU hours for encoding + clustering.

### 4.3 TOOL-EXTENDED-REAL: Simulated Proprioceptive Test
Resource: PyBullet or MuJoCo physics simulation (available in standard robotics Python
packages). Use a Kuka IIWA arm or Franka Panda model grasping objects of varying mass and
compliance.

Protocol:
(a) Record joint-torque + end-effector force streams during free-hand motion (no tool) and
    during tool-grasping motion (hammer, screwdriver mass profiles).
(b) Encode each trial's sensor stream as an HD atom via time-series feature extraction
    (mean + variance + peak torque) followed by PCA-whitening to substrate N.
(c) After N_use trials with a given tool, run the body-schema update (bundle tool HD vector
    into peripersonal space representation).
(d) Measure membership_AUC of used-tool HD vector against body-part HD vectors.

Pre-registered gates:
HARD-PASS: membership_AUC >= 0.75, tool_delta >= 0.05 (positive and above noise floor).
HARD-FAIL: AUC < 0.65 or tool_delta <= 0.00 (used tool not distinguishable from unused tool).
MIDDLE-BAND: AUC 0.65-0.75; mechanism holds but requires more use episodes.

Note: simulated data is not "fully real" but it introduces the transduction chain that
PP-317 bypasses. The key question is whether the transduction chain is transparent enough
for the substrate algebra to function. Cost: ~1-2 CPU days (physics simulation).

### 4.4 KB-SHARD-REAL: TransE on FB15K-237 Test
Resource: FB15K-237 (272K triples, 14K entities, 237 relations). Pre-trained TransE or
RotatE embeddings available from PyKEEN or DGL-KE (150-dimensional, downloadable in ~5 min).

Protocol:
(a) Project 150-dim KGE embeddings to substrate N via PCA-whitening (production protocol).
(b) Partition 14K entities into 40 shards by relation type (e.g., each of the top-40 most
    frequent relations forms a shard; entities with membership in that relation go into that
    shard).
(c) Build composite shard bundles as in PP-313.
(d) Test retrieval: given a query entity and target relation type, retrieve correct shard.
(e) Measure recall@1 across 200 test queries sampled from the held-out triples.

Pre-registered gates:
HARD-PASS: recall@1 >= 0.80 (same threshold as PP-313 production threshold).
HARD-FAIL: recall@1 < 0.70; indicates intra-shard KGE correlation is collapsing retrieval.
MIDDLE-BAND: 0.70-0.80; mechanism holds but needs N increase or whitening recalibration.

Cost: ~4-6 CPU hours. This is the most structurally similar to the synthetic test and has
the highest prior P_deflated (0.45-0.55), making it the best first candidate for Sprint 2.

## SECTION 5: FAILURE MODES TO INSTRUMENT

### 5.1 Cleanup Margin Distribution on Real Data
The cleanup-margin discriminator relies on a clean gap between "stored" and "not-stored" items.
On synthetic random vectors, this gap is approximately (1/sqrt(N)) * Gaussian spread.
On real embeddings with rho > 0.05, the margin shrinks because not-stored items are not
orthogonal to the probe -- they are correlated. The key diagnostic: plot the cleanup-margin
histogram on real data and compare to the synthetic histogram. If the distributions overlap
substantially, AUC will drop even if the mechanism is correct.

### 5.2 AUC Drop as Noise Function
Expected: AUC decreases monotonically as input noise (epsilon) increases from 0 (synthetic)
to real-world levels. The functional form is approximately AUC(epsilon) ~ 1 - f(epsilon / margin)
where margin is the cleanup decision boundary and f is an increasing function. Pre-register
this curve shape: AUC should be > 0.75 at epsilon = 0.15 (typical real-encoder noise) to
claim real-data viability.

### 5.3 Polysemy-Driven Purity Floor
For PP-316, the cluster purity floor on real abstract concepts is set by polysemy, not by
encoding quality. Even a perfect encoder will produce multi-schema clusters for polysemous
concepts. The honest claim should be: "substrate implements image-schema algebra; on real data,
purity is limited by concept polysemy, not by substrate failure." Pre-register this distinction
in the Sprint 2 test: report purity SEPARATELY for mono-schema and multi-schema concepts.

### 5.4 Schema Confounding in Real Vocabulary
Real abstract concept vocabularies have domain-specific schema usages (legal, scientific,
everyday). Evaluate cluster purity BY DOMAIN separately from pooled purity. If substrate
clusters within-domain correctly but cross-domain purity drops, this indicates domain-specific
schema variation, not a fundamental failure. This is a navigable finding.

## SECTION 7: MECHANISM VS SCALE SEPARATION

### 7.1 What Sprint 1 Establishes (and does not)

What is established:
(a) The algebraic mechanism for each primitive is implemented correctly in the substrate.
(b) The mechanism works under ideal conditions (near-orthogonal random input vectors,
    clean label assignments, exact bundling).
(c) The shard architecture provides capacity scaling beyond flat indexing (PP-314 gap
    analysis is a genuine result -- flat bundle collapses at story scale where shard succeeds).
(d) The primitives compose with each other within the v3.0 compositional framework.

What is NOT established:
(a) That the primitives work when inputs come from real encoders producing correlated vectors.
(b) That the primitives work when the input signal is noisy (sensor noise, paraphrase,
    missing data).
(c) That the grounding claims (image-schema, body-schema, intrinsic motivation) hold for
    real-world inputs at the scale and complexity of real applications.
(d) That the performance holds beyond the tested scale (40K atoms is not a production KB;
    FB15K-237 is ~280K entities; production target may be millions).

### 7.2 Honest Sprint 1 Claim Language

Supported: "The substrate implements [boredom/image-schema/tool-extended body-schema/KB-shard]
algebraically, as demonstrated on controlled synthetic encodings."

Not supported: "The substrate provides [boredom detection/embodied grounding/tool awareness/KB
retrieval] for real-world applications."

The latter requires Sprint 2+ validation. This is not a criticism of Sprint 1 -- it is the
standard mechanism-demonstration protocol. Sprint 1 is necessary before Sprint 2; it does not
replace Sprint 2.

### 7.3 Sequencing

Sprint 1 (DONE): Mechanism demonstration on synthetic. Establishes that the algebra is correct.
Sprint 2 (next): Real-data pre-test on each primitive. Cheap: 2-4 CPU days total.
Sprint 3: If Sprint 2 HARD-PASS: sweep N, shard count, and noise levels for production
          parameter selection.
Sprint 4: Integration with real encoder pipeline; test on end-to-end production stack.
Production claim: only after Sprint 4.

## SECTION 8: CHEAP DECISIVE TEST

The cheapest and most informative single Sprint 2 test is the KB-SHARD-REAL test (Section 4.4):
encode FB15K-237 entity embeddings (TransE, publicly available) via PCA-whitening to substrate
N, construct 40 relation-based shards, test recall@1.

Reasons this is the decisive test:
(a) PP-313 has the highest P_deflated (0.50) and the most tractable failure mode (KGE correlation
    is the dominant risk, and it is quantifiable).
(b) The FB15K-237 data is publicly available and the KGE embeddings are downloadable in minutes.

1. Sprint 1 primitives are demo-ready at the mechanism level. For a demo that shows "the

   becomes a qualified product claim that can be demonstrated against LLM-baseline comparison.

## PLAIN-LANGUAGE SUMMARY

Sprint 1 ran four experiments, all on synthetic data, and all passed. The results confirm
that the substrate algebra is implemented correctly and works under ideal conditions. That is
a real and meaningful result: it establishes that the mechanism is sound.

What Sprint 1 cannot tell us is whether the mechanism works when the inputs come from real
encoders, real knowledge bases, real sensors, or real text. These inputs are noisier, more
correlated, and more ambiguous than synthetic inputs, and the gap is predictably large.

The biggest risk is the image-schema primitive (PP-316): real abstract concepts are
simultaneously classified under multiple schema categories, which will reduce cluster purity
substantially. The smallest risk is the KB-shard primitive (PP-313): the failure mode
(correlated KGE inputs) is well-understood and has a known fix (increase N or add whitening).

Sprint 2 tests for all four primitives can be run in roughly 2-3 CPU days total, using
publicly available data (FB15K-237, ConceptNet, PyBullet simulations). These tests should
happen before any production claims are made for these primitives. The realistic P_deflated
for Sprint 2 validation is 0.28-0.50 per primitive, with an average of 0.40.
