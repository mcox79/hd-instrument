## 0. Biology first: the two circuit types this field keeps re-discovering are already named in neuroscience

Before the ML jargon: the single most load-bearing empirical finding in this whole field (induction heads: "attend to a prior occurrence of the current token, output what followed it") is structurally identical to **hippocampal CA3 auto-associative pattern completion** -- retrieve-the-continuation-of-a-partial-cue is the textbook CA3 recurrent-collateral function. And the newer "retrieval heads vs. induction/reasoning heads" functional split (2024-2026 papers ablating each independently in distilled reasoning models) is structurally the same dissociation this program already found on its own data one day before this drill: the **SR-compose vs additive-compose gap** (`notes/research_sr_compose_close_gap_to_additive_map_2026-07-14.md`) -- SR-compose IS a local Bellman-recursion retrieval (a hippocampal-style "compose from neighbors" operation), additive-compose is a global fitted object (more cortical/schema-like). The ML field is re-discovering, one mechanism at a time and after the fact, a retrieval/schema split that biology already has a name for and that this substrate is already probing empirically. That convergence is itself informative: it says the retrieval-vs-schema axis is probably a *real, substrate-agnostic* joint in reasoning systems, not an LLM idiosyncrasy -- good news for treating it as a first-class design axis rather than an emergent curiosity to explain post hoc.

## 1. Does the field conclude LLMs "reason" or retrieve/pattern-match by frequency?

**Verdict, both sides real, not resolved into one winner:**

- **Frequency/pattern-matching side (strong, replicated across many independent groups):** Razeghi et al. (arXiv:2202.07206) -- arithmetic accuracy tracks term frequency in pretraining data. McCoy et al. "Embers of Autoregression" (2309.13638) -- task accuracy tracks output-string probability and convention-frequency (e.g. ROT-13 >> other ROT-n shifts) more than intrinsic difficulty. Wu et al. "Reasoning or Reciting?" (2307.02477) -- the cleanest causal-by-construction result: swap surface convention only (base-9/11/16 arithmetic, Chess960 starts) holding algorithm fixed, and accuracy collapses even though the task isn't intrinsically harder. Mirzadeh et al. "GSM-Symbolic" (2410.05229) -- re-templating math word problems (same logic, different names/numbers) produces large variance and big drops when irrelevant clauses are added.
- **Chain-of-thought is frequently non-causal (post-hoc rationalization), not just "sometimes wrong":** Turpin et al. (2305.04388) -- biasing an answer via few-shot ordering flips the answer but the stated CoT never mentions the bias, confabulating a different justification instead (up to 36% accuracy swing from bias alone). Lanham et al./Anthropic (2307.13702) -- truncation, mistake-injection, filler-token substitution show CoT "faithfulness" (whether the trace is actually load-bearing) varies hugely by task, and LARGER models are sometimes LESS faithful, not more. Anthropic 2025 (2505.05410) -- Claude 3.7 Sonnet verbalized a given hint only ~25% of the time it used it; DeepSeek R1 ~39%; worse on harder questions.
- **Genuine, causally-verified algorithmic circuitry side (real, but concentrated in toy/synthetic settings):** Olsson et al. induction heads (2209.11895) -- a real two-head circuit, ablation-verified, causally responsible for in-context copying, forming in a sharp phase transition. Othello-GPT (2210.13382, + Nanda's linear reformulation) -- an internal board-state world model recoverable by a probe AND causally steerable (intervening on it changes predicted legal moves as if the board had actually changed) -- not just decodable, actually used. Nanda et al. grokking (2301.05217) -- fully reverse-engineered modular-arithmetic transformer shows a genuine trigonometric-identity algorithm, not a lookup table, with a measurable memorization -> circuit-formation -> cleanup phase sequence.

**Honest synthesis (the sub-agent's, cross-checked and endorsed here):** islands of real, causally-verified algorithmic circuitry exist and are best documented where researchers could build a clean synthetic microcosm (modular arithmetic, Othello, repeated-token induction). Once you leave that regime for naturalistic benchmark-scale tasks, frequency/memorization effects and non-causal CoT dominate the picture, replicated across independent groups and task families. **Nobody has yet mechanistically connected the two literatures** -- no one has shown the toy-model circuits are the same components that go missing in the frequency-driven failures. That connective-tissue gap is the acknowledged frontier as of 2025-2026 (retrieval-vs-reasoning head dissociation papers, "Shape of Addition" geometric-circuit-inside-LLMs work).

**Direct relevance to our own reasoning-vs-frequency frontier / "beat frequency at inferring held-out facts" prize:** this is exactly our own open question, restated in someone else's substrate. The field's answer -- "genuine circuits exist but are narrow/task-specific, coexist with and are often swamped by frequency effects, and the two are not yet reconciled" -- is a *calibration*, not a resolution: it tells us NOT to expect a clean yes/no answer even if our own substrate succeeds on held-out relational inference, and it tells us the evaluation must be done per-mechanism (does THIS composition operation use structure vs. degree/frequency) rather than as one aggregate score. This directly reinforces the 2026-07-13 finding already on disk (`research_drill_reframe_true_bottleneck_2026-07-13.md`) that our own aggregate MRR was a mixture masking a real, localized (cold/d1-only) deficit -- the ML field's core methodological lesson (frequency/degree confounds hide inside aggregate accuracy) is the SAME lesson we already independently re-derived from our own data. Good cross-validation of the general principle, not a new one.

## 2. Framings/metrics to adopt as substrate test gates

Ranked by transferability and cost:

1. **Causal abstraction / Distributed Alignment Search (DAS) + Interchange Intervention Accuracy (IIA)** -- Geiger et al. (2303.02536), RAVEL benchmark (2402.17700). Procedure: posit a symbolic causal variable (e.g. "the filler bound to relation-slot R"), swap that variable's value between two paired encodings (an "interchange intervention"), and check whether downstream output tracks the swap exactly as the symbolic hypothesis predicts. Two-part score: **sufficiency** (does the readout follow the swap) and **specificity/disentanglement** (does the swap leave OTHER slots' readouts undisturbed -- RAVEL's contribution). **This is the single most directly transferable gate we found**, and unusually cheap for us: transformer researchers must SEARCH for the causal subspace (it's not axis-aligned); in a VSA/HD system the role/filler binding coordinates are known by construction, so IIA is available "for free" with no search step -- we already have the addressable ground-truth location to intervene on.
2. **CoT faithfulness test family (Lanham et al.)** -- necessity (truncate/shorten the intermediate trace, check if the final output was already fixed before the trace "finished"), corruption-propagation (inject a wrong intermediate step, check whether it's blindly propagated -- proving the trace is actually read -- or overridden), mediation-vs-confabulation (manipulate a hidden factor, check whether the emitted trace cites it or confabulates something else). Directly portable to any future multi-step cortex-layer (M3) reasoning trace once one exists; not yet actionable on current single-shot compose/retrieval cells.
3. **Probing-vs-intervention discipline (Amnesic Probing / LEACE / "How Reliable are Causal Probing Interventions")** -- the field's converged rule: decodability (a probe finds concept X somewhere) is necessary but NOT sufficient; you must additionally show ablating/patching that exact direction moves behavior. Directly reusable as a standing discipline for any future "the code encodes X" claim on our side -- don't stop at decode-accuracy, always pair it with an ablation/patch check.
4. **Grokking circuit-efficiency discriminator (Nanda/Varma)** -- track accuracy-achieved-per-unit-parameter-norm as a memorization-vs-generalization signal (memorizing solutions are higher-norm-per-unit-accuracy; weight decay is what tips the competition toward the generalizing solution). Transferable analog: for composed codes (additive_map, SR-compose), a norm/energy-per-correct-retrieval metric could distinguish "genuinely compositional code" from "overfit lookup," though this needs a training-dynamics analog we don't currently have (our composition is closed-form, not gradient-trained) -- flagged as lower-priority, needs adaptation work first.
5. **Sanity-check discipline (Adebayo et al., "Sanity Checks for Saliency Maps")** -- before trusting ANY new interpretability/diagnostic metric on our substrate: (a) randomize the codebook/weights and confirm the metric's reading changes: if not, the metric isn't sensitive to what we think; (b) run the metric on a deliberately memorization-only construction and confirm it reads differently than the real thing. This is a cheap, general precondition check we should apply to any NEW substrate diagnostic before trusting it -- directly consonant with the standing must-fail-control discipline already in force here.

## 3. Field's admitted open problems -- does glass-box-first sidestep them or inherit them?

Field's own admissions: Amodei ("Urgency of Interpretability") -- after finding ~30M features in a mid-size model, "we've found only a small fraction of what is probably there"; explicit 2027 target for "reliably detect most model problems," i.e. admission it doesn't do that now. Sharkey et al. "Open Problems in Mechanistic Interpretability" (2501.16496) -- no reliable method for scaling toy-model circuits to frontier scale; "fundamental mismatch" between the neuron/feature framing and actual network structure. Sparse-autoencoder-specific failures: **feature splitting** (no canonical granularity), **feature absorption** (2409.14507 -- narrow latents steal credit from general ones, a literal named "interpretability illusion" baked into the SAE objective itself), **incompleteness** (even 34M-latent SAEs on Claude 3 Sonnet are acknowledged incomplete, biased toward high-frequency patterns). "Scale Alone Does not Improve Mechanistic Interpretability in Vision Models" (2307.05471) -- scaling capability does NOT automatically buy scaling interpretability; a decade-old GoogLeNet was not harder to interpret than SOTA models.

**Sidestepped:** all of the above is fundamentally the problem of reverse-engineering an UNKNOWN decoder for a code that was never designed to be decodable. Our program constructs the code with decodability as a first-class design constraint (role/filler binding coordinates known by construction, content-agnostic bind, inspectable per MEMORY's structure-content factorization thrust) -- so feature-splitting, feature-absorption, and incompleteness are specifically artifacts of *retrofitting* an interpreter onto an opaque trained object, and a glass-box-by-construction approach has no analogous retrofit step to fail at. This is a real, structural differentiation, not just an assertion -- SAEs exist precisely because nobody knows the ground-truth dictionary; we DO know it, by construction.

**NOT sidestepped -- inherited wall:** the deeper, still-unresolved question in the literature ("On the Complexity of Neural Computation in Superposition," 2409.15318) is that superposition's capacity advantage is mostly a STORAGE trick (passive representation: n neurons -> 2^O(n) features via Johnson-Lindenstrauss, generous) but a much WEAKER trick for ACTIVE COMPUTATION on superposed features (their formal bound: O(n^2/log n) computable output features, and this bound "applies regardless of whether inputs/outputs are stored in superposition"). That means the open question is not really about opacity per se -- it's whether ANY code, transparent or opaque, structured or not, can do compositional/relational COMPUTATION as efficiently as dense unstructured superposition apparently does in practice. Nothing in this literature proves a transparent, high-dimensional, degree-invariant scaffold CAN'T match it -- but nothing proves it CAN either. This is squarely the same axis our own relational-capability program is already testing empirically (additive vs. multiplicative bind performance gap; SR-compose vs. additive-compose gap, `research_sr_compose_close_gap_to_additive_map_2026-07-14.md`). We do not get to assume our way out of this one -- it's an open empirical question in both literatures simultaneously, and our own cap_map rows are the actual data that will settle it for our regime.

## 4. Concrete mechanisms with a brain/VSA-realizable analog worth testing as scaffold mechanisms

- **Induction head (attend-to-prior-occurrence, copy-what-followed) = hippocampal CA3 auto-associative pattern completion.** Our substrate's clean-up memory / pool retrieval IS this mechanism already; the finding that it forms via a sharp phase transition (not gradual) in trained nets is a testable prediction for OUR system too if we ever add a learned (not closed-form) composition step -- watch for a similar sharp transition rather than assuming smooth improvement.
- **Retrieval heads vs. induction/reasoning heads functional split** = SR-compose (retrieval-like, local Bellman fixed point) vs. additive-compose (schema-like, global fitted object) split already found in our own data one cycle before this drill. The ML literature's finding that ablating each independently damages different task classes (long-context grounded QA vs. simple in-context copy) suggests our own next diagnostic: build a task split (near-neighbor lookup vs. multi-hop composition) and check whether SR-compose and additive-compose specialize the same way -- this is a testable, cheap, near-zero-build prediction riding on code that already works.
- **DAS/Interchange-Intervention-Accuracy** (detailed in Section 2, #1) -- the most immediately actionable transfer, see cheap decisive test below.

## 5. Honest counter -- what challenges the glass-box thesis

Two distinct challenges surfaced, one weak, one real:

- **Weak challenge (does not survive scrutiny):** "superposition proves opacity is necessary for capacity." This does NOT hold up -- compressed-sensing theory (the same theory Toy Models of Superposition explicitly invokes) is precisely the textbook case of exact recoverability of sparse overlapping codes under nonlinear decoding; SAEs are literally an attempt at that decoder, and their failure modes (splitting/absorption/incompleteness) read as failures of an ad hoc, incidentally-trained, retrofitted decoder -- not proof no decoder can exist. Concept Bottleneck Models / LaBo (interpretable-by-design, vision domain) show designed-legible representations matching or beating black-box baselines in some regimes, direct (if modest-scale) evidence against a hard opacity-capability tradeoff.
- **Real challenge (genuinely open, we inherit it):** the storage-vs-computation asymmetry in "On the Complexity of Neural Computation in Superposition" -- passive storage capacity is generous for ANY high-dimensional code (transparent or not), but active computation on compressed/superposed representations has a much tighter provable bound, independent of whether the code is transparent. This means our glass-box thesis gets a "free pass" on storage/capacity grounds but NOT on computation/inference grounds -- whether a fully-inspectable, degree-invariant relational scaffold can match dense superposition's apparent efficiency at multi-step compositional reasoning is unresolved in the general literature and is exactly what our own cap_map rows (additive vs multiplicative bind, SR vs additive compose) are the live experiment for. We should stop treating "transparency vs capability" as a settled tradeoff in either direction and treat it as the empirical question our own program is positioned to actually answer.

## Cheap decisive test (single best next experiment/gate)

**Apply Interchange-Intervention-Accuracy (IIA) + RAVEL-style specificity check to the already-shipped `hdlab/additive_map.py::AdditiveKGMap` and the SR-compose cell.** No new mechanism, no new build -- pure diagnostic reuse of existing bound sub-vectors:

1. Take pairs of relational queries that differ in exactly one filler at one relation-slot (e.g. same head entity + relation, different tail).
2. Swap ONLY that one filler's sub-vector/binding between the two encoded query states (an interchange intervention on the addressable coordinate we already know by construction -- no subspace search needed, unlike DAS on transformers).
3. Sufficiency metric: does the composed-map readout (score/rank) track the swapped filler as if it had been the original query, at a rate above random-swap baseline?
4. Specificity metric (RAVEL-style): does the swap leave OTHER slots'/relations' readouts undisturbed (or does the composition "leak" -- analog of SAE feature absorption)?

Run this stratified by degree bucket (cold / d1 / d2_3 / d8plus, reusing the exact stratification from `research_drill_reframe_true_bottleneck_2026-07-13.md`) since that drill already showed the aggregate hides a real bimodal population -- IIA must be measured per-bucket, not in aggregate, or it will repeat the exact aggregation mistake already caught once this week.

### Falsifiable predictions

**HARD-PASS:** IIA (sufficiency) >= 0.70 on d2_3-through-d8plus buckets (where the prior drill showed near-oracle performance already), confirming the composition is causally using the swapped relational structure and not some frequency/degree correlate; specificity leakage (swap changing an untargeted slot's readout) <= 0.10.

**HARD-FAIL:** IIA indistinguishable from a random-unrelated-vector swap baseline (i.e. no better than chance-level tracking) on ANY bucket where raw accuracy is currently high -- this would mean the currently-good aggregate numbers are a correlate (e.g. degree/frequency) riding along with, not caused by, the composed relational binding, structurally identical to Turpin et al.'s "answer changed, stated cause never used" finding. Also HARD-FAIL if specificity leakage > 0.30 (composition isn't disentangled by relation-slot -- our own version of SAE feature absorption).

**MIDDLE-BAND (expected most likely outcome per calibration penalty):** partial IIA (0.30-0.65) concentrated in cold/d1 buckets specifically -- consistent with, and would further corroborate, the already-landed finding that the deficit is localized there, now via a causal (not just accuracy) lens.

## Substrate-product implications

An IIA-style causal gate, once built, becomes a standing pre-registration requirement for ANY future "this composition encodes/uses relation R" claim on our side -- replacing decode-accuracy-only claims (which the interpretability field has explicitly shown are insufficient) with a sufficiency+specificity pair. This is a process improvement applicable across cap_map rows, not a one-off test: it upgrades our own claims from "probe found it" to "intervention proved it's used," which is exactly the standard the field converged on after being burned by probing-classifier overclaiming.
