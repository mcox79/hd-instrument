## (a) HEADLINE

**The biology and the classical engineering literature converge on the SAME shape of answer we already found for relation-grounding: there is a real, buildable middle tier between "closed schema" and "fully open text," and a real, cited wall past it.** Children do not jump from known-vocabulary to open text in one step — they ride a specific, empirically-ordered sequence (function-word scaffolding first, syntactic-frame-driven OOV-word category inference second, possessive/existential constructions third because their SYNTAX is closed even though their NOUN SLOT is open, wh-/yes-no questions fourth, and non-local/discourse-dependent pronoun resolution last — still maturing at age 7-10 and explicitly world-knowledge-dependent). Classical (rule-based + statistical, no neural network) NLP independently confirms a **precision-favoring, recall-limited** envelope on open text — the honest, benchmark-verified ceiling is **P: 60-85% / R: 30-55%** on curated simple-sentence slices (NOT the ~70-85%P/50-70%R figure originally hypothesized in the task brief — real classical Open IE systems (ClausIE, OLLIE) top out near **R=0.496 on CaRB**, the best classical recall ever benchmarked, with P in the 0.40-0.57 range on out-of-domain text). This is not a disappointing correction — it is a **structural match** to our own design: our closed-schema parser is already precision-favoring (P~1.0) with abstain-heavy recall; the classical open-text envelope says extending outward should PRESERVE that shape (keep precision high, accept recall in the 30-55% band) rather than chase an unrealistic high-recall target. A concrete, fully glass-box-legal toolchain exists TODAY to take the smallest step: a classical POS tagger (NLTK's averaged-perceptron tagger or TnT, ~96-97% on Penn Treebank) + a classical dependency parser (MaltParser or the Collins/BLLIP PCFG parser) + ReVerb-style shallow POS-pattern triple extraction, evaluated against Universal Dependencies English-EWT/GUM gold parses, applied to OneStopEnglish or Simple-English-Wikipedia-register text. The one sharp legal trap: ClausIE and OLLIE are glass-box-legal ONLY if explicitly pinned to a classical parser backend — both tools, and most modern NLP toolkits generally (spaCy default, Stanza, Stanford's neural dependency parser), silently default to a neural component that would break glass-box legality if not pinned away from.

## (b) Cheap decisive test

Before building a full open-text pipeline, run ONE cheap, cheaply-falsifiable test that isolates the single highest-value, lowest-risk extension: **OOV-noun coverage via POS-tagging + suffix morphology, holding the existing closed-schema grammar rules fixed.**

- **Arm CURRENT:** the existing closed-schema symbolic parser, unchanged, run against a held-out slice of OneStopEnglish or Simple-Wikipedia sentences containing nouns NOT in the hand-built lexicon. Expected: near-total abstain (the parser has no lexicon entry to hang a role on).
- **Arm POS-AUGMENTED:** identical grammar rules, but any unknown noun slot is now filled by a classical POS tagger's tag (NOUN/PROPN) + a cheap suffix/word-shape morphology check (capitalization, common noun suffixes -tion/-ment/-er/-ist, plural -s) instead of requiring a lexicon hit — i.e., the parser now accepts "this fills a NOUN slot" as sufficient to proceed, without knowing WHAT the noun means.
- Measure: (i) sentence-level coverage (non-abstain rate) on the OOV-noun slice; (ii) precision on the sentences that DO get extracted (does accepting untyped nouns introduce wrong extractions, or does the existing grammar's syntactic constraints still gate correctly).

**HARD-PASS:** coverage increases by >=15 percentage points over Arm CURRENT while precision on the newly-covered sentences stays >=0.90 (the precision-favoring design survives the OOV extension, matching the classical-envelope prediction that structure, not lexicon lookup, is what was doing the precision work all along).
**HARD-FAIL:** either coverage gain is <5 points (POS+morphology signal is too weak at this scale to matter) OR precision on newly-covered sentences drops below 0.75 (accepting untyped noun slots breaks more parses than it fixes — would indicate the closed-schema grammar's precision was silently relying on the lexicon as a disambiguation filter, not just a role-filler).

## (e) Substrate-product implications

1. **Do not chase the originally-hypothesized ~70-85%P/50-70%R envelope — target P:60-85%/R:30-55% instead, and treat recall in the 30% range as a SUCCESS, not a shortfall,** on any open-text extraction cell. This is the corrected, benchmark-grounded ceiling (ClausIE's R=0.496 on CaRB is the best classical recall ever reported), and it is a favorable match to our already-precision-favoring, abstain-heavy design philosophy — no redesign needed to accommodate it.
2. **Smallest concrete buildable step:** NLTK's averaged-perceptron POS tagger (or TnT) + suffix/word-shape OOV features, feeding untyped NOUN/PROPN slots into the EXISTING closed-schema grammar rules (no new parser, no new schema) — this is Prediction 1 / the cheap decisive test, and is buildable with zero new infrastructure beyond a POS tagger call.
3. **Second step (if step 2 passes):** add a ReVerb-style shallow POS-pattern triple extractor as a SEPARATE, parallel arm for sentences the closed-schema parser still fully abstains on (not a replacement) — this respects the existing gate architecture (precision-1.0 core + a lower-precision, lower-recall auxiliary arm feeding a separate confidence tier) rather than blending mechanisms.
4. **Existential-there and possessive constructions are candidate "cheap wins"** — closed syntactic pattern, open noun slot — recommend building these via targeted grammar-rule extension BEFORE investing in the heavier classical-parser/Open-IE machinery needed for wh-/yes-no questions and general OOV-verb sentences, per the curriculum ordering in section 1.
5. **DO NOT attempt non-local/discourse-dependent pronoun resolution via any purely structural/statistical extension.** This is now doubly confirmed (substrate-internal 2x-drill closure + independent developmental-literature convergence) as needing cortex-layer/world-knowledge routing, not a bigger classical parser. Label it explicitly as a permanent-abstain construction for the glass-box extraction layer (see section 6), the same honest way Tier-3 relation-meaning is labeled in the sibling note.
6. **Glass-box-legal trap to build a hard guard against:** if any future cell reaches for ClausIE or OLLIE (or any "off-the-shelf Open IE" library), it MUST explicitly pin the parser backend to a classical (non-neural) dependency parser (MaltParser, MSTParser, or a Collins/BLLIP-style PCFG parser) — the default backend for most modern toolkits, including these two, is neural and would silently break glass-box legality without an explicit pin-check in the cell's pre-registration.
7. **Evaluate against Universal Dependencies English-EWT/GUM gold parses** for any POS-tagging/parsing accuracy claim on this pipeline — this gives a real, external, non-self-graded benchmark rather than only self-reported precision on our own held-out slice, closing a methodological gap the current closed-schema parser evaluation does not have (it is graded against its own hand-built gold set, not an independent treebank).

## 1. Biology-first: how children get from known-vocabulary to open text

**Syntactic bootstrapping gives category + rough role for an unfamiliar word from its sentence frame, before meaning, and startlingly early.** Fisher's structure-mapping account (Fisher 2010, developmental origins review, PMC7004857) and the classic Gleitman/Naigles line of work converge: children as young as ~14 months use the syntactic frame a novel word appears in (transitivity, argument count, position) to infer its rough grammatical category and role — noun vs. verb, agent vs. patient — without needing to know what the word MEANS yet or drawing on world knowledge. This is the direct developmental analog of Prediction 1: accept a syntactic role for an unknown item before resolving its content.

**Function words are CAUSALLY load-bearing, not incidental scaffolding.** Shipley, Smith & Gleitman's classic 1969 removal experiment (and its lineage) directly manipulated the presence/absence of function words (articles, auxiliaries, prepositions) in comprehension tasks and found comprehension collapses disproportionately when function words are stripped relative to content words — i.e., closed-class function words carry more of the STRUCTURAL parsing load than their small vocabulary size would suggest. This argues for making function-word coverage a PREREQUISITE gate (Rung 1) before OOV-content-word inference is attempted, not something addressed after the fact.

**Empirically-established developmental order of the specific constructions in scope** (Brown 1973's morpheme-order tradition; Rowland & Pine 2003 and Santelmann 2002 on question-form acquisition; Song & Fisher 2005 on possessive/pronoun-related structure):
- **Earliest:** possessive -'s and simple what/where-questions.
- **Middle:** who/how/why-questions and yes/no-question auxiliary-inversion.
- **Later:** which/whose-questions and pronominal possessives (his/her/their as opposed to bare 's).
- **Latest, and qualitatively different in kind:** non-local pronoun resolution (an antecedent not locally given in the same clause) — still maturing at ages 7-10, and the literature explicitly ties residual errors at this age to WORLD-KNOWLEDGE-DEPENDENT cues (implicit causality biases — e.g. "John blamed Bill because HE..." requires verb-semantics/causal-role knowledge, not just syntax) rather than syntactic immaturity.
- **Gap, explicitly flagged:** existential "there is/are" acquisition order could not be independently placed in the surveyed literature this cycle — treated as an open item, not a documented finding (see section (d) gap note).

**This directly implies a concrete rung ordering** (not merely "these five things exist" but "this is the order the biology suggests introducing them"):

- **Rung 1 — function-word scaffolding.** Ensure closed-class function words (articles, prepositions, auxiliaries, wh-words) are fully and reliably parsed FIRST — per Shipley/Smith/Gleitman, this is the causal substrate everything else leans on.
- **Rung 2 — syntactic-frame OOV inference.** Accept an unknown content word's syntactic role/category from its frame position (Fisher/Gleitman structure-mapping) — this is Prediction 1 / the cheap decisive test.
- **Rung 3 — possessive + existential constructions.** Closed syntactic pattern, open noun slot; possessive-'s is empirically among the EARLIEST-acquired constructions in scope, suggesting real tractability; existential-there's placement is a flagged unknown, hence Prediction 4's partial-structural-reasoning basis.
- **Rung 4 — questions (wh- and yes/no).** Empirically later than possessives; what/where first, then who/how/why + auxiliary-inversion, then which/whose.
- **Rung 5 — non-local pronoun resolution.** Deferred as a DISTINCT, harder subsystem, not a natural extension of the parser — matches the substrate's own already-landed closure (section (d)).

## 2. Where structure hits a wall vs. where it suffices

Sections (a) and (d) above already state the core finding plainly: syntactic/structural cues (frame position, morphology, closed-class function words) carry OOV word-category inference, existential/possessive pattern recognition, and question-form parsing — no world knowledge required for any of these, per the lane 1 literature. Non-local pronoun resolution is the one construction in scope where the literature is explicit and convergent that structure alone is insufficient — implicit-causality and discourse-coherence cues (which verb's semantics implies which participant is more likely to be re-referenced) are necessary, and these require exactly the kind of world/situational knowledge the substrate's already-closed Q2-coref capability box does not have access to. This is a clean, two-way split (structure suffices vs. structure insufficient), not a fuzzy gradient — consistent with, and now doubly-evidenced alongside, the three-tier relation-grounding split in the cross-thread synthesis.

## 3. The classical (non-neural) NLP engineering envelope — corrected

**Initial framing was optimistic; the benchmark-verified numbers are lower on recall and this is a GOOD match to our design, not a shortfall.** The lane 2/lane 3 lit-scan found:

- **POS tagging:** classical (HMM/CRF, non-neural) taggers — Brants' TnT (2000) and CRF-based successors — report ~96-97% accuracy on Penn Treebank-style benchmarks (per the ACL Wiki POS-tagging state-of-the-art tracking page). OOV-word accuracy drops roughly 10-11 points relative to in-vocabulary accuracy; suffix/word-shape/capitalization features and gazetteer lookups close most, not all, of that gap.
- **Open Information Extraction — the corrected headline number.** On the standard OIE evaluation benchmarks — OIE2016 (Stanovsky & Dagan 2016) and CaRB (Bhardwaj, Aggarwal & Mausam 2019) — classical (pre-neural, rule/statistics-based) systems ClausIE (Del Corro & Gemulla 2013) and OLLIE report **Precision roughly 0.40-0.57, Recall roughly 0.12-0.50**, with **ClausIE's R=0.496 on CaRB being the best classical Open-IE recall ever independently benchmarked.** The originally-hypothesized ~70-85%P/50-70%R envelope from the task brief is NOT supported by these independent, out-of-domain benchmark numbers — it is closer to what these systems achieve on their OWN curated demonstration examples than on an independent held-out benchmark.
- **Honest composite ceiling for our use case:** **Precision 60-85% / Recall 30-55%**, with the upper end of both ranges reachable ONLY on curated, simple-sentence slices (short declaratives, minimal embedding) — NOT on general-domain newswire-style text, which is what OIE2016/CaRB actually test. Our target register (early-reader, simple-sentence, controlled-vocabulary-adjacent) is narrower and simpler than CaRB's source text, so lane 3 flags an unmeasured but plausible register advantage — we should likely land toward the favorable end of this range, but this is an extrapolation, not a citation, until tested (this is exactly what the cheap decisive test and Prediction 3 are designed to check).
- **Why this is a GOOD result for us, not a disappointing one:** our existing closed-schema parser is ALREADY precision-favoring (P~1.0) with an abstain-heavy design that accepts incomplete recall in exchange for near-zero false positives. The classical open-text envelope says the SAME shape of tradeoff (precision-favoring, recall-limited) is the honest ceiling for rule/statistics-based extraction generally — we are not fighting the grain of the engineering literature by staying abstain-heavy on the recall side; we are already positioned exactly where the classical literature says the achievable frontier is.

## 4. The glass-box-legal line — what's legal, what secretly isn't

- **Legal (classical, no neural network):** NLTK's built-in averaged-perceptron tagger and TnT-style HMM taggers (POS); MaltParser (transition-based, feature/SVM) and MSTParser (graph-based) and the Collins/Charniak-lineage / BLLIP PCFG parsers (dependency/constituency); ReVerb (POS-pattern-based Open IE — fully legal by construction, since it only consumes POS tags and shallow chunks, no parser dependency at all); Morfessor (unsupervised morphology induction), Porter/Snowball stemmers, and WordNet's morphy function (all rule/statistics-based morphological analysis).
- **Legal ONLY if explicitly pinned:** ClausIE and OLLIE — both are clause-/dependency-parse-based Open IE systems whose DEFAULT pipeline in most current distributions pulls in a neural dependency parser; they remain glass-box-legal only if the parser backend is explicitly swapped for a classical parser (MaltParser/MSTParser/BLLIP) and that pin is verified, not assumed, at cell-registration time.
- **Illegal by default (secretly neural even though the container library sounds classical):** spaCy's default English pipeline (its default parser and tagger are neural/transformer-based, despite spaCy being usable in fully rule-based configurations if you go out of your way to disable the neural components), Stanford's Stanza toolkit (neural throughout), and Stanford CoreNLP's modern neural dependency parser (`nndep`) — CoreNLP DOES still ship its much older Java-based statistical constituency parser (the Klein & Manning PCFG lineage) as a legal classical alternative, but the DEFAULT modern CoreNLP pipeline is not that component.
- **Corpora (all free, all directly usable):** Universal Dependencies English-EWT and GUM (gold-standard POS + dependency-parse annotations — the correct external benchmark, per implication 7); OneStopEnglish (CC BY-SA, three-difficulty-level parallel simple-English text, useful as a difficulty ramp); CHILDES (free with registration, real child-directed and child speech, useful as a naturalistic register source); Simple English Wikipedia (open, CC BY-SA, large volume of controlled-simplicity prose, directly overlapping with the sibling curriculum note's vocabulary/grammar sourcing).

## 5. The concrete smallest step

**Tagger:** NLTK's averaged-perceptron POS tagger (or TnT) — free, mature, well-documented OOV behavior. **Parser (only needed once past step 2/Prediction 1):** MaltParser, pinned explicitly as the backend if a clause-based Open-IE tool (ClausIE/OLLIE) is later added; ReVerb needs no parser at all and can be added first without this dependency. **Corpus:** OneStopEnglish or Simple English Wikipedia for the extraction text itself; Universal Dependencies English-EWT/GUM for gold-standard evaluation of the tagger/parser components independent of our own extraction pipeline's self-graded precision. **Morphology:** Morfessor or Snowball stemmer for OOV suffix features feeding the cheap decisive test in section (b). This is a fully free, fully open-license, fully glass-box-legal stack, buildable with existing widely-available libraries — no licensing negotiation, no proprietary corpus, no neural component anywhere in the chain if the ClausIE/OLLIE pin (section 4) is respected.

## 6. Permanent-abstain gap list (constructions that will correctly ABSTAIN, not a fixable coverage gap)

1. **Non-local / discourse-dependent pronoun resolution requiring world knowledge or implicit-causality inference** — doubly confirmed: substrate-internal 2x-drill capability closure (Q2 coref, `skunkworks_landed_VET_..._2026-06-28`) AND independent developmental literature (still maturing at 7-10y, explicitly world-knowledge-gated). This is the single most load-bearing permanent-abstain item — do not re-attempt via bigger classical parsers or richer structural features; the wall is evidence-TYPE, not evidence-QUANTITY (same shape as the relation-meaning wall in the sibling note).
2. **Fine-grained / idiosyncratic word meaning for genuinely novel content words** — matches the sibling relation-grounding note's Tier-3 finding; syntactic-frame bootstrapping (Rung 2) gets category and rough role, never exact denotation, without an external anchor.
3. **Sentences requiring world-knowledge disambiguation of genuine syntactic ambiguity** (e.g. PP-attachment cases resolvable only by knowing which reading is plausible in the world, not by any syntactic cue) — a structural super-set of the pronoun-resolution wall; expect the same abstain behavior for the same underlying reason.
4. **Idiomatic / non-compositional expressions** — by construction, any glass-box compositional grammar treats these as parse failures or mis-parses; this is a known, accepted limitation of rule-based systems generally in the classical-NLP literature, not specific to us.
5. **Answering wh-/yes-no questions (as opposed to correctly PARSING their syntactic structure)** — Rung 4 covers structural parsing of question form; producing a correct ANSWER requires the underlying fact/knowledge base to already contain the relevant grounded content, which is a downstream retrieval question, not an extraction-pipeline question, and is out of scope for this drill.
