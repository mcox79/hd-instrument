## 1. The feature-ambiguity claim, stated precisely

**ESTABLISHED (this is how proponents state it, consistent across sources):** Bussey, Saksida & Murray's "perceptual-mnemonic/feature conjunction" (PMFC) model holds that perirhinal cortex sits at the apex of a hierarchical continuum of representations running caudal-to-rostral through the ventral visual stream (V1 → V2 → V4 → TEO → TE → perirhinal cortex), with representational complexity increasing at each stage. Early/posterior stages encode simple, low-order visual features; perirhinal cortex encodes **conjunctions** of those features (whole-object/complex-feature-conjunction representations). "Feature ambiguity" is defined operationally as a property of a *visual discrimination problem*, not of a single stimulus: a discrimination is feature-ambiguous when individual low-order features are shared between the rewarded (S+) and unrewarded (S–) stimuli, i.e., when a feature that predicts reward on some trials/stimuli predicts non-reward on others. Because no single feature is diagnostic, the problem cannot be solved by simple feature-based (elemental/linear) association and instead requires a conjunctive ("configural") representation that binds multiple features into a single higher-order unit that is unique to the correct object. The model predicts perirhinal-lesioned animals/patients will be selectively impaired as feature ambiguity increases, while feature-based discriminations of matched raw difficulty (e.g., color, shape, size oddity) should be spared.

- Key citation: Bussey TJ, Saksida LM (2002) "The organization of visual object representations: a connectionist model of effects of lesions in perirhinal cortex." *Eur J Neurosci* — companion connectionist-model paper (pubmed 11849301, not independently verified in full here).
- Review statement: Bussey TJ, Saksida LM, Murray EA (2005) "The Perceptual-Mnemonic/Feature Conjunction Model of Perirhinal Cortex Function." *Behavioural Brain Research* — cited widely as the canonical statement (secondary source only for me; I did not get its abstract text directly).

**Operational designs used to manipulate feature ambiguity (ESTABLISHED as design descriptions, drawn from multiple studies converging on the same scheme):**
- **Minimum / Intermediate / Maximum Feature Ambiguity conditions** (Bussey, Saksida & Murray 2002, monkeys): concurrent-discrimination stimulus pairs constructed by blending two "parent" images so that increasing proportions of visual features are shared between the rewarded and unrewarded compound stimuli.
- **Morphing paradigms** (Norman & Eacott 2004; Clark et al. 2011, rats): two-choice discrimination between two base images with 14 graded morph levels between them, creating a continuum from minimal to maximal feature overlap.
- **Biconditional discrimination / transverse patterning** (Buckley & Gaffan 1998; rodent/monkey configural-learning literature): each stimulus is rewarded in one pairing and unrewarded in another (A+ vs B–, B+ vs C–, C+ vs A–), so no single stimulus/feature has a constant reward value — the paradigm case of "negative patterning"/biconditional structure requiring configural (conjunctive) solutions.
- **Oddity discrimination with feature-overlapping vs feature-distinct sets** (Buckley, Booth, Rolls & Gaffan 2001, monkeys; Barense et al., Erez/Lee/Barense human studies): the "odd one out" among simultaneously presented near-identical exemplars (e.g., novel greebles sharing many parts) vs. easy feature-based oddity (color/shape/size).

## 2. Primary empirical evidence

### Monkey perirhinal-lesion studies

**Bussey, Saksida & Murray (2002).** "Perirhinal cortex resolves feature ambiguity in complex visual discriminations." *European Journal of Neuroscience* 15(2):365–374. DOI 10.1046/j.0953-816x.2001.01851.x. [Abstract text pulled via EuropePMC API — reasonably trustworthy tier.] Rhesus monkeys with vs. without perirhinal cortex lesions were tested across Minimum/Intermediate/Maximum feature-ambiguity concurrent discriminations. Result pattern: **no impairment at Minimum ambiguity, mild impairment at Intermediate, severe impairment at Maximum** ambiguity — a graded dose-response supporting the model. **ESTABLISHED (primary, abstract-level detail confirmed); exact n and percent-correct numbers UNVERIFIED-DETAIL** — I could not get the full-text numeric results (PDF fetches for this and the companion 2003 EJN paper both failed with 403/binary errors), so I cannot report actual animal counts or percent-correct scores for this specific paper; only the qualitative graded-impairment pattern is confirmed at abstract level.

**Bussey, Saksida & Murray (2003).** "Impairments in visual discrimination after perirhinal cortex lesions: testing 'declarative' vs. 'perceptual-mnemonic' views of perirhinal cortex function." *European Journal of Neuroscience* 17:649–660. PMID 12581183. Designed to pit the PMFC account against the "declarative memory" account (Squire's view) by testing whether impairment tracks perceptual/feature-ambiguity factors or simply the rapid/one-trial-learning demands the declarative account emphasizes. **SINGLE-STUDY, UNVERIFIED-DETAIL** — could not retrieve numeric results (PDF unreadable); described in secondary sources only.

**Buckley, Booth, Rolls & Gaffan (2001).** "Selective Perceptual Impairments After Perirhinal Cortex Ablation." *J Neurosci* 21(24):9824–9836. DOI 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.21-24-09824.2001. [Full text via PMC (PMC6763048), WebFetch-extracted — moderately trustworthy.] **n = 8 macaques (3 PRh-ablated, 5 controls)**, oddity-discrimination battery. Results: PRh group significantly impaired on **object-level oddity requiring view-invariant abstraction** (p = 0.016), on **face-identity oddity** (p = 0.036), and on **scene discrimination** (p = 0.027); **no significant impairment on simple feature-based oddity — color (p = 0.12), shape (p > 0.1), size (p > 0.1)** even matched for raw difficulty. This is one of the cleanest single-study demonstrations of the predicted feature-based/conjunctive dissociation. **ESTABLISHED (single study), n small (3 lesion animals) — note statistical power caveat.**

**Buckley & Gaffan (1998).** "Perirhinal cortex ablation impairs configural learning and paired-associate learning equally." *Neuropsychologia* 36(6):535–546. **3 cynomolgus monkeys with PRh ablation vs. 3 controls**; touchscreen alphanumeric-character stimuli; visual paired-associate task and visual configural-learning (biconditional-type) task. Finding: **PRh ablation impaired both tasks to a similar degree** (both groups made fewer errors on configural than paired-associate overall, but the lesion effect was comparable across task types). **CONTESTED interpretive relevance**: this equal-impairment pattern has historically been read by the Gaffan camp as evidence *against* a perception-specific/feature-ambiguity account (since a purely associative/relational-memory account predicts the same "equal impairment across associative-structure tasks" pattern) — i.e., this result is compatible with either the PMFC account or a pure declarative/associative-memory account, and is therefore not fully discriminating. This tension is part of the broader Bussey/Saksida–Murray vs. Gaffan/Buckley theoretical divergence noted in reviews (SINGLE-STUDY data point; the "which theory it favors" reading is CONTESTED).

**Buffalo, Stefanacci, Squire & Zola (1998).** "A reexamination of the concurrent discrimination learning task: the importance of anterior inferotemporal cortex, area TE." *Behavioral Neuroscience* 112(1):3–14. DOI 10.1037//0735-7044.112.1.3. [Abstract via EuropePMC API.] Re-analyzed histology/behavior from earlier monkey MTL-lesion studies and found that **unintended damage to area TE (immediately adjacent to and reciprocally connected with perirhinal cortex) was more predictive of concurrent-discrimination-learning impairment than damage to any MTL structure including perirhinal cortex itself.** **ESTABLISHED methodological critique**, important because it means a nontrivial fraction of the older monkey "perirhinal impairment" literature on discrimination-learning tasks may be confounded by inadvertent area-TE damage — a standing caveat on interpreting lesion-extent-outcome correlations in this literature generally, though it targets a different (paired-associate/concurrent-discrimination-learning) task family than the later PMFC feature-ambiguity paradigm specifically.

### Human studies

**Barense, Bussey, Lee, Rogers, Davies, Saksida, Murray & Graham (2005).** "Functional Specialization in the Human Medial Temporal Lobe." *J Neurosci* 25(44):10239–10246. DOI 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2704-05.2005. Compared amnesic patients with selective hippocampal damage vs. more extensive MTL damage (including perirhinal cortex) on an object-discrimination task adapted from the monkey feature-ambiguity literature. Found dissociable performance profiles between the two patient groups (arguing against a unitary declarative-memory account of MTL function). **ESTABLISHED (secondary paraphrase only — I did not get patient n or accuracy numbers directly).**

**Lee, Buckley, Pegman, Spiers, Scahill, Gaffan, Bussey, Davies, Kapur, Hodges & Graham (2005).** "Specialization in the medial temporal lobe for processing of objects and scenes." *Hippocampus* 15(6):782–797. Oddity tasks with amnesic patients: **selective hippocampal damage → impaired scene learning but spared face/dot-pattern learning; broader MTL damage (hippocampus + perirhinal) → impaired scene AND face discrimination learning, dot patterns spared.** This double dissociation (faces/complex-feature-objects need perirhinal; scenes need hippocampus) is one of the more frequently cited human building blocks of the representational-hierarchical account. **ESTABLISHED (secondary paraphrase; exact n/accuracy UNVERIFIED-DETAIL).**

**Erez, Lee & Barense (2013).** "It does not look odd to me: Perceptual impairments and eye movements in amnesic patients with medial temporal lobe damage." *Neuropsychologia* 51(1):168–180. DOI 10.1016/j.neuropsychologia.2012.11.003. [Full text via PMC, WebFetch-extracted.] **4 amnesic patients (2 selective-hippocampal: HC2, HC3; 2 broader-MTL-including-perirhinal: MTL2, MTL3) vs. 11 controls**; four-alternative oddity discrimination (novel-object greebles, familiar objects, faces, scenes), with eye-tracking. Reported accuracy table (percent correct):

| Condition | Controls | MTL2 | MTL3 | HC2 | HC3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Novel objects (greebles) | 64% | 26%* | 23%* | 51% | 71% |
| Familiar objects | 71% | 29%* | 34%* | 66% | 46%* |
| Faces | 79% | 42%* | 55%* | 75% | 75% |
| Scenes | 80% | 44%* | 44%* | 67%(†) | 59%* |

(*p<.01; †p<.05 trend). The MTL-damage (perirhinal-involving) patients were impaired across *all* conditions including faces/objects, while the hippocampal-only patients were relatively spared on faces/objects (more impaired on scenes). **ESTABLISHED per this single study; very small n (2 patients per subgroup) — SINGLE-STUDY caveat, not independently replicated with this exact patient set.**

**Barense et al. Neuropsychologia 2007** ("The human medial temporal lobe processes online representations of complex objects", vol 45:2963–2974) — oddity paradigm across greebles/familiar objects/faces/scenes; perirhinal-damaged patients reported more impaired on novel-object (greeble) discrimination than familiar-object discrimination. **UNVERIFIED-DETAIL**: I could not extract exact patient counts or percent-correct numbers (PDF fetch failed / returned only navigation text); this claim is a secondary paraphrase only.

**Devlin & Price (2007).** "Perirhinal Contributions to Human Visual Perception." *Current Biology* 17(17):1484–1488. fMRI in healthy humans. Finding (per secondary source): **perirhinal cortex activation scaled with the perceptual/feature-integration demands of oddity judgments — recruited during difficult object discriminations requiring feature integration but not during simple color/shape discriminations.** **SINGLE-STUDY, secondary paraphrase only** — I did not verify effect sizes or exact contrast statistics directly from primary text.

### Direct conflicting/null human findings (Squire camp — see also §4 below)

**Levy, Shrager & Squire (2005).** "Intact visual discrimination of complex and feature-ambiguous stimuli in the absence of perirhinal cortex." *Learning & Memory* 12(1):61–66. DOI 10.1101/lm.84405. [WebFetch-extracted.] Amnesic patients with MTL/perirhinal damage tested on complex-object (face) discrimination and feature-ambiguous same/different judgments; patients performed comparably to controls on perception tasks; the one deficit that emerged required trial-by-trial memory, suggesting a memory-based rather than perceptual account. **FAILED-REPLICATION relative to the perirhinal-perceptual claim (direct empirical conflict with the PMFC prediction in humans).**

**Shrager, Gold, Hopkins & Squire (2006).** "Intact Visual Perception in Memory-Impaired Patients with Medial Temporal Lobe Lesions." *J Neurosci* 26(8):2235–2240. DOI 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4792-05.2006. [Abstract via EuropePMC API.] **6 memory-impaired patients** (hippocampal-only or hippocampal-plus-additional-MTL lesions) across four challenging visual-discrimination experiments: **patients performed as well as controls on all experiments.** **FAILED-REPLICATION (direct null result contesting the perceptual-deficit claim in humans).**

## 4. Failed replications / null results

- **Clark, Reinagel, Broadbent, Flister & Squire (2011).** "Intact performance on feature ambiguous discriminations in rats with lesions of the perirhinal cortex." *Neuron* 70(1):132–140. DOI 10.1016/j.neuron.2011.03.007. [Full text via PMC, WebFetch-extracted.] **6 perirhinal-lesion vs. 6 control female Long-Evans rats**; two-choice discrimination using stimuli (paintbrush vs. flashlight) morphed across **14 graded feature-ambiguity levels**, 150 probe trials/level. Result: performance declined from **~87% correct at Level 1 to chance (50%) at Level 14 in BOTH groups**, and **lesioned and control animals were statistically indistinguishable at every single difficulty level**, despite the same lesioned rats being impaired on a separate recognition-memory test. Authors' explicit conclusion: perirhinal cortex is important for memory, not perception, in this paradigm — directly contradicting the PMFC prediction. **FAILED-REPLICATION, strong (matched design, graded manipulation, memory-task positive control included in the same animals to rule out lesion-inefficacy)** — this is one of the most cited direct challenges to the feature-ambiguity account.

- **Norman & Eacott (2004).** "Impaired object recognition with increasing levels of feature ambiguity in rats with perirhinal cortex lesions." *Behavioural Brain Research* 148:79–91. This is the earlier, PMFC-*supporting* rat study using a broadly similar graded-morph logic; it reported a genuine impairment that scaled with ambiguity. **Important note:** the Clark et al. (2011) paper above is essentially a targeted attempt to test the same class of claim with a cleaner design (different stimulus pair, larger probe-trial counts, an explicit memory-task control) and reports the opposite result. **This pairing constitutes a genuine within-species (rat), same-broad-paradigm empirical conflict — flag as CONTESTED/FAILED-REPLICATION at the rat-perceptual level specifically**, not merely a cross-species discrepancy.

- **Levy, Shrager & Squire (2005)** and **Shrager, Gold, Hopkins & Squire (2006)** — both above — are the human-side null/failed-replication results (Squire lab), asserting perirhinal/MTL-damaged amnesics show intact visual perception on tasks designed to be feature-ambiguous or otherwise perceptually demanding, with deficits confined to memory-dependent (trial-by-trial retention) versions.

- **Buffalo, Stefanacci, Squire & Zola (1998)** — not a null result exactly, but a **methodological invalidation** of part of the earlier monkey lesion base (area-TE confound), which functions as a standing caveat rather than a direct feature-ambiguity replication failure.

- General pattern: **there is an entrenched two-camp controversy** — Bussey/Saksida/Murray/Graham/Barense/Lee (perceptual-mnemonic / representational-hierarchical account: perirhinal cortex does real perceptual work) vs. Squire/Zola/Shrager/Clark/Levy (declarative-memory-only account: perirhinal cortex subserves memory exclusively; apparent perceptual deficits are attributable to incidental damage elsewhere, task memory-demands, or eye-movement/strategy confounds). Both camps have published matched-design studies reaching opposite conclusions on similar (sometimes near-identical) paradigms in both monkeys/rats and humans. **This whole area should be treated as CONTESTED at the theoretical level even where individual empirical results are ESTABLISHED at the single-study level.**
