# Research (neuro lens): cheap high-capacity codes + where exploitable structure comes from for arbitrary items (drillA_neuro_capacity_structure)

**Filed by:** research sub-agent. **Trigger:** direct dispatch (topic statement, not a routing file). **Given context:** the residue-number-system / Chinese-Remainder-Theorem (RNS/CRT) multi-module lever proposed earlier the same day in `research_deployable_representational_capacity_levers_relational_map_builder_2026-07-13.md` (GO, P_deflated=0.35, leading candidate) has since failed in practice — the entities being coded are arbitrary structureless labels, so there is no numeric/relational substructure for a residue split to exploit, and (per the dispatcher) you cannot cheaply compress randomness. ## HEADLINE

**Two separable findings, plus one hard boundary condition that retroactively explains why RNS/CRT was the wrong shape of fix.**

1. **Capacity-without-structure is real and does not require RNS/CRT's premise.** Dense/modern associative memory (Krotov-Hopfield line; Demircigil et al. 2017; Ramsauer et al. 2020) gets *exponential*-in-N pattern capacity for genuinely **random/unstructured** patterns — no structure in the content is needed or exploited. The mechanism is purely the *sharpness* of the readout/energy function (higher-order/exponential separation instead of pairwise dot-product), not any property of the stored items. This is the correct answer to "cheap capacity for arbitrary labels": it doesn't try to compress the label, it changes how sharply the cleanup/readout discriminates between labels. Separately, DG/cerebellum-style sparse-expansion + decorrelation (Babadi & Sompolinsky 2014; Marr-Albus) raises *effective* (crosstalk-limited) storage capacity for arbitrary, uncorrelated content by reducing pattern overlap before storage — also structure-agnostic, because it only needs items to be *not too correlated*, not to carry exploitable substructure.

2. **Structure, when the brain has it, is never derived from an item's own arbitrary identity — it is a separate, learned relational scaffold fit to the STATISTICS of how items co-occur/transition/relate, which is then bound onto whichever arbitrary item fills a slot** (schema formation: Tse et al. 2007/2011; hippocampal-entorhinal cognitive map / Tolman-Eichenbaum Machine: Behrens/Whittington et al. 2018/2020; successor representation as a learned low-rank eigenbasis of transition statistics: Stachenfeld/Botvinick/Gershman 2017; latent-cause Bayesian inference: Gershman & Niv). Critically, grid-cell periodicity itself is **not innate** — it emerges only after ~3 weeks of active exploration in developing rats (Langston et al.; Wills et al. 2010, *Science*) and generalizes to purely abstract, non-spatial dimensions once learned (Constantinescu, O'Reilly & Behrens 2016, *Science* — hexagonal grid-like code for arbitrary bird-stimulus dimensions). **This means the RNS/CRT lever mis-modeled the grid-cell analogy**: it tried to split the *arbitrary item's own identity* into residues, as if an entity ID had intrinsic modular-arithmetic structure the way continuous position does. But biological grid modules don't get their structure from position's numeric properties either — they get it from a *separately learned* transition/path-integration statistic, bound to place via hippocampal conjunction. The correct generalization of "grid-cell-style" thinking is: learn a low-rank relational scaffold from the graph's own co-occurrence/transition statistics, then bind it to the (still full-cost, still arbitrary) entity code — not: factor the entity's raw ID.

3. **Hard boundary condition (high confidence, cites settled math, not a deflated novel-synthesis claim):** Johnson-Lindenstrauss is *provably optimal* for arbitrary/incompressible point sets under any linear map (Larsen & Nelson 2017, closing a 30-year question) — you cannot beat O(log N / eps^2) dimensions for genuinely arbitrary content, full stop. Modern-Hopfield's exponential *pattern* capacity does not violate this: per the Gardner-bound analysis, the gain is paid for in higher-order interaction/parameter cost, keeping information rate at ~O(1) bit/synapse — it is a *currency swap* (parameter-order cost instead of dimension cost), not a violation of the entropy floor. And Shannon/Kolmogorov give the same message from the source-coding side: a genuinely incompressible i.i.d. symbol cannot be compressed below its own entropy. **This is not a new failure to explain away — it is the literature's own explicit confirmation that "search for a cleverer compression of the raw arbitrary label" is a closed line of attack, not merely one where RNS/CRT happened to be the wrong specific instance.** That reframing is itself the single most useful negative result of this drill: stop looking for compression tricks on the label; look either at readout-sharpness (finding 1) or at a separate bound relational scaffold (finding 2).

**Honest tension to flag up front, not bury:** finding 2's brain-grounded mechanism (learned relational scaffold bound to arbitrary content) sounds, at first read, like exactly what the substrate already tried and already lost on. `grounding_learned_sr_heldout_reasoning_v1` (3 seeds, FULL, 2026-07-10, per `relational_capability_track_record_scour_2026-07-10.md` section E) trained a successor-representation-style code and found it routes **no better than random codes on held-out edges** (reach@2 0.1148 vs random-code control 0.104, delta 0.011 < margin) — a directly-adjacent prior negative. The resolution, worked out below, is that the brain literature's positive evidence is specifically about a **stable schema, already built from many prior congruent examples, cheaply absorbing a NEW item that fits it** — not about inferring a genuinely unseen relational fact from nothing. Those are different claims. The narrower, honest, not-yet-tested claim (schema-congruent items get cheaper/lower-dimensional encoding once a schema is stable) is NOT what `grounding_learned_sr_heldout_reasoning_v1` tested (that cell tested inductive prediction of unseen edges) — but it is adjacent enough, and the substrate's overall held-out-inductive-inference wall is confirmed independently 3x now, that this claim should carry a heavily deflated P and an explicit falsification path (below), not be oversold as a fresh escape route.

## Cheap decisive test

**Two tests, targeting the two separable findings — both reuse existing harnesses, neither requires new ingest.**

### Test 1 (targets finding 1 — readout sharpness, no structure required, PRIMARY recommendation)

Swap the cleanup/associative-recall step's similarity/energy function from linear/pairwise (dot-product or cosine argmax) to a sharpened higher-order separation function (e.g. raise similarity scores to a power p>1 before softmax/argmax, mimicking the modern-Hopfield exponential/polynomial energy family) — **zero change to entity/relation codes, zero new ingest, one readout-function swap.** Re-run the existing capacity-ladder / oracle-ceiling cert harness at matched N and matched entity codes, sweeping p.

**HARD-PASS:** max recoverable capacity M* (load before recall accuracy crosses the existing cert threshold) rises >=1.5x at matched N and matched total footprint vs. the current linear/pairwise cleanup baseline, reproduced across >=2 seeds, AND a must-fail control (a shuffled/randomized higher-order kernel that destroys the true similarity ranking while preserving its magnitude distribution) collapses back to the linear baseline — confirms the lift is genuinely from separation-function sharpness, not an accounting artifact.

**HARD-FAIL:** M* gain <1.15x at matched footprint, OR the sharpening only helps at a p so large that per-recall compute cost becomes comparable to a monolithic dimension bump (i.e. the Gardner-bound cost is being paid via a hidden compute increase, not a genuine sub-quadratic win) — in which case deprioritize this lever and treat the O(n^2) dimension-increase cost as the only currently-known route to higher raw capacity for arbitrary content.

### Test 2 (targets finding 2's narrow honest reading — schema-congruent cheap encoding, SECONDARY)

Fit a low-rank successor/co-occurrence basis ONCE over the bulk of the already-ingested graph (reuse existing ingest, no new data). Then compare, at a REDUCED dimension d' < N: (a) recall/capacity for new arbitrary entities whose true edges are relationally CONGRUENT with that basis (edges used only to measure congruence, never to fit the basis) vs. (b) recall/capacity for equally-arbitrary entities placed with no relation to the basis (incongruent controls).

**HARD-PASS:** congruent-entity recall at reduced dimension d' is statistically indistinguishable (within 5%) from full-N recall of arbitrary entities, while incongruent-entity recall at the same d' degrades sharply (>=20% relative drop) — confirms a genuine schema-congruence compression effect, distinct from "any extra structure helps."

**HARD-FAIL:** no significant congruent-vs-incongruent gap at reduced dimension — this would be the 4th independent confirmation that structural/schema manipulation over the SAME graph does not move the needle (joining relation-type richness, k-core density, and structure-aware encoder training, all already HF per the track record), fully closing the "confer structure via graph-internal relational scaffolding" avenue and redirecting all remaining capacity work to Test 1's readout-sharpness route plus the standing grounding/active-referent (ingest-more-knowledge) diagnosis.

## Substrate-product implications

- **If Test 1 HARD-PASSes:** a genuinely cheap, structure-agnostic capacity lever — a one-line readout change, no re-encoding, no re-ingest — that raises recoverable pattern capacity for arbitrary labels by exploiting the same mathematical principle (higher-order/exponential separation) that makes modern associative memory work, independent of whether the entities have any relational structure at all. This is the most deployable, lowest-risk finding in this drill.
- **If Test 1 HARD-FAILs:** confirms that the O(n^2) dimension-increase cost is, for now, the only known route to raising raw arbitrary-content capacity, and that readout-side tricks cannot substitute for it — a genuinely informative negative that closes off an entire class of "smarter cleanup function" proposals rather than leaving it open to be re-tried.
- **If Test 2 HARD-PASSes (narrow reading):** would be a real, novel, capacity/efficiency win specifically for schema-congruent new content — NOT a fix for the held-out inductive-inference wall, and should not be marketed as one; it would mean new entities that are relationally similar to already-ingested structure can be encoded more cheaply, which is a genuine (if narrower-than-hoped) capacity gain.
- **If Test 2 HARD-FAILs:** the 4th independent confirmation that graph-internal structural manipulation cannot raise capacity or induction on the same underlying graph — this should be treated as strong, converging, multi-angle evidence (math floor + brain literature's own honest caveats + now 4 empirical HF cells) to STOP proposing further "richer internal structure" levers and to route all remaining relational-capability investment toward (a) the readout-sharpening lever (structure-agnostic, still open) and (b) the standing grounding/active-referent ingest-more-knowledge direction.
- **Either way, this is a construction-proof/mechanism-clarification, not a capability win yet** — nothing here claims the capacity or structure wall is solved; both tests are cheap, falsifiable, and reuse existing harnesses specifically so the cost of finding out is low relative to the value of closing (or opening) these two remaining angles cleanly.

## Intuitive summary

**What we studied:** we asked the brain two related questions. First: when you have to remember a huge number of totally arbitrary things (no hidden number pattern to exploit), how do you do it cheaply instead of just building one enormous, expensive memory? Second — the harder, more important question — when does the brain ever manage to make arbitrary things easier to remember by giving them structure, and where does that structure actually come from if the things themselves started out arbitrary?

**What we found, part 1 (cheap capacity for arbitrary things):** there's a genuinely different, cheap trick the brain and the underlying math both support that has nothing to do with compressing the item itself. Instead of trying to shrink what you're storing, you can sharpen HOW you tell memories apart when you retrieve them — using a "sharper," more decisive matching rule instead of a soft one lets the same amount of raw storage hold exponentially more distinct memories before they get confused with each other. This works for completely random, structureless items just fine, which is exactly our situation — it sidesteps the whole "no pattern to exploit" problem instead of trying to solve it.

**What we found, part 2 (where structure comes from):** the brain never invents structure out of an arbitrary item's own identity. Instead it builds a separate mental "map" or "template" from noticing patterns in how MANY different things relate to and follow each other over time, and then attaches that pre-built map to whatever new, still-arbitrary item shows up. This is important because it explains why our earlier idea (splitting an arbitrary label into pieces the way the brain's navigation system splits position into several "clocks") was the wrong shape of fix — the brain's navigation system doesn't get its structure from position's own numeric properties either; it learns that structure separately, from movement experience, and only afterward attaches it to a specific place. The honest catch: the brain's own evidence for this is really about a stable, already-learned template quickly absorbing ONE new matching item cheaply — not about correctly guessing brand-new relationships it has never seen any evidence for, which is a much harder trick nobody's evidence (ours or the literature's) currently supports.

**Why it matters, honestly:** we now have one strong, low-risk, structure-agnostic lever (the sharper-matching trick) that plausibly raises capacity for arbitrary items directly, and one narrower, more speculative, but honestly-scoped lever (schema-congruent cheap encoding) worth one cheap confound-controlled check before being written off as the same already-failed idea in different clothes. We also confirmed, from settled mathematics rather than our own trial and error, that there is no clever compression shortcut for truly arbitrary content — which means we should stop looking for one and spend the next round of effort on these two levers instead.
