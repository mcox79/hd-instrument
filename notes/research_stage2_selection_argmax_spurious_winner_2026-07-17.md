# Research: Stage-2 selection — why argmax-over-M-completions gets WORSE with M, and the brain's fix

Filed by: research (Sonnet), 2026-07-17. Negative-drill 2x on the shortlist-narrowing pipeline (Stage-1 index 96.6% shortlist-recall, Stage-2 naive-argmax final accuracy only ~56%, degrading further as M grows).

## HEADLINE

The brain does not have this failure mode because it does not do "generate M full candidates, then argmax an independent per-candidate confidence score" in the first place. Within one hippocampal network, CA3 resolves ambiguous cues via a **single continuous joint settle** (recurrent excitation + feedback inhibition = winner-take-all baked into the dynamics, not a post-hoc scoring stage) — so there is no M-way comparison to corrupt. But that model applies to *within-shard* completion, which our system already has and which is not where the failure lives. The failure lives at the **cross-shard candidate comparison** stage, which has no direct CA3 analog (CA3 is one recurrent net, not M independent nets scored and compared). The nearest brain mechanism that *does* face this exact problem — several plausible retrieved candidates, one of them wrong-but-confident — is **human recognition memory's dual-process architecture**: a fast continuous "familiarity" signal that is a known, well-characterized source of confident false positives, paired with a slower, independent **verification** stage (recollection / "recall-to-reject" / CA1 predicted-vs-actual mismatch detection / prefrontal post-retrieval monitoring) that checks the candidate against *detail*, not against its own confidence score. Classical coding theory names the identical mathematical problem (extreme-value inflation of the max of M noisy scores, i.e. the winner's-curse / multiple-comparisons phenomenon — provably gets worse as M grows, matching the observed HARD_FAIL) and the identical fix class: an **outer verification/check step whose false-accept rate is fixed and does not scale with M** (CRC-aided list decoding), which is strictly preferred over re-ranking, joint-scoring, or confidence calibration alone (calibration was shown in the lit-scan to sometimes make selection *worse*, not better).

Three independent literatures (attractor-network theory, recognition-memory dual-process theory, coding theory) converge on the same structural prescription: **stop trusting the candidate's own confidence score as the sole arbiter; add an independent verification check that regenerates/reconstructs the original cue from the candidate and scores match-to-cue, not match-to-self.**

## Biology, in order of relevance

**(1) CA3 attractor completion is one settle, not generate-then-rank.** Retrieval is a single dynamical trajectory: recurrent excitatory reverberation + feedback (basket-cell) inhibition continuously suppress all but the dominant assembly *during* convergence — winner-take-all is a property of the dynamics, not a separate scoring step applied to finished candidates (Marr 1971; Treves & Rolls 1991/1992; Rolls 2010/2015; O'Reilly & McClelland CLS framing, PMC4792674, PMC3904133). This is why CA3 itself never faces an "M-way argmax" problem: there aren't M separately-computed candidates to compare, there's one attractor landscape. **Substrate implication:** our Stage-2 per-shard attractor completion (pinv/resonator cleanup within a shard) is already the brain-faithful analog of this stage and is not implicated in the failure — the failure is a level up, at the stage that compares *outputs of M separate settles*, which has no CA3-internal analog at all.

**(2) The failure mode DOES have a Hopfield-theory analog: spurious deep-but-wrong attractors.** Above/near capacity, or when two stored patterns have similar overlap with a cue, the energy landscape develops spurious minima — stable, deep basins that are not any stored pattern but trap the dynamics and are reported with high confidence (Amit-Gutfreund-Sompolinsky 1985 capacity theory; arXiv 2510.17593, 2505.01218). Basin depth functions as a confidence-like quantity, and "deep but wrong" is a real, theoretically-grounded phenomenon — directly analogous to the observed spurious-high-confidence wrong completion. Gap flagged by the lit-scan: a literal quantitative "confidence = basin depth" mapping is well-motivated in abstract Hopfield theory but not empirically pinned down in CA3 physiology specifically.

**(3) Theta-gamma phase multiplexing keeps competitors separate, but does not itself resolve which is correct.** Lisman-Idiart-style theta-gamma coding represents multiple competing candidates in separate gamma sub-cycles nested in a theta cycle (temporal division, not blended superposition) (Gatsby Lisman PDF; Nat Rev Neurosci nrn3668; Nature 2010 phase-locking/strength paper). This is a complementary decorrelation mechanism (reduces cross-candidate interference/correlated noise) but is not itself a selection rule — it doesn't tell you which of the temporally-separated candidates to trust. Relevant as a **secondary** lever (reduce correlation among the M scores, which shrinks the extreme-value inflation, see below) but not the primary fix.

**(4) Recognition memory's dual-process + verification architecture is the closest full analog to the actual problem we have.** Yonelinas' dual-process theory: familiarity is a fast, continuous, non-specific strength signal that is a *known, well-documented* source of confident false alarms, especially as lure/competitor similarity rises; recollection is a slower, threshold-like, detail-bound signal whose behavior is largely *independent* of the false-alarm rate driven by familiarity (Yonelinas 1994, 2001). Fuzzy-trace theory's "recall-to-reject" / "recollection rejection" makes the mechanism explicit: the system retrieves a *distinctive/verbatim detail* of the true item and compares it against the probe; a mismatch actively rejects a plausible-but-false lure that the gist/familiarity signal alone would have accepted (Brainerd & Reyna; Odegard & Lampinen 2011, PMC3059110). This recall-to-reject stage becomes *more* engaged, not less, precisely when competitor similarity/retrieval difficulty is high — i.e., it is a targeted fix for exactly the regime where naive familiarity fails. Separately, CA1 is proposed as an automatic match/mismatch comparator (predicted completion vs actual input), operating independent of the completion's own strength (Vinogradova; Duncan/Ketz/Inati/Davachi 2012), and PFC provides a further strategic/anatomically distinct post-retrieval monitoring stage (Johnson source-monitoring framework; Simons & Spiers 2003, Nat Rev Neurosci). All three converge: **verification is a separate architectural stage from confidence, engaged specifically to catch high-confidence wrong answers, and it works by checking the candidate against independent detail/context, not by re-scoring the candidate's own signal.**

## Classical/engineering mirror (exact mathematical form of the failure + the standard fix)

**Mechanism, named precisely:** with M candidate scores that are true-quality-plus-noise, the *maximum* of M noise draws grows with the pool size — for Gaussian-type noise, E[max] ≈ σ√(2 log M) (Gumbel-type extreme value theory). So as M grows, the probability that some wrong candidate's noise excursion exceeds the true candidate's honest score rises — this is the same phenomenon as the winner's-curse / multiple-comparisons selection bias in statistics. This is a precise, quantitative account of "gets worse as M grows," matching the observed HARD_FAIL exactly (not just an analogy).

**Ranked fixes from the lit-scan:**
1. **Outer verification/parity check (strongest, most standard)** — CRC-aided list decoding (5G polar codes, Reed-Solomon list decoding): generate a shortlist, then apply an independent, near-zero-false-positive check (CRC/parity) and only rank by likelihood *among candidates that pass the check*. False-accept probability of the check is fixed (~2^-r for an r-bit CRC) and **does not grow with list size** — this is the literature's explicit rationale for why verification, not confidence-ranking, is the standard fix, and why list size can safely grow once verification is in place (King et al., arXiv:2201.07843; Guruswami-Sudan list-decoding theory).
2. **Regenerate-and-compare / cycle-consistency** — reconstruct the original cue from each candidate, score by round-trip fidelity to the actual cue rather than the candidate's self-confidence (back-translation QE literature, arXiv:2004.13937; Hopfield spurious-attractor energy checks). This is the general-purpose analog when there's no formal outer code. Caveat from the lit-scan: works well with a *good* distance/consistency metric (semantic-level), poorly with a naive surface-match metric.
3. **Joint/single-shot decoding** (shared-metric decoding across candidates rather than fully independent scoring) reduces but does not eliminate the curse — this is the coding-theory analog of the CA3 "one settle" solution, and is the most brain-faithful but the most expensive to retrofit onto an already-sharded architecture (would require merging shards back into one joint solve).
4. **Confidence calibration (weakest fix)** — recent work shows calibration and selection efficiency are in tension (arXiv:2402.04344): well-calibrated scores can produce *larger* effective ambiguity, not smaller. Calibration fixes the scale of the confidence score, not the selection-bias mechanism. Do not expect calibration alone to fix this.

## Substrate-product implications

The product-relevant fix is NOT "make the confidence score better calibrated" (weakest lit-supported fix, may backfire) and NOT "run the full expensive joint solve across all M shards" (most brain-faithful for CA3-style single-settle, but expensive and requires re-architecting away from sharding, which was adopted specifically for cost reasons in the prior drill). It is: **add a cheap, independent, per-top-K verification step that regenerates the query cue from each candidate completion and re-ranks (or gates) by fidelity-to-cue, not by the completion's own internal confidence.** This is a glass-box, observable, cheap addition (one more bind/unbind + cosine-similarity per top-K candidate, K small e.g. 2-4, not all M) — it does not require touching the Stage-1 router or the per-shard completion machinery. It is the direct engineering analog of recall-to-reject / CA1 mismatch detection, and it inherits the coding-theory guarantee that its error rate is a property of the check, not of M — which is exactly what's needed to reverse the "gets worse with M" trend rather than just slow it down.

## Cheap decisive test spec (fix-cell, real can-fail baseline, difficulty-on at high corruption + large M)

**Baseline (REAL, must be able to fail):** existing Stage-2 pipeline exactly as currently implemented — Stage-1 shortlist of size M, per-shard attractor completion, final answer = argmax over each candidate's own internal completion-confidence. Measure accuracy vs M at M in {4, 8, 16, 32} at each of 2-3 cue-corruption levels (include the corruption level where the HARD_FAIL was already observed). Expect (and this is the pre-registered baseline behavior): accuracy flat-to-declining as M grows, consistent with prior observation.

**Fix-cell:** identical Stage-1 shortlist + identical per-shard completion. Change only the SELECTION step: take the top-K candidates by original confidence (K=2 and K=4 as two conditions, not just K=1), for each REGENERATE the query cue by re-applying the same forward encode/bind operation used to construct the original query from the candidate's completed content, and score by similarity(regenerated_cue, actual_query_cue). Select the candidate with max regenerated-cue fidelity. Add a threshold-gated variant too: if max fidelity < tau, return "no confident match" (abstain) rather than force a pick — this is the recall-to-reject / CRC-analog branch and should be scored separately (accuracy-when-answered vs abstention-rate), not folded into a single accuracy number.

**Metrics to log:** accuracy vs M for both arms at each corruption level; abstention rate and accuracy-conditional-on-answering for the threshold-gated fix variant; cost (extra bind + cosine-sim ops per query) to confirm it's cheap relative to the existing per-shard completion cost.

## Falsifiable predictions

**HARD-PASS (verification-by-cue-regeneration is the correct fix):** at the largest tested M (32) and the previously-HARD_FAIL corruption level, fix-cell accuracy is FLAT or IMPROVING in M (does not decline the way baseline does), AND recovers at least 85% of the Stage-1 shortlist-recall ceiling (i.e. accuracy_fix(M=32) >= 0.85 x 0.966 ~ 0.82), while baseline continues to decline or stays at/below its previously observed ~56%. The threshold-gated variant should show abstention concentrated on genuinely-ambiguous cases (accuracy-conditional-on-answering notably higher than unconditional accuracy).

**HARD-FAIL (verification-by-cue-regeneration does NOT decouple from the same noise structure that broke confidence-argmax):** fix-cell shows the SAME qualitative decline-with-M pattern as baseline (i.e., the regenerated-cue fidelity score is itself subject to the same extreme-value/correlated-noise inflation — meaning cue-regeneration fidelity is not an independent enough signal from completion confidence), OR fix-cell accuracy at M=32 is < 1.15x baseline accuracy at M=32 (no more than noise-level improvement over doing nothing). In the HARD-FAIL case, the correct next move is NOT to iterate on this fix (calibration-style patches were shown in the lit-scan to be the weakest class of fix) but to test fix-option 3 (joint/single-shot decode across the shortlist, i.e. a cheaper partial version of the CA3-style single-settle, e.g. a small cross-shard consistency-relaxation pass over just the top-K rather than full re-architecture) as the next-tier, more-expensive fallback.

## P_deflated

Raw confidence in the general principle (independent verification decoupled from raw confidence beats naive argmax, and its error rate doesn't scale with M) is high — three independent literatures converge on it (attractor theory's spurious-minima framing, dual-process recognition memory, coding theory's CRC-aided list decoding), which is unusually strong convergence for a drill of this kind. However, this exact mechanism (regenerate-cue-fidelity as the verification signal, on THIS substrate's specific completion/bind operations) has not been tested here — applying a well-established cross-domain principle to a novel implementation. Per lit-scan calibration discipline (deflate 0.15-0.25, cap novel-synthesis at 0.50): **P_deflated = 0.50 (capped)** for the primary cue-regeneration verification fix; **P ~ 0.30** (uncapped, genuinely lower confidence, not just deflated) for the fallback joint/single-shot cross-shard consistency route being needed instead, since that route is more speculative for this architecture and untested even in concept here.
