## HEADLINE

**All 3 angles converge on the same two-part answer, and the convergence includes an explicit, load-bearing CORRECTION to the naive plan: (1) a LEARNED reachability signal is real and well-precedented — TD-bootstrapped SR (Dayan 1993, biologically realized via a temporally-asymmetric Hebbian/anti-Hebbian rule per Bono et al. 2023 eLife) and its ML analog, co-occurrence-factorization embeddings (node2vec/DeepWalk, PROVEN by Qiu et al. 2018 to factorize exactly the same resolvent-like matrix as SR/PPR) both derive M = (I-gamma*T)^-1 from SAMPLED transitions, never from a known T; (2) BUT raw sampled/TD-learned SR does NOT by itself generalize beyond directly-visited transitions — rows/columns for unvisited state-action pairs are never populated (Russek et al. 2017, Momennejad 2017; Barreto's own successor-features paper explicitly flags this boundary). The thing that generalizes to UNOBSERVED structure is not the table of visited transitions but SMOOTHNESS IN THE LEARNED FEATURE/CODE SPACE — successor features (dynamics decoupled from reward via a shared learned phi), Nystrom/heat-kernel diffusion extension of a resolvent from a node subsample (error grows with graph-distance from observed anchors, not randomly), and node2vec-style embedding smoothing all point at the same mechanism: propagate the SR/resolvent signal from directly-sampled edges to withheld/unseen ones via SIMILARITY IN THE SUBSTRATE'S OWN LEARNED CODE SPACE, not via re-deriving the missing edges from nowhere.**

**This is the field's own answer to "how do you tell reasoning from search": hold out an entire disjoint subgraph (not just scattered edges) and check whether the reachability signal degrades GRACEFULLY (via code-space smoothing = generalization = CG) or COLLAPSES TOWARD CHANCE specifically on the held-out/unseen-combination items while staying near-ceiling on trained items (= memorized lookup = still MM, just missing some rows now). "When No Paths Lead to Rome" (arXiv 2510.23532) names this exact memorization signature directly; inductive KG completion (unseen-entity splits, GraphSAGE) is the field's standard experimental design for it.**

## Angle 1 — Brain: the hippocampal SR is LEARNED, never a known transition matrix

1. **Dayan (1993, Neural Computation)** — the original SR is a sample-based TD update, not a matrix inversion: on observing s -> s_next, `M(s,s') <- M(s,s') + alpha*[1{s_next=s'} + gamma*M(s_next,s') - M(s,s')]` for all s', bootstrapping off the ROW for s_next. This converges to the same fixed point as `(I-gamma*T)^-1` asymptotically, but requires only single-step sampled transitions — no model of the whole graph is ever built or needed. This is the direct disanalogy fix: the just-certified cell used the known T; the brain (and Dayan's own formalism) never does.
2. **Stachenfeld, Botvinick & Gershman (2017, Nat Neurosci)** — place cells encode rows of the learned M; grid cells are a compressed spectral (eigenvector) basis of M. Barrier-induced place-field remapping is evidence the map is experience-derived, not geometric ground truth.
3. **Bono et al. (2023, eLife, "Neural learning rules for generating flexible predictions and computing the successor representation")** — CONFIRMED primary, the most concrete biological answer to "what is the eligibility trace, mechanistically": a temporally-asymmetric Hebbian potentiation term (pre-post activity across one timestep) plus a local anti-Hebbian/normalization depotentiation term, with recurrent CA3 connectivity implementing T implicitly and acetylcholine proposed to modulate the discount/learning-rate. No global T is ever represented; T is implicit in synaptic weights updated locally from single-step co-activity.
4. **Mattar & Daw (2018, Nat Neurosci, "Prioritized memory access...")** — replay reactivates stored (s,a,s') samples off-policy, prioritized by gain x need, propagating reachability/value information across the graph FAR faster than raw online TD, but still purely from stored samples, never a fully-known map.
5. **The generalization boundary, stated honestly** (Russek et al. 2017 PLOS Comp Biol; Momennejad review 2020): SR generalizes well to NEW REWARDS under the SAME learned transition statistics, but transfers poorly when the actual transition structure changes or extends to never-visited states — SR-TD must be relearned via further experience for genuinely new structure. This is the biological confirmation of the same boundary the ML angle finds independently (below).

## Angle 2 — ML: successor features / learned world models generalize to new GOALS, not to new STRUCTURE — and the field's own reasoning-vs-lookup discriminator

1. **Barreto et al. (2017, NeurIPS, Successor Features + Generalized Policy Improvement)** — decouples dynamics (psi, a learned feature-occupancy expectation) from reward (w); generalizes ZERO-SHOT to new reward functions given a library of previously-learned policies. Explicitly does NOT generalize to new/unvisited transition structure — psi itself becomes progressively overfit to the transitions experienced under the policy that produced it; several follow-ups (Zhu/Filos "Successor Feature Sets" 2021, Universal SR 2018) name this as the method's boundary.
2. **Learned world models do not automatically generalize** — MuZero's learned model "cannot effectively generalize to evaluate unseen policies"; Anand et al. (2022, ICLR, "Procedural Generalization by Planning with Self-Supervised World Models") show genuine held-out-seed transfer requires planning + self-supervision + procedural DATA DIVERSITY jointly, and even then the effect is benchmark-specific, not a general reasoning capacity.
3. **Inductive vs. transductive graph ML is the field's standard split for this exact question.** GraphSAGE (Hamilton et al. 2017) is the canonical "learned aggregation function generalizes to unseen nodes/graphs without retraining" result — the aggregator plays the role of a learned T_hat, inductively applied. Knowledge-graph completion's canonical cautionary tale (Toutanova & Chen 2015; Dettmers et al. 2018 ConvE, introducing WN18RR/FB15k-237) is inverse-relation test-set LEAKAGE: naive benchmarks let models "memorize the relation-inverse map" rather than model structure — i.e. a memorization shortcut can masquerade as generalization unless the held-out design specifically kills it.
4. **SR = resolvent = Personalized PageRank (Millidge, arXiv 2512.24722), and what changes when T must be estimated from samples**: TD-learned SR is "a fixed point over EXPERIENCED transitions — rows/columns for unvisited state-action pairs are never populated, so the SR cannot route through edges never traversed even if they are structurally inferable" (Russek/Momennejad framing). This is the precise, load-bearing statement of why raw sampled-TD-SR alone is insufficient for the held-out test; feature-space generalization (successor features / embedding smoothness) is required to bridge the gap.
5. **The reasoning-vs-retrieval discriminator, general pattern**: systematic/compositional splits (SCAN held-out primitive combinations, CLEVR-CoGenT attribute-palette swap) are designed so a lookup/co-occurrence strategy saturates in-distribution but PROVABLY fails out-of-distribution. The diagnostic signature: near-ceiling on trained/seen structure, collapse specifically on structurally-novel combinations — not aggregate accuracy.

## Angle 3 — Substrate realization: deriving reachability from learned CODE-SPACE structure + the decisive held-out discriminator

1. **node2vec/DeepWalk ARE a learned SR-over-embeddings, proven exactly.** Qiu et al. (2018, arXiv 1710.02971, "Network Embedding as Matrix Factorization") prove DeepWalk factorizes a normalized-Laplacian-derived matrix and node2vec factorizes a matrix tied to the transition-probability tensor of a random walk — i.e. these embeddings ARE a learned, low-rank encoding of a `(I-gamma*T)^-1`-like power series, recovered from SAMPLED co-occurrence, never from a known adjacency matrix. This is the closest existing formalization of a code-space resolvent.
2. **Heat-kernel / diffusion-map extension propagates a resolvent signal from OBSERVED to UNOBSERVED nodes**, with accuracy degrading by graph-distance from observed anchors (Thanou et al.; arXiv 2601.18938 progressive subgraph diffusion) — this gives a testable, expected error-vs-distance shape to compare against, distinct from either "perfect generalization" or "random collapse."
3. **Nystrom recovery of a resolvent from an O(s) node subsample fails specifically when the sampled landmarks don't preserve the graph's smallest eigenvalues/spectral structure** (Drineas & Mahoney) — a concrete, checkable failure precondition for the held-out design below.
4. **Sampled-walk Monte Carlo PPR gives the sample-complexity floor**: O(1/delta) forward walks for constant relative error (Fujiwara-family algorithms; classic PPR literature). A code-space-smoothed estimate beating this floor specifically ON UNSAMPLED nodes is direct evidence of genuine generalization beyond raw Monte Carlo.
5. **The decisive discriminator, directly on-point**: "When No Paths Lead to Rome" (arXiv 2510.23532) benchmarks systematic multi-hop relational reasoning via held-out edges/paths/SUBGRAPHS/relation-types and defines the memorization signature exactly as needed here — near-ceiling accuracy on in-distribution (seen-path) cases combined with SHARP accuracy collapse specifically on unseen-path-combination instances, while genuine compositional reasoners hold performance flat. Inductive KG-completion (disjoint train/test entity sets) is the companion gold-standard design. Both insist on holding out an entire disjoint SUBGRAPH, not scattered single edges — single held-out edges under-stress the multi-hop/compositional case.

## Convergent mechanism (>=2 angles agree; here effectively 3/3)

- **A genuinely LEARNED reachability signal is computable without a known T**: TD-sampled-transition SR (Dayan 1993 / Bono et al. 2023 biological realization) and co-occurrence-factorization embeddings (node2vec/DeepWalk, proven equivalent to a resolvent factorization by Qiu et al. 2018) are two independently-converging realizations of the SAME idea — derive M from SAMPLES, never from a handed T.
- **Raw sampled/visited-only SR does NOT generalize to unvisited structure by itself** — this is the corrective finding that must gate the substrate realization: brain angle (Russek/Momennejad) + ML angle (Barreto's own stated boundary; SR's "rows for unvisited state-action pairs never populated") independently agree. Any substrate realization that skips this and just claims "learned SR generalizes" would be quietly wrong.
- **The bridge that DOES generalize to unobserved structure is smoothness in a learned FEATURE/CODE space, not the raw transition table** — successor features (psi shared across tasks via phi), Nystrom/heat-kernel diffusion (error grows with graph-distance from anchors, not randomly), and node2vec-style embedding smoothing all converge on this as the mechanism of genuine structural generalization.
- **The reasoning-vs-search discriminator is a single, field-standard design, independently named by both the ML angle (inductive KG completion, GraphSAGE, systematic/compositional splits) and the substrate-realization angle (arXiv 2510.23532's explicit memorization signature): hold out a disjoint SUBGRAPH (not scattered edges), and check for graceful degradation (generalization/CG) vs. collapse-to-chance specifically on unseen-combination queries while staying near-ceiling on trained queries (memorization/MM).**

## ONE concrete substrate realization (buildable on the existing SR-routing harness)

**Learned/code-space SR estimated from a PARTIAL, sampled transition set, with reachability to withheld structure supplied by code-space similarity smoothing rather than the withheld edges themselves — same argmax hop-selection loop as the certified cell, new construction of the reachability signal.**

1. **Partial-T construction (the disanalogy fix):** instead of building T from the KB graph's FULL known edge set, withhold a contiguous, disjoint subgraph (a set of nodes and all their edges) from T's construction. The visible portion of T is built from sampled/observed transitions only (either literal TD-style online updates over sampled walks, per Dayan 1993, or a single sparse solve/truncated Neumann series restricted to the visible adjacency — computationally cheap either way at n~4,440).
2. **Code-space smoothing extension (the part that must be genuinely learned, not handed):** for any node v NOT in the visible T (withheld), do not attempt to read M[v,G] from the (undefined) visible-T resolvent. Instead, estimate M_hat[v,G] as a similarity-weighted average of M[u,G] over v's k-nearest neighbors IN THE SUBSTRATE'S OWN LEARNED VECTOR-CODE SPACE (not graph-topological neighbors — code-space neighbors, found by ordinary cosine/dot-product similarity among the substrate's already-learned codes). This is the Nystrom/heat-kernel/node2vec-style extension: propagate the resolvent signal through code-space smoothness rather than through the withheld edges.
3. **Hop-selection (identical argmax loop, unchanged):** at current node C, score each TRUE local neighbor v (existing primitive) by M_hat[v,G] (visible-T resolvent value if v is inside the visible graph, code-space-smoothed estimate if v is withheld), argmax exactly as the certified cell already does.
4. **Two conditions run side by side on the same harness:** (a) FULL-GRAPH condition — identical to the already-certified cell (all edges visible); (b) HELD-OUT condition — X% of nodes/edges (a disjoint subgraph) withheld from T at CONSTRUCTION time, reachability to/through withheld nodes supplied only via step 2's code-space smoothing. Critically, the learned codes used for smoothing must themselves have been formed WITHOUT access to the withheld edges (a genuine train/test split) — otherwise the held-out condition secretly leaks the answer back in through the embedding.

## Decisive discriminator (one line, pre-registerable)

**Held-out-subgraph reach@2 (code-space-smoothed SR, X% of nodes/edges withheld from T at construction time) — HARD-PASS: reach@2 >= 0.32 (>=~80% retention of the already-certified full-graph reach@2=0.434) AND performance collapses toward the memoryless floor (0.121) when the learned-code term is aliased-to-useless on the SAME held-out condition (confirming the codes, not residual leakage, are doing the generalizing work) -- vs -- HARD-FAIL: held-out reach@2 <= 0.20 (collapses toward the memoryless floor, i.e. no generalization beyond directly-observed edges -- still MM with a hole punched in the map) OR held-out reach@2 is UNCHANGED when the learned-code term is aliased-to-useless (meaning even this "learned" version isn't actually using the codes to generalize, i.e. some other leakage/artifact is doing the work, not genuine code-space reasoning).**

This is the exact structure the field itself uses (inductive KG completion / "When No Paths Lead to Rome" memorization signature): near-full-graph performance retained under a held-out subgraph = CG (genuine generalization from learned structure); collapse to the memoryless floor = MM (still search, just now missing rows it can't fill in).

## Honest risk

1. **Raw TD/sampled-walk SR over the visible portion alone will NOT generalize — this is not a hypothetical, it is the explicit, independently-confirmed boundary from BOTH the brain angle (Russek/Momennejad: unvisited state-action rows never populated) and the ML angle (Barreto's stated SR/SF boundary).** All of the generalizing work in this proposal is loaded onto step 2 (code-space smoothing). If the substrate's learned codes do not actually correlate with graph-topological proximity — i.e. if two nodes that are graph-neighbors are NOT reliably code-space-similar — then step 2 degenerates to noise and the held-out condition collapses regardless of mechanism quality. This is testable cheaply (correlation of code-space cosine similarity vs. graph hop-distance on the already-visible portion) and should be checked before funding the build; it is also the same "does this graph have real neighborhood structure" question flagged three times already in this thread (expander-vs-community risk), now applied to CODE space rather than graph space.
2. **Even a fully successful learned/held-out SR cannot itself lift the 0.500 binding-fidelity retrieval/commit ceiling** — the just-certified result showed routing intelligence and retrieval fidelity are SEPARATE bottlenecks (routing reach@2=0.434 vs binding-fidelity cap 0.500). This proposal targets ONLY the routing half being genuinely learned; it does not and cannot, by itself, raise the retrieval ceiling. A HARD-PASS here is a reasoning-vs-search result, not a capacity result.
3. **The held-out design itself has a leakage risk that must be engineered around, not assumed away**: standard embedding methods (node2vec/DeepWalk) are normally TRAINED on random walks over the FULL graph, which would leak the withheld edges back into the codes used for smoothing. If the substrate's codes were formed with any exposure (direct or indirect, e.g. via co-training on the full KB) to the withheld subgraph, a HARD-PASS would be a false positive (memorization masquerading as generalization) — this is exactly the WN18/FB15k inverse-relation-leakage cautionary tale from the ML angle, transplanted to this design. The construction of the held-out split must guarantee the codes never saw the withheld structure.
4. **Realistic expectation should be set below full-graph parity, not at it** — held-out/generalization performance is inherently harder than the already-certified full-graph case; the discriminator's 0.32 (80% retention) threshold is a considered choice, not an arbitrary one, but the true achievable number on this specific KB graph is unmeasured. A MIDDLE-BAND result (0.20-0.32) is plausible and should be diagnosed via the code-space-vs-hop-distance correlation check (risk 1) before declaring a verdict, exactly as the discipline applied to the two prior middle-band/hard-fail results in this thread.
5. **No literature runs this exact three-part comparison** (SR/resolvent on a KB-graph-shaped structure, held out by disjoint subgraph, discriminated by code-aliasing) — the convergence is strong (a proven embedding-resolvent equivalence, a named memorization-signature benchmark design, and an independently-confirmed generalization boundary) but this specific synthesis is an extrapolation bridging three literatures, not a verbatim precedent.

## Intuitive summary

**What we did:** the previous piece of work gave the substrate a way to find its own route toward a distant goal that worked well (reach@2=0.434, beating both older attempts) -- but a careful check showed it was secretly cheating in an interesting way: it was doing ordinary map-reading (like a GPS following roads it's been GIVEN in full), not actual reasoning, because when we disconnected the substrate's own learned understanding from the calculation, the routing worked exactly the same. So we asked: what would it take to make the routing genuinely come from what the substrate has LEARNED, rather than from a map we handed it?

**What we found:** real brains never get a handed map either -- the hippocampus builds its "which way leads where" sense purely from sampled experience, one step at a time, using a simple learning rule confirmed down to the level of specific synapse-strengthening patterns. And a well-known AI trick (the embeddings behind tools like node2vec) is PROVEN mathematically to build the exact same kind of "which way leads where" signal purely from sampled walks, no handed map required. But both literatures also honestly admit a catch: a system that only learns from what it has directly sampled can't say anything about a path it never walked -- it needs to also "feel" that unvisited places are similar (in its own learned sense of similarity) to nearby visited ones, and generalize through that similarity, the way you can guess a new neighborhood is probably like a nearby one you know even without walking every street in it.

**The plan we're proposing:** build the substrate's route-finder the same two-part way -- learn the "which way leads where" signal only from a PARTIAL, sampled version of its knowledge map (deliberately hiding a chunk of it), and for the hidden part, let the substrate's OWN learned sense of similarity fill in the gaps, instead of handing back the missing map. Then run a very clean test: does performance on the hidden, never-shown part stay nearly as good (proving it generalized, i.e. real reasoning) or does it collapse to guessing (proving it was only ever good at reading a map it was given)? We also re-run the earlier "disconnect the substrate's own understanding" check specifically on the hidden part -- if disconnecting it now actually hurts (unlike last time, when it didn't matter at all), that is direct proof the substrate's own learning is doing real work.

**Why it matters, honestly:** this is the actual fork in the road between "an impressively-built GPS" and "a system that reasons about what it hasn't been shown," which is the deep prize this whole research thread has been working toward. We are being upfront that this could still fail in a specific, informative way -- if the substrate's learned sense of similarity doesn't actually track real neighborhood structure, filling in the gaps could turn into pure guessing, collapsing the hidden-part performance back down no matter how good the mechanism is on paper. And even a full success here only fixes the ROUTING half of the system -- the separate cap on how reliably it can retrieve/commit an answer (already known to plateau around half the time) would still need its own separate fix.
