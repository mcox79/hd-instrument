## (a) HEADLINE

**Human language acquisition is not one smooth curve -- it is a chain of qualitatively different computational regimes, each gated by a specific, identifiable prerequisite the previous stage supplies, and every stage transition in the chain is evidenced by a DISCONTINUITY (a narrowing, a threshold, an insight, an over-generalization-then-correction), never a smooth ramp.** Six stages, in strict evidenced order:

1. **Prelinguistic distributional tuning (birth-9mo).** Newborns already discriminate nearly all phonetic contrasts used in any human language (Eimas et al. 1971 categorical perception; universal discrimination documented cross-linguistically). Two parallel processes narrow this: (i) **phonetic category narrowing / the "perceptual magnet effect"** (Kuhl 1991; Werker & Tees 1984) -- native-language contrasts sharpen while non-native contrasts are lost between ~6-12 months, driven by pure exposure-frequency statistics (distributional learning of the ambient phoneme-frequency distribution, no supervision signal beyond input statistics); (ii) **statistical/transitional-probability segmentation** (Saffran, Aslin & Newport 1996) -- infants extract word-like units from a continuous syllable stream using nothing but transitional-probability statistics, in as little as 2 minutes of exposure in the lab. Both are UNSUPERVISED, INPUT-STATISTICS-ONLY mechanisms -- no reference, no meaning, no social signal required. **Trigger for exit:** sufficient cumulative exposure volume to the native distribution (not a fixed age -- exposure-dependent, hence why bilingual/reduced-input infants show delayed/different narrowing timelines).

2. **Joint attention / the "9-month revolution" (6-14mo).** A qualitative shift from dyadic (infant-object OR infant-caregiver) to TRIADIC attention (infant-caregiver-object jointly) -- Tomasello's term for the cluster of new behaviors appearing together: gaze-following, proto-declarative pointing, social referencing. This is the hinge point of the entire trajectory: it converts perception into REFERENCE. Before this stage, a sound-pattern is just a statistically-regular chunk; after it, a sound-pattern can be BOUND to a caregiver's referential intent. **Trigger:** maturation of triadic social-cognitive capacity (theory-of-mind precursors) layered on top of already-tuned phonetic perception from Stage 1 -- Stage 1 supplies the "chunks," Stage 2 supplies the first mechanism for ATTACHING one of those chunks to a specific external referent rather than just co-occurring statistics.

3. **First words (10-15mo), comprehension-production gap.** Comprehension of specific words reliably precedes production by months (large, well-replicated gap; MacArthur-Bates CDI norms). First words are constrained by whole-object bias and (emerging) taxonomic bias (Markman) -- these act as HYPOTHESIS-SPACE-NARROWING PRIORS that make the reference-mapping problem (the "gavagai" problem: an ostended object licenses infinitely many candidate referents -- the object, its color, its parts, the action) tractable from very few (often one) exposures. **Trigger:** Stage 2's joint-attention/referential-intent-tracking machinery, applied to Stage 1's phonetic chunks, PLUS these innate/early-acquired constraint biases that prune the hypothesis space enough for one-shot mapping to succeed at all.

4. **The vocabulary spurt / acceleration (~50-200 words, commonly ~18mo, though real controversy on whether this is a true discontinuity -- see open question).** Multiple partially-independent accelerants stack: fast-mapping and cross-situational statistical learning (one-shot/parallel mechanisms, NOT compounding -- established in today's companion note), a threshold-gated mutual-exclusivity bias (onset ~100-200 known words), and critically a TRAINABLE, GENERALIZING shape-bias (Smith, Jones et al. 2002) that is a genuine "learning-to-learn" cascade -- the one true compounding accelerant identified in the companion note. Population-level, the spurt is best modeled as a percolation-like phase transition in a growing lexical network (Stella et al. 2018) -- new CONNECTIONS appearing, not existing pathways carrying signal further. **Trigger:** crossing a network-density/vocabulary-size threshold at which the shape-bias/mutual-exclusivity inductive templates, trained on the first ~50-100 words, become reliable enough to apply productively to new words with minimal exposure -- i.e., the trigger for Stage 4 is Stage 3 accumulating enough labeled instances to TRAIN the generalizing bias, not a maturational clock.

5. **Word combination / two-word stage (~18-24mo, frequently gated by a ~200-300-word vocabulary threshold more than by age alone).** Braine's "pivot grammar" (later critiqued as too rigid/not capturing the real variety of early combinations) and Bloom's semantic-relations approach describe the earliest multi-word utterances. Telegraphic speech (functor-word omission: dropping articles/prepositions/inflections while retaining content words) co-occurs, reflecting production/planning-capacity limits more than a competence gap (children comprehend function words earlier than they produce them, mirroring the Stage-3 comprehension-production gap pattern recurring at a new level). **Trigger:** a vocabulary-SIZE threshold (not pure age) -- strong circumstantial evidence that grammar onset depends on having enough labeled lexical material for combinatorial patterns to become statistically detectable/useful, echoing Stage 4's threshold-gated mutual exclusivity.

6. **Item-based constructions -> abstract syntax, with overregularization as the signature of rule extraction (24-48mo+).** Tomasello's usage-based account: early combinations are tied to SPECIFIC lexical items ("more juice," a specific verb-frame) -- "item-based islands" -- before generalizing to abstract, slot-based constructions. The clearest, most replicated evidence that abstraction/rule-extraction is really happening (not just memorized item-based patterns) is the **U-shaped overregularization curve** (Marcus et al. 1992; Pinker's "Words and Rules"): children produce correct irregular past-tense forms early (rote-memorized whole forms, e.g. "went," "broke"), then begin producing overregularization errors ("goed," "breaked") once a general "add -ed" rule is extracted and over-applied, then gradually correct back to accurate irregular+regular usage as the rule and the irregular-exception list are reconciled. Error rates are typically a MINORITY of irregular-verb uses (widely cited ~10-20%, not a majority), and the dip/recovery shape itself (not just the errors' existence) is the diagnostic signature of a genuine rule being extracted FROM a large-enough set of instances and then refined against counterexamples. **Trigger:** sufficient instance density of the regular pattern (a "big enough N" of regular past-tense exemplars) for a general rule to become statistically dominant over item-specific memorization -- the overregularization dip is direct behavioral evidence of the moment generalization overtakes memorization, then gets corrected by continued exposure to irregular counterexamples.

**The single strongest cross-stage pattern:** every transition in this chain is gated by the PRIOR stage supplying either (i) a narrowed/tuned representational substrate (Stage 1 -> 2, phonetic tuning enables robust word-form tracking), (ii) a new binding mechanism (Stage 2 -> 3, joint attention supplies the reference-binding operation), (iii) enough accumulated LABELED instances to train a generalizing bias (Stage 3 -> 4, Stage 4 -> 5, Stage 5 -> 6). This is NOT a fixed maturational clock ticking through six stages -- it is a data-and-mechanism-dependent cascade where each stage's OUTPUT (tuned phonemes; referential bindings; a growing labeled lexicon; combinatorial instances) is the NEXT stage's raw material. Critically, **stages 1 and 2 do not require any labeled/referential data at all** (pure input-statistics + social-attention mechanisms) -- only from Stage 3 onward does the system require external referential grounding, which directly connects to today's companion finding (`research_native_encoder_relational_structure_vs_grounded_meaning_2026-07-09.md`) that relational/statistical closure alone cannot manufacture grounding -- joint attention is precisely the biological "exogenous channel" that supplies it, structurally identical in kind to the "independent correction channel" pattern found convergently across three other domains today.

## (b) Cheap decisive test

**Curriculum-order ablation on the existing substrate (no new architecture required).** Take whatever ingest/training pipeline the substrate currently uses to build its relational/lexical structure, and run it under THREE orderings of an otherwise-identical data stream:
- **Arm BIO-ORDER**: present data in the biologically-evidenced order -- (1) pure distributional/co-occurrence exposure only (no referential grounding) long enough to build stable atom-level structure ("phonetic tuning" analog), THEN (2) a modest referentially-grounded seed set (analogous to joint-attention-scaffolded first words) THEN (3) scale up grounded vocabulary THEN (4) introduce relational/combinatorial (multi-atom) training only once the grounded vocabulary crosses a size threshold.
- **Arm SCRAMBLED**: identical total data budget, same items, random order (no staged gating).
- **Arm GRAMMAR-FIRST**: relational/combinatorial training presented BEFORE the vocabulary threshold is reached (inverts Stage 5's evidenced dependency), as a deliberate probe of whether the threshold-gating is load-bearing or incidental.

Measure, at matched total-data checkpoints: (i) grounded-attribute accuracy (reuses today's Prediction-B grounding metric), (ii) relational/multi-hop propagation accuracy (reuses today's grounding-cascade metric), (iii) a stability/collapse metric (does GRAMMAR-FIRST show worse generalization or more brittle/overfit-to-noise behavior, mirroring how real telegraphic-speech/overregularization errors are diagnostic of PREMATURE-but-real rule extraction rather than failure).

- **HARD-PASS (curriculum order matters, biology-first is better):** BIO-ORDER exceeds SCRAMBLED by a pre-registered margin (>=10 points) on (i) and/or (ii) at matched data budget, AND GRAMMAR-FIRST shows measurably worse or more brittle generalization than BIO-ORDER at the same checkpoint (an "overregularization-like" pattern: over-general application before the supporting instance-density is present).
- **HARD-FAIL (curriculum order is incidental, only total data volume matters):** all three arms converge to statistically indistinguishable performance at matched total data budget -- would mean the developmental ORDER is a biological accident of real-time constraints (a child can't literally receive all data at once) rather than an information-theoretic requirement, and a substrate not bound by that real-time constraint gains nothing from mimicking the order.

## (c) Falsifiable predictions (HARD-PASS / HARD-FAIL)

**Prediction 1 -- staged/threshold-gated curriculum beats scrambled-order at matched data budget.** Per Test (b) above. P estimate: **P=0.30** (deflated; capped at 0.50; further deflated from a naive higher prior because developmental staging in biology is partly a REAL-TIME/embodiment constraint -- infants cannot literally receive combinatorial syntax data before they have a vocabulary, because their caregivers naturally scale utterance complexity to the child's demonstrated vocabulary; this confound must be controlled for, since a substrate given all data upfront does not have this constraint and the ordering benefit could be an artifact of TEACHER behavior, not LEARNER structure).

**Prediction 2 -- an unsupervised, purely-distributional pre-training phase (Stage-1 analog: phonetic/statistical tuning with zero referential grounding) measurably improves later grounded-learning efficiency vs skipping straight to grounded training.** Concretely: pre-train atom/relational structure on pure co-occurrence statistics for N steps before introducing ANY grounded seed atoms, vs introducing grounded seeds from step 0. HARD-PASS: the pre-trained-first arm reaches a given grounded-accuracy target with FEWER grounded/labeled examples than the from-scratch arm (a sample-efficiency win from the unsupervised phase, directly analogous to phonetic narrowing making later word-segmentation more efficient). HARD-FAIL: no sample-efficiency difference, or the pre-trained-first arm is WORSE (would suggest the substrate's existing distributional-only representation actively interferes with later grounding rather than scaffolding it -- an important negative if found). P estimate: **P=0.35** (deflated, capped 0.50) -- this is the most literature-grounded and mechanistically clean of the predictions (distributional pre-training improving downstream sample efficiency is well-evidenced in adjacent ML literature too, though per house discipline that is evidence-of-plausibility, not the target to imitate).

**Prediction 3 -- mutual-exclusivity-style bias only helps AFTER a vocabulary-size threshold is crossed, and HURTS (or is neutral) before it.** Concretely: implement a simple mutual-exclusivity heuristic (a new label is preferentially NOT assigned to an already-labeled atom) and test its effect on labeling accuracy at small (<50), medium (100-200), and large (500+) existing-vocabulary sizes. HARD-PASS: the heuristic is neutral-to-harmful at small vocabulary and measurably helpful (>=10 points labeling accuracy) at medium/large vocabulary, replicating the threshold-gated pattern from child development. HARD-FAIL: the heuristic helps uniformly at all sizes or hurts at all sizes (no threshold signature) -- would mean the mechanism doesn't transfer to the substrate's actual atom-collision structure in the developmentally-predicted way. P estimate: **P=0.30** (deflated, capped 0.50) -- cheapest test of the three (pure post-hoc heuristic, no architecture change), but lowest confidence because mutual-exclusivity's threshold-gating in children is itself only medium-confidence in the literature (flagged in the companion cascade note as "medium confidence, threshold-gated weak accelerator").

**Prediction 4 -- premature combinatorial/relational training (before a vocabulary/instance-density threshold) produces an "overregularization-like" transient error pattern (over-general application, followed by correction with more data) rather than either clean learning or flat failure.** HARD-PASS: the GRAMMAR-FIRST arm from Test (b) shows a measurable RISE in a specific over-generalization error type (relations applied indiscriminately across atoms that shouldn't share them) followed by a DECLINE as more grounded data arrives within the same arm -- a genuine U-shaped signature, not just "worse throughout." HARD-FAIL: GRAMMAR-FIRST shows flat, undifferentiated poor performance with no characteristic rise-then-fall shape -- would mean the substrate's failure mode under premature training doesn't mechanistically resemble the child U-curve (still informative, rules out this specific analogy). P estimate: **P=0.20** (deflated, capped 0.50; most speculative -- requires the substrate to have something that behaves like "rule extraction from instance density" at all, which is not yet established to exist in the current architecture).

## (e) Substrate-product implications

1. **Curriculum order is a genuinely testable, near-zero-cost hypothesis against EXISTING substrate machinery** -- Test (b) requires no new architecture, only re-sequencing an already-built ingest pipeline across three arms at matched data budgets. This should be the very next buildable cell in this thread; per house discipline it is a cheap decisive test, not a big new-construction commitment.
2. **The single highest-value curriculum design implication: front-load a referentially-UNGROUNDED, pure-distributional/co-occurrence phase before introducing any labeled/grounded seed atoms** (Stage-1 analog). This is Prediction 2, the most literature-clean and mechanistically-motivated of the four predictions, and is compatible with (does not require abandoning) the near-random-code, correlation-hurts-capacity design principle already established -- the distributional phase builds STRUCTURE (co-occurrence statistics, atom relations), not semantic correlation of the codes themselves.
3. **Mutual exclusivity and shape-bias-style mechanisms should be introduced curriculum-STAGE-GATED, not applied uniformly from the start** (Predictions 1 and 3) -- i.e., these are not universal always-on heuristics but techniques whose USEFULNESS is itself vocabulary-size-dependent; naively baking them in from step 0 may be neutral-to-harmful per the child-development analogy, and should be tested rather than assumed.
4. **Overregularization-style transient errors, if observed in a substrate trained out-of-order, should be read as a DIAGNOSTIC SIGNAL of premature-but-real generalization, not simple failure** (Prediction 4) -- if the substrate ever shows a rise-then-fall error pattern under an aggressive/early combinatorial-training regime, the correct product framing is "the mechanism is extracting general structure ahead of its supporting data density," which is informative and fixable by re-sequencing, not a fundamental capability gap.
5. **The REPLICABLE mechanisms, ranked by expected substrate-fit (no external LM required for any of them):**
   - **Fast-mapping** -- HIGH replicability. Structurally this is just "one-shot atom-label binding from a single co-occurrence event," which is close to what an associative/HD store already does natively; the substrate's core operation (bind a code to a context in one exposure) IS a fast-mapping analog already, no new mechanism needed, only a training-order test.
   - **Cross-situational statistical learning** -- HIGH replicability. This is literally co-occurrence-frequency accumulation across multiple exposures with ambiguous single-instance mapping, resolved by aggregate statistics -- an extremely natural fit for a substrate whose native operations are statistical/frequency-based; likely the EASIEST of the three to demonstrate directly on existing machinery.
   - **Mutual exclusivity** -- MEDIUM replicability. Implementable as a simple heuristic constraint (down-weight assigning a new label to an already-well-labeled atom), but per Prediction 3 its BENEFIT is only expected to appear above a vocabulary-size threshold -- so replicating the mechanism is cheap, but demonstrating that it HELPS in the developmentally-predicted (threshold-gated) way is the real, only-medium-confidence test.
   - **Shape bias / trainable inductive-bias transfer** (already flagged in the companion note as the most novel, highest-payoff-if-true direction, P=0.25 there) -- LOWER replicability confidence, HIGHEST payoff if it works, since it is the one mechanism that would give the substrate an actual ACCELERATING learning-to-learn curve rather than a flat or merely-efficient one.
   - **Verdict on bake-in vs. learn:** per the developmental evidence itself (mutual exclusivity and shape bias are both ACQUIRED, not present at birth -- children under ~18-24mo do not show adult-strength versions of either bias), these should be TRAINED/EMERGENT from curriculum exposure, not hand-coded as permanent architectural priors, if the goal is genuine biological fidelity. Fast-mapping and cross-situational learning, by contrast, are closer to CORE, always-on operations of the associative-memory substrate from the start (not something that itself needs to be "acquired" -- they are closer to what memory IS, not a skill layered on top) and can reasonably be treated as bake-in.

## Sharpest open question

**Is the vocabulary spurt a genuine discontinuity in the underlying learning mechanism, or is it a curve-fitting/aggregation artifact that disappears once individual growth trajectories are modeled correctly** (Ganger & Brent 2004-style individual growth-curve critiques of the classic aggregate "spurt" claim), **and does this distinction even matter for curriculum design?** This matters directly for Test (b)/(c) above: if the "threshold" is a real mechanistic discontinuity (a percolation-like phase transition per Stella et al. 2018, or a genuine shape-bias-training crossover), then curriculum design should deliberately engineer toward crossing that threshold efficiently (front-load exactly the data that gets a substrate across it fastest). If instead the population-level spurt is substantially an averaging artifact over children with different but individually-SMOOTH onset times, then the "threshold" framing may be over-fit to a statistical illusion, and curriculum design should instead target smooth, monotonic sample-efficiency gains (Prediction 2) rather than engineering for a specific inflection point. This is answerable directly and cheaply from the substrate's own data (fit individual-style vs. aggregate-style growth curves to the substrate's own learning trajectory under Test (b)'s BIO-ORDER arm) without needing a definitive answer from the human literature first -- recommend treating this as a substrate-native measurement question, not a literature-dependent blocker.
