## (a) HEADLINE -- (S1) Staged trajectory map with transition triggers

Six stages, each gated by a specific prerequisite the prior stage supplies. Every clean transition in this literature is evidenced by a genuine discontinuity or threshold signature (a narrowing, a threshold, a U-shaped curve) -- but **several of the "classic" discontinuities are now actively contested as possible aggregation artifacts of averaging across individually-smooth trajectories**, which is the single most important calibration finding of this drill (see Prediction 2 / open question).

**Stage 0 -- prosodic/rhythmic bootstrapping (in utero -- birth).** Newborns already prefer their mother's voice (DeCasper & Fifer 1980) and their native language's prosody over a foreign one (Moon, Cooper & Fifer 1993), driven by passive in-utero exposure to the only acoustic information that transmits through the uterine wall (low-frequency pitch/rhythm). **No trigger needed beyond exposure duration** -- this stage requires zero supervision, zero reference, only passive statistical exposure to filtered ambient sound.

**Stage 1 -- prelinguistic distributional tuning (birth-9mo), TWO parallel tracks with a documented HANDOFF between them.**
- **Phonetic category narrowing**: universal discrimination at birth narrows toward native-only contrasts over 6-12mo (Werker & Tees 1984; Kuhl's Native Language Magnet theory, Kuhl et al. 2006). Causally shown to be driven by distributional/frequency statistics of the input (Maye, Werker & Gerken 2002 -- infants exposed to a unimodal vs. bimodal acoustic distribution along a /da/-/ta/ continuum form category boundaries accordingly). **Contested nuance**: narrowing is NOT a single uniform curve -- different cue types (stress/tone, then vowels, then consonants ~10mo) narrow on different timetables (Tsuji & Cristia 2014 meta-analysis), and recent work argues the CAPACITY to use distributional statistics is itself age-gated, not a constant background process.
- **Statistical word-segmentation**: infants extract word-like units from a continuous stream using pure transitional-probability statistics, with as little as 2 minutes of exposure (Saffran, Aslin & Newport 1996; mechanism refined to conditional/transitional probability specifically by Aslin, Saffran & Newport 1998). **Contested mechanism**: whether this is really transitional-probability computation or incremental chunk-formation (Perruchet's PARSER model, empirically distinguished from TP-tracking in several studies) is genuinely unresolved.
- **The handoff (the sharpest true sequence-dependency finding in this entire stage)**: Thiessen & Saffran (2003) show a **cue-weighting handoff** at ~9 months -- at 7mo, infants rely on statistical cues over prosodic/stress cues when the two conflict; by 9mo, infants now favor the native-language stress pattern OVER raw statistics. Their reading: general-purpose statistical learning is used FIRST to bootstrap discovery of the native stress pattern, and primary reliance is then handed off to that now-learned, native-specialized cue. **Trigger for exit: statistics-derived discovery of a native-specific regularity reaches reliability, at which point the system re-prioritizes the derived regularity over the general-purpose mechanism that discovered it** -- a clean, citable instance of "bootstrap with a general mechanism, then specialize."
- Feldman, Griffiths, Goldwater & Morgan (2013) computational modeling argues the causal arrow can also run in REVERSE (lexical/segmentation information helps refine phonetic categories) -- evidence AGAINST a strict serial "phonetics must finish before segmentation" gate, in favor of parallel, mutually-reinforcing development.
- **Babbling**: canonical babbling onset ~6-8mo is motor-maturation-driven (Oller), but its CONTENT is causally, experimentally shown to be shaped by contingent parental feedback (Goldstein & Schwade 2008: infants given real-time contingent vocal feedback rapidly restructure babbling toward mature forms; non-contingent feedback produces no such effect). Critically, the loop runs **both ways**: infant babbling itself causes caregivers to simplify their own speech in real time (Elmlinger, Schwade & Goldstein 2019) -- **the learner's own noisy output elicits a simplified curriculum from the environment**, a self-generated scaffolding effect distinct from anything else in this trajectory.

**Stage 2 -- joint attention / the shift from perceiving to referring (6-14mo), with an internal two-step structure.** The classic "9-month revolution" (Tomasello) framing -- a discrete shift from dyadic to triadic (infant-caregiver-object) attention -- is **directly contested by a 2025 study**: Salter & Carpenter (*Infancy*, 2025), using structured elicitation rather than free play, found joint-attention BIDS present in 44% of infants by 6mo, 72% by 7mo, 92% before 9mo, with **no discrete month-to-month jump** -- gradual, continuous emergence from earlier dyadic capacities, not a revolution, with the classic "9-month" figure likely partly a measurement artifact of prior free-play-only methodology. Carpenter, Nagell & Tomasello (1998) established the reliable ORDER within this window regardless of abruptness: **sharing -> following -> directing** attention (responding-to-joint-attention, RJA, reliably precedes initiating-joint-attention, IJA). **A second, later sub-stage is required for full function**: Baldwin's discrepant-labeling paradigm (1991, 1993) shows infants only reach adult-like use of the SPEAKER's (not their own) attentional focus to resolve word reference gradually across the second year -- 14-15mo show no reliable preference, 16-17mo still default to their OWN object, only 18-19mo correctly use the speaker's gaze even when it conflicts with their own focus. **So joint-attention-as-infrastructure (~9mo) and joint-attention-as-reference-resolution-tool (~18-19mo) are two different achievements, roughly a year apart, not one event.** Predictive value for later vocabulary is robust and multiply replicated (Brooks & Meltzoff gaze-following; Mundy & Gomes RJA/IJA dissociation; Lasch, Carlson & Elison 2023 controlling for concurrent ability) but **two important tempering findings**: (i) Yu, Suanda & Smith (2018), using dual head-mounted eye-tracking, found infant SUSTAINED ATTENTION to an object -- not joint attention per se -- is the better/proximal predictor of later vocabulary; JA's predictive power may be mediated by JA's role in producing sustained attention, not by JA itself as the causal ingredient; (ii) Kirk et al.'s (2022) meta-analysis of 25 pointing studies puts the true pointing-to-vocabulary effect at only r=0.20 (real but modest, moderator-dependent), materially smaller than the popular single-study narrative implies. **Trigger for exit: Stage 1 supplies tuned phonetic chunks; Stage 2 supplies the reference-binding mechanism (first as raw triadic infrastructure, then as a mature intention-tracking tool) that lets a chunk attach to a specific external referent rather than merely co-occur with one.**

**Stage 3 -- first words, large comprehension-production gap (9-15mo).** Canonical babbling (established precursor, its absence past ~10-12mo is a clinical risk marker) gives way to protowords/phonetically-consistent-forms (~9mo) then true referential words (10-13mo). Benedict (1979) is the classic quantified source for the comprehension-production gap: 50 words COMPREHENDED by ~13mo vs. 50 words PRODUCED not until ~18-19mo -- a 5-6 month lag, comprehension proceeding at roughly 2x the rate of production (small-N=8 original study; direction is high-confidence, exact magnitude medium-confidence). Whole-object and taxonomic biases (Markman) are described in the literature as operative from **~18 months onward** -- i.e. emerging AFTER first-word onset, functioning specifically during the rapid-growth phase to narrow the hypothesis space once words are being mapped faster with less per-word contextual support -- not as a pre-condition for the very first words. **Trigger for exit: Stage 2's reference-binding mechanism, applied to Stage 1's phonetic chunks, crosses a usability threshold once a small labeled set (protowords) has accumulated.**

**Stage 4 -- the vocabulary "spurt" (contested as a real discontinuity -- see below), ~50-300 words, commonly dated ~18mo.** Three competing/incompletely-reconciled accounts of WHAT triggers acceleration: (i) the classic "naming insight" (Gopnik & Meltzoff-style symbolic-realization account) -- oldest, least mechanistically supported today; (ii) **McMurray (2007, *Science*) / Mitchell & McMurray (2009)'s parallel-statistical-learning account** -- acceleration is a MATHEMATICAL by-product of learning many words in parallel from a difficulty distribution with few easy + many hard words; no cognitive change, no insight, no discontinuity in the child at all is required -- **currently the best-supported, most parsimonious account** in the modeling literature, and directly consistent with the individual-trajectory-smoothness finding below; (iii) the percolation/network-density-threshold account (Stella et al. 2018, established in the companion note) -- a structurally different, network-growth-based frame, **not yet reconciled with (ii) in the literature surveyed**. Layered on top: fast-mapping/cross-situational learning (one-shot, not compounding), trainable shape bias (Smith/Jones 2002 -- genuine compounding accelerant), and threshold-gated mutual exclusivity (~100-200 words) -- all established in the companion note and not re-derived here.

**Stage 5 -- word combinations (18-24mo), gated by a VOCABULARY-SIZE threshold more than by age.** Nelson (1973) established the classic ~50-productive-word threshold for the ONSET of any two-word combination (range 15-24mo). Bates & Goodman (1997), using MacArthur-Bates CDI data, found vocabulary size and grammar score correlate at **r=.84** and describe grammatical competence as "strictly linked to the lexical stage" -- but this second finding is tied to a DIFFERENT, higher threshold (~200-300 words) associated with the grammar-BURST/acceleration phase, not bare two-word onset. **These are two distinct gates and should not be conflated**: ~50 words gates first combination; ~200-300 words gates rapid morphosyntactic acceleration. An independent, earlier-appearing predictor: gesture-speech combination onset (a point + a word, e.g. "eat" + point at cookie) reliably PRECEDES and predicts the onset of two-word speech -- representational/combinatorial readiness shows up in gesture before it shows up in speech. Braine's (1963) "pivot grammar" (a closed pivot-word class + open content-word class) was the first descriptive framework but is now understood as too simple (Bloom's critique: the identical surface string, e.g. "Mommy sock," can encode different underlying semantic relations depending on context, which pivot/open cannot distinguish) -- superseded by Bloom's semantic-relations approach (agent-action, possessor-possession, etc.). Telegraphic speech (content words retained, function words/inflections dropped) co-occurs; the balance of evidence (comprehension of function words outpacing production, mirroring Stage 3's pattern recurring at a new grammatical level) favors a production/performance-limitation account over a true competence gap, though this remains actively, not fully, settled. **Trigger for exit: crossing the ~50-word threshold for onset, then the ~200-300-word threshold for acceleration -- vocabulary SIZE, not age, is the best-evidenced gating variable in this whole trajectory.**

**Stage 6 -- item-based constructions -> abstract syntax, with overregularization as the clearest behavioral signature of rule extraction (24-54mo).** Tomasello's usage-based/"verb-island" account: early combinations are tied to SPECIFIC lexical items, not abstract slot-fillers. Akhtar (1999)'s novel-verb word-order experiment is the sharpest evidence-timeline data point: children 2.5-3ish REPRODUCE a taught ungrammatical (non-SVO) word order with a novel verb (no general rule applied yet); only by ~4.5 years do children reliably self-correct novel verbs into canonical order, evidencing full abstraction. **This is genuinely, actively contested**: "no verb is an island" corpus/experimental findings (Ninio and others) show considerable inter-item transfer EARLIER than the strict verb-island account predicts, and Pine, Lieven & Rowland (1998) found some children organize early syntax around pronouns rather than verbs -- flag as live theoretical debate, not settled. The clearest, most-replicated evidence of genuine rule-extraction is the **U-shaped overregularization curve** (Marcus et al. 1992): correct irregular forms first (rote-memorized) -> overregularization errors ("goed," "breaked") once a general rule is extracted and over-applied -> gradual correction. **Precise, well-replicated numbers**: onset ~age 2-3, error rate a genuine MINORITY of irregular-verb uses (Marcus et al.'s own corpus figure: ~2.5% of irregular-verb tokens overregularized, not the 10-20% loosely cited elsewhere), and the pattern PERSISTS at a low, roughly constant rate into the school-age years rather than spiking and cleanly vanishing. The mechanistic interpretation (Pinker/Marcus dual-mechanism: symbolic rule + associative memory for irregulars, vs. Rumelhart & McClelland's single associative network reproducing the same U-shape via changing input statistics with no symbolic rule at all) remains a live, actively-relitigated debate (modern RNN work, e.g. Kirov & Cotterell 2018, revisits it favorably for the connectionist side). **Trigger for exit: sufficient instance density of the regular pattern for a general rule to become statistically dominant over item-specific memorization -- the U-shaped dip is the direct behavioral marker of the moment generalization overtakes memorization, before being corrected back against irregular counterexamples.**

**The single strongest cross-stage pattern, confirmed and SHARPENED by live lit-scan (vs. the weaker version originally hypothesized):** every transition is gated by the PRIOR stage supplying either (i) a narrowed/tuned representational substrate (Stage 1's own internal handoff -- Thiessen & Saffran's statistics-bootstraps-then-hands-off-to-stress-cue finding is the cleanest single citation for this pattern in the ENTIRE trajectory), (ii) a reference-binding mechanism built in two sub-steps (Stage 2's infrastructure-then-pragmatic-competence split), or (iii) enough accumulated instances to cross a threshold (Stage 3->4, Stage 4->5, Stage 5->6, each independently evidenced with real threshold numbers: ~50 words, ~100-200 words, ~200-300 words). **Critically, Stages 0-1 require ZERO labeled/referential data** (pure input-statistics + motor-practice mechanisms); only from Stage 2 onward does the system require an external referential/social-attention channel -- directly connecting to today's cross-domain meta-pattern (self-referential/statistical closure cannot manufacture the property it lacks; an exogenous channel is required), with joint attention as the biological instance of exactly that exogenous channel (Baldwin's discrepant-labeling studies are the sharpest proof infants use the SPEAKER's tracked attentional state, not their own co-occurring perception, to resolve reference).

## (b) Cheap decisive test

**Curriculum-order ablation on the existing substrate (no new architecture required).** Run whatever ingest/training pipeline the substrate currently uses under THREE data-presentation orderings at matched total data budget:
- **Arm BIO-ORDER**: (1) pure distributional/co-occurrence exposure only, no referential grounding (Stage 0-1 analog), for a fixed budget, THEN (2) a modest referentially-grounded seed set introduced (Stage 2-3 analog) THEN (3) scale grounded vocabulary past a pre-registered threshold analog (Stage 4-5 gate) THEN (4) introduce relational/combinatorial multi-atom training only after that threshold is crossed (Stage 5-6 gate).
- **Arm SCRAMBLED**: identical total data budget and items, random order, no staged gating.
- **Arm GRAMMAR-FIRST**: relational/combinatorial training presented BEFORE the vocabulary-threshold analog is reached, deliberately inverting Stage 5's evidenced dependency, to probe whether the threshold-gating is load-bearing or incidental.

Measure at matched checkpoints: (i) grounded-attribute accuracy (reuses today's Prediction-B metric), (ii) relational/multi-hop propagation accuracy (reuses today's grounding-cascade metric), (iii) an over-generalization/brittleness signature (does GRAMMAR-FIRST show a rise-then-partial-recovery error pattern analogous to overregularization, or just flat poor performance).

- **HARD-PASS (curriculum order matters, biology-first wins):** BIO-ORDER exceeds SCRAMBLED by a pre-registered margin (>=10 points) on (i) and/or (ii) at matched data budget, AND GRAMMAR-FIRST shows a measurable overregularization-like transient (rise then partial correction) rather than either clean learning or flat failure.
- **HARD-FAIL (curriculum order is incidental):** all three arms converge to statistically indistinguishable performance at matched total data budget -- would indicate the human developmental ORDER is substantially a real-time/embodiment constraint (a child physically cannot receive all data at once, and caregivers naturally scale input complexity to demonstrated vocabulary) rather than an information-theoretic requirement, so a substrate not bound by that real-time constraint gains nothing from mimicking the order.

## (c) Falsifiable predictions (HARD-PASS / HARD-FAIL)

**Prediction 1 -- staged/threshold-gated curriculum beats scrambled-order at matched data budget.** Per Test (b). P estimate: **P=0.28** (deflated; capped 0.50; deflated further than a naive prior because the McMurray/Mitchell parallel-statistical-learning finding for Stage 4 argues acceleration can emerge WITHOUT any special staged mechanism at all -- if that generalizes, ordering effects elsewhere in the trajectory may be similarly weaker than the "threshold" framing implies, and because developmental staging is partly a real-time-constraint confound the substrate does not share).

**Prediction 2 -- the vocabulary-spurt threshold effect, if it appears in the substrate at all, will most likely appear as an INDIVIDUAL-CONFIGURATION-DEPENDENT phenomenon (only some parameter settings show a true discontinuity), NOT a universal discontinuity -- directly modeled on Ganger & Brent (2004)'s finding that only 5 of 38 children (~13%) showed trajectories genuinely better fit by a discontinuous/logistic model vs. a smooth quadratic one, and Goldfield & Reznick (1990)'s finding that only 13 of 18 children ("spurters") showed any multi-month acceleration at all.** Buildable cell: run the substrate's vocabulary-accumulation process across a sweep of initialization seeds / hyperparameter settings and individually curve-fit each run (logistic/discontinuous vs. quadratic/smooth), rather than only inspecting the population-AVERAGED curve (which can manufacture an apparent discontinuity via aggregation even when no individual run has one -- this is the precise statistical artifact Ganger & Brent identified in the child data). **HARD-PASS**: a genuine, reproducible MINORITY (not all, not zero) of individual runs show a statistically-preferred logistic fit, mirroring the ~13% human figure order-of-magnitude, while the aggregate/averaged curve looks smoother-and-more-universal than any single run's true shape. **HARD-FAIL**: either ALL runs show clean discontinuities (would suggest the substrate's percolation/network-growth account, not the human individual-variability account, is the operative analogy) or NONE do (smooth throughout, no threshold signature at all, at any configuration). P estimate: **P=0.35** (deflated, capped 0.50) -- highest-confidence prediction in this note, because it is a nearly-free re-analysis of curve-SHAPE on data the substrate likely already produces, requiring no new architecture, only a different statistical lens (individual-trajectory fitting vs. aggregate-curve inspection) -- directly reusable methodology from Ganger & Brent with no substrate-specific assumptions.

**Prediction 3 -- an unsupervised, purely-distributional pre-training phase (Stage-0/1 analog: statistics with zero referential grounding) measurably improves later grounded-learning sample efficiency vs. skipping straight to grounded training, AND a demonstrable "statistics-bootstraps-then-hands-off" pattern (a Thiessen & Saffran analog) is detectable -- i.e., a general-purpose statistical signal is used early, then a more specialized/derived signal becomes dominant once it reaches sufficient reliability.** HARD-PASS: (i) the pre-trained-first arm reaches a grounded-accuracy target with fewer grounded examples than a from-scratch arm, AND (ii) inspecting which signal the readout weights most heavily over the course of training shows an early reliance on the general/distributional signal followed by a measurable shift toward reliance on a more specific derived signal once THAT signal's own reliability crosses a threshold. HARD-FAIL: no sample-efficiency difference, or no detectable handoff pattern (the same signal stays dominant throughout, or the derived signal is never favored over the general one regardless of its reliability). P estimate: **P=0.30** (deflated, capped 0.50) -- part (i) alone is well-supported by adjacent literature; part (ii), the specific handoff-detection sub-claim, is more speculative and the primary reason this isn't rated higher.

**Prediction 4 -- mutual-exclusivity- and taxonomic/whole-object-bias-style heuristics only help AFTER a vocabulary-size threshold is crossed (mirroring their real developmental onset ~100-200 and ~18mo+ respectively, i.e. AFTER not BEFORE first words), and are neutral-to-harmful before it.** HARD-PASS: implementing these as simple constraints and testing at small (<50), medium (100-200), and large (500+) existing-vocabulary sizes shows the predicted threshold pattern (neutral/harmful small, helpful medium/large, >=10-point swing). HARD-FAIL: uniform effect (helps or hurts at all sizes, no threshold signature). P estimate: **P=0.28** (deflated, capped 0.50) -- cheapest test of the four (pure post-hoc heuristic, no architecture change), but the underlying developmental threshold-gating claim is itself only medium-confidence in the literature.

**Prediction 5 -- premature combinatorial/relational training (Stage 5-6 analog, before the vocabulary-threshold analog) produces an overregularization-like transient (a rise in over-general error followed by partial correction with more data), not flat failure.** Per Test (b)'s GRAMMAR-FIRST arm. P estimate: **P=0.18** (deflated, capped 0.50; most speculative of the five -- requires the substrate to have something that behaves like "rule extraction from instance density" at all, not yet established to exist in the current architecture).

**Connects to `research_native_encoder_relational_structure_vs_grounded_meaning_2026-07-09.md` and the same-day cross-domain meta-pattern** ("self-referential/statistical closure cannot manufacture the property it lacks; an exogenous/independent channel is required"). Joint attention (Stage 2) is the sharpest biological instance: Stage 1's pure distributional statistics, however rich, cannot by themselves produce REFERENCE -- Baldwin's discrepant-labeling studies are direct proof infants use the SPEAKER's tracked attentional/intentional state, not their own co-occurring perception, to resolve reference. **New nuance this drill adds to that meta-pattern**: the exogenous channel itself is not delivered instantaneously or completely -- Baldwin's own data show infrastructure (~9mo) and mature functional use of that infrastructure (~18-19mo) are separated by roughly a year, meaning "the exogenous channel becomes available" is itself a staged sub-process, not a single event. This is a useful refinement for the wider research-program pattern: an exogenous grounding channel isn't just present-or-absent, it has its OWN maturation curve, and a substrate design that adds an exogenous channel should expect (and test for) a similar ramp rather than assuming instant full function the moment the channel exists.

**New pattern this drill surfaces, not previously flagged today: self-elicited curriculum scaffolding.** Goldstein & Schwade (2008) and Elmlinger, Schwade & Goldstein (2019)'s bidirectional babbling-feedback loop is a genuinely different mechanism class from the "exogenous independent channel" pattern above: here the LEARNER's own (still-immature) output causes the teacher/environment to simplify ITS input in response, meaning the learner is not just receiving a fixed exogenous curriculum but actively shaping the curriculum it receives. Worth flagging as a candidate SIXTH mechanism class (distinct from fast-mapping, mutual exclusivity, cross-situational learning, shape bias, and percolation) for a future drill: a training loop where the substrate's own current output statistics feed back into what training data/complexity is subsequently presented to it, rather than a fixed, pre-ordered curriculum.

## (e) Substrate-product implications -- (S2) MAPS-TO-SUBSTRATE

**Concrete curriculum order recommendation (six stages, in order):**
1. **Pure distributional/co-occurrence exposure, ZERO referential grounding** (Stage 0-1 analog) -- build atom/relational structure from raw co-occurrence statistics alone first. No new mechanism needed; this is compatible with (does not require abandoning) the near-random-code/correlation-hurts-capacity design principle already established, since this phase builds STRUCTURE, not semantic correlation of the codes.
2. **Introduce a small, referentially-grounded seed set** (Stage 2-3 analog) -- analogous to joint-attention-scaffolded first words. Per the Baldwin timeline, expect (and test for) a TWO-PHASE ramp here: raw exogenous-channel infrastructure coming online first, full/mature use of that channel for reference-resolution arriving measurably later, not simultaneously.
3. **Scale grounded vocabulary past a pre-registered threshold analog** (Stage 4-5 gate, ~50-word and ~200-300-word human analogs) before introducing relational/combinatorial training -- vocabulary SIZE, not "amount of time trained," is the best-evidenced gating variable in the whole human trajectory, and should be the substrate's gating variable too, tested directly via Test (b).
4. **Introduce relational/multi-atom (combinatorial) training only after that threshold**, expecting an overregularization-like transient if introduced prematurely (a genuine diagnostic signal, not simple failure, if observed).

**Curriculum order is a near-zero-cost, immediately buildable hypothesis** (Test (b)) against EXISTING substrate machinery -- next buildable cell in this thread, no new architecture required, per house discipline (cheap decisive test before big new construction).

**REPLICABLE mechanisms, ranked by expected substrate-fit (no external LM required for any):**
- **Cross-situational statistical learning** -- HIGH replicability, likely the EASIEST to demonstrate directly. This is co-occurrence-frequency accumulation across multiple exposures with ambiguous single-instance mapping resolved by aggregate statistics -- an extremely natural fit for a substrate whose native operations are already statistical/frequency-based.
- **Fast-mapping** -- HIGH replicability. One-shot atom-label binding from a single co-occurrence event is close to a native associative-store operation already; likely needs a training-ORDER test more than a new mechanism.
- **Mutual exclusivity** -- MEDIUM replicability. Cheap to implement as a heuristic (down-weight assigning a new label to an already-labeled atom), but per Prediction 4 the real developmental evidence says its BENEFIT is threshold-gated (only helps after ~100-200 "words"), so demonstrating the mechanism is easy, demonstrating it works in the developmentally-predicted (threshold-gated) way is the real, medium-confidence test.
- **Shape bias / trainable inductive-bias transfer** (companion note, P=0.25 there) -- LOWER confidence, HIGHEST payoff if true (the one mechanism giving an actual accelerating learning-to-learn curve).
- **Self-elicited curriculum scaffolding** (new candidate this drill surfaces) -- UNTESTED, most novel; would require the substrate's own output to feed back into subsequent training-data selection, a genuinely different architecture question from the other four.

**Bake-in vs. learn verdict:** per the developmental evidence, mutual exclusivity and shape bias are both ACQUIRED over the first 1-2 years (not present at birth; children under ~18-24mo show no adult-strength version of either), so per biological fidelity these should be TRAINED/EMERGENT from curriculum exposure, not hand-coded permanent priors. Fast-mapping and cross-situational learning are closer to CORE, always-on properties of associative memory itself (present from the earliest data in some form) and can reasonably be treated as bake-in. Joint-attention-style exogenous grounding is a THIRD category: it requires an external channel that does not yet exist purely as a training-order effect -- it needs an actual second data source (a referential/labeling signal), which is an architecture decision, not just a curriculum-order decision.

**Replicability verdict, honestly stated:** three of five mechanisms (cross-situational, fast-mapping, mutual-exclusivity-as-heuristic) are near-term buildable with existing machinery and a training-order/threshold test; shape-bias-as-generalizing-template and self-elicited-scaffolding are genuinely open research bets requiring new measurement or new architecture respectively, not curriculum re-ordering alone.

## Sharpest open question -- (S3)

**Is EVERY threshold/discontinuity claim in this trajectory (the 9-month "revolution," the vocabulary "spurt," item-based-to-abstract-syntax generalization) a genuine population-level phenomenon, or are some/most of them aggregation artifacts of averaging across children with individually-smooth-but-staggered trajectories -- and does the answer differ stage by stage?** This drill found DIRECT, citable evidence that this question has ALREADY been raised and partially answered differently at two different stages: the 9-month joint-attention "revolution" is now directly challenged as gradual (Salter & Carpenter 2025, no discrete monthly jump), while the vocabulary spurt shows a MIXED answer -- real for a majority-but-not-universal subgroup (Goldfield & Reznick's ~72% "spurters") under a looser criterion, but real for only a small minority (~13%) under Ganger & Brent's stricter individual-growth-curve statistical test. **If this pattern generalizes (most "discontinuities" in this literature are actually individual-level-smooth, population-level-apparent), the strongest curriculum-design implication flips**: rather than engineering a substrate to deliberately CROSS a specific threshold efficiently, the better design target may be smooth, monotonic sample-efficiency gains at every data volume (Prediction 3), with any observed "threshold" in the substrate's own learning curves expected to be a per-run/per-configuration idiosyncrasy (per Prediction 2) rather than a universal target to engineer toward. This is answerable directly and cheaply from the substrate's OWN data (individual-run curve-shape fitting, exactly Ganger & Brent's method, applied to whatever vocabulary/atom-accumulation process the substrate already runs) without needing the human literature to settle it first -- recommended as a substrate-native measurement question, not a literature-dependent blocker, and the single most reusable methodological import from this entire drill.
