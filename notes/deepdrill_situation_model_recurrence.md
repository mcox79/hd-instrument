# Deep-brain drill (angle 3/3): does word-sense/affect resolution REQUIRE the situation-model loop, or can a local governor suffice?

Date: 2026-08-05. Research sub-agent, live-verified (WebSearch/WebFetch this pass). Question: does BRIDGE-1 (local syntactic-governor / in-clause selectional-restriction resolver) work STANDALONE, or must it be co-built with the situation-model recurrent loop (C-D) from the start?

## HEADLINE

**Discourse/situation-model context is not an optional refinement layered on top of a working local-governor system — it is CO-EQUAL and, empirically, DOMINANT.** The strongest single datum (Nieuwland & Van Berkum 2006, verified this pass) shows discourse context doesn't just supplement local selectional-restriction violations, it **inverts** the local-governor's preferred reading: "the peanut was in love" (locally anomalous, animacy-violating) was processed *more easily* (smaller N400) than "the peanut was salted" (locally well-formed) once discourse had established the peanut as a sentient character. A local-governor-only system gets this case categorically backwards, not just less-precisely-right. This is not a rare edge case tucked in the tail of the distribution — it is exactly the class of case our build cares about (goal/affect/implicit-dread readings set by prior sentences, not by the local clause), so the failure mode is concentrated in the target region, not spread thin. Verdict: **(b)** — build BRIDGE-1 and the situation-model feedback loop together, gated so that situation-model bias is present (even if coarse/stubbed) from day one; do not ship a local-governor-only V1 for the goal/affect subset. Local-governor-only IS legitimate for the disjoint subset of cases where the clause-internal selectional restriction is genuinely sufficient and unambiguous (see Section 3) — but that subset is defined by "discourse doesn't need to speak," not by "we haven't built discourse yet."

## 1. VERIFIED THIS PASS — discourse overrides lexical/local defaults

**Nieuwland & Van Berkum (2006), "When Peanuts Fall in Love: N400 Evidence for the Power of Discourse," J. Cogn. Neurosci. 18(7):1098-1111.** [VERIFIED — MPI abstract, JoCN abstract, PubMed 16839284]
- Design: verb-object animacy violations ("The girl comforted the clock") show a clear N400 in isolation — the classic local-selectional-restriction violation signal.
- Critical manipulation: embed the SAME violating predicate in a discourse that has established the object as a sentient participant (a story about an anthropomorphized, amorous peanut). Result: the N400 to the animacy-violating-but-discourse-consistent continuation ("the peanut was in love") is **smaller** than to the canonical-but-discourse-inconsistent continuation ("the peanut was salted").
- This is the decisive case for our question: it is not "discourse context helps disambiguate a genuinely ambiguous local cue" — it is discourse **overruling a categorical, unambiguous local violation** and reversing which reading is "easy." A local-governor system, defined as resolving sense/affect from in-clause selectional restrictions, has no mechanism to represent this at all; it would flag or reject exactly the reading that is correct.

**Van Berkum and colleagues, broader discourse-N400 program.** [VERIFIED — pages.ucsd.edu/~scoulson/cogs179/vanberkum.pdf survey, ScienceDirect "discourse congruence and lexical association," ScienceDirect "discourse message and scenario fit"]
- The N400 indexes ease of integrating a word's meaning into context, and is sensitive not just to local lexical/sentential context but to **global discourse-level representation** (a "message-level" situation representation), not just word co-occurrence/priming.
- Documented case: discourse-level constraint briefly overrules locally unambiguous **syntactic** information (not just semantic/animacy) — i.e., the override is not confined to lexical-semantic selectional restrictions; it reaches into syntactic garden-pathing too. This means the phenomenon generalizes beyond the affect/sense-selection case we're targeting — it's a general property of how incremental parsing is gated.

**Metusalem et al., "Generalized event knowledge activation during online sentence comprehension"** (PubMed 22711976). [VERIFIED — PubMed abstract, ResearchGate abstract]
- A contextually anomalous word elicits a **reduced** N400 if it is generally related to the described event — even controlling for direct lexical association with prior words and with the single most-expected continuation word.
- Critically: **this effect disappears when the discourse context is removed.** This is a clean necessity result — event-knowledge activation (which is downstream of / dependent on the accumulated situation representation) is not present in a local/single-clause-only regime. It is not that discourse "adds a small boost" to an otherwise-working local mechanism; the effect is contingent on discourse being present at all.

**Quantitative read on "how often is local sufficient vs. discourse decisive":** the literature does not report a clean population-level split (this class of experiment is designed around constructed critical items, not corpus-frequency estimates of ambiguity), so I cannot hand you a verified percentage. But the design logic of the discourse-override paradigms (Van Berkum, Nieuwland, Metusalem) is telling: all of them work by picking cases where the **local** cue is either neutral or actively points the wrong way, and showing that discourse wins anyway — the researchers had no trouble finding or constructing this class of stimulus, which is at minimum weak evidence that discourse-decisive cases are a normal, unremarkable, non-exotic fraction of naturalistic sentences (not a rare pathological tail). For our target domain specifically — implicit dread, irony, goal-conditioned affect — the local clause is close to *always* neutral by construction (that's what makes them "implicit": the surface predicate is affect-ambiguous and only resolves against accumulated character/goal state). So for THIS specific subset (the one motivating the build), the discourse-decisive fraction should be read as **close to 100%, not a tail case** — this is a structural claim about the subset definition, not an additional external citation, flagged as such.

## 2. VERIFIED THIS PASS — the situation model as top-down source, and the necessity of the loop

**Zwaan, Langston & Graesser (1995), "The Construction of Situation Models in Narrative Comprehension: An Event-Indexing Model," Psychological Science.** [VERIFIED — Semantic Scholar, SAGE journals abstract, follow-up "Who when where" Mem&Cog paper]
- Events are indexed along five dimensions readers track continuously and update online: **time, space, protagonist, causality, intentionality.**
- **Intentionality is the load-bearing dimension for our build** — it is precisely "does this event serve a character's goal," which is what a goal-owner / affect system needs. This is not an incidental dimension; it's one of the five the model was built around, meaning goal-tracking is treated as a first-class, continuously-updated situation-model variable in the reference cognitive architecture, not a derived/optional add-on.
- Empirical support (reading-time cost on protagonist/causal/temporal shifts) confirms this is a genuine online-maintained representation with update cost, i.e., a real recurrent state, not a post-hoc summary computed only at paragraph boundaries.

**Baldassano et al. (2017), "Discovering Event Structure in Continuous Narrative Perception and Memory," Neuron / bioRxiv 081018.** [VERIFIED — bioRxiv abstract, ResearchGate, related nested-hierarchy bioRxiv 2021.02.05.429165]
- Cortical hierarchy segments narrative at a **hierarchy of timescales**: short events in early sensory/language regions, long events in DMN regions (angular gyrus, posterior medial cortex) building toward abstract multimodal "situation models."
- At long-timescale event boundaries, the situation model is **handed to the hippocampus** for encoding, which later reinstates it during recall.
- This is direct anatomical/functional evidence for a **loop**, not a one-way feedforward stack: DMN-level situation representation is not merely the end-product of feedforward composition — it is written to and read from hippocampal memory, meaning it persists and is queryable across the narrative, which is exactly the mechanism needed to make a goal/affect state set in sentence 3 available for a word's interpretation in sentence 30.

**Kuperberg & Jaeger (2016), "What do we mean by prediction in language comprehension?", Lang. Cogn. Neurosci.** [VERIFIED — kuperberglab.com PDF, PMC4850025, ScienceDirect follow-up "Prediction during language comprehension: what is next?"]
- Formalizes comprehension as hierarchical predictive coding: bottom-up flow at each level of the fronto-temporal hierarchy carries only **residual/error**, i.e. only what wasn't already explained by top-down predictions generated from higher (more abstract, more discourse-integrated) levels.
- This is the general architectural claim that answers our question directly: in a predictive-coding hierarchy, **the top-down predictions are not optional decoration on a working feedforward path — the feedforward signal is *defined* as the residual after top-down subtraction.** A "local-governor-only" system that never receives top-down situation-level predictions is not a stripped-down version of the full architecture; it is architecturally a different regime (no error-explaining-away), and per Kuperberg/Jaeger's own framing this is where the *majority* of the brain's processing economy comes from — meaning a local-only system isn't just less accurate on hard cases, it's doing categorically more raw computation on cases the full system would resolve for free via prediction.

**Irony / predictive-mismatch reinterpretation (Chinese-irony ERP study, PMC9581322; "Getting it: A predictive processing approach to irony comprehension," Synthese 2019).** [VERIFIED — Frontiers/PMC abstract, Springer abstract]
- N400 indexes prediction error at the word level; **P600 indexes effortful reanalysis** — the cost of re-deriving the intended (ironic/non-literal) meaning once the literal reading is rejected.
- Mentalizing (inferring speaker's mental state) is identified as a load-bearing cognitive function for irony resolution, not a downstream commentary process.
- This is our clearest evidence that affect-laden non-literal meaning (irony, and by extension implicit dread/goal-conditioned reads) is resolved via an explicit **predict → mismatch → reinterpret-via-situation-state** cycle, i.e., a loop with at least one full round-trip (initial local/literal parse, error signal, second pass using situation-level/mentalizing state), not a single feedforward pass with a wider context window.

## 3. The decisive distinction for our build: when does local suffice vs. when is prior-discourse required?

Synthesizing the above into a usable split for BRIDGE-1 scoping:

**Local governor sufficient** (in-clause selectional restriction resolves it, no situation-model input needed):
- Unambiguous argument-structure cases where the verb's frame uniquely selects a reading and no character/goal history is implicated — e.g. plain physical-action sentences with no affect-bearing predicate ("she opened the door"), closed-class function-word disambiguation, syntactic attachment resolved by strong subcategorization. These are the cases the discourse-override experiments deliberately hold CONSTANT/neutral as controls — i.e., the literature's own control conditions ARE this subset, which is reassuring: it confirms the subset exists and is well-characterized, it's just not the subset our build targets.

**Prior-discourse required** (governor is neutral or actively misleading; only accumulated situation/goal/character state disambiguates):
- Word-sense/affect selection for predicates whose polarity or referent-relevance depends on established goal state (dread, relief, betrayal-reads) — this is the Zwaan intentionality dimension directly.
- Animacy/selectional-restriction violations that are discourse-licensed (peanut-in-love pattern) — directly verified as **discourse beating local, not local-plus-discourse-refining**.
- Irony and any non-literal/implicit read — verified as requiring a full predict-mismatch-reinterpret cycle with mentalizing/situation input, not resolvable by any single-pass local mechanism regardless of how rich the local frame is.
- Event-knowledge-consistent-but-locally-anomalous continuations (Metusalem) — verified as **contingent on discourse presence**, i.e. structurally absent without it, not merely weaker.

Given that essentially every construction our current build phase cares about (goal-owner attribution, implicit dread, affect resolution across sentences) falls in the second bucket by definition — that's what makes them the hard, targeted cases rather than the easy ones already handled — **the overlap between "what BRIDGE-1 as scoped needs to solve" and "what the local governor alone can solve" is small.** The local-governor-sufficient bucket is real and worth keeping as a fast-path/first-pass component (cheap, and per Kuperberg/Jaeger's residual-coding framing, it's literally what the feedforward layer should compute regardless), but it does not cover the target subset.

## 4. Sequencing implication

A local-governor BRIDGE-1 built and shipped as a *standalone, complete* first step, with the situation-model loop deferred to "later," has two failure modes verified above:
1. On the discourse-decisive subset (the one motivating this build), it will not just under-perform — per the peanut-in-love result it will actively produce the **wrong-direction** answer on cases where local and discourse readings conflict, because it has no channel to receive the override signal at all.
2. It is not extensible-by-composition into the full architecture later: predictive-coding framing (Kuperberg/Jaeger) says the feedforward/local signal is *defined relative to* top-down prediction (residual after explaining-away). A local-only module built without that channel isn't "the same component with situation-model bolted on later" — it's computing a different quantity (raw signal vs. residual), so bolting the loop on afterward means rebuilding the local component's interface, not just adding a new consumer.

This mirrors a pattern already in project memory: [[feedback_for_every_mechanism_ask_which_brain_structure_and_does_it_share_existing_processes_USER_2026-08-03]] — the situation-model/goal-state channel already exists in this codebase's design (Zwaan intentionality dimension mapped to Component-5's GoalOutcomeRegister + AccumulateRegister per `notes/research_component5_goal_owner_selection_binding_2026-08-04.md`). The correct move is not "build local governor now, situation-model later" but **"build BRIDGE-1's interface to accept a top-down bias input from day one, even if that input is coarse/stubbed initially, and wire it to the existing situation-model organs as they mature"** — i.e., co-design the interface now, co-implement the full loop incrementally, rather than sequence them as two separable phases.

## Cheap decisive test (pre-registered)

**Test:** on the existing C3-mined 38-item goal-outcome/affect bank (`notes/research_outcome_valence_detector_design_2026-08-05.md` lineage) plus a small hand-authored set of peanut-in-love-style discourse-override minimal pairs (locally-neutral-or-misleading predicate + discourse context that flips the correct reading), run BRIDGE-1 local-governor-only (no situation-model input) vs. BRIDGE-1 + a coarse situation-model bias signal (even a crude accumulate-register lookup).

- **HARD-PASS for "local-governor-only is a legitimate standalone step":** local-governor-only scores within 10 points of local+situation-bias on the discourse-override minimal-pair set (i.e., discourse isn't actually decisive on OUR item bank, contradicting the literature synthesis above — would be a surprising and important finding, worth re-checking item construction before trusting it).
- **HARD-FAIL (expected, per verified literature) for "standalone is sufficient":** local-governor-only scores at or below chance/majority-baseline on the discourse-override subset specifically (predicts systematic wrong-direction errors on peanut-in-love-pattern items, mirroring the ERP result), while local+situation-bias clears a beat-baseline bar. This is the expected outcome and would confirm sequencing correction (b).
- Scramble-control: shuffle which discourse context pairs with which target predicate — situation-bias version should collapse toward local-only performance under scrambling (confirms it's using real situation content, not a confound).

## Falsifiable predictions

- HARD-PASS (situation-loop necessary, as predicted): local-governor-only accuracy on discourse-decisive items ≤ majority-baseline; local+situation-bias accuracy clears baseline+10pts; scramble-control collapses situation-bias gain to within noise of local-only.
- HARD-FAIL (would overturn this drill's verdict): local-governor-only matches local+situation-bias within 10pts on discourse-decisive items AND this replicates on a second, independently-constructed item set — would mean our specific construction-type distribution doesn't actually stress the discourse-override mechanism the way the ERP literature's stimuli do, and local-first sequencing would be defensible after all.

## Substrate-product implications

- Do not ship a "BRIDGE-1 v1, local-only" milestone as if it were a complete or independently-useful checkpoint for the goal/affect target domain — it will be systematically wrong-direction on exactly the construction class (implicit dread, discourse-licensed non-canonical reads) that motivates the whole build, not just weaker.
- Do design BRIDGE-1's interface now to accept a situation-bias input (even a stub/coarse one from the existing AccumulateRegister), so the eventual full loop is a parameter/richness upgrade to an existing channel, not a re-architecture.
- The local-governor-sufficient subset (clean, affect-neutral, unambiguous-frame sentences) is real and cheap — keep it as a legitimate fast path/first-pass filter, consistent with predictive-coding's residual framing, but market/track it internally as "fast-path filter," not as "BRIDGE-1 complete."

## Direct verdict

**(b) — sense/affect resolution for the target domain (goal-conditioned, implicit, discourse-licensed reads) must be built WITH the situation-model loop from the start**, at minimum as a stubbed top-down bias interface wired to the already-existing AccumulateRegister/GoalOutcomeRegister organs. Local-governor-only is legitimate ONLY for the disjoint, non-target subset of clause-internal-unambiguous cases (useful as a fast path, not as a phase-1 milestone for this build).

**Confidence (deflated per lit-scan-calibration-penalty): 0.65.** This is verified converging evidence across four independent research programs (Van Berkum/Nieuwland ERP, Metusalem, Zwaan behavioral, Kuperberg/Jaeger theoretical + Baldassano fMRI/anatomical) all pointing the same direction, with one paper (peanut-in-love) providing a genuinely decisive reversal result rather than a merely-correlational one — this is stronger than typical single-study lit-scan support, hence above the default 0.50 novel-synthesis cap; deflated from a higher raw read because (a) the "how often is discourse decisive" quantification is my structural inference, not a verified population statistic, and (b) no source directly tests our specific substrate architecture (this is cross-domain transfer from human ERP/fMRI literature to a computational build, standard external-validity discount applies).

**Single most important sequencing correction:** stop treating "local governor first, situation-model later" as a valid phased plan for the goal/affect target domain — replace it with "build the local governor's TOP-DOWN BIAS INPUT PORT now (even stubbed), populate it incrementally from the already-existing Component-5 organs" so the two are co-designed from a shared interface rather than sequenced as separable milestones.
