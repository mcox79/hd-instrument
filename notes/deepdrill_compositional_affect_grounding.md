# Deep-brain drill (angle 2/3): compositional vs. holistic affect grounding — BRIDGE-1 verification

Date: 2026-08-04
Method: live WebSearch across ~10 queries (Hagoort/LIFG unification, Bemis&Pylkkänen LATL/MEG minimal composition, McRae thematic fit, Pylkkänen coercion/AMF, Barrett constructionist emotion, Scherer/Lazarus appraisal, N400 selectional-restriction studies, vmPFC/OFC valuation). All claims below tagged VERIFIED (confirmed this pass via search) or TRAINED-ONLY (prior knowledge, not re-confirmed live).

## Question restated
BRIDGE-1 premise: a polysemous word's grounding (e.g. "hard" -> HARM vs non-HARM) is licensed by its syntactic GOVERNOR (the predicate that takes it as argument) — governor picks the sense, sense maps to appraisal-dims, appraisal-dims get valued. Is this brain-faithful? Is the GOVERNOR the right conditioning variable, or should it be the whole predicate-argument EVENT?

---

## 1. Compositional vs holistic construction — does the brain compose meaning from head+argument, and does the governor modulate the argument's interpretation?

**VERIFIED.** Yes, unambiguously, on two independent converging literatures:

- **LIFG unification (Hagoort).** Hagoort's unification model places combinatorial semantic/syntactic integration in LIFG (BA44/45/47), with unification effects (P600, and BA47 distributional-knowledge effects) appearing when a word's meaning must be integrated with its governing context. Search confirmed: "distributional knowledge in the LIFG (BA47) may enable semantic combinatorics in the context of language use," and syntactic/semantic unification manifests as early as ~80ms when context is predictive (noun/verb grammatical-class modulation of IFG). This is a real-time, online mechanism — not a late post-hoc patch — meaning the governor's category/expectations are influencing argument interpretation within the same processing window as lexical access, not after a "holistic" whole-sentence readout.

- **LATL minimal composition (Bemis & Pylkkänen).** MEG shows LATL activity increases specifically for composable two-word phrases ("red boat") vs non-composable pairs ("cup boat," "xptq boat") at ~200-250ms post-target-word-onset. This is evidence for a dedicated, early, head-driven combinatorial operation — the second word's representation is measurably altered by being an argument of/modified-by the first, at the *phrase* level, well before full-sentence integration could occur. VERIFIED this is a real, replicated composition effect (confirmed via multiple independent papers in search: LATL/AMF/vmPFC/angular-gyrus network).

- **Coercion (Pylkkänen et al., AMF).** "The boy finished the pizza" -> pizza coerces an eventive reading of finish ("finish eating") at ~400ms, localized to the Anterior Midline Field. This is the single strongest piece of evidence that the ARGUMENT can also reshape the PREDICATE's construal (bidirectional composition, not governor-only unidirectional licensing) — VERIFIED.

**Implication for BRIDGE-1:** the composition mechanism is real, early, head-argument-local, and neurally dissociable from bare lexical access. But it is bidirectional (predicate shapes argument AND argument shapes predicate reading — coercion), which already complicates a strict "governor licenses argument" one-way model.

## 2. Selectional restrictions / thematic fit as the mechanism of governor-driven constraint

**VERIFIED.** McRae et al. (1998) "thematic fit" — the degree to which a filler noun satisfies a verb's role expectations (e.g., "cop"/"thief" for "arrest") — is established as an incremental, online constraint that measurably affects comprehension (reading times, N400) as the sentence unfolds. Confirmed via search: "verbs activate expectations about their typical arguments... subjects... quickly use this knowledge to anticipate upcoming linguistic input." This is a genuinely predictive, governor-to-argument constraint-satisfaction process — not just post-hoc filtering.

**N400 evidence (VERIFIED, with an important nuance/correction).** Search surfaced a real complication: selectional-restriction violations do NOT uniformly produce N400 effects. Specifically: "Semantic relatedness did not modulate the N400 evoked by inanimate Agent arguments that violated the preceding verb's animacy selection restriction, suggesting that different types of violations are processed differently," and elsewhere "selection restriction violated verbs that were semantically related to their preceding context failed to produce any N400 effect." This means the brain's use of governor-constraints is **not a single unified gating mechanism** — different restriction types (animacy vs. thematic-role vs. plausibility) dissociate neurally. This is a real correction to any model that treats "governor licenses argument sense" as one clean gate: it's several partially-independent constraint channels (lexical association, thematic fit, animacy, event plausibility) that jointly and asynchronously shape interpretation, some resolved earlier (N400/lexical) and some later (P600/structural integration, AMF/coercion ~400ms).

**Construction Grammar (Goldberg) — TRAINED-ONLY, not re-verified this pass.** Prior knowledge: constructions themselves (not just individual verbs) carry meaning and can coerce a verb's argument-structure reading (e.g. "sneeze the napkin off the table" — caused-motion construction overrides sneeze's normal intransitivity). This generalizes governor beyond "the verb" to "the whole licensed construction" — relevant to the verdict below but flag as trained-only pending live re-check.

## 3. Is affect/valence specifically constructed from the situated event, not a fixed lexical tag?

**VERIFIED, and this is the load-bearing finding for BRIDGE-1.**

- **Barrett's theory of constructed emotion.** VERIFIED via search: "a single physical signal or pattern of signals can have different psychological meanings in different situations, in the same way that any object or event can be conceptually categorized in a flexible, situated manner" and specific emotions are "psychological constructions based on interoceptive stimulation, individual learning history, and (social) context." Core affect (valence+arousal) is a continuously-varying state; discrete emotion/valence *categories* are constructed by situated conceptualization, not retrieved as fixed lexical tags. This directly supports treating a word's affective coloring as NOT a static lookup — it must be computed per-instance from context.

- **Appraisal theory (Scherer/Lazarus), VERIFIED.** "An emotion cannot arise until the individual has first assessed the significance of an event" (cognitive appraisal). Goal congruence — "whether the event supports or blocks what the person wants" — is a core appraisal dimension. Critically, VERIFIED: "Events do not have significance in of themselves, but only by virtue of their interpretation in the context of an individual's beliefs, desires, intentions and abilities." Scherer's Component Process Model appraises **the event** (novelty, pleasantness, goal-relevance, causal attribution, outcome probability, goal-conduciveness, urgency) — the appraisal object is explicitly the EVENT (an integrated situation: agent + action + patient + outcome + goal-relation), never an isolated word or even an isolated predicate-argument pair stripped of goal context.

**This is the key correction to BRIDGE-1 as stated.** The literature's appraisal object is one level higher than "predicate + argument." It is the *whole represented situation*, including (a) the composed predicate-argument event, (b) the experiencer's/agent's goals, and (c) causal/outcome expectations. "hard" under governor "studied" only resolves to non-HARM once the fuller situation (effort toward a goal, no causal patient being damaged) is available — the governor is a strong CUE toward the right situation-frame, but the appraisal computation itself operates on the assembled situation-representation, not on the governor token in isolation.

## 4. Is valuation (OFC/vmPFC) of the composed event the right target, with governor as conditioning variable?

**VERIFIED, with a refinement.** vmPFC "receives multimodal information about stimulus attributes from posterior sensory and association cortices, and then integrates this information into an overall stimulus value" with value-related activity emerging relatively late (~400ms+) — i.e., AFTER upstream composition/situation-assembly has had time to run (consistent with LIFG ~80-300ms, LATL ~200-250ms, AMF/coercion ~400ms all preceding or coinciding with vmPFC valuation onset). Separately, VERIFIED: vmPFC engages in "iterative feedback loops with the hippocampus and posterior neocortex, progressively integrating successive scenes... into a temporally unfolding mental scenario" for event construction — again, the valuation substrate operates over an assembled EVENT/SCENARIO representation, not a raw lexical-governor signal.

This temporal ordering (LIFG/LATL compose the local phrase early -> N400/thematic-fit channels partially constrain sense/plausibility -> AMF-level coercion/event-type resolution ~400ms -> vmPFC valuation ~400ms+) is itself evidence for a **pipeline**, not a single-step lookup: governor-licensing is the FIRST, EARLIEST stage of a multi-stage process whose output (a composed, plausibility-checked, coercion-resolved local representation) is what actually feeds valuation — not the governor identity itself.

## DIRECT VERDICT ON BRIDGE-1

**Partially brain-faithful, with one necessary correction.**

**What's right:** The core premise — that a polysemous word's sense/grounding is disambiguated compositionally by its syntactic/semantic governor, in real time, via a dedicated neural combinatorial mechanism (LIFG unification, LATL minimal composition) that is dissociable from bare lexical retrieval — is strongly brain-faithful and VERIFIED across independent literatures. This is not a hand-wave; it's one of the best-established findings in the neurobiology of language. Routing "word conditioned by its governing predicate" through a compositional stage before valuation is the right shape.

**What needs correction:** The chain as stated ("governor licenses grounding -> maps to appraisal-dims -> valued") treats the GOVERNOR (the single predicate token) as the conditioning variable. The literature's actual conditioning variable, at the stage where APPRAISAL/VALENCE specifically is computed, is the assembled EVENT/SITUATION representation (predicate + all its arguments + goal-context + causal/outcome expectations), not the governor in isolation. Two independent pieces of evidence force this:
1. Coercion is bidirectional (argument reshapes predicate reading too — AMF ~400ms), so "governor licenses argument" is too unidirectional.
2. Appraisal theory's explicit object of evaluation is always "the event," scored on goal-congruence/causal-attribution/outcome-probability — dimensions that require the FULL situation (who did what to whom, toward what goal), not the governor token alone. "hit" vs "studied" as bare governors don't carry goal-congruence information; only the composed event (agent, patient, goal-relation) does.

**Practical fix for BRIDGE-1:** keep the governor as the CUE/GATE that triggers retrieval of the correct sense/frame (this part is exactly right and well-supported — governor-conditioned sense selection is real, early, and mechanistically distinct from holistic whole-sentence reading), but make the INPUT to the appraisal-dim mapping be the **composed predicate-argument-goal event representation**, not the governor token by itself. I.e., two-stage: (1) governor selects/licenses which sense-frame of the polysemous word applies (LIFG/LATL-level, ~100-300ms mechanism — this stage IS governor-conditioned and that's correct); (2) the resulting frame instance, filled with its full argument+goal context, is what gets appraisal-scored and valued (vmPFC-level, ~400ms+ mechanism — this stage is EVENT-conditioned, not governor-token-conditioned). Collapsing these two stages into "governor -> appraisal-dims" directly skips the event-assembly step that the appraisal literature says is load-bearing.

**Confidence (deflated per lit-scan calibration penalty):** P(this two-stage correction is the right fix) = 0.62 (before penalty ~0.80, deflated 0.18 for lit-scan-only verification, no direct behavioral/neural test of the substrate's specific implementation). P(bare "governor -> appraisal-dims," i.e. BRIDGE-1 exactly as stated, is sufficient without the event-assembly stage) = 0.15 — capped low because the appraisal-theory evidence (goal-congruence requires full event, not token) is fairly direct and would need the governor token to somehow encode goal-relational content it structurally doesn't carry.

**Single most important correction:** appraisal (valence/affect) is computed over the COMPOSED EVENT (predicate+arguments+goal-relation), not over the governor token alone — the governor is correctly the disambiguation CUE for sense-selection, but must hand off to an assembled situation representation before valuation, per Scherer's Component Process Model and Barrett's constructionist account (both explicit that appraisal targets events/situations, never isolated words or governors).

## Cheap decisive test (pre-registered)
Build a minimal 3-condition probe over the existing substrate's polysemous-word set (e.g. "hard"):
- Condition A (governor-only): grounding decision uses governor identity alone.
- Condition B (event-assembled): grounding decision uses governor + filled argument roles + inferred goal-relation (the two-stage fix above).
- Condition C (holistic/no-governor): grounding decision uses bag-of-sentence-words with no structural governor privileging.

HARD-PASS for the correction: B beats A by a clear margin (not noise) on cases where governor alone is ambiguous even after sense-selection (e.g., "hard" under governor "hit" with patient = abstract goal "hit the deadline" vs literal patient "hit the wall" — governor identical, event differs, HARM-vs-non-HARM grounding differs). If B does not beat A on these governor-matched/event-differing minimal pairs, the correction is falsified and BRIDGE-1's original governor-only form should stand.
HARD-FAIL for BRIDGE-1 as originally stated: any case where two sentences share an identical governor token but differ in gold-standard affect/HARM-grounding due to argument/goal content alone — if such cases are common in the substrate's target corpus, governor-only necessarily fails on them by construction, independent of implementation quality.

## Falsifiable predictions
- HARD-PASS (event-assembled correction confirmed): accuracy on governor-matched/event-differing minimal pairs improves by >=15pp moving from governor-only conditioning to event-assembled conditioning, on a held-out set of >=30 such pairs.
- HARD-FAIL (correction rejected, governor-only sufficient): event-assembled conditioning shows no improvement (<5pp) over governor-only on the same minimal-pair set — implies governor token position/identity already implicitly encodes enough goal/patient information (e.g. via strong verb-sense priors) that explicit event-assembly is unnecessary overhead.

## Substrate-product implications
- Do not implement grounding as `governor_token -> appraisal_dims -> value`. Implement as `governor_token -> sense_frame_selection -> fill_frame(args, goal_relation) -> assembled_event -> appraisal_dims -> value`. The frame-selection step is governor-conditioned (brain-faithful, keep as-is); the appraisal-scoring step must be event-conditioned (needs the fix).
- This reuses Component-3 (thematic-role/frame labeling, already in flight per 08-04 focus) as the event-assembly stage — no new organ required, directly per [[feedback_for_every_mechanism_ask_which_brain_structure_and_does_it_share_existing_processes]].
- N400 dissociation (animacy vs thematic-fit vs lexical-association violations behave differently) implies the substrate's plausibility/constraint-satisfaction layer, if built as one unified gate, is non-brain-faithful — brain uses several partially-independent, asynchronously-resolving constraint channels. Lower priority than the main correction but flag for the constraint-satisfaction/coercion sub-module design.
