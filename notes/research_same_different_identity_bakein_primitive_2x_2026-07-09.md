## HEADLINE

Same-different splits into two mechanistically distinct tiers, and the split is the finding: **item-level identity match** (is this thing the same as that thing) is a structural, non-learned comparator that both the brain (hippocampal match-mismatch circuit, present from infancy) and VSA/HDC (fixed similarity metric on quasi-orthogonal random hypervectors) get **for free by construction** — no training, generalizes to any novel item automatically. **Relational (second-order) same-different** — RMTS, "is the relation between A1/A2 the same as the relation between B1/B2," the actual gold-standard test that separates species and breaks feedforward nets — is NOT free in either domain: it requires an explicit individuation + relation-extraction step (comparator exposed only to a relation value, never raw content) that both brains (via extensive training / symbolic scaffolding in non-human primates) and VSA (via bind/unbind relation-extraction, unproven at scale) must build, and both show a real, honest capacity ceiling once relation-extraction has to survive scene/composition complexity growth. Bake-in target: build the FREE item-level primitive structurally (it costs nothing and the brain has it from birth in essence); pre-register the relational tier as the harder, honestly-bounded next rung.

## Cheap decisive test

Sample N fresh, never-before-seen random hypervector pairs from the SAME codebook the substrate already uses (no retraining, no new construction step):
- "same" trials: `(A, A)` for freshly-drawn atoms A never used to build any codebook/pool.
- "different" trials: `(A, B)` for two independent freshly-drawn atoms.
- "relational-same" trials: two DIFFERENT novel pairs `(A1, A2)` and `(B1, B2)` constructed to instantiate the identical relation (e.g. same bound offset/transformation vector) but with all four items mutually dissimilar.

Compute a fixed similarity threshold once (no per-trial fitting) and score: (1) item-level same/different classification accuracy on held-out novel items vs. accuracy on items seen during codebook construction — the gap is the test; (2) relational-same accuracy via unbind-then-compare (`R = bind(A1, inverse(A2))`, compare `R1` vs `R2` similarity) vs. the quasi-orthogonal chance baseline.

This is cheap (CPU, minutes, pure linear algebra on existing atom/codebook machinery — no new training loop).

## Falsifiable predictions

**Item-level same-different (structural, free primitive):**
- HARD-PASS: novel-item same/different classification accuracy is statistically indistinguishable (within CI) from accuracy on items used to build the codebook — i.e. zero generalization gap, because there is no training step to overfit. Threshold: accuracy >= 0.95 on both seen and novel sets at a single fixed similarity cutoff, gap <= 2 percentage points.
- HARD-FAIL: novel items show a measurably worse accuracy than seen items (a real generalization gap appears) — this would mean the "similarity" primitive is secretly benefiting from some construction-time correlation/leakage, not a pure structural fixed metric, and the "free by construction" claim is false for this substrate's actual codebook (e.g. codebook not truly quasi-orthogonal at the operating dimension/sparsity).

**Relational (second-order) same-different (the harder rung — pre-registered as the honest, beatable bar, NOT expected to be free):**
- HARD-PASS: unbind-then-compare similarity between two independently-sampled relation-instantiations of the SAME abstract relation is significantly above the quasi-orthogonal chance baseline (e.g. z > 3 relative to the null distribution of unrelated-pair similarities), even though the four constituent items are themselves mutually dissimilar.
- HARD-FAIL: relation-vector similarity for same-relation pairs is statistically indistinguishable from the chance/null baseline — meaning the unbind step is not isolating relation-invariant structure from filler identity (the literature's own honest ceiling: Plate's structural/semantic-similarity conflation, and the scene-complexity "straining" effect from Kim/Ricci/Serre). This is the EXPECTED failure mode to pre-register, not a surprise — a HARD-FAIL here is informative, not a program-ending result, and should route to the relational-bottleneck-style fix (expose only the relation value downstream, individuate items into separate channels before binding) rather than be treated as "same-different doesn't work here."

## Cross-thread synthesis (where the two angles converge)

**Angle 1 — neuro/innate (2 sub-drills: developmental core-knowledge + comparative/animal):**
- Item-level match/mismatch (recognition, novelty detection) is early, broadly shared, and circuit-identified: hippocampal comparator (Kumaran & Maguire 2007; Vinogradova 2001) and PFC single-neuron abstract same/different rule-coding that generalizes across novel stimulus pairs (Wallis, Anderson & Miller, *Nature* 2001) are both solid, replicated findings. Infant habituation-dishabituation studies (Ferry, Hespos & Waxman, *Cognition* 2016) show abstract stimulus-general same-different by ~7-10 months — early, but not neonatal, and NOT one of Spelke's canonical core-knowledge systems (it's either embedded in the object-identity/object-file system or a domain-general comparator that operates across the core systems).
- Relational (RMTS, "relation between relations") same-different is genuinely contested: Marcus (1999, *Science*; *The Algebraic Mind* 2001, replicated 2023 Geambașu et al.) argues for an innate algebraic same/different operator from 7-month-old ABB/ABA rule extraction; Premack and Penn/Holyoak/Povinelli ("Darwin's Mistake," *BBS* 2008) argue the relational/RMTS-level form requires trained symbolic scaffolding even in chimps, marking a real human/nonhuman discontinuity — a claim partly undercut by crows spontaneously transferring from mere identity-training to relational matching (Smirnova et al. 2015, *Current Biology*).
- The comparative-cognition literature resolves the tension mechanistically: transfer-to-novel-stimuli is a genuine **set-size-expansion phase transition** (Katz, Wright & Bachevalier 2002), not an all-or-nothing innate/learned binary — pigeons need ~256 training exemplars, monkeys ~128, before item-specific memorization flips to rule-based relational encoding. The skeptical alternative (entropy/oddity-based perceptual heuristics mimicking abstraction, Penn/Holyoak/Povinelli) is real for weak/multi-item designs but does not survive controlled two-item RMTS designs with adequate set size — i.e. low-level heuristics ARE a genuine confound at small scale, exactly analogous to a saturation-vacuous smoke test.

**Angle 2 — computational + VSA realization:**
- Kim, Ricci & Serre ("Not-So-CLEVR," 2018) diagnose CNN same-different failure as a **feature-entanglement / lack-of-individuation** problem: fixed filters can't generalize across the combinatorially-growing space of spatial arrangements; the fix (Siamese/separate-channel object individuation) removes the binding problem structurally rather than adding capacity. Marcus's classic argument is the same diagnosis one level up: MLPs conflate "same value" with "same weight pattern," so they cannot bind an abstract role to an arbitrary novel filler outside the training subspace.
- The convergent architectural fix across Relation Networks (partial — still strains without individuation), attention-as-comparator (ViT, Tartaglini et al. 2023), and explicit relational-bottleneck/symbolic-binding approaches (ESBN, Webb/Sinha/Cohen 2020; Relational Bottleneck framework, Webb et al. *TiCS* 2024) is: **individuate the two objects into separate representational slots, then force all downstream computation through an inner-product relation value, never raw content.** This is the most explicit, empirically-tested minimal-sufficiency claim in the ML literature, and it generalizes to strictly held-out novel entities where content-exposed baselines fail.
- VSA/HDC gives this for free at the item level: random hypervectors are individuated by construction (quasi-orthogonal, no entanglement) and similarity (cosine/dot/Hamming) is a fixed, non-learned metric available for any pair, including ones never seen (Kanerva 2009; Plate 2003; Kleyko/Rahimi/Frady/Sommer survey, *ACM Comput. Surv.* 2023). Two explicit 2024 papers (LARS-VSA, arXiv:2405.14436; RESOLVE, arXiv:2411.08290) directly operationalize the relational bottleneck IN a VSA via bind/unbind, citing the cognitive-science relational-bottleneck literature as their motivation — this is a real, direct (not inferred) bridge between the two angles.

**Where the 2x converges (the actual answer to the task question):** the minimal mechanism for abstract same-different, across brain, comparative-cognition, and ML/VSA literatures independently, is **a comparator that operates over two INDIVIDUATED representations and exposes only a RELATION VALUE (match/mismatch or relation-vector similarity) downstream — never raw content.** Item-level identity match is the trivial case of this comparator (relation value = raw similarity) and is free in a well-conditioned VSA codebook. Relational/second-order same-different is the same comparator applied one level up (relation value = similarity between two independently-extracted relation-vectors via bind/unbind) — and every literature independently flags this harder rung as capacity-limited (scene/composition complexity, the human/nonhuman discontinuity debate, VSA's own similarity-conflation problem), not free.

- Build the item-level same-different primitive as a genuinely BAKED structural op (fixed similarity threshold on the existing codebook, zero training) — this is the cheapest possible bake-in win on this track: it composes directly with the object-permanence MM and dual-number MM already prioritized, since all three are "structural comparator/counter over the existing representational space, no learned readout."
- Pre-register the relational (second-order) tier as the harder, HONEST next rung, not a bundled claim — ship it as a SEPARATE discriminator so a HARD-FAIL there doesn't contaminate the (very likely to pass) item-level result. This directly extends the live reader-crosstalk thread: "telling learned codes apart at scale" is literally the item-level same-different primitive already implicated as load-bearing for multi-hop retrieval (per the same-day crosstalk-cleanup delivery), so this bake-in also strengthens that front.

## Intuitive summary

We asked two independent questions that turned out to have the same answer. First: how do brains and animals do "same-different" — is it built in from birth, or does it have to be learned? Second: why do plain neural networks famously struggle with this exact judgment, and what fixes it? Both literatures gave the identical two-part answer. Telling two things apart when you can look at them side-by-side (like recognizing "this is the same object I just saw") is cheap and appears very early in brains and pigeons alike — it just requires keeping the two things as separate, uncombined representations and comparing them. Telling two RELATIONSHIPS apart (like recognizing "the relationship between these two new objects is the same kind of relationship as between those other two, totally different objects") is the hard, contested version — the one that takes animals thousands of training examples, marks a real human/animal debate, and is exactly where plain neural networks break down until you force them to compare only abstract relationship-values and never the raw objects themselves. The good news for our substrate: the easy, item-level version comes essentially for free with how our vector representations already work (no training needed, works automatically on things never seen before) — so we can bake that in immediately as a real structural primitive, the same way we're baking in a number-sense and an object-permanence sense. The harder, relationship-level version we're pre-registering honestly as the next, harder rung — with a fair, beatable pass/fail bar rather than pretending it will just work, because every line of evidence we found (brain, animal, and machine-learning) agrees that's exactly where things get harder and starts to break down at scale.
