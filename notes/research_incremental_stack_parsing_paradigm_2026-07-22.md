## HEADLINE

**Incremental stack-based transition parsing is a real, well-motivated, substrate-native-buildable mechanism-class fix for the SPECIFIC failure signature diagnosed (positional default when the true answer is buried inside a completed, syntactically-irrelevant subtree) — but it is narrower and less airtight than the task's framing suggests on three counts, each load-bearing for an honest GO decision:**

1. **It is not a structural guarantee, only a structural risk-reduction.** Hand-tracing arc-standard/arc-eager through "the key to the cabinets are on the table" confirms the PP does get built as a completed subtree and popped/reduced off the stack before the verb is reached — IF the local REDUCE/attach decisions along the way are correct. Those decisions are themselves made by a classifier (even a simple glass-box one), and McDonald & Nivre (2011) directly document that transition parsers are WORSE, not better, than graph-based parsers on longer dependencies via error propagation — and the true dependency here (key→are) is the LONGER one while the wrong attractor pull (cabinets→are) is SHORT. This is the single biggest risk and it cuts against the task's optimistic framing, not with it.
2. **It does not resolve the b2 hierarchy-INDUCTION wall.** The stack discipline (depth = number of permutations/depth-role bindings, opened by function words) is a hand-specified architectural scaffold — exactly the kind of "hierarchical prior handed in advance" that the induction-wall note's entire literature triangulation (Perfors 2012; ABL/ADIOS survey) already identified as the universal precondition for glass-box structure-handling. This is a genuine, useful upgrade on the SELECTOR side (per that note's own selector/inducer distinction) — it gives the substrate an explicit, inspectable depth representation it never had — but it is not an escape from the induction wall; it is a better-fitted instance of exactly the same "prior-required" category as every other glass-box mechanism surveyed there.
3. **No literature precedent exists for the VSA-stack assembly itself.** All three lit-scan threads independently confirm: permutation-based sequence/stack encoding is standard HDC (Kanerva), one directly-named prior system exists (Yerxa/Anderson/Weiss 2018, "Hyperdimensional Stack Machine" — closest hit, purity of its glass-box-ness unconfirmed from available text), and the closest incremental (not whole-tree-batch) VSA parsing precedent is a THEORY-ONLY paper (Weiss/beim Graben 2020, FockBox + left-corner, no working action classifier). A full VSA hypervector transition parser with bind/unbind/cleanup/permutation as live state plus a simple action classifier has never been published. This is a genuinely novel construction, not an assembly of proven parts — moderate-to-high engineering risk, concentrated exactly where you'd expect: stack-depth crosstalk/capacity.

Despite these three caveats, the answer to the GO/NO-GO question is **GO**, scoped correctly (see §5): the current batch decode is disk-confirmed to be the weakest possible instantiation of arc-factored parsing (not even MST-optimal), the incremental paradigm is mechanistically the right diagnosis for the buried-subject signature specifically, the substrate already has every needed primitive (permutation, bind/unbind, cleanup, and a proven glass-box sequence-classifier family at 0.90-0.95 F1 for POS/chunking/SRL) — and the VSA-stack capacity-cliff risk, if it materializes at depth ~3-5, is plausibly BRAIN-FAITHFUL (Miller & Chomsky 1963 center-embedding breakdown at the same depth) rather than disqualifying.

## 1. BRAIN: incremental structure-building and the stack/depth question

**Immediacy (ESTABLISHED):** Marslen-Wilson & Tyler (1980, *Cognition*) — comprehension integrates syntactic/semantic/pragmatic information within ~200-250ms of word onset, not at clause/sentence boundaries. Behavioral (shadowing latency, mispronunciation/anomaly detection), later corroborated by independent ERP/eye-tracking literatures.

**Anticipation, including SYNTACTIC role anticipation (ESTABLISHED):** Altmann & Kamide (1999) — anticipatory looks to the only edible referent before the noun is named, driven by verb selectional restrictions (visual-world eye-tracking, well-replicated). Crucially, **Kamide, Scheepers & Altmann (2003)** extends this from semantic plausibility to actual thematic-ROLE anticipation: German OVS sentences, case-marking on the first NP drives anticipatory looks to the correct upcoming AGENT before it is named — i.e., the parser predicts WHO WILL DO WHAT TO WHOM from morphosyntactic cues alone, not just "what's edible." INFERRED (weaker): generalization to English/non-case-marked cues is plausible but under-evidenced — most confirmatory work leans on case-marking languages (German/Japanese/Turkish).

**Incremental commitment / garden-path (ESTABLISHED effect, CONTESTED mechanism):** Frazier & Rayner (1982) garden-path model (Minimal Attachment, Late Closure) — real, triangulated evidence (RT, eye-tracking regressions, P600/ERP) that the parser commits early and pays a measurable cost on reanalysis. CONTESTED: whether commitment is single-path/serial (Frazier) or graded-parallel/constraint-based (MacDonald, Pearlmutter & Seidenberg 1994; Tanenhaus visual-world ambiguity studies) — both camps agree on incremental commitment, disagree on architecture.

**The stack/depth crux — the most important calibration in this section (CONTESTED, not established):** Center-embedding difficulty is one of the most robust findings in the field (Miller & Chomsky 1963; Yngve 1960) and Gibson's Dependency Locality Theory (2000) formalizes it as integration cost + storage cost (number of open dependencies) — but DLT is explicitly framed WITHOUT a literal stack. Lewis & Vasishth's (2005) ACT-R model explains the SAME reanalysis-cost data via cue-based content-addressable retrieval under similarity/decay, also without a stack. **Left-corner parsing (Resnik 1992; Abney & Johnson 1991; van Schijndel & Schuler 2013) is the specific psycholinguistically-favored STRATEGY precisely because its stack-depth profile — shallow for left/right-branching, +1 element per center-embedding — uniquely matches the human difficulty pattern.** This is real and specific, but it is a computational/algorithmic-level (Marr's sense) claim about which STRATEGY best fits the behavioral profile, not direct neural/implementational evidence for a literal push/pop data structure. No single-neuron, ERP, or fMRI signature uniquely identifies stack-push/pop as opposed to graded-activation/interference dynamics — "stack" is the best-fitting computational-level label among several implementational stories that all account for the same data equally well at current resolution.

**Field consensus, stated honestly:** the parser incrementally builds a bounded set of currently-open dependencies as words arrive, and processing cost scales with how many/how far those open dependencies are — everyone agrees on this. Whether that "open state" is stack-shaped (left-corner), a locality-weighted dependency set with no stack (Gibson DLT), or cue-based decaying memory (Lewis & Vasishth) is genuinely unresolved. **Left-corner's bounded-stack account is the best-fitting STRATEGY-level explanation of the center-embedding data specifically — that is the actual warrant for building a stack, not a claim that the brain literally has one.**

## 2. Glass-box NLP prior art: transition-based (stack+buffer) vs batch (arc-factored/graph-based)

**Nivre's transition systems (Nivre 2003/2004/2008, *Computational Linguistics* 34(4)) — ESTABLISHED mechanics:** configuration = (stack, buffer, arcs); actions SHIFT / LEFT-ARC / RIGHT-ARC / REDUCE; arc-standard pops the dependent immediately on an arc action, arc-eager additionally allows an explicit REDUCE once a stack-top item has its head and needs no more dependents. Linear-time, O(n). Genuinely glass-box BY CONSTRUCTION (not by inference): stack and buffer contents are literal enumerable token lists at every timestep, fully loggable — a categorical advantage over interpreting a dense arc-score matrix.

**Hand-traced structural claim, CONFIRMED with a real caveat:** tracing arc-standard/arc-eager through "the key to the cabinets are on the table" — given CORRECT local decisions, "to the cabinets" is fully built (cabinets attached under "to", "to" attached under "key", both reduced/popped) several transitions before "are" is even shifted onto the stack. Only "key" remains exposed. **This is a real property of the stack discipline, confirmed mechanically — but it is not structurally GUARANTEED, only structurally FAVORED**, because the REDUCE/attach decisions along the way are learned classifier predictions, not hard-wired grammar rules. A misprediction anywhere upstream leaves the attractor noun still live on the stack when the hard decision arrives. What the mechanism actually buys is a REFRAMING: one hard, global, long-distance decision becomes a sequence of easy, local, high-confidence decisions resolved and removed from contention before the hard one is reached — a real, valuable risk-reduction, not a proof of impossibility of the failure mode.

**Contrast with batch/graph-based (McDonald 2005 MST/Eisner 1996; McDonald & Pereira 2006 2nd-order):** arc-factored scoring has NO built-in signal that a candidate arc is already "spoken for" by a better competing arc elsewhere — each pair scored in isolation. True MST/Eisner decoding DOES enforce single-head-per-word globally (a real tree), which is strictly stronger than this substrate's current greedy-local-argmax-plus-cycle-breaking (weaker than even McDonald's own 2005 baseline). **McDonald & Nivre (2011, *CL* 37(1)) directly compared the paradigms and found transition-based parsers perform WORSE on longer dependencies and root-adjacent attachments, due to error propagation — a genuine complication for this exact case, since the correct dependency (key→are) is the LONGER one while the wrong attractor pull (cabinets→are) is the SHORT one.** No published study isolates PP-attachment/agreement-attraction constructions specifically to compare the two paradigms head-to-head — this is a real literature gap, not a settled result either way. Anderson & Gómez-Rodríguez (2020, LREC) name this general phenomenon directly: transition systems have an "inherent dependency displacement bias" that can help or hurt depending on construction — not a universal fix.

**Left-corner / incremental CCG (Resnik 1992; Steedman; Hassan et al. 2009):** optimized for cognitive fidelity (bounded, human-matching stack depth) rather than raw engineering throughput; shift-reduce dominates applied NLP for computational simplicity and provable linear time. Both are incremental; they differ in what they claim to model.

**Bottom-line verdict from this thread (stated in the lit-scan's own words, adopted as-is):** "switching to arc-standard/arc-eager stack+buffer decoding would very likely REDUCE THE RATE of the buried-subject failure... but it would not STRUCTURALLY ELIMINATE the failure mode, since that removal itself depends on learned (fallible) classifier decisions and transition parsers carry their own well-documented long-distance-attachment weaknesses." Treat the task's framing ("the attractor never competes for the main subject") as the OPTIMISTIC case, not the guaranteed case.

## 3. VSA/HDC realizability: can a glass-box stack be built on existing primitives?

**Stack-as-hypervector (ESTABLISHED as a standard tool, one direct system-level hit):** permutation-indexed superposition (`A + Π(B) + Π²(C) + ...`) is THE standard HDC sequence-encoding device (Kanerva 2009) — push = one more permutation, pop = inverse permutation + cleanup readout. Direct precedent: **Yerxa, Anderson & Weiss (2018), "The Hyperdimensional Stack Machine"** builds exactly this (HD stack + FSA primitives → pushdown automata, tested on string reversal/RPN execution) — closest hit in the literature, though whether their headline demonstration ran in a fully discrete/non-differentiable mode (vs. a soft variant kept available for gradient controllers) is unconfirmed from accessible text.

**Recursive HRR is BATCH, not incremental (ESTABLISHED, important distinction):** Plate's tree encoding (bind role⊗filler, recursively sum/bind children) requires the whole tree in hand — "compile the finished parse" not "maintain live state while reading." The closest genuinely incremental VSA precedent is **Weiss, beim Graben et al. (2020, arXiv 2003.05171, FockBox)** — left-corner-parser states embedded live in Fock-space/tensor-algebra representations — but this is a representation-THEOREM paper, not a working parser with an action classifier.

**No published VSA/HDC transition parser exists (NOT FOUND, plainly, across all three sub-agents' search terms).** The individual ingredients (VSA sequence/tree encoding, transition-based parsing, simple glass-box action classifiers) are each independently well-published; the specific combination — bind/unbind/cleanup/permutation as live parser state, function-word-triggered discrete control, a simple (not deep-gradient) action classifier on top — has never been assembled and reported. This is a genuine literature gap, i.e. a genuinely novel construction with moderate-to-high engineering risk, not an assembly-of-proven-parts task.

**The one clean architectural seam worth naming:** Dyer et al.'s stack-LSTM (2015, ACL; extended 2017 CL) already separates "the stack is a plain discrete data structure" (persistent linked list of composed representations, O(1) push/pop) from "the summary/controller is learned" (LSTM + MLP action classifier) — this is exactly the seam to cut along: keep the discrete stack, replace the learned gating with hand-specified function-word-triggered rules, and use a simple glass-box classifier (this substrate's own structured-perceptron family, already at 0.90-0.95 F1 for POS/chunking/SRL — its best-performing NLP mechanism family by a wide margin over dep-parse's 0.7875) for the residual ambiguous cases only.

**Predicted failure mode (own synthesis, not a citation) — and why it may be a feature:** crosstalk/capacity limits on superposition-based cleanup memory (Frady, Kleyko & Sommer 2018, *Neural Computation*) predict clean retrieval for a handful of stack depths, then a fairly sharp accuracy cliff — not graceful degradation to zero. This qualitatively parallels the human center-embedding breakdown at ~3-4 levels (Miller & Chomsky 1963; Yngve 1960). **No VSA paper explicitly makes this connection** — it is this drill's own inference, not literature-established — but if the substrate's stack shows the same graceful-then-sharp breakdown at the same depth, that is a strong, falsifiable, brain-faithful signature worth treating as validating rather than disqualifying.

## 4. Generalization: is this the general unlock, or narrower?

**Narrower than "fixes the whole structure-induction wall," but real and useful within its scope.** Three distinct claims need separating, because the task's framing risks conflating them:

- **Does it fix the diagnosed failure signature (batch decode defaults to positional heuristic when the true answer is buried inside a completed, irrelevant subtree)?** Yes, as a mechanism-class — this covers subject-verb agreement-attraction, PP/complement attachment ambiguity broadly, and plausibly small-scale coreference/antecedent selection (an antecedent no longer competes once it sits inside a closed-off relative clause) — any task reducible to "what is currently exposed on the stack vs. buried inside something already reduced." This is the real, GO-worthy scope.
- **Does it give the substrate "the hierarchical feature it has been missing everywhere" (the b2 framing)?** Partially, and this needs to be stated honestly, not oversold: it gives an EXPLICIT, inspectable depth/hierarchy representation the flat/bundle representation never had — a genuine architectural upgrade. But per the induction-wall note's own selector/inducer framework, handing in this stack discipline (nested bracketing driven by function-word categories) IS supplying a hand-specified hierarchical prior — the same fairness tier as ABL/ADIOS's "weak hierarchical-capacity scaffold" (no POS tags, no lexical head template, but yes a nested-structure CAPACITY built into the architecture). **It does NOT answer b2 in the sense of "hierarchy emerges from truly flat/raw learning without a prior" — it is a new, better-motivated instance of exactly the same prior-required category, not an escape from it.** Report any resulting win as "we built a better hierarchical scaffold and decode incrementally over it," never as "we solved structure induction from scratch."
- **Does it generalize to cross-sentence comprehension or the WSD/frequency-ceiling frontier?** No — those are different mechanism classes entirely (per the session's own frontier-map: comprehension has been blocked 3x for lack of a real-text task requiring it; WSD is exhausted at the MFS/frequency ceiling). An incremental stack fixes WITHIN-SENTENCE role/attachment selection; it says nothing about which SENSE a word takes or whether cross-sentence state is being tracked. Do not scope this drill's win beyond agreement + attachment + small-scale role-selection.

## 5. GO/NO-GO + minimal design

**Verdict: GO**, scoped as an incremental-decode replacement for the batch arc-factored reader (agreement/attachment/small-scale role-selection), explicitly NOT scoped as a resolution of hierarchy-induction (b2) or comprehension/WSD frontiers.

**Why GO despite the three caveats in the headline:**
- The current batch decode is disk-confirmed to be the weakest possible arc-factored instantiation (not even MST-optimal) — almost any principled alternative should beat it, and the incremental paradigm is the mechanistically correct diagnosis for the SPECIFIC symptom observed (positional default, buried-subject failure), independent of whether it is a complete cure.
- Every needed primitive already exists and is independently validated on this substrate: permutation (position/depth encoding), bind/unbind, cleanup memory, and — critically — a proven glass-box sequence-classifier family (structured perceptron) already at 0.90-0.95 F1 for POS/chunking/SRL, the substrate's best-performing NLP mechanism family, directly reusable as the action classifier.
- The one genuinely open/novel-construction risk (VSA-stack crosstalk capacity) has a plausible brain-faithful floor (Miller & Chomsky depth ~3-4), so even a "failure" at extreme depth is informative rather than simply broken, provided shallow-to-moderate depth (which covers the vast majority of real sentences, including the buried-subject test construction, which needs only depth-2: NP → PP-inside-NP) works cleanly.

**Minimal design sketch:**
- **State:** `stack` = permutation-indexed hypervector, one depth-role per currently-open constituent (`D_0` = top/most-recently-opened ... `D_k` = outermost); `buffer` = remaining raw tokens (plain list, not a hypervector — no need to encode what hasn't been processed yet).
- **Actions, cued by function-word CATEGORY (closed-class, cheaply and reliably POS-taggable, already at 0.9499 F1 on this substrate):**
  - Determiner → SHIFT + push a new "open-NP" depth marker.
  - Preposition → SHIFT + push a new "open-PP" depth marker (nested inside the current NP if one is open).
  - Complementizer/relative pronoun → SHIFT + push a new "open-clause" depth marker.
  - Any other content word → SHIFT onto the current top depth (bind as filler at the current `D_top`).
  - REDUCE, triggered by a lookahead/closure heuristic (e.g., next token's category is incompatible with continuing the current constituent, or a verb is reached while a lower PP/NP is still open) → bind the current top depth's contents into a single completed-constituent filler, attach it as a dependent of the next-lower depth's head, pop `D_top` (decrement the depth pointer).
- **Action selection:** default to the deterministic function-word-triggered rules above for SHIFT-vs-open-new-depth; use the existing structured-perceptron classifier ONLY for the residual genuinely ambiguous cases (REDUCE timing, attachment target when more than one open depth is a plausible target) — this keeps the design maximally glass-box and minimizes where a learned component can misfire.
- **Readout at the hard decision (e.g., verb agreement):** read whichever filler occupies the CURRENT, lowest open depth (`D_top` at the moment the verb is reached) via cleanup memory — not a query over the whole sentence bundle. This is the entire mechanism of the fix: the hard decision is answered by "what's exposed right now," which is well-defined and small, instead of "search the whole bundle and default to position."
- **Testbed mapping:** directly reuses the 29443 real-text Linzen agreement/attractor testbed and the buried-subject construction named in the task — no new corpus needed. Primary discriminator: does held-out accuracy on the attractor subset clear the existing fixed-linear-readout tie (~0.63) and the batch-decode baseline (~0.30) by a real margin; content-dependence discriminator: scrambling which noun carries which number feature while preserving structure should flip the answer accordingly (rules out silent fallback to position).

**Deflated odds (calibration penalty applied per protocol — deflate 0.15-0.25, cap novel-synthesis at 0.50):**
- P(ARM_STACK clears a real, non-trivial margin over both the batch-decode baseline (~0.30) and the fixed-linear-readout tie (~0.63) on the buried-subject/attractor subset, WITH the content-dependence discriminator firing correctly) = **0.40**. This sits below the novel-synthesis cap of 0.50 because of genuine, stacked uncertainty: (a) no direct literature precedent for the exact assembly (VSA stack + transition parsing + glass-box action classifier) — moderate-to-high novel-construction risk on its own; (b) McDonald & Nivre (2011)'s error-propagation finding is a real, on-point complication specifically for THIS length profile (correct dependency is the longer one), not a hypothetical; (c) the REDUCE-timing decisions are the load-bearing risk and are exactly where a classifier can misfire silently. Residual upside: the substrate's structured-perceptron family is unusually strong (0.90-0.95 F1) relative to typical POS/chunking baselines, which is a real, substrate-specific reason for optimism above a pure literature-only prior.
- P(the underlying mechanism-class diagnosis is correct — i.e., IF action-selection is accurate, exposed-vs-buried readout structurally outperforms whole-bundle readout on this failure signature) = **0.70** — this is a near-tautological, low-risk claim (it follows directly from the hand-traced mechanics in §2) and is not deflated as heavily; the uncertainty is concentrated entirely in whether action-selection accuracy is high enough in practice, not in the mechanism's logic.

**Biggest risks, named plainly, in priority order:**
1. **Error propagation on REDUCE/attach timing (the load-bearing risk).** The stack discipline does not eliminate the buried-subject failure by construction — it converts one hard global decision into several easier local ones, but a misprediction on any of those local decisions (especially plausible near center-embedding or coordination) leaves the attractor exposed. McDonald & Nivre (2011)'s finding that transition parsers do worse on LONGER dependencies via error propagation is directly on-point here (key→are is the longer dependency). Mitigation: keep REDUCE-timing decisions on the deterministic function-word-triggered path wherever possible; route only genuinely ambiguous cases through the learned classifier; measure error-propagation rate directly (how often does an upstream misprediction, not the final decision itself, cause the eventual failure) as its own diagnostic.
2. **VSA-stack capacity/crosstalk at depth, unprecedented combination.** No published system has assembled this exact combination; the capacity cliff is real (Frady/Kleyko/Sommer) but its exact onset depth for THIS parsing task is untested. Mitigation: the buried-subject test construction itself only needs depth-2 (PP nested in NP) — validate at the depths the actual testbed needs before worrying about deeper center-embedding; treat a graceful failure at depth 3+ as a validating (brain-faithful), not disqualifying, signature.
3. **Scope creep / overclaim risk.** This fixes a specific, real, well-scoped failure signature (agreement/attachment/small-scale role-selection under buried-subject conditions). It does not solve hierarchy-induction (b2 stays closed as previously found), comprehension, or WSD. Any write-up of a resulting PASS must state this scope explicitly to avoid the "cosmetic win read as a bigger win" trap this session has repeatedly had to guard against (Lachter & Bever critique pattern, recurring across three separate notes now).

## Substrate-product implications

If ARM_STACK clears its bands: this would be the substrate's first incremental (as opposed to batch) reader mechanism, and — properly scoped per §4 — a reusable primitive for any task needing "what's currently exposed vs. buried" (agreement, attachment, small-scale coreference, possibly operand-role binding in word problems where an intervening phrase should not compete for an operator's argument slot). It would NOT be a resolution of the induction wall or comprehension frontier, and should not be reported as such.
