# Research: the path to ROBUST glass-box (no-LLM) parsing of real prose — biology + classical-IE toolkit + honest coverage ceiling

**Date:** 2026-07-16. **Filed by:** research (Sonnet lit-scan x3, Opus synthesis). **Trigger:** the real-prose read->grow cell (`experiments/exp_read_grow_foundation_endtoend_v1.py`) currently uses a **positional-SVO parse only** (word-order slot assignment, no dependency structure) and will hit modest coverage by design. This drill answers the deeper question the cell itself cannot answer: what is the realistic ROBUST-parsing architecture and honest coverage ceiling for glass-box (no-LLM) fact extraction from real prose, biology-first.

**Method:** 3 parallel Sonnet lit-scan sub-agents, live WebSearch+WebFetch with primary-source PDF verification where possible, one lane each: (1) incremental-parsing psycholinguistics, (2) classical/rule-based Open IE + SRL toolkit coverage/precision/failure modes, (3) dependency-parsing accuracy/coverage ceilings + the "narrow neural parser as glass-box component" question. All three explicitly flagged where a claim is a direct citation vs. an honest inferential synthesis — that distinction is preserved throughout this note rather than smoothed over.

## (a) HEADLINE

**The literature converges on a specific, buildable glass-box architecture, and on one clean, well-supported carve-out for a narrow neural component — but it also converges on a genuine, register-invariant literature GAP (no study measures OIE/SRL precision-recall directly on graded/simple prose) and one failure mode (coreference) that does NOT shrink with simpler prose and is unaddressed by every reviewed technique.**

The brain's own solution to robust incremental parsing is **not** a giant memorized language model — it is a small toolkit of general-purpose mechanisms: (1) two structural default rules (Minimal Attachment / Late Closure, Frazier) for the first-pass commitment, with serial reanalysis on failure; (2) a generic activation/decay/similarity-interference memory-retrieval equation (Lewis & Vasishth cue-based retrieval) that resolves long-distance dependencies, agreement, filler-gaps, AND ellipsis-antecedent access via the same content-addressable mechanism; (3) a compact vocabulary of thematic-role cues (canonical word order, animacy, case marking) combined by validity-weighted competition (MacWhinney's Competition Model, Dowty's proto-role entailment-counting) for syntax->semantics mapping; (4) a graded expectation/surprisal signal computable from a small structured grammar (a PCFG, not a giant corpus LM — Hale 2001, Levy 2008) for calibrating reanalysis cost; and (5) an explicit "good-enough, task-gated depth" control (Ferreira & Patson) that deliberately accepts a shallow heuristic parse when full compositional analysis isn't required — with well-documented systematic error signatures (passive misinterpretation ~25% of the time, agreement attraction, persistent garden-path residue) as the diagnostic fingerprint of exactly which cheap heuristic fired. None of this requires a large learned distributional model; all five pieces are small-parameter, hand-specifiable mechanisms (though cue *weights* are frequency-tuned by exposure, the cue *vocabulary* itself is small and universal).

The classical NLP Open-IE/SRL literature independently arrives at a compatible recipe. **ClausIE (Del Corro & Gemulla 2013)** is the strongest single glass-box backbone found — dependency-parse-based clause-type classification (7 Quirk clause types) that natively handles passive voice (via grammatical role, not surface order) and relative clauses (via the parse), unlike **ReVerb**, whose fixed POS-pattern structurally cannot see 15% of true relations (coordination, relative-clause fronting — verified directly from ReVerb's own Table 3). **ClausIE's own paper names dependency-parse error as its dominant error source** ("in most of the cases, ClausIE's extraction errors were due to incorrect dependency parses") and names **coordination** as the single largest source of its own imprecision (disabling CC-handling raises precision 5-10.7pts but drops recall 11-27% — a genuine trade-off, not a free win). A separate, dedicated coordination pre-splitting pass (**CALM**, Saha & Mausam 2018) gets a **precision-positive** 1.6-1.8x yield gain instead of a trade-off — this is the single highest-leverage, best-evidenced upgrade found in the whole scan. **RELNOUN** (hand-curated relational-noun rules) is needed for nominalization coverage, which none of the verb-centric extractors touch at all. **Hearst patterns** (1992) remain the archetypal glass-box lexico-syntactic technique but only for taxonomic (is-a) relations specifically, with high precision / structurally low recall (they fire only on explicit list-triggers like "such as," "including"). **OpenIE6 is explicitly disqualified** from a no-LLM pipeline — its core extractor is a BERT+transformer neural grid-labeler; only its *training losses* are rule-derived, not its runtime mechanism.

**Coreference and (verb-phrase) ellipsis are the two failure modes NOT solved by any of the six systems reviewed, and — unlike coordination/relative-clauses/passive, which all shrink or vanish under simplified grammar — coreference does NOT shrink with simpler prose, and plausibly WORSENS**, because text simplification characteristically substitutes pronouns for long noun phrases to reduce sentence length (Niklaus et al.'s sentence-simplification-for-IE literature; general observation cross-checked against the Niklaus et al. 2018 OIE survey's explicit statement that coreference is "a field that has largely been ignored to date" in Open IE). This is a genuine, honestly-flagged gap this note treats as a first-class design requirement, not an afterthought.

On the **narrow-neural-syntax-parser carve-out** (the USER's stated allowance): the field draws exactly the distinction the carve-out relies on — dependency parsing is structured prediction over a **closed, small, fixed output space** (Universal Dependencies: 17 POS tags, ~37 relation labels) applied only to tokens already present in the input, versus semantic/fact extraction, which is open-vocabulary. Dedicated confidence/confusion-scoring tooling exists specifically because parser output is closed-space and auditable (Ohta et al. 2013; the general structured-prediction confidence-estimation literature). **No single paper makes the precise claim "a dependency parser cannot hallucinate facts because it only outputs structure, never new entities" — this is this note's own straightforward inference from the closed-vs-open-output-space distinction, honestly flagged as synthesis, not citation.** It is a low-risk inference (a parser is, by construction, a labeled tree over pre-existing tokens; it cannot emit a token or entity that was not in the input), and it is empirically the dominant lever available: since ClausIE's own paper names its parser as the dominant residual error source, and modern neural dependency parsers reach 95.7% UAS / 94.1% LAS on clean text (Dozat & Manning 2017 biaffine parser) versus ~89-91% UAS for classical statistical parsers (MSTParser/MaltParser era), swapping in a narrow structure-only neural parser is very plausibly close to a strict quality improvement with **zero loss of fact-level provenance**, because the parser never touches the fact-bearing layer.

**No study in this scan — nor found by any of the three lit-scan lanes — directly measures OpenIE/SRL/triple-extraction precision-recall on native graded/simplified prose (Simple English Wikipedia, children's stories, elementary readers).** This is a confirmed, honest literature gap. The best indirect evidence is Niklaus et al.'s finding that applying rule-based syntactic *simplification* as a preprocessing step before extraction improves state-of-the-art Open IE precision by up to 32% and recall by up to 30% on complex source sentences — strongly suggestive that *native* simple prose would out-perform newswire-derived OIE benchmark numbers by a comparable margin, but this remains an extrapolation, not a measurement, and is treated as such (deflated) throughout this note's P estimates.

## (b) Cheap decisive test

Don't theorize further — **triage the ALREADY-RUNNING real-prose read->grow cell's own extraction misses against the literature's predicted failure taxonomy.** This is cheap (it's a re-analysis of existing/near-term output, no new build), and it is decisive: it tells you whether the classical-IE-toolkit roadmap below is directly actionable, or whether the current cell has a more basic problem (the positional-SVO parser itself, not the extraction-rule layer) that must be fixed first.

**Procedure:** for every extraction MISS logged by the real-prose cell (or its next iteration), bucket it into one of: `coordination` / `relative-clause` / `passive-voice` / `nominalization` / `coreference-unresolved` / `parse-structure-error-other` / `lexicon-mapping-error` / `unclassifiable`. Compare the observed distribution against the literature's predicted ranking: dependency-parse-structure errors dominate overall (per ClausIE's own error analysis), coordination is the single largest RULE-level failure category, coreference is a categorical (100% miss) blind spot rather than a partial-credit one, and passive/relative-clause failures should be LARGELY ABSENT if a real dependency/clause-type parse is used (they are ONLY a failure mode for a positional-order parser, per ReVerb's Table 3 and ClausIE's native handling).

**HARD-PASS:** >=60% of the real-prose cell's logged misses fall cleanly into the named literature-predicted buckets (coordination / relative-clause / passive / nominalization / coreference / parse-error). This means the literature's taxonomy explains the substrate's own failures, and the roadmap in section (e) below is directly actionable as the next build order.

**HARD-FAIL:** <30% of misses fall into these buckets — i.e., the substrate's specific miss profile is dominated by something the classical literature doesn't predict (most likely: positional-SVO-specific errors orthogonal to real dependency-parse failure modes, e.g. simple word-order slot misassignment that a real parser would never produce). This would mean the PARSER stage itself (not the extraction-rule stage) is the priority fix — swap in even a minimal real dependency/clause-type parse before investing in the ClausIE/CALM/RELNOUN recipe, since that recipe assumes a real parse as its input and cannot be meaningfully tested against a positional parser's error profile.

MIDDLE otherwise — partial taxonomy match; prioritize whichever bucket has the largest share.

## (c) Falsifiable predictions (HARD-PASS / HARD-FAIL)

**Prediction A — dependency/clause-type-based parsing (ClausIE-style) recovers passive-voice facts that a positional-SVO parser misses, because subject/object roles come from grammatical dependency relations, not surface word order.**
P estimate: **P=0.40** (deflated; the mechanism is well-supported directly from ClausIE's own paper, which explicitly credits its dependency-parse-based approach with "inherently capturing" passive constructions — but untested on THIS substrate's specific sentence set and current lexicon-mapping layer, hence capped below the calibration ceiling).
HARD-PASS: passive-sentence extraction accuracy improves by >=20 points when swapping the positional parse for a dependency/clause-type-based parse, at matched sentence set and matched lexicon.
HARD-FAIL: <5-point improvement (would indicate the passive-sentence failures in the real corpus trace to lexicon-mapping errors, not structural-parse errors, and the fix lies elsewhere — most likely the learned-lexicon disambiguation step, not the parser).

**Prediction B — a dedicated rule-based coordination pre-splitting pass (CALM-style: rewrite "X eats seeds and worms" into two atomic sentences BEFORE extraction, rather than handling CC in-line during extraction) increases fact recall on coordinated sentences WITHOUT the precision cost that ClausIE's own in-line CC handling incurs.**
P estimate: **P=0.35** (deflated; CALM's own paper reports a precision-POSITIVE 1.6-1.8x yield gain over the base pipeline on real Web/newswire text — real precedent, but not yet demonstrated as precision-positive specifically on graded/simple prose, and not yet implemented on this substrate).
HARD-PASS: coordination-pre-split recall gain >=15 points on the coordinated-sentence subset, with precision drop <=2 points.
HARD-FAIL: precision drop >5 points (would indicate the pre-split step introduces its own errors on this substrate's grammar, mirroring ClausIE's in-line CC trade-off rather than avoiding it — meaning the pre-split approach doesn't transfer and in-line handling with an accepted precision cost is the more honest design).

**Prediction C (biology-grounded) — a small, explicit, hand-specifiable set of thematic-role-assignment cues (canonical word-order + animacy + case-marking, weighted per MacWhinney's Competition Model) plus Frazier-style Minimal-Attachment/Late-Closure structural defaults resolve a majority of LOCAL structural ambiguities within a closed-vocabulary, closed-grammar curriculum (Rungs 1-3 of the early-reader ladder), without needing a general-purpose parser (real or neural) at all.**
P estimate: **P=0.38** (deflated; strong direct biological precedent — this is close to a literal restatement of the human parser's own default mechanism — and cheap to implement as 4-6 explicit weighted rules, but untested whether the closed-vocabulary regime is exactly where these heuristics stay sufficient; the literature's own documented failure cases for these SAME heuristics are concentrated on non-canonical structures — passives, object-relatives — which the curriculum ladder introduces starting at Rung 3+, so this prediction is explicitly scoped to the EARLY rungs only).
HARD-PASS: a 4-6-rule cue-weighted heuristic parser (no learned grammar, no neural net) matches or exceeds the current positional-SVO parser's accuracy on curriculum Rungs 1-3, while additionally correctly resolving >=50% of the LOCAL ambiguities (e.g. single-PP attachment, subject/object identity under partial word-order ambiguity) that a pure-positional parser cannot.
HARD-FAIL: statistically indistinguishable from (or worse than) the current positional parser even on Rungs 1-3 (would indicate the heuristic-cue layer adds complexity without benefit at this curriculum stage — effort should go straight to a real dependency-based upgrade instead of an intermediate heuristic layer).

**Prediction D — coreference resolution (rule-based: Hobbs' algorithm or a centering-theory resolver) is a NECESSARY, non-optional pipeline stage by curriculum Rung 3+, because pronoun density does not decrease (and plausibly increases) as prose simplifies, unlike every other reviewed failure mode.**
P estimate: **P=0.42** (deflated; well-supported directly — Niklaus et al. 2018's OIE survey explicitly names coreference as an ignored gap field-wide, and the simplification-substitutes-pronouns mechanism is a straightforward, low-risk observation about how graded/controlled-register prose is constructed — but the exact MAGNITUDE of the coverage loss from leaving coreference unresolved is not measured in this scan, hence capped).
HARD-PASS: on a held-out sample of Rung-3+ curriculum-style sentences (or Simple-Wikipedia-derived sentences), pronoun-bearing sentences constitute >=25% of all sentences AND >=80% of pronoun references are extraction-blocking (i.e., the referent is not the same token as any argument already bound in the current sentence) — confirming coreference is both frequent and load-bearing at this curriculum stage, not a rare edge case.
HARD-FAIL: pronoun-bearing sentences are <10% of the curriculum-style corpus OR most pronoun references resolve trivially within-sentence (e.g. reflexives, same-clause antecedents already captured by the role-filler bind) — would mean coreference can be deferred further down the roadmap than this note recommends.

## (d) Cross-thread synthesis

- **Directly informs, does not duplicate, the in-flight `exp_read_grow_foundation_endtoend_v1.py` cell.** That cell's own header explicitly documents its parser as "glass-box parse (positional SVO)" — i.e., word-order slot assignment only, NOT a real dependency parse. Every technique surveyed in section (b) of the classical-IE lane (ClausIE, ReVerb's lexical filter, RELNOUN, CALM) assumes at minimum a real dependency parse as INPUT. This means the current cell is several rungs BELOW the literature's own entry point on the glass-box-parsing ladder — its modest coverage is not evidence the ceiling is low, it is evidence the parser stage hasn't been built yet. This is the single most important finding to carry forward: **the classical-IE-toolkit recipe below is a strict upgrade path, contingent on first landing a real (dependency- or clause-type-based) parse, not an alternative to the current cell.**
- **Converges tightly with `notes/research_early_reader_language_acquisition_curriculum_2026-07-16.md`'s grammar ladder.** That note independently identified Simple English Wikipedia's own house-style grammar progression (`Wikipedia:HOW`: SVO declarative -> SV+indirect-object -> one-subordinate-clause-max, no compound-complex, no passive, no contractions) as the curriculum's grammar rungs. This drill finds that progression maps **almost exactly** onto ClausIE's own clause-type taxonomy (SV / SVO / SVOA / SVOC / SVOO) — the CURRICULUM ORDER (what content to introduce when, for pedagogical/acquisition reasons) and the EXTRACTION-ARCHITECTURE ORDER (what parsing/extraction complexity is needed to correctly read that content) are, essentially, the SAME LADDER, independently re-derived from two completely different literatures (child-language-acquisition pedagogy vs. computational-linguistics clause typology). This is a strong, load-bearing convergence: build the parser/extractor complexity in lockstep with the curriculum's own grammar rungs (a bare positional-SVO-sufficient parser for Rung 1-2, ClausIE-style clause-typing becoming necessary exactly at Rung 3+ where passives/relative-clauses/agreement first appear per that note's own Rung 3/4 definitions).
- **The USER's original glass-box-the-reading directive** (rule-based IE, provenance, accept lower coverage, gate cleans up; narrow-neural-syntax-parser carve-out) is directly validated by this scan on two counts: (1) every rule-based extractor reviewed genuinely IS fully provenance-traceable (every triple maps to one sentence + one rule/clause-type firing on one parse edge — this property survives even when a neural component supplies the underlying parse, since the neural component's output space is provably closed to structure over existing tokens), and (2) "accept lower coverage" is not a compromise forced by the glass-box constraint specifically — it is a GENUINE, literature-wide property of ALL open-IE approaches, including ones that DO use neural extraction end-to-end (OpenIE6 itself only reaches ~0.53 F1 on the CaRB benchmark, meaning even a fully-neural SOTA system does not achieve high coverage on general text either). This substantially de-risks the "accept lower coverage" directive: the coverage gap between glass-box and neural-end-to-end IE is real but smaller than one might assume, since neither approach is anywhere near ceiling on real text.
- **Ellipsis, as covered by the biology lane (§2 of the lit-scan, cue-based-retrieval VP-ellipsis resolution), is a DIFFERENT phenomenon than the coreference gap identified by the classical-IE lane** — worth keeping distinct. Human VP-ellipsis ("John left, and Mary did too") is resolved via the same generic memory-retrieval mechanism as any other long-distance dependency (a cheap, already-generalizable glass-box mechanism, IF a retrieval-style memory-access primitive exists in the pipeline). Genuine verb-gapping/ellipsis constructions are also, per the dependency-parsing lane, rare-to-absent in short/controlled-grammar prose (they are a complex-register phenomenon), so this specific failure mode is low-priority for the early curriculum rungs. Pronominal COREFERENCE, by contrast, is common in even the simplest graded prose and is architecturally unaddressed by any Open-IE technique reviewed — this is the higher-priority gap.

## (e) Substrate-product implications

1. **Immediate next build: a real dependency- or clause-type-based parser stage, curriculum-rung-gated, NOT a wholesale neural-parser adoption.** For curriculum Rungs 1-2 (SVO-only, no passive, no subordinate clauses, per the early-reader note's own rung definitions), the current positional-SVO parser is likely already adequate or close to it — no upgrade needed yet. **Rung 3 is the concrete trigger point** where a real parse becomes necessary (agreement, past-tense, possessive — still no clause embedding), and **Rung 4** (one-subordinate-clause-max, per Simple Wikipedia's own house style) is where relative-clause and passive handling become load-bearing. Recommend building a **small, hand-written, glass-box clause-type classifier** (a rule set directly modeled on ClausIE's SV/SVA/SVC/SVO/SVOA/SVOC/SVOO decision tree, restricted to the closed grammar of the curriculum's own Rung 3-4 vocabulary) rather than importing a general-purpose parser at this stage — the closed-grammar regime is exactly where a hand-specified rule set is both tractable and provably 100% glass-box, no neural component needed yet.
2. **Defer the narrow-neural-syntax-parser carve-out to curriculum Rung 5+ (open-ended CEFR B1+ text, per the sibling curriculum note), where grammar variety first exceeds what a hand-written closed-grammar pattern set can cover.** At that point, the literature supports the carve-out cleanly: a structure-only dependency parser with a closed output space (POS tags + dependency arcs over existing tokens only) cannot introduce new fact content, preserves full triple-level provenance, and empirically the parser is the dominant error source in every rule-based extractor reviewed (per ClausIE's own error analysis) — so this is the single highest-leverage place to accept a narrow neural component, and nowhere else in the pipeline.
3. **Highest-leverage, best-evidenced NEAR-TERM upgrade to the extraction-rule layer specifically: a dedicated coordination pre-splitting pass (CALM-style rule-based rewrite), not in-line CC handling.** This is the one upgrade in the whole scan with a documented PRECISION-POSITIVE yield gain (not a trade-off) in the primary literature (Saha & Mausam 2018). Concretely: before running clause-type extraction, scan for coordinating conjunctions joining parallel constituents (objects, verb phrases) and rewrite into N atomic sentences.
4. **Budget explicit engineering effort for a rule-based coreference pass (Hobbs' algorithm or a centering-theory resolver) as a first-class pipeline stage by curriculum Rung 3+, not an afterthought.** This is the one failure mode in the entire scan that does not shrink with grammar simplification and is unaddressed by every classical IE technique reviewed — if left unbuilt, it becomes an increasingly binding coverage ceiling exactly as the curriculum introduces more natural (pronoun-using) prose.
5. **Log every extraction miss with an explicit reason-code** (coordination / relative-clause / passive / nominalization / coreference-unresolved / parse-error-other / lexicon-mapping-error), per the cheap decisive test in section (b). This turns "modest coverage by design" into a directed, measurable engineering roadmap rather than an unstructured shortfall — directly operationalizes the pre-registered definition of "good-enough glass-box reading" below.
6. **RELNOUN-style hand-curated relational-noun rules should be added whenever the curriculum introduces its first non-verb-mediated fact** (e.g. "Obama, the president" / possessive constructions like "his wife," which the early-reader note's own Rung 2+ possessive-'s morphology already anticipates) — flag this as a coupled dependency between the two curriculum threads (grammar-rung introduction of possessives triggers a corresponding extraction-rule requirement).
7. **Hearst-pattern-style extraction should be added as a narrow, SEPARATE supplementary extractor specifically for taxonomic (is-a) facts** ("a robin is a bird") once the curriculum introduces category/hyponymy vocabulary — this is architecturally distinct from the SVO/clause-type extractors (it fires on list/apposition triggers, not verb predicates) and should not be conflated with the main clause-based pipeline.

## Pre-registered definition: what "good-enough glass-box reading" looks like

Per the task's explicit request to pre-register this, and drawing directly on Ferreira & Patson's "good-enough processing" framework from the biology lane (the human parser also deliberately settles for incomplete, heuristic representations rather than full compositional analysis) combined with this substrate's own core invariant (zero hallucination, full provenance):

A glass-box reading pipeline counts as **good-enough** when, simultaneously:
1. **Zero-hallucination provenance holds absolutely:** every accepted fact is 100% traceable to one specific sentence + one specific rule-firing (or one clause-type classification decision) on one specific parse. This is non-negotiable and is the substrate's core invariant, not a tunable target.
2. **Precision on ACCEPTED facts is near-ceiling** (target >=90%), because the ingest gate's schema-fit/novelty check is a *second* line of defense, not the *only* line — the extraction layer itself should not be a primary source of contamination.
3. **Recall/coverage is honestly modest and EXPECTED to be modest** — realistically in the 50-70% range on graded/simple prose (an EXTRAPOLATED, deflated estimate; no direct study measures this — see section (a)), NOT matching an LLM's near-complete coverage, per the USER's original directive ("accept lower coverage, gate cleans up"). A pipeline hitting 55% recall at 92% precision on Rung-3 curriculum text should be read as SUCCESS, not shortfall.
4. **Every extraction gap is logged with an explicit reason-code**, not silently dropped (per section (e) point 5) — coverage growth becomes a directed roadmap (build the coordination pre-pass, then the coreference pass, then RELNOUN, in that literature-supported priority order) rather than an open-ended mystery.
5. **The ONE place a neural component may appear is the structure-only parsing stage** — a dependency/POS labeler with a closed, fixed output vocabulary (tags + arc labels) applied only to tokens already in the input, never emitting new entity/fact content. This is the literature-supported carve-out (section (a)); every other pipeline stage (clause-type classification, relation extraction, coreference resolution) remains rule-based/hand-specified.

## Realistic coverage ceiling estimates (deflated, capped per novel-synthesis rule)

- **Fully classical pipeline (rule-based extraction over a classical/hand-built parse, no neural component anywhere):** precision **~70-85%**, recall **~50-70%** on graded/simple prose. This is an EXTRAPOLATION from cross-register numbers (ClausIE's best-performing register in its own eval was already Wikipedia text, its simplest register tested; the Niklaus et al. simplification-as-preprocessing literature's +30-point-scale gains when sentences are pre-simplified before extraction) — no direct measurement on native graded/simple prose exists in the literature found. **P(this ceiling estimate is roughly right, within +-10 points) = 0.35** (deflated per lit-scan calibration; this is the honest confirmed-gap case).
- **Adding a narrow, structure-only neural dependency parser** (closed POS/arc-label output space, no fact content, feeding the same rule-based clause-type/extraction logic downstream): estimated **+5 to +15 relative points on both precision and recall**, concentrated in cleaning up the "incorrect parse" error category that dominates ClausIE's own error analysis, while fully preserving provenance-traceability (the neural component never emits anything beyond a tag/arc-label on an existing input token). **P(this is a net win with fully preserved auditability) = 0.45** (deflated below the 0.50 novel-synthesis cap; this is closer to established engineering practice — swapping a classical parser for a modern one is a well-trodden move — than a genuinely novel claim, hence the slightly higher confidence than the fully-classical ceiling estimate above, but still capped).
- **Coreference and nominalization remain UNBOUNDED coverage losses** until their dedicated rule-based passes (Hobbs/centering-theory; RELNOUN-style hand-curated relational nouns) are built — neither is included in the ceiling estimates above, since no technique reviewed handles them, and their omission means the true achievable ceiling without them is LOWER than the numbers above, by an amount this scan could not quantify (flagged honestly as unquantified, not papered over with an invented number).
