# Research: precision of top-down sense prediction & the human ceiling on subordinate-sense override

**For:** `notes/problems/wire_the_situation_model_as_a_top_down_predictive_coding_sense_selector/` — feeds a SOLVED.md located-negative submission.
**Method:** 4 parallel Sonnet lit-scan lanes (precision machinery / human subordinate-bias ceiling / N400 directional-detector evidence / world-knowledge-vs-lexical-graph), live WebSearch+WebFetch of primary psycholinguistics/ERP/computational-WSD literature, synthesized here. 2x-drill depth (builds on, does not re-derive, the parent's already-PINNED citations: Duffy/Rayner reordered access, Desimone-Duncan biased competition, Feldman-Friston precision-weighting, Rabovsky N400-as-prediction-error, Lambon-Ralph ATL hub-and-spoke).

## HEADLINE

The literature does **not** hand back a clean human benchmark for `a_s` (override accuracy) or a neural benchmark for `p` (detector precision) — both quantities the solver's decomposition needs are genuinely **unmeasured** in cognitive science, not just unmeasured by us. What IS PINNED: (1) even the human brain's override of a dominant sense is graded/incomplete, never categorical, even under near-ceiling biasing context — some non-zero "residual dominance cost" is intrinsic to the mechanism; (2) the entire subordinate-bias-effect (SBE) literature studies **binary** (2-sense) homographs and measures **online RT/gaze cost**, never final-interpretation accuracy on a multi-way sense inventory — so `a_s=0.35` (right sense among possibly several rare candidates, given correct trigger) has **no direct human analog to compare against at all**; (3) the "N400 = directional error against the dominant reading" framing the solver's detector uses is a **PLAUSIBLE, never-isolated** extrapolation — no ERP/MEG study has isolated directionality from ordinary relatedness, and no decoding study quantifies its selectivity, so the solver's own AUC 0.74 is not shown to be inferior to any documented brain number; (4) independent computational evidence (UKB personalized-PageRank-over-WordNet, F1 42–61%, at/below MFS baseline across benchmarks) **externally confirms** the solver's own located negative that plain graph-spreading structurally defaults to the frequency prior; (5) the one concrete lever the literature corroborates as still-untested is exactly what the solver's own DESIGN doc already names — lifting the detector from sentence-local structural conflict to discourse-scale generalized-event/situation knowledge — but with a specific, named reason it may still fail on the canonical example class: spatial/object world knowledge (river/bank-style cases) is the ONE situation-model dimension (of space/time/causation/goal/protagonist) **not** shown to be automatically, online available (Zwaan & Radvansky event-indexing).

## THE 5 QUESTIONS, DIRECTLY ANSWERED

### 1. What makes the brain's top-down prediction precise enough to override a ~99% frequency prior?

No source in any of the 4 lanes supplied an explicit numeric precision/reliability weight comparing situational constraint to frequency (both the precision-machinery lane and the world-knowledge lane hit this as an **explicit gap**, independently). What IS supported:

- **PINNED** (Matsuki et al. 2011, *JEP*; Metusalem et al. 2012, *J. Mem. Lang.*): generalized event/scenario knowledge exerts fast, granular, ONLINE lexical constraint at the word level (multiple converging ERP/RT studies). **PLAUSIBLE** (not directly tested) that this specific mechanism is what flips dominant→subordinate selection rather than merely facilitating expected words.
- **PLAUSIBLE, single paradigm but directly on-point** (Hoeks et al. 2007, *Brain Research*): message-level discourse constraint (situation model proper) is functionally and topographically **dissociable** from mere schema/scenario-level fit — scenario priming alone cannot account for the full discourse-dependent N400. This is the clearest evidence that situation-model-level representation, not schema activation alone, carries the more precise signal.
- **PINNED across many independent labs** (Chen & Boland; Duffy et al. 1988; Rayner et al. 1994/2006; Kambe et al. 2001; Huettig & Altmann 2007): even at **near-ceiling, singularly-supportive subordinate bias**, the dominant sense's competitor remains fixated above chance 500–700ms post-onset, and in visual-world paradigms the dominant referent is still fixated 45–49% of trials mid-sentence. Quoted conclusion from the strongest single source (Chen & Boland): dominant-meaning activation "probably cannot be completely overridden." **The brain's own override is graded and probabilistic, not categorical — consistent with a precision-weighted computation, but never formally quantified as a weight anywhere in the literature scanned.**
- A rival "Selective Access" school (Kellas, Vu, Martin — cross-modal priming/naming) reports full elimination of dominance under strong two-sentence discourse context; this is **contested**, not settled — a 25+-paper, decades-long theoretical dispute (Rayner/Binder/Duffy 1999 vs. Vu & Kellas reply; 2013 replication) that is finer-grain-dependent: coarser/offline tasks look "resolved," the finest eye-movement grain shows residual cost persists.

### 2. THE KEY ONE — is the human brain ALSO see-saw-limited on subordinate senses?

**Yes and no — the honest answer splits into two different quantities, only one of which the literature actually measures.**

- **PINNED, replicated across independent labs** (Duffy, Morris & Rayner 1988; Rayner, Pacht & Duffy 1994; Sereno, O'Donnell & Rayner 2006 — explicitly ruled out as a control-matching artifact; Binder & Rayner 1998 + 2013 replication): a residual processing cost from the dominant competitor **persists even under context singularly supportive of the subordinate sense**. It is never fully eliminated at the finest (eye-movement) measurement grain. Magnitude in the cleanest comparable numbers found (Leinenger & Rayner 2013, PMC3780419, directly fetched): gaze-duration differences 14–20ms, go-past 37–42ms, total-time 27–51ms across two experiments (illustrative of scale, not a direct ambiguous-vs-control comparison). A related study found the disambiguation cost **bleeds into the next sentence region** (elevated downstream fixations). **So: genuine, non-eliminable task difficulty is real and PINNED — some see-saw-like tension is intrinsic to the mechanism, not 100% avoidable even by a perfect comprehender.**
- **But this is an online RT/gaze-cost measure, not a final-answer accuracy measure.** Neither lit-scan lane found ANY study reporting offline comprehension-error rate, "which meaning did you understand" judgment accuracy, or misinterpretation rate specifically for lexical (as opposed to syntactic) ambiguity, broken out by condition. Comprehension-accuracy figures that exist in the SBE literature (92–96%) are pooled manipulation checks, not sensitive to ambiguity condition. **This is a genuine, explicitly-flagged gap in the field** — the extensive Christianson-style "lingering misinterpretation" paradigm exists for syntactic garden-paths but has apparently not been extended to lexical ambiguity.
- **Critically: the entire SBE literature studies BINARY (2-sense) homographs.** It says nothing about a system's accuracy picking the RIGHT sense among 3+ candidate rare senses once correctly triggered to override — which is what `a_s` actually measures in the solver's decomposition. **There is no human benchmark for this quantity at all.** This is a task-granularity mismatch, not evidence for or against the solver's `a_s=0.35`.
- **Calibrated read:** the LOW value of `p` (detector precision, ~0.5) has partial genuine-difficulty backing — the human literature shows even a strongly-biased context leaves detectable residual competition, and (per Q3) no neural study has ever shown the brain's own directional detector is more precise than chance-adjacent extrapolation. But the LOW value of `a_s` (0.35, right sense among candidates) has **no documented human analog whatsoever** — nothing in the literature suggests humans, once correctly cued to expect a rare sense, pick the wrong one among candidates ~65% of the time; human sense selection is generally described as fluent/accurate once resolved. This makes `a_s`'s low value look more like a **fidelity/task-scope gap** (a genuinely unstudied sub-problem — fine-grained multi-way rare-sense discrimination — not a proven ceiling) than a documented intrinsic human limit.

### 3. The neural "when-to-suppress" detector and its precision

- **PINNED, several independent labs** (Van Petten & Kutas 1987; Swaab, Brown & Hagoort 2003; Klepousniotou et al. 2012): N400 mismatch is asymmetric — subordinate-context words elicit a larger N400 than dominant-context words (which pattern near unambiguous baseline).
- **Critical, explicitly-checked caveat: no study isolates a directional (dominance-specific) signal from plain relatedness/cloze-probability.** Both accounts predict the same asymmetry, since dominant-consistent trials are simply more related to context. **The solver's own framing ("N400-style directional detector") is a PLAUSIBLE, never-isolated extrapolation in the literature, not a demonstrated neural finding.**
- A possible alternative locus: Federmeier's graded-prediction work suggests the marker for a strongly-violated, confident prediction may live in a **later frontal positivity** (post-N400), not N400 amplitude itself — a nuance suggesting the solver's detector, framed as N400-style, may not be targeting the most diagnostic real signal.
- **PLAUSIBLE, single very recent study, directly on-point** (Boudewyn et al. 2025, *Cogn. Affect. Behav. Neurosci.*): using dominance-normed homographs (dominant-norm 86.6% / subordinate 8.32% — strikingly close to the solver's own ~0.5 fired-precision regime), midfrontal theta (200–400ms, ACC-style domain-general conflict marker) is **sequentially dissociated** from N400 (300–500ms): theta = conflict detection, N400 = downstream lexical facilitation. **But their design lacks a dominant-resolved-ambiguous control, so cannot prove theta is directional rather than symmetric** — an acknowledged open gap that mirrors the solver's own finding that a directional detector (AUC 0.74) only modestly beats a symmetric conflict detector (AUC 0.71).
- **No EEG/MEG single-trial decoding study was found quantifying separability/selectivity of any of these candidate signals.** There is **no neural precision benchmark to compare the solver's AUC 0.74 or p=0.5 against** — the brain's own directional-suppression-trigger precision has never been quantified as a decodable signal anywhere in the literature. **This means the solver's AUC 0.74 is not shown to be inferior to any documented brain number — it may already be at or near what's empirically known about this signal's separability, full stop.**

### 4. What does the brain's predictor have that a co-occurrence + WordNet/ConceptNet graph lacks?

Ranked by evidentiary strength:

1. **PINNED for offline, contested for online** (Bower, Black & Turner 1979 — classic false-recall of unstated script actions is PINNED/replicated, but this is a POST-comprehension memory measure; online availability during reading is thinner evidence, PLAUSIBLE only).
2. **A genuinely live, unresolved 30+-year dispute** (Graesser, Singer & Trabasso 1994 constructionist theory vs. McKoon & Ratcliff 1992 minimalist hypothesis): whether "instantiation of noun category" — the inference class closest to what rare-sense world-knowledge disambiguation needs — happens automatically online by default, or only on-demand for local coherence. **The solver's problem should not treat this as settled science either way.**
3. **PINNED with an important, specific boundary** (Zwaan & Radvansky 1998 event-indexing): automatic online situation-model tracking is well-supported for TIME, CAUSATION, GOALS/MOTIVATION, and PROTAGONIST — but **NOT reliably for SPACE** unless the reader already has an explicit prior spatial model (e.g., a memorized map). **The canonical disambiguation examples (fishing → river/bank) are exactly spatial/object world knowledge — the one dimension shown weakest for automatic online availability.** This is a specific, load-bearing reason to temper optimism about any situation-model-based fix for exactly this class of examples.
4. **PINNED, direct external confirmation of the solver's own located negative**: UKB (Agirre & Soroa 2009, personalized PageRank over WordNet — the closest published analog to the solver's own baseline family) scores F1 42–61% across standard benchmarks, generally at or below the MFS baseline; surveys note knowledge-based/graph WSD systems are commonly coupled to an MFS backoff for uncertain cases. **A different lab, different dataset, same structural finding: plain graph-spreading has no distinct mechanism for rare senses and defaults to frequency.**
5. **A genuine literature gap, not a refutation or confirmation**: no source directly compares FrameNet-frame-element, script-chain-induction (Chambers & Jurafsky), or selectional-preference (Resnik 1997) approaches against plain graph-spreading with an MFS-vs-non-MFS breakdown. This specific comparison appears not to exist in the published literature.
6. Grounded/sensorimotor feature norms (McRae et al. 2005): PINNED to exist; PLAUSIBLE-only (inferred, not measured) that they carry sense-differentiating content, since separate feature lists were needed per sense for ~20 homonyms in the norming process. Coverage capped at 541–4,436 concrete nouns — far short of full-vocabulary need.

### 5. Any specific buildable glass-box lever NOT yet tried?

Three candidates surfaced; none is a clean, literature-proven new mechanism:

- **(a) Selectional-preference / verb-argument typicality models (FrameNet/PropBank/VerbNet-trained).** Already tried and refuted per the solver's own PROBLEM.md "ALREADY TRIED" list ("selectional-preference/typical-usage as the sense cue — DOMINANCE-reinforcing, −0.085 subordinate, circular in isolation"). The externally-buildable FrameNet-frame-**element**-inference variant (detecting UNSTATED frame elements, not verb-argument typicality) is subtly different and technically untried, but the general family is dominance-reinforcing by the solver's own measurement — expectations should be deflated further, not raised.
- **(b) Narrative/script-chain induction** (Chambers & Jurafsky-style unsupervised event chains). Genuinely untried in the solver's own list. But cognitive-science evidence for its ONLINE availability during reading is contested (Q4 point 2), and no computational precedent shows script-chain features beating a graph baseline on non-MFS items specifically. Most novel candidate, but carries two independent uncertainty flags.
- **(c) Discourse-scale generalized-event/situation-model conflict (via coref), replacing sentence-local structural conflict.** This is **exactly** what the solver's own DESIGN doc already names as "the untested, brain-faithful lever" / "the discourse generalization." The literature independently corroborates it as the right next step (Matsuki/Metusalem event-knowledge is PINNED fast/online/granular, unlike static schema activation; Hoeks et al. show situation-model-level constraint is functionally more precise than schema-level fit) — but does **not** supply a new mechanism beyond what's already named, and comes with the Q4-point-3 caveat baked in: spatial/object world knowledge (the canonical example class) is the one dimension NOT shown to be automatically available, so even a well-built discourse extension may underperform specifically on river/bank-style cases while doing better on time/causation/goal-driven sense distinctions.

**No lit-scan lane surfaced a mechanism beyond what the solver's DESIGN doc already identifies.** The research corroborates (c) as worth one more measured attempt; it does not hand over a new untried lever.

## VERDICT — fidelity gap vs. genuine difficulty

**Split, not clean.** Genuine task difficulty is real but modest in the one place it's actually measured: the brain's own dominance-override is never categorical even under best-case bias (PINNED, tens-of-ms/minority-fraction residual cost), and the brain's own directional when-to-suppress signal has never been isolated or quantified as precise in neural data — so a break-even bar around `p*a_s ~ 0.3` may be intrinsically hard to clear by a wide margin, for anyone, human or artificial. That argues part of the wall is genuine. But the specific, large shortfall — `a_s=0.35`, picking the wrong sense among candidates even when correctly triggered — has **no documented human analog at all**: the entire behavioral literature studies binary dominant/subordinate homographs, not multi-way WordNet-granularity selection, so this piece looks more like an **understudied fidelity/task-scope gap** than a proven ceiling. **The honest conclusion is NOT yet "needs the full generative world-knowledge situation model" as a distinct large program** — that conclusion would be premature, because the one lever the literature actively corroborates as untested (discourse-scale generalized-event/situation conflict via coref, exactly what the solver's own DESIGN doc names) has not been run yet. If that specific extension is built and STILL nets ~0 — and especially if it fails specifically on spatial/object-knowledge cases (the one situation-model dimension the literature shows is NOT automatically available, per Zwaan & Radvansky) while helping on time/causation/goal-driven cases — THAT outcome, with that specific pattern, would upgrade this to a well-evidenced located negative pointing at the full situation-model North Star. Until then, this is a fidelity gap with one clear next experiment, not a closed ceiling.

## Cheap decisive test

Reuse the solver's existing structural-precision detector harness (already built, AUC 0.74) and existing `situation_reader` coref chains — no new infrastructure. Build the SAME detector using generalized-event-knowledge features drawn from the discourse's ALREADY-ESTABLISHED events/entities (via coref), scored at Hoeks-et-al.'s "message level" (entity+event history), not sentence-local dependency structure. Re-run the identical `p`/`a_s`/`c_d` decomposition on the same held-out subordinate population. Add one oracle arm: gold coref chains substituted for extracted ones, to separate "is it the SIGNAL (discourse-level constraint doesn't carry it) or the EXTRACTION (situation_reader recall ~0.32 loses it)" — this is the same diagnostic the solver's own DESIGN doc already proposes for this exact wall.

## Falsifiable predictions

- **HARD-PASS:** discourse-scale detector AUC ≥ 0.80 (beats the sentence-local 0.74) AND net = fired*[p·a_s − (1−p)·c_d] crosses the break-even ~0.3 threshold CI-separated from 0 on the held-out subordinate population, with the oracle-coref arm showing the gain is not solely an extraction-recall artifact.
- **HARD-FAIL:** discourse-scale AUC ≤ 0.76 (no material improvement over sentence-local) OR net stays within the null band (~0, CI-overlapping) even under the oracle-coref arm — this would localize the wall to the SIGNAL itself (discourse-level generalized-event constraint doesn't carry rare-sense information on this data), matching the "spatial/object dimension not automatically available" prediction from Q4, and would be the strongest evidence yet that the North-Star full situation model (not an incremental extension) is required.
- **Stratified falsifier (diagnostic of WHICH failure mode, per Q4 point 3):** if the discourse extension helps on time/causation/goal-typed subordinate items but not on spatial/object-typed items specifically, that is direct, literature-predicted confirmation that spatial world-knowledge is the load-bearing missing piece — a positive, actionable finding even inside an overall HARD-FAIL.

## Substrate-product implications

For the product: rare/subordinate word-sense selection is a genuine hard sub-problem, not a solved-elsewhere capability the reader is failing to replicate — no existing published system (human-behavioral or computational-WSD) has been shown to solve fine-grained multi-way rare-sense selection cleanly. That reframes the located negative from "our comprehension pipeline is missing something known to work" to "this is a frontier sub-problem where one more specific, cheap, already-designed experiment (discourse-scale event conflict via coref) is worth running before concluding the full situation-model program is required." If that experiment also nets ~0, the product-relevant conclusion is to defer full rare-sense override (route those cases to the safe dominant-sense default, which is already 99% correct on the dominant-congruent population) rather than ship a system that actively hurts dominant-item accuracy chasing an unproven subordinate gain.
