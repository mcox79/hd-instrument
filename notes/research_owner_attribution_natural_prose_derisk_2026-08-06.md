# research: does owner-attribution actually matter on NATURAL (non-trap-curated) goal-bearing prose?

**Type:** analysis drill (read-only, no build/run, no organ modified). Deliverable is this note.
**Trigger:** de-risk the owner-attribution / goal-relation companion lever BEFORE investing a dedicated build, mirroring the generalization-probe de-risk pattern that validated the did-it-happen pivot. Context: `notes/research_goal_bearing_eval_driver_decomposition_2026-08-06.md` found goal-relation load-bearing (primary-or-companion) on 69% of the TRAP-CURATED original eval (36 items); `notes/research_goal_bearing_driver_generalization_probe_2026-08-06.md` re-ran the same coarse driver rubric on a fresh 21-item natural set and found goal-relation's PRIMARY share dropped to 19% (4/21), flagged as "likely a sampling artifact of trap-curation, not the mechanism vanishing" but not independently re-examined. `notes/director_POST_COMPACTION_BACKUP_2026-08-04.md` records the real, MEASURED McGuffey generalization diagnostic (`exp_real_text_goal_owner_generalization_diagnostic_v1`, 10 real passages): owner-acc 0.30 organ vs recency/first_mention 0.70, and separately (after the Tier-2 open-vocab typing fix) owner-acc 0.60/0.60 (tied recency) — with the dominant failure tallied as `OUTCOME_NEVER_TYPED` (6/10, typing never fires) and only `TIEBREAK_MISS` (1/10) as a pure owner-misattribution miss. This note asks the owner-specific question directly, with a dedicated rubric, rather than inheriting the coarser A/B/C/D driver labels.

## Method

Assembled 24 natural goal-bearing passages: the generalization-probe's 21-item set (Sherlock Holmes x9, OneStop x7, RACE x5 — confirmed zero-overlap with the trap-curated original eval by corpus/id, per that note's own exclusion list) plus 4 fresh items from previously untouched sources, sampled for whatever a passage naturally supplied rather than hunted for owner-conflict: McGuffey Fourth Reader Lesson 1 (kite story), OneStop "Nelson Mandela" obituary, RACE `middle1.txt` (Zhang Qi/Wang Lin classroom article — read in full, quoted below), and re-read the full text of OneStop "Gangs" and "Banksy" (previously only summarized) to check for owner-ambiguity the coarser driver rubric wasn't designed to surface. 24 total, within the requested ~20-25 range.

**Rubric (new, owner-specific — distinct from the driver-decomposition's A/B/C/D):**
- **(a) owner-unambiguous** — single continuous protagonist; a cheap positional default (first-mention / majority-mention) already names the correct goal-owner AND the outcome clause is directly about that same entity. No dedicated lever needed.
- **(b) owner-attribution-deciding** — getting the owner wrong (wrong NAME, or right name but outcome bound to the wrong ENTITY) would flip owner-acc or MET/UNMET. Sub-flavors seen: recency/majority-mention picks the wrong entity outright; owner-name is right but the satisfying FACT is a different entity's state (owner/beneficiary split, antagonist-comeuppance); same event has opposite valence depending which of several entities is the evaluand (goal-relative polarity flip); multiple independent goal-owners share one document and a global single-protagonist default would cross-contaminate their outcome registers.
- **(c) owner-attribution necessary but not sufficient** — owner-scoping is genuinely required AND getting it right still leaves the item unsolved without additional machinery (world-knowledge, numeric threshold, etc.).

**Within (b), a further split matters for sizing the build:**
- **Pure owner-NAME misattribution** (a cheap majority/first-mention default would pick the WRONG entity outright): 2/24 (8.3%) — `os_banksy_haringey_campaign`, `os_gangs_arrest_policy`.
- **Owner-NAME correct, cross-entity OUTCOME-BINDING required** (the right owner is easy to name, but the satisfying fact belongs to a different entity and must be related back): 4/24 (16.7%) — `sh_noble_bachelor_st_simon`, `sh_speckled_band_stoner`, `race_crocodile_rescue`, `race_zhangqi_teamleader`.

This split is the important finding: **most of the real owner-attribution burden on natural prose is not "which name is correct" (rare, ~8%) but "which entity's state satisfies this owner's goal" (~17%)** — a cross-entity relational-binding problem, not a candidate-selection problem.

The 25-29% figure sits between the trap-curated 69% ("touches," deliberately engineered to stress owner-mismatch — not the right comparison for a natural-prose sizing decision) and the fresh-set's own coarse-rubric 19% ("sole primary driver" under a rubric built for a different question, MET/UNMET polarity attribution, not owner-naming). It is **higher than 19%**, not lower, because a dedicated owner lens surfaces cross-entity-binding cases (Stoner/Roylott, St. Simon/Hatty, Banksy campaign/auction-house, Zhang/Wang) that the coarser A/B/C/D driver rubric folded into B (did-it-happen) or didn't flag as C at all, since those items' MET/UNMET polarity was locally unambiguous even though their goal-owner BINDING was not. **This is not noise or a rubric artifact favoring a bigger number** — re-reading the full text of `os_banksy_haringey_campaign` and `os_gangs_arrest_policy` (previously only summarized) is what surfaced 2 of the 6 (b) items; the other 4 were already visible in the original per-item descriptions, just not previously counted under an owner-specific lens.

This also triangulates against the one piece of REAL measured data available (`exp_real_text_goal_owner_generalization_diagnostic_v1`, N=10 McGuffey passages): pure owner-NAME misattribution (`TIEBREAK_MISS`) was only 1/10 (10%), closely matching this note's 8.3% pure-NAME-miss estimate. The much larger measured failure mode, `OUTCOME_NEVER_TYPED` (6/10), is a broader bucket that plausibly subsumes several of this note's cross-entity-binding misses (Stoner/Roylott-style cases would present as `OUTCOME_NEVER_TYPED`, not `TIEBREAK_MISS`, under the current organ, because the organ never searches outside the goal-owner's own clauses) alongside genuine open-vocabulary typing gaps. **The two data sources agree on the small number (pure naming) and are consistent on the larger number being real but currently invisible behind the typing/search gate.**

## Cheap decisive test

Wire the ALREADY-BUILT goal-coherence candidate-generation (`hdlab/goal_owner_select.py`, the `b1b1ce460` enumerate-roster argmax used for the primacy-trap fix) to also serve as the OUTCOME search space — i.e., when no clause local to the goal-owner types an outcome, search all entities' typed states (not just the goal-owner's own clauses) for one that resolves the goal-owner's specific goal-content, same mechanism already validated for candidate GENERATION on primacy-trap (12/12), now applied to candidate SEARCH on outcome-binding. Add a per-owner outcome register (reuse the `GoalOutcomeRegister` pattern from the affect-state bridge, `f99d1024f`) so a second goal-owner's clause in the same document cannot overwrite the first owner's entry. Re-run against a held-out natural (non-trap) subset built from the 6 (b)-class items identified here plus ~6 more drawn the same way (naturally, not hunted) from a fourth untouched corpus (e.g. `litbank_coref_conll` narrative spans if readable prose can be recovered, or additional McGuffey Fourth/Fifth Reader lessons) — N=12, pre-registered before running.

- **HARD-PASS:** owner-acc AND outcome-binding-correctness on the 6 named (b)-items improves over the current organ's baseline (own baseline measured first) by a real margin, with ZERO regression on the 17 (a)-items (cheap-default-sufficient cases must stay correct — this is the discriminating check that cross-entity search doesn't introduce false cross-links on the easy majority).
- **HARD-FAIL:** no net-new-correct on the 6 (b)-items, OR any regression on the 17 (a)-items (would mean the cross-entity search over-fires and corrupts the easy majority), OR the fix requires a genuinely NEW organ rather than reuse of the existing candidate-gen + register pattern (would mean this note's "mostly-reuse, thin build" sizing was wrong).

## Falsifiable predictions

1. **Prediction:** owner-attribution-deciding items on natural prose cluster in the 20-30% range (this note: 25-29%), not near the trap-curated 69% and not near the coarse-rubric 19%. **HARD-FAIL if:** a larger confirmatory batch (N>=40, same owner-specific rubric) lands outside 15-40%, which would mean this 24-item sample was itself unrepresentative.
2. **Prediction:** most of the owner-attribution burden is cross-entity OUTCOME-BINDING (right name, wrong/missing outcome-search), not owner-NAME misattribution — this note found the split 17%/8% in that direction. **HARD-FAIL if:** a confirmatory measurement (e.g. the cheap decisive test above) finds pure-naming misses dominate instead (would mean the sizing recommendation below — reuse existing candidate-gen over building new disambiguation — is wrong).
3. **Prediction:** the existing goal-coherence candidate-gen + per-owner register (both already built for other purposes) can be reused for the outcome-binding companion with no new organ. **HARD-FAIL if:** the cheap decisive test requires a materially new mechanism beyond wiring these two together (would upgrade this from a "thin companion" to a real build, changing the sizing call).
4. **Prediction:** cheap protagonist-default (first-mention specifically, the strongest of the substrate's 3 benchmarked positional baselines at 0.80 on the modern eval) already resolves the majority (~70%) of natural owner-bearing items correctly, so a dedicated lever's job is narrow triage, not a general replacement. **HARD-FAIL if:** first-mention's real accuracy on a larger natural sample drops materially below ~65-70% (would mean the (a)-class is smaller than measured here and the lever needs to be bigger/earlier-priority than recommended).

## Verdict: sized recommendation

**BUILD IT — but as a thin, reuse-heavy companion wired AFTER did-it-happen lands, not deferred and not a large dedicated system.**

Reasoning:
- Owner-attribution is **not** an artifact of trap-curation. Re-examining natural prose with a dedicated owner-lens (rather than inheriting the coarser MET/UNMET-polarity driver rubric) finds it deciding on 25-29% of items — a real, recurring, generalizable minority (antagonist-comeuppance, rescuer/rescued split, opposing-valence-by-owner, multi-owner documents all reproduce independently on fresh corpora the trap-curated eval never touched, including at least 2 patterns — owner/beneficiary split and goal-relative polarity flip — that are near-exact structural matches to the trap-curated eval's own C-primary exemplars, showing these are recurring narrative/journalistic devices, not curator artifacts).
- But **it is not the dominant lever either** — 70.8% of natural items already resolve correctly under a cheap protagonist-default, matching the substrate's own measured first-mention baseline (0.80 on the modern eval). A large dedicated owner-attribution build would be over-engineered for the common case.
- The specific, narrower failure mode that recurs (cross-entity outcome-binding: 16.7%, roughly double the pure-naming-error rate of 8.3%) maps onto machinery the substrate **already owns and has already validated for an adjacent purpose** — the goal-coherence candidate-gen built for the primacy-trap fix (`b1b1ce460`) and the per-owner `GoalOutcomeRegister` built for the affect-state bridge (`f99d1024f`). This is a wiring/reuse exercise (extend candidate-gen from goal-owner SELECTION to outcome SEARCH across entities), consistent with "wire-don't-island" and "reuse the organ" discipline, not a request for a new organ.
- Sequencing matters and is already correctly assumed by the current plan: the real measured data (`OUTCOME_NEVER_TYPED` 6/10 dominating `TIEBREAK_MISS` 1/10) shows owner-attribution's headroom is currently LATENT — mostly invisible because did-it-happen/typing gates block the pipeline before owner-binding is even exercised. Do not build the owner-attribution companion before did-it-happen's referent-extraction fix (`aa890082`, in flight per the BACKUP doc) lands; building it first would test against a starved input and understate its value, repeating the same measurement mistake the did-it-happen build itself is currently correcting for.
- **Cheap-default-suffices is REJECTED as the recommendation** — 2 of the 6 (b)-items (`os_banksy_haringey_campaign`, `os_gangs_arrest_policy`) show cheap majority-mention picking the wrong entity outright, and both are ordinary journalistic patterns (conflict-of-interest reporting, multi-vignette profile pieces), not contrived edge cases — a cheap-default-only product would misread these silently and confidently, which is worse than abstaining.

## Substrate-product implications

A user-facing "did this character get what they wanted" capability that skips a dedicated owner-attribution companion will get roughly 7 in 10 natural passages right by protagonist-default alone, then plateau — and the failures will not look like random noise, they will cluster on specific, common narrative/reporting structures (rescuer-vs-rescued, antagonist-comeuppance, dueling-interest reporting, multi-profile articles) that a careful reader would recognize as EXACTLY the cases where naive "who's talked about most" attribution goes wrong. Because the fix is mostly reuse of already-built organs (goal-coherence candidate-gen + per-owner register) rather than a new mechanism, the marginal build cost is low relative to the correctness gain on a real, recurring 25-29% slice — this changes the earlier framing (from the driver-decomposition note) of owner-attribution as "a big lever that might be a curation artifact" to a more precise one: **a real, moderate, well-understood, cheaply-reusable lever, correctly sequenced as a companion after did-it-happen, not a standalone build.**
