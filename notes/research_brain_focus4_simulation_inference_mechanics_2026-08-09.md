## HEADLINE

The brain does not do simulation and causal inference "inside" the ~4-chunk focus. **The rich situation model is held active in a much larger field of activated long-term memory; the small focus is a fast-updating INDEX/CUE set that is cycled, clause by clause and inference by inference, across that field, retrieving one thing at a time.** Three converging literatures independently establish this as close to consensus, not speculation: Ericsson & Kintsch's (1995) Long-Term Working Memory theory (skilled comprehenders build practiced *retrieval structures* in LTM so a small set of active cues can instantly, reliably address a much larger stored body -- "a significant part of the activated information is stored in LTM and is accessible through retrieval cues in STM," Kintsch & Rawson 2005); McElree's speed-accuracy-tradeoff (SAT) program, which shows the single most-recently-operated-on item needs literally no retrieval operation (~40-50% faster / ~85ms rate advantage, McElree Foraker & Dyer 2003) while everything else -- including material far back in a text, a chess game, or a list of dinner orders -- is retrieved via a *content-addressable, direct-access* operation whose SPEED stays roughly constant regardless of distance (only its success PROBABILITY degrades with interference); and Kintsch's Construction-Integration (CI) model, an actual running computational architecture that processes text in small clause-sized cycles, carries over only a handful of propositions between cycles (recency+importance "leading-edge" selection), and on every coherence break triggers a *reinstatement search* back into the stored text representation to pull ONE bridging proposition into the active buffer.

The number itself decomposes cleanly once Oberauer's (2002, 2013) three-component model is taken seriously, and this is a genuine correction to how "the ~4-chunk focus" should be talked about going forward: there are **three nested layers, not two** -- (1) activated LTM (unbounded, requires a retrieval/search step), (2) a **region of direct access** (~4 chunks, interference-limited not slot-count-limited -- this is what Cowan 2001/2010 calls "the focus of attention"), and (3) a **focus of attention proper** (capacity exactly 1 -- this is McElree's construct, and what Oberauer's model actually names "focus of attention," distinguishing it explicitly and by name from Cowan's ~4-item construct). Causal inference and forward simulation both run as the SAME bounded operation, iterated: the current event sits in the innermost 1-item focus as "now"; a retrieval cue (causal-antecedent query, or a predictive/consequent query) is emitted; a candidate proposition is pulled via content-addressable retrieval from the larger activated field into an adjacent slot in the ~4-item region; it is VALIDATED against world knowledge (Singer et al. 1992's bridging-inference-validation paradigm, with a measurable verification-time signature) rather than accepted passively; if it passes, the "now" pointer advances. Zacks' Event Segmentation Theory and Bar's proactive-brain account show the forward-predictive case is mechanistically identical, just run in the "what happens next" direction using script/schema knowledge instead of a backward causal-antecedent query. No literature surveyed proposes a structurally different architecture (e.g., one big parallel computation holding a whole causal chain at once) -- four independent theoretical traditions (text-comprehension CI models, constructionist inference theory, situation-model/event-indexing theory, event-cognition/predictive-brain theory) converge on the same bounded, serial, retrieve-check-advance picture.

## (1) WHY ~4, and what a "chunk" is -- Miller vs Cowan vs Oberauer/McElree, reconciled

**Miller (1956, *Psychological Review*, "The magical number seven, plus or minus two")**: a chunk is any group of elements that has been recoded (via LTM associations, learned groupings) and is subsequently treated as one unit -- capacity is measured in chunk-COUNT after this recoding has already happened. Miller's own illustration: Morse-code learners progress from counting dits/dahs, to letters, to words, holding a roughly stable ~7 chunks throughout even as information-per-chunk grows. This number is explicitly a POST-RECODING figure -- it conflates a smaller "pure" capacity with strategy-driven gains.

**Cowan (2001, *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* 24:87-185, "The magical number 4 in short-term memory"; Cowan 2010, *Current Directions in Psychological Science* 19:51-57)**: when rehearsal/recoding/chunking strategies are experimentally BLOCKED (concurrent articulatory suppression, unattended-channel probes, running memory span with unpredictable list-ending, change-detection, enumeration/subitizing), a smaller, more stable capacity of ~3-5 (average ~4) emerges. This is what Cowan calls the "focus of attention," embedded inside a larger pool of "activated" long-term memory (his embedded-processes model). Cowan explicitly declines to settle on a single mechanism for WHY ~4, laying out two competing accounts: a *capacity-as-weakness* story (a temporal/neural-cycling constraint -- representations must be reactivated within short cycles or decay/interfere; insufficient neural resources to hold more than ~4 active at once) versus a *capacity-as-strength* story (~4 is an adaptively-tuned optimum -- efficient search granularity, natural beginning-middle-end grouping, tractable exception-tracking). Neither is resolved in the primary literature.

**Oberauer (2002, *JEP:LMC* 28:411-421; Oberauer & Hein 2012, *Current Directions*; Oberauer 2013, *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience* 7:673)** is the theorist who built a model containing BOTH numbers at once, and this resolves what would otherwise look like a live empirical contradiction. Oberauer's three components:
- **Activated LTM** -- everything linked closely enough to be "easy to retrieve" but not currently central.
- **Region of direct access (~4 chunks)** -- "roughly corresponds to the broad focus in Cowan's theory." Critically, Oberauer's own primary-source language (verified via PMC full text) states its capacity "is not a fixed number of items or chunks that can be maintained, but rather arises from interference between temporary bindings" -- i.e. a SOFT, interference-driven limit, not a hard slot count. The paradigm that revealed this (Oberauer's memory-updating/arithmetic task, and Garavan 1998's object-switch-cost paradigm): response latencies scale with the size of the currently-relevant "active set" but stay flat for a "passive set" retained-but-unused, up to the ~4-item region limit; switching which item within the active set is being operated on produces a measurable RT cost, the empirical signature of a further, inner selection mechanism.
- **Focus of attention (capacity exactly 1)** -- and here is the direct, primary-source reconciliation of what would otherwise be a genuine field disagreement (Cowan says "focus of attention" = ~4; McElree and Oberauer say "focus of attention" = 1): Oberauer (2013) states plainly that his focus "corresponds to McElree's single-chunk focus" and is explicitly "different from the capacity limit of the focus of attention in Cowan's theory." Oberauer's resolution is a deliberate renaming move, not an empirical adjudication -- he keeps the term "focus of attention" for McElree's capacity-1 construct and coins "region of direct access" for Cowan's capacity-~4 construct, because both Cowan and McElree/Oberauer independently used the phrase "focus of attention" for two DIFFERENT boxes at two different grain sizes of the same architecture. Jonides et al. (2008, *Annual Review of Psychology* 59:193-224) documents this as a genuine open split in the field (accuracy-discontinuity paradigms point one way, retrieval-latency/SAT paradigms point the other) without adjudicating -- Oberauer's model is the field's best current attempt at reconciling rather than picking a winner.

**Why capacity-1 for the innermost focus, mechanistically (Oberauer 2013, direct quote verified via PMC3797978):** "the focus of attention is typically limited to a single item-context conjunction, but this limitation arises not from a capacity limit in the system, but from FUNCTIONAL considerations: ... the function of the focus of attention is to selectively represent one item and one context, so that the item can be exclusively bound to its context at encoding, and the item can be exclusively selected as output at retrieval." In Oberauer's computational implementation this is enforced by an active-clearing operation: item-layer activation is entirely cleared on completion of each encoding/retrieval; context-layer activation is squashed but not cleared. This is a SELECTION-EXCLUSIVITY design constraint, not a storage-capacity constraint -- a materially different kind of "why" than Cowan's neural/interference story for the outer ~4-item region.

**Bottom line for (1):** "the ~4-chunk focus" as commonly stated conflates Cowan's outer region (interference-limited, ~4, revealed by accuracy-discontinuity paradigms) with a DIFFERENT, inner, capacity-1 selection device (McElree/Oberauer's actual "focus of attention," revealed by retrieval-latency/switch-cost paradigms). Both are real, both are well-evidenced, and they answer different questions ("how much is concurrently accessible without search?" vs "how much is the immediate object of processing/output at once?").

## (2) The focus <-> LTM interplay (the crux)

**Ericsson & Kintsch (1995, *Psychological Review* 102:211-245, "Long-term working memory")**: skilled performance does not extend raw STM capacity -- it builds **retrieval structures**, practiced organizations of retrieval cues held in LTM, that let a small set of currently-active STM cues rapidly and reliably address large, specific bodies of prior content. Evidenced by three classic skilled-memory paradigms: digit-span expert SF (Chase & Ericsson 1982) held a NORMAL ~7 raw items in STM but built a hierarchical retrieval structure (running-time groups -> distance category -> context node -> specific record) that let him regenerate 80+ digits via LOCATIONS, not the digits themselves; expert waiter JC (Ericsson & Polson 1988) used table-seat position as the retrieval organization for ~20 simultaneous dinner orders; chess experts use a board-square-keyed retrieval structure for rapid whole-position encoding. The theory's three principles: (i) new information is elaborated with prior knowledge at encoding, (ii) a separate LTM meta-structure tracks presentation order, (iii) both encoding and retrieval speed approach normal STM speed WITH PRACTICE.

**Kintsch & Rawson (2005, "Comprehension," in *The Science of Reading*, Blackwell -- read in full via lit-scan)** applies this directly to text comprehension and states the crux question's answer explicitly (direct quote): "Working memory, when we are reading a text in a familiar domain, is composed not only of a limited-capacity short-term memory but also includes a long-term component. This component contains all items in the reader's long-term memory that are linked to the current contents of short-term memory (which for this purpose can be equated with consciousness or focus of attention) via retrieval structures. Thus, retrieval structures make available information stored in long-term memory that is directly relevant to the task at hand without the need for time- and resource-consuming retrieval processes." They give the explicit numeric motivation: STM/WM capacity is "known to be quite small, only about four chunks" and reading-span measures are "too low to account for everything a good reader must maintain" -- LTWM exists precisely to close that gap. Their reinterpretation of individual differences in reading span follows directly: high-span readers are not readers with bigger buffers, they are readers with better-elaborated retrieval structures.

**McElree's SAT program (McElree & Dosher 1989, *JEP:General* 118:346-373; McElree 2001, *JEP:LMC* 27:817-835; McElree, Foraker & Dyer 2003, *Journal of Memory and Language* 48:67-91 -- read in full)** supplies the exact QUANTITATIVE cost structure for this interplay, and it is a three-way structure, not a simple "focus fast / everything else slow" binary:
- **Content already in the innermost focus**: no retrieval operation at all -- 40-50% faster SAT dynamics, an ~85ms rate-parameter advantage measured directly in sentence comprehension (McElree Foraker & Dyer 2003, Experiment 2: the cost of a subject NP being "displaced from focal attention by intervening material," their own phrase for exactly this quantity).
- **Content outside the focus but content-addressable** (whether nominally "short-term" or genuinely long-term/textual): retrieved via a DIRECT-ACCESS process whose SPEED (SAT rate and intercept) stays roughly CONSTANT regardless of set size or how much material intervened -- only the PROBABILITY of successful retrieval (asymptotic accuracy) declines as more material accumulates and cue-distinctiveness degrades. McElree's own words (paraphrased from the lit-scan's verified read): "the same timecourse patterns are found in both supra- and sub-span lists... indicating that direct access is a property of both short- and long-term representations" -- the direct empirical bridge to Ericsson & Kintsch's claim that expert LTM retrieval can be just as FAST as ordinary STM retrieval, given a good enough cue.
- **Order/relational information specifically** (e.g. "which item came more recently," not "what is this item"): genuinely SLOW, a true serial search -- both speed AND accuracy decline with distance (McElree & Dosher 1993 measured intercept differences up to 500ms across a six-item list).

**Oberauer's caution against an over-literal "scanning" metaphor**: Oberauer's 2013 model explicitly resists the picture of the focus "sweeping" or "scanning" across a larger store. In his account, activating a context cue in a binding matrix directly retrieves the associated item into the selection state -- content-addressable in the same sense as a hash lookup, not a cursor moving through a list. This matters for the substrate mapping below: "the focus cycles over a larger field" should be read as repeated CUE-TRIGGERED RETRIEVAL, not literal positional scanning.

**Direct answer to the crux question**: the simulation is held in the larger activated-LTM field, NOT inside the small focus. The focus (at whichever of the two nested capacities, ~4 or 1, is relevant to the sub-question) functions as a moving INDEX/CUE SET -- exactly the "moving pointer/window over a larger activated situation model" framing the task asked about, confirmed as the field's dominant reading across all four independently-dispatched lit-scans, with McElree's SAT parameters giving the exact cost shape of the pointer's operation (free-if-already-focal, flat-cost-if-content-addressable-elsewhere, distance-costly-only-for-order-information).

## (3) Dynamics -- entry/exit, chunking, binding

**Attentional refreshing (Barrouillet, Bernardin & Camos 2004, *JEP:General* 133:83-100; Barrouillet, Portrat & Camos 2011, *Psychological Review*; formalized as cognitive load `CL = a*N/T`)**: the Time-Based Resource-Sharing (TBRS) model holds that a single domain-general attentional bottleneck is rapidly time-shared between ongoing processing and **refreshing** -- briefly redirecting attention to each decaying memory trace IN TURN to boost its activation. Refreshing is explicitly SERIAL (one item-context binding at a time), at a rate on the order of tens of milliseconds per item (commonly-cited ballpark ~50ms/item from the Vergauwe/Camos/Barrouillet line -- flagged by the lit-scan as needing exact primary-source page confirmation, though the order of magnitude is convergent across the TBRS literature). Lemaire, Pageot, Plancher & Portrat (2017/2018) modeled the exact temporal ORDER of refreshing and found simple round-robin forward-sweep is too rigid a description -- actual refresh order is more flexible/context-dependent, though still fundamentally serial. Whether items falling out of the actively-refreshed region are lost to pure TIME-based decay or to representation-based INTERFERENCE remains a genuinely unresolved, actively-contested debate in the field (Lewandowsky, Oberauer & Brown 2009, *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 13:120-126, "No temporal decay in verbal short-term memory," vs. Barrouillet, Portrat, Vergauwe, Diependaele & Camos 2011's direct reply defending genuine decay) -- report this as open, not settled.

**Binding**: no single fully-agreed mechanism exists, but four converging lines give a defensible composite picture. Baddeley's episodic buffer (Baddeley 2000, *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 4:417-423; Baddeley 2012, *Annual Review of Psychology*) names the COMPONENT responsible for multimodal binding but leaves the HOW underspecified -- acknowledged as a live weakness of the model. Treisman's Feature Integration Theory lineage (Treisman & Schmidt 1982, *Cognitive Psychology*; Wheeler & Treisman 2002, *JEP:General* 131:48-64) establishes that FOCUSED ATTENTION is required to conjoin separately-registered features into one bound object -- when attention is diverted or overloaded, features from different objects get wrongly recombined ("illusory conjunctions"), and there is a SEPARATE capacity-limited store for the bindings themselves, distinct from and more fragile than the per-feature stores. Oberauer & Lin (2017, *Psychological Review* 124:21-59) give the most computationally explicit current account: binding is a FAST, Hebbian-like, TEMPORARY association of content-to-context (item-to-position or item-to-location), explicitly distinguished from slow long-term SEMANTIC associative binding; capacity in this model emerges from INTERFERENCE BETWEEN COMPETING BINDINGS (similar contexts competing as retrieval cues), not a fixed slot count -- directly consistent with Oberauer's earlier claim that the region-of-direct-access limit is interference-driven. Bays, Catalao & Husain (2009, *Journal of Vision*) show empirically that under load, errors increasingly take the form of "swap errors" (a probed item's feature is reported as belonging to a DIFFERENT stored item) rather than pure loss -- binding integrity degrades before raw item content does. Synchrony-based binding (Singer's oscillatory binding proposal) remains a genuinely contested hypothesis with a serious unanswered critique (Shadlen & Movshon 1999, *Neuron*, "Synchrony Unbound") -- report as one candidate mechanism among several, not established fact, and NOT the load-bearing account for WM chunk binding specifically.

**Recency-default but relevance-overridable**: the retro-cue paradigm (Griffin & Nobre 2003, *Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience* 15:1176-1194, one of two founding papers; comprehensively synthesized in Souza & Oberauer 2016, *Attention, Perception & Psychophysics*, "In search of the focus of attention in working memory: 13 years of the retro-cue effect") shows attention CAN be redirected within an already-encoded WM set to prioritize an older-but-currently-relevant item over a more-recent-but-now-irrelevant one, with benefits plateauing ~300-400ms post-cue. This relevance-driven reprioritization is ADDITIVE with, and dissociable from, the default recency advantage (a distinct "last-item" boost) -- i.e., the active region is recency-biased by default but flexibly, rapidly reorderable by top-down relevance signals, not a rigid FIFO.

**A suggestive (not confirmed) cross-level convergence**: Lisman & Idiart's (1995, *Science*) neural-oscillation model proposes each WM item rides one gamma cycle nested within a slower theta cycle, with capacity set by how many gamma cycles fit in one theta cycle (~4-7 items) -- the same order of magnitude as the behavioral ~50ms/item refresh-rate estimate above. The two literatures were not designed to test the same claim; treat this as an interpretive hypothesis worth watching, not an established fact.

## (4) HOW simulation + causal inference actually run

**Kintsch's Construction-Integration model (Kintsch 1988, *Psychological Review* 95:163-182; Kintsch 1998, *Comprehension: A Paradigm for Cognition*; precursor Kintsch & van Dijk 1978, *Psychological Review* 85:363-394)** is the single most directly relevant existence proof, because it is an actual RUNNING computational architecture for comprehension built explicitly around the small-buffer constraint. Mechanism: text is processed in **cycles keyed to clauses/sentences** (because the WM buffer building the propositional textbase can only hold a few propositions at once). Each cycle has a **construction phase** (loose, "dumb," bottom-up spreading activation -- generates a network of candidate propositions INCLUDING contextually-inappropriate ones, deliberately noisy/promiscuous, no filtering yet) followed by an **integration phase** (a constraint-satisfaction / spreading-activation SETTLING process -- coherent nodes gain activation, incoherent noise from construction loses activation and drops out; this is a relaxation process, not symbolic search). Only a small **carryover buffer** of propositions survives from one cycle to the next, selected by the **"leading-edge rule"** -- favoring the most recent AND most macrostructurally-important propositions from the prior cycle (recency+importance, not pure recency, not a random subset). When incoming text has NO connective/argument overlap with what's currently in the small active buffer (a coherence break), the model triggers a **reinstatement search** back into the accumulated long-term text representation to retrieve an earlier proposition that can bridge the gap, pulling it into the active buffer. (Flag: the exact numeric buffer size could not be pinned to a verified primary-source digit across three independent fetch attempts in this lit-scan -- secondary sources gave inconsistent numbers, "a few"/"a handful" is the safely-attested description; treat any specific digit as unverified.)

**Graesser, Singer & Trabasso's constructionist theory (1994, *Psychological Review* 101:371-395)**: the "search (or effort) after meaning" principle governs WHICH inferences get pulled into the active representation online versus deferred. Online (generated automatically, in the active focus, during reading): inferences needed for local/global coherence and EXPLANATION specifically -- **superordinate goals** and **causal antecedents** of the just-read action/event. Offline (constructed later only if a task demands it): many elaborative inferences (some noun-category instantiations, subordinate-goal detail). **Singer's causal-bridging validation studies (Singer, Halldorson, Lear & Andrusiak 1992, *Journal of Memory and Language* 31:507-524)** show this is not passive acceptance -- reading a cause-effect pair (their example: "Mary poured water on the bonfire. The fire went out.") triggers computation of a mediating causal proposition which is then **actively VALIDATED against world knowledge**, with a measurable verification-time signature (faster for causal than temporal bridges, slower with greater inferential distance or cause/effect order-reversal) -- direct behavioral evidence of an active check operation, not a default-accept.

**Zwaan & Radvansky's event-indexing model (Zwaan, Langston & Graesser 1995, *Psychological Science* 6:292-297; Zwaan & Radvansky 1998, *Psychological Bulletin* 123:162-185)** plus the complementary **resonance model (Myers & O'Brien 1998, *Discourse Processes* 26:131-157)**: readers monitor/index events along five dimensions (time, space, protagonist, causality, intentionality); reading-time cost spikes specifically at DISCONTINUITIES on these dimensions, implying incremental, dimension-specific checking rather than uniform full-model reprocessing on every clause. Myers & O'Brien's resonance model gives the complementary mechanism for the REST of the situation model: most of it is NOT actively searched or reprocessed -- it sits passively activated, available for cheap, automatic "resonance" reactivation proportional to feature overlap with current WM content, with no strategic search required. Only when resonance/overlap-driven access fails (a coherence break) does the more effortful, strategic reinstatement-search machinery (per Kintsch, above) engage.

**Forward simulation is the SAME operation, run forward.** Zacks' Event Segmentation Theory (Zacks, Speer, Swallow, Braver & Reynolds 2007, *Psychological Bulletin* 133:273-293; Reynolds, Zacks & Braver 2007, *Cognitive Science*) holds that comprehenders maintain a working "event model" of the CURRENT event, used to generate perceptual PREDICTIONS about what happens next by retrieving script/schema knowledge about event structure and actors' goals from LTM; a spike in prediction error triggers an event boundary, at which point the event model is reset and updated using both incoming input and freshly-retrieved schema content. Bar's proactive-brain account (Bar 2007, *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 11:280-289; Bar 2009, *Phil Trans Royal Soc B*) states the retrieval-cued-prediction mechanism explicitly and generally: rapidly-extracted coarse input features form an analogical link to an existing memory representation; finding that link ACTIVATES the associations relevant to that analogy in the current context, and that activation constitutes the prediction itself. Ericsson & Kintsch's LTWM (above) is the general-purpose statement of the same shape (small STM cue -> large LTM structure), applied here in the predictive/forward direction rather than the backward-causal-antecedent direction.

**Direct answer**: causal inference and forward simulation both look like **(a)** from the task's framing -- a small, bounded "reasoning register" holds the current event as "now," ITERATIVELY and SERIALLY retrieves one causal link (antecedent OR consequent, depending on query direction) at a time from a much larger, mostly-passively-activated knowledge field into an adjacent slot, checks it for coherence/validity against world knowledge (a real, measurable verification step, not passive acceptance), and advances. No source surveyed across four independent lit-scans proposes a structurally different architecture (a large parallel computation holding a whole causal chain at once). This is not one paper's verbatim thesis -- it is a well-supported synthesis assembled across four independent theoretical traditions (CI-model text comprehension, constructionist inference theory, situation-model/event-indexing theory, event-cognition/predictive-brain theory) that converge without having been designed to test the same unified claim.
