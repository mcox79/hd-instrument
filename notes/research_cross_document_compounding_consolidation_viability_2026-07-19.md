## HEADLINE

**All three lit-scans converge on the same structural answer, and it is more cautious than the initial framing
assumed: cross-document compounding is a real, literature-supported human phenomenon (Documents Model
integration, prior-knowledge-activation transfer, a measured knowledge threshold), but it is GATED by three
independent conditions our own on-disk history already gives us direct evidence to worry about.** (1) Prior
knowledge must be CORRECT, not just present — erroneous prior knowledge measurably *hurts* subsequent
comprehension (Kendeou & van den Broek), so consolidation must filter, not just accumulate. (2) That filter's
confidence signal must be genuinely DECORRELATED from the extractor's own error mode, or it inherits the
extractor's systematic mistakes rather than removing them (self-training/co-training theory) — and CCL is a
directly-relevant, already-observed on-disk instance of exactly this failure (the topical-coherence cue was
systematically, not randomly, anti-correlated with correctness). (3) There is empirical evidence of a
MINIMUM knowledge/skill threshold below which background knowledge and comprehension become uncorrelated, and
of a CONJUNCTIVE (not additive) knowledge-x-skill interaction where strong knowledge alone does not rescue a
low-skill reader on some comprehension measures. Deflated per lit-scan-calibration discipline; a novel-synthesis
composite capped below P<=0.50: **P_deflated = 0.22** for the full cross-document HARD-PASS (lower than a
typical first novel-synthesis cap, because CCL supplies direct on-disk evidence for gate (2)'s hardest failure
mode, not just an abstract literature worry).

## Angle 1 — The Matthew effect / knowledge-guided comprehension (cross-document, the core)

**Mechanism.** Stanovich's (1986) reciprocal-causation model: early decoding fluency -> more successful
reading -> more vocabulary/knowledge -> easier future reading, a genuine bootstrapping loop, not a one-time
head start. Bransford & Johnson (1972) established the moment-to-moment mechanism a schema provides: it
supplies assimilation slots, biases what gets encoded vs. discarded, drives gap-filling inference, and
structures later retrieval — this is WHY prior knowledge should help the *next* thing read, not merely a
correlational story.

**Citation.** Stanovich (1986, *Reading Research Quarterly*); Bransford & Johnson (1972); Britt, Perfetti &
Rouet's Documents Model Framework (1999) and its MD-TRACE task-layer extension (Rouet & Britt, 2011), which
formalizes cross-text integration as building both an integrated situation model AND an intertext
(source-relation) model.

**Implication.** The mechanism is real and well-established, but — critically, per this drill's fresh
lit-scan — the strongest EMPIRICAL demonstrations are almost all within-session multi-document integration
(several texts read together for one task) or single-shot before/after transfer, not a genuine SEQUENTIAL,
3+-document rising curve at matched difficulty. No study located in this scan measures exactly that curve.
This mirrors the CCL note's own honest within-document gap (Angle 3 there) one level up: cross-document
compounding is mechanistically well-motivated but, as a *specific rising-curve measurement*, is an open
question, not an imported settled finding. Building the test is itself the contribution.

## Angle 2 — Consolidating imperfect knowledge (the honest concern at 0.557)

**Mechanism.** Kendeou & van den Broek's (2005, 2007, *Memory & Cognition*) program is the decisive finding
here: readers holding an ERRONEOUS prior belief produce MORE invalid inferences and recall LESS from a
standard (non-refutation) text than readers with no prior belief at all — wrong knowledge does not merely
fail to help, it actively degrades subsequent comprehension relative to a no-knowledge baseline. The fix
requires a specific structural cue: van den Broek's "co-activation" mechanism — the wrong and right
representations must be simultaneously activated (e.g. by an explicit refutation structure) for revision to
occur; absent that cue, the misconception persists and keeps biasing encoding. Beker, Kim, van Boekel, van
den Broek & Kendeou (2019, *Contemporary Educational Psychology*) show the transfer of corrected knowledge to
a genuinely DIFFERENT subsequent text is real but TIME-FRAGILE: it held when the second text followed
immediately, and disappeared when a delay was inserted between the two reading episodes.

**Citation.** Kendeou & van den Broek (2005, 2007); van den Broek (2008, *Applied Cognitive Psychology*,
co-activation mechanism); Beker et al. (2019, *Contemporary Educational Psychology* 56:67-78); Kendeou &
O'Brien's KReC / KReC-MD framework (*Educational Psychology Review*).

**Implication.** This settles the "does the brain consolidate everything or filter" question decisively in
favor of FILTER: consolidating unfiltered, possibly-wrong extractions is worse than not consolidating at all,
not merely suboptimal. It directly validates the 07-18 note's schema-fit-gated consolidation design as
necessary, not optional, and adds a NEW consideration this drill contributes: the transfer window may be
brittle to elapsed distance between doc-1 and doc-2 (both in humans and plausibly in any "staleness" analog
on the substrate — foundation entries consolidated long before doc-2 is read may behave differently from
freshly-consolidated ones; untested here, flagged for a future drill).

## Angle 3 — The specific cross-document mechanisms (entity/relation, vocabulary, schema)

**Mechanism.** The lit-scan found strong support for (c) SCHEMA carry (Documents Model integration; O'Reilly,
Wang & Sabatini's knowledge-threshold result, Angle 4 below) and (b) VOCABULARY-adjacent carry (well-replicated
within-passage repetition priming, persisting across intervening text and larger for low-frequency words) —
but a genuine, flagged GAP for (a) ENTITY/RELATION carry specifically ACROSS separate documents: no study was
located that measures a named entity or fact learned in text A demonstrably speeding recognition or resolving
ambiguity for the SAME entity/fact appearing in a DIFFERENT text B. The ambiguity-resolution literature
(homograph/lexical-ambiguity studies) is entirely within-single-passage.

**Citation.** Repetition-priming-during-reading literature (Psychonomic Bulletin & Review 2017; Memory &
Cognition 2012 — within-passage only); Documents Model Framework (as Angle 1); O'Reilly, Wang & Sabatini
(2019, *Psychological Science*, n=3,534) — see Angle 4.

**Implication.** Of the three candidate carries, SCHEMA is the best-evidenced and (per the reasoning-map /
factorization already on the substrate) the most buildable: the reader's already-compositional readout can
score a candidate binding's fit against a durable, cross-document schema/relation store exactly as the CCL
note's document-coherence feature scored fit against a within-document Tier-2 bundle — the difference this
time is the reference structure is the STRUCTURED role/relation store, not a topical centroid (the specific
fix VET's redirect already demanded), and it persists ACROSS documents via Tier 3 rather than being flushed
at document end. ENTITY/RELATION carry is the second most buildable (the reasoning-map already carries typed
relations) but has no direct human literature precedent either way — a second genuinely novel measurement,
like Angle 1's curve. VOCABULARY carry is the cheapest but weakest lever (a grounded word is already known;
the marginal cross-document gain is small once a word is in WordNet).

## Angle 4 — The reader-precision floor (decisive for viability)

**Mechanism.** Two convergent-but-distinct findings bound viability. First, O'Reilly, Wang & Sabatini (2019,
*Psychological Science*, n=3,534 high-schoolers, broken-line regression): background-knowledge score has a
measured INFLECTION POINT at approximately 59% correct — BELOW that threshold, background knowledge and
comprehension outcome are statistically UNCORRELATED; ABOVE it, comprehension rises with knowledge. Second,
O'Reilly & McNamara (2007, *AERJ*, n=1,651): science knowledge and reading skill show a CONJUNCTIVE
interaction on multiple-choice comprehension — neither knowledge nor skill alone had a major effect UNLESS
the learner had high levels of BOTH; reading skill benefited high-knowledge students more than low-knowledge
students (skill amplifies knowledge, not the reverse, on this measure). Neither of these establishes a
"poor comprehenders can't compound" result cleanly (Protopapas, Sideridis, Mouzaki & Simos 2011 casts doubt on
whether the classic fan-spread pattern replicates at all, plausibly a measurement-floor/ceiling artifact,
unverified beyond secondary summary) — but both are independent, quantified findings that knowledge-driven
facilitation is THRESHOLD-GATED and possibly AND-gated with a separate skill factor, not a free additive
bonus at any skill level.

**The self-training/co-training literature adds the THIRD, most load-bearing constraint, and it is not
abstract for us — CCL already demonstrated it directly.** Confidence-based filtering of a mediocre extractor's
output does not reliably raise the filtered subset's accuracy; what governs "helps vs. hurts" is whether the
extractor's errors are RANDOM/independent (self-training / weak-supervision aggregation can average these out
even from noisy sources) or SYSTEMATIC/correlated (self-training entrenches these — a 2026 theoretical
framing splits this explicitly into a denoising term vs. a signal-forgetting term, with self-generated
systematic bias being *more* damaging than uniform random noise of the same magnitude; treat the exact
theorem as unverified, the qualitative split as well-supported across the confirmation-bias literature more
broadly). Co-training theory (Blum & Mitchell 1998; Balcan et al.'s weaker expansion-assumption relaxation)
gives the fix: a second filtering signal helps only to the degree it is genuinely error-DECORRELATED from the
first — a second signal drawn from the same underlying feature family gives little real protection (Nigam &
Ghani 2000; Du et al. 2011: real-world view pairs usually violate strict independence, and protection degrades
in proportion to how correlated the views' errors actually are).

**Why this is not hypothetical for us:** CCL's own topical-coherence cue is a textbook instance of the
"systematic, correlated error" regime the ML literature calls the dangerous one — it did not fail randomly,
it consistently substituted a topically-salient wrong answer for the correct one (3/3), i.e., its errors were
CORRELATED WITH its own confidence, not independent of it. The substrate's coherence gate (schema-fit +
local-surprise) is architecturally closer to the LCCP's own scoring features than an independently-sourced
signal would be — it is not yet established that it is decorrelated enough from the LCCP's own error mode to
serve as a safe consolidation filter. This is the single most important calibration fact this drill
contributes: **the precision-floor question is not just "is 0.557 high enough" — it is "is the confidence
signal we would use to filter for consolidation independent enough of the extractor's own mistakes," and we
already have one on-disk data point (CCL) suggesting the answer can be no for at least one candidate signal.**

**Citation.** O'Reilly, Wang & Sabatini (2019, *Psychological Science*); O'Reilly & McNamara (2007, *AERJ*
44:161-196); Protopapas, Sideridis, Mouzaki & Simos (2011, unverified beyond title/abstract); Blum & Mitchell
(1998, COLT); Balcan et al. (weaker independence relaxation); Nigam & Ghani (2000); a 2026 self-training
denoising-vs-signal-forgetting theoretical paper (title/abstract only, mechanism plausible not independently
re-derived).

**Implication.** Compounding is not flatly blocked at 0.557 — 0.557 sits numerically close to, and just above,
O'Reilly/Sabatini's ~59% inflection point (flagged explicitly as a striking but UNVERIFIED coincidence: their
measure is a background-knowledge test score in a different population on a different task, not our reader's
extraction precision; do not treat this as a literal transfer of their threshold). What IS directly actionable
is the co-training lesson: before trusting ANY confidence signal to filter consolidation-eligible extractions,
check whether that signal's errors are correlated with the extractor's own error mode (the CCL failure
pattern) or genuinely independent of it. If the only available filter is architecturally entangled with the
extractor (as CCL's cue was), cross-document consolidation risks reproducing CCL's exact failure at the
consolidation step instead of the reading step — this is the most likely single point of failure for the
whole redirect, and it is cheaply checkable before building anything new (see Cheap decisive test).

## Angle 5 — DESIGN VERDICT: a glass-box cross-document compounding test

**Ranked brain mechanism (name it): THRESHOLD-GATED, DECORRELATED-CONFIDENCE SCHEMA CONSOLIDATION.** Read
doc-1 -> filter extractions by a confidence signal proven (not assumed) to be error-decorrelated from the
extractor's own mistakes -> consolidate the structured (role/relation, not topical) survivors into a durable
cross-document store -> read doc-2 with that store available as a schema/entity prior -> measure whether
doc-2 comprehension rises, using a control that specifically rules out the generic order/practice-effect
confound that killed CCL.

**Concrete design, composing existing pieces (no new store invented):**

1. **Read doc-1** with the existing stacked reader (LCCP + arg/adjunct + quotative), patient-lens 0.557
   baseline, unchanged.
2. **Filter for consolidation-eligibility** using the coherence gate's existing schema-fit + local-surprise
   score, PLUS a genuinely independent second view before trusting it as a filter: redundancy across
   multiple independent local contexts within doc-1 (does the same relation/entity get extracted from >=2
   separate sentences/mentions, a same-document corroboration check) crossed with WordNet-grounded lexical
   plausibility (an external resource, not derived from the parser's own scoring) — this is the
   Blum-Mitchell/Balcan "second view" the co-training literature says is required, chosen specifically
   because it is NOT built from the same construction-cue features the LCCP and coherence gate already
   share.
3. **Consolidate** the doubly-filtered (schema-fit-gated AND redundancy/lexical-corroborated) survivors as
   STRUCTURED role/relation entries (subject-verb-object-typed, per VET's explicit redirect caveat), not a
   topical centroid, into the durable Tier-3/Frontier-2 foundation (reusing the 07-18 note's consolidation
   design, not re-inventing it).
4. **Read doc-2** on a related topic, with the consolidated store available as a schema/entity prior for
   disambiguation (WITH-consolidation arm) vs. not (NO-CARRY control).
5. **The CCL-lesson control (the critical addition):** a THIRD arm reads doc-2 with knowledge consolidated
   from an UNRELATED, topically-irrelevant doc-1 — same amount of "reading practice" and consolidation
   machinery exercised, zero relevant content. This isolates genuine content-driven knowledge-carry from a
   generic warm-up/practice effect, which is exactly the confound CCL's own arm-A control exposed for the
   within-document claim. Any doc-2 improvement must beat BOTH the no-carry control AND the
   irrelevant-consolidation control to count as real compounding.

## The FAIR can-fail test (full specification)

**Real baseline:** doc-2 read cold, by the exact same stacked reader (LCCP + arg/adjunct + quotative +
coherence gate), with no consolidated cross-document store at all — the system as it already exists, not a
strawman.

**Can-fail (both directions):** consolidation can hurt, not just fail to help — if the double-filter (schema-
fit + redundancy/lexical) still lets through a systematically-wrong structured relation (the CCL failure mode,
recurring one level up), doc-2 precision could go DOWN relative to the no-carry control, exactly as CCL's
topical cue actively made things worse rather than merely not helping.

**One variable per arm:**
- Arm 1 (no-carry control): doc-2 cold, no consolidated store.
- Arm 2 (irrelevant-consolidation control, the CCL-lesson addition): doc-2 with a store built from an
  unrelated doc-1 — isolates generic practice/warm-up effects.
- Arm 3 (with-consolidation): doc-2 with a store built from a topically-related doc-1, double-filtered per
  step 2 above.

**Held-out:** doc-2 gold annotations produced independently of the substrate's own consolidation decisions
(never self-graded); doc-1/doc-2 pairs chosen so Arm 3's doc-1 is topically related to doc-2 while Arm 2's
doc-1 is matched in length/difficulty but topically unrelated (a genuine difficulty-matched, not just
random, negative control).

**Measured:** (a) doc-2 patient-lens precision, Arm 3 vs Arm 1 vs Arm 2; (b) for any items where a
consolidated entry was actually consulted (traceable, since every consolidation entry is an inspectable
structured relation, not a black-box weight), whether consultation correlates with a correct or incorrect
disambiguation — this is the direct, cheap check of whether the filter is doing its job on the items that
actually used it.

**HARD-PASS:** Arm 3 beats BOTH Arm 1 and Arm 2 by a real margin (>=0.03-0.05 absolute precision, the
project's standard small-to-medium effect bar, matched to the size of the quotative lever's own +0.042 gain)
on doc-2, replicated across >=3 doc-1/doc-2 pairs — genuine, content-specific cross-document compounding, not
a generic order effect (which Arm 2 would also show if present).

**HARD-FAIL:** Arm 3 does not beat Arm 2 (i.e., any apparent gain is explained by generic
practice/consolidation-machinery exercise, not topically-relevant content — the exact CCL failure pattern
recurring at the consolidation layer), OR Arm 3 UNDERPERFORMS Arm 1 (consolidation actively hurts, the more
serious negative, directly paralleling CCL's Axis 1 "the cue hurts precision" result) — either would mean
cross-document consolidation, as designed here, is not the answer either, and the honest fallback is that
compounding requires either a cleaner base reader or a genuinely better-decorrelated filtering signal than
anything currently on the substrate.

## Cheap decisive test (do this FIRST, before building anything new)

Additive-only, zero new compute, reusing already-collected eval data:

1. **Compute the correlation between the coherence gate's existing confidence score and gold correctness** on
   the ALREADY-EXISTING patient-precision eval set. This directly answers the Angle-4 question before building
   anything: if confidence and correctness are uncorrelated or (worse) anti-correlated — as CCL's topical cue
   was — that is a decisive, free, early kill signal for using THAT SPECIFIC signal as a consolidation filter,
   exactly mirroring the free re-bucketing check the 07-18 note already proposed for CLS replay allocation.
2. **Separately compute the same correlation for the proposed redundancy/lexical-corroboration second view**
   (step 2 of the design above) on the same existing data, to check it is actually LESS correlated with the
   coherence gate's own errors than the coherence gate is with itself — the direct, cheap test of the
   co-training decorrelation requirement, before trusting it as an independent filter.

If either check shows the confidence signal is anti-correlated with correctness (CCL's pattern), STOP before
building the cross-document harness — the honest next step is finding or building a better-decorrelated filter
signal, not proceeding to the full test with a filter already known to be unsafe.

## Falsifiable predictions — HARD-PASS / HARD-FAIL

**Prediction A (the confidence-filter is usable at all).** P=0.35 (deflated; CCL is direct prior evidence
against a naive version of this). HARD-PASS: coherence-gate confidence correlates positively with gold
correctness on the existing eval set (Spearman/Pearson >=0.15, a modest but real signal). HARD-FAIL:
correlation is null or negative — directly recurring CCL's finding one level up, meaning the current
confidence signal cannot safely gate consolidation without a genuinely independent second view.

**Prediction B (the second view is decorrelated enough to help — the co-training condition).** P=0.30
(deflated; this is the drill's own untested synthesis). HARD-PASS: the redundancy/lexical-corroboration
signal's errors are measurably less correlated with the coherence gate's errors than the coherence gate is
with itself (a real decorrelation gap, not just "different in name"), AND the double-filtered subset shows
higher gold-correctness rate than either single filter alone. HARD-FAIL: the two signals' errors are highly
correlated (both derived close enough to the same underlying construction features) — meaning no safe filter
currently exists on the substrate for this purpose, a serious negative independent of the cross-document
question.

**Prediction C (cross-document compounding, the composite claim).** P=0.22 (deflated furthest; composes
Predictions A and B plus a genuinely untested human-literature gap per Angle 1/3). HARD-PASS/HARD-FAIL as
specified in the FAIR can-fail test above (Arm 3 vs Arm 1 vs Arm 2).

## Brain-check (outcome not pre-assumed)

**Cross-document knowledge-guided comprehension is a real, well-established human capability** (Stanovich's
Matthew effect, Documents Model integration, Kendeou's knowledge-revision program) — this is not a case of
the brain lacking the mechanism.

**Where the brain-check reveals a real, shared structural bound (same-limit, accept):** the brain itself does
NOT compound freely from noisy knowledge — erroneous prior knowledge measurably HURTS subsequent comprehension
(Kendeou & van den Broek), and knowledge-driven facilitation is THRESHOLD-GATED (O'Reilly/Sabatini) and
possibly CONJUNCTIVELY gated with a separate skill factor (O'Reilly & McNamara) rather than a free bonus at
any skill level. A substrate at 0.557 precision attempting unfiltered cross-document consolidation would be
reproducing exactly the failure mode the human literature documents for readers with erroneous prior beliefs
— this is not a substrate-specific weakness, it is the SAME bound biology hits, and the fix biology uses
(refutation-structure-triggered co-activation, i.e., an explicit conflict-detection step before revision) is
the direct precedent for why filtering-before-consolidating is mandatory, not an extra safety margin.

**Where the brain may NOT face our specific second problem, or faces it differently:** the co-training
decorrelation requirement (our filter's confidence must be independent of our extractor's error mode) does
not have a clean biological analog surfaced in this scan — biological schema-consistency checking
(prelimbic mPFC gating per the 07-18 note's Tse citations) may or may not be architecturally decorrelated
from the hippocampal encoding process it gates; this scan did not find literature directly addressing whether
the brain's OWN consolidation-eligibility signal is provably independent of its OWN encoding errors. Flag as
open, not resolved either way — a plausible substrate-specific risk (glass-box signals built from shared
feature families are easier to accidentally correlate) rather than a confirmed brain-analog.

**The one genuinely open honest risk:** no literature located, in either direction, measures a 3+-document
rising comprehension curve at matched difficulty (Angle 1) or entity/relation-specific cross-document carry
(Angle 3) — if Prediction C HARD-FAILs, this would not contradict an established human finding (none exists
to contradict); it would be the first direct test of this specific question, informative regardless of
outcome, exactly as the CCL note framed its own within-document Prediction 2.

## Substrate-product implications

Not a publication angle — a build-priority and honesty angle. If Prediction A HARD-FAILs at the cheap
decisive test (near-zero cost), that is the single most valuable outcome this drill can produce: it stops a
larger build before it starts, and redirects effort toward finding/building a genuinely decorrelated
confidence signal rather than assuming the coherence gate is safe to reuse for consolidation-gating. If
Predictions A and B both HARD-PASS but C HARD-FAILs, the product story becomes "the substrate can safely
filter its own knowledge for consolidation" (a real, differentiated auditability claim — every filtering
decision is an inspectable score, not a black box) without yet a proven "gets better as it reads more"
capability — an honest, sellable partial result. If C HARD-PASSes, the full claim ("reading document N+1 is
measurably easier because of documents 1..N, and every step of why is inspectable") is a genuine
differentiator against black-box context-window or fine-tuning-based competitors. If C HARD-FAILs the way
CCL did (order effect indistinguishable from content-carry), the honest fallback is: compounding via
consolidation is not yet demonstrated at 0.557 precision with current signals, and the priority should shift
to either (i) a precision-improvement push before revisiting compounding, or (ii) hunting for a genuinely
independent second filtering view (a different feature family entirely, not an architectural variant of the
existing coherence gate).
