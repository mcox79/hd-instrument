## HEADLINE

The RAW McGuffey texts (not the cleaned corpus we staged for reading) contain a real, escalating, five-book teaching apparatus — front-matter teacher instructions, per-lesson vocabulary lists, per-lesson definitions, per-lesson comprehension questions, and a fixed-interval review schedule — and **our cleaning pipeline strips essentially all of it from the graded (2nd-4th) corpus**, confirmed by direct raw-vs-cleaned diff below. The apparatus does **not** escalate monotonically: it is vocabulary-list-heavy in Primer/1st/2nd, **disappears almost entirely in the 3rd Reader**, then reappears in the 4th Reader as a *different* mechanism (selective end-of-lesson definitions + comprehension questions, not exhaustive head-of-lesson word lists). Read as a method for the teacher, this is a genuine "fade exhaustive pre-teaching, introduce selective glossing + active comprehension-checking" curriculum arc — a concrete, historically-validated instance of the scaffolding-fade-with-competence principle the companion pedagogy lit-scan already flagged more abstractly (Fountas & Pinnell gradients, Gradual Release of Responsibility). Two genuinely new, concrete, actionable numbers come out of primary-source mining that the secondary lit-scan did not have: **~6 new lexical items per lesson at the novice stage** (the Primer's own stated pacing) and **~1.7-2.2x new-word-type growth per grade-book** (measured from the corpus's own `n_word_types`: 453 -> 1005 -> 1921 -> 3176 -> 6111). The oral-elocution/articulation/diacritical apparatus is present in all five books' front matter and is correctly excluded as non-transferable (decoding, not comprehension). The comprehension-question apparatus exists in useful, human-authored form **only in the 4th Reader** (43 blocks, ~150-200 questions) — real but narrow, and only ~25-35% of those questions are clean single-answer argument-structure ("who/what did X to Y") questions; the rest are recall/retell, vocabulary/figurative-meaning, or opinion/moral questions that do not reduce to a role-filler check. No answer key is given anywhere — expected answers must be hand-derived from the lesson text.

## Part 1 — What is actually IN the raw books (catalog with real quotes, per book)

### 1a. Front-matter "how to read" / teacher-method instructions

- **Primer preface:** *"Only about six new words are to be mastered in each lesson. These new words and the new elementary sounds are always to be found in the vocabulary of the lesson in which they are first used."* — explicit per-lesson vocabulary-introduction RATE, stated by the authors themselves, not inferred.
- **First Reader, "Suggestions to Teachers" (explicit method menu):** four named methods the teacher chooses/combines from — **I. Phonic Method** (teach elementary sounds/letters first, then word formation from those sounds); **III. Word Method** (teach whole words at the head of each lesson by sight first, defer letter/spelling instruction); **IV. Word + Phonic Combined** (teach words as words first, then layer phonics once several lessons are read). *"Having read a few lessons in this manner, begin to use the Phonic Method, combining it with the Word Method."* This is a genuine multi-strategy-with-diagnostic-switching design, not a single fixed pipeline.
- **First Reader preface, principle 2:** *"A proper gradation has been carefully preserved. All new words are placed at the head of each lesson, to be learned before the lesson is read... All words in these vocabularies are used in the text immediately following."* — a **100%-coverage guarantee**: every pre-taught word is guaranteed to occur in the very next passage (no orphaned pre-teaching).
- **Second Reader preface:** *"All new words are given at the head of each lesson. When these are mastered, the main difficulties left for the pupil are those of expression. In the latter portion of the book the simpler derivatives... are omitted if the primitive word has been given."* — an explicit **lemma-dedup rule**: once the root/primitive form is taught, its morphological derivatives (plurals, possessives, verb forms) are NOT re-listed as "new." Also: *"If the pupil is not familiar with the diacritical marks, he should be carefully drilled... until the marked letter instantly suggests the correct sound. He is then prepared to study his reading lessons without any assistance from the teacher."* — an explicit **automate-the-low-level-skill-to-fluency-first** principle: decoding must reach near-automatic mastery before the pupil is expected to self-manage comprehension.
- **Third Reader preface:** *"Spelling exercises are continued through the first half of the THIRD READER... In the latter half of the book, definitions are introduced. It is hoped that the teacher will extend this defining exercise to all the words of the lesson liable to be misunderstood. The child should define the word in his own language sufficiently to show that he has a mastery of the word in its use."* — a **production/paraphrase comprehension check** (define-in-your-own-words), not mere recognition. NOTE (data-provenance caveat): this Project Gutenberg transcription of the Third Reader does **not** actually carry the per-lesson spelling/definition apparatus the preface describes — verified by direct inspection of Lessons I, II, XXI (no word list, no definitions block present in the raw digitized text at all). This is a **transcription gap in the source digitization**, not an artifact of our own cleaning pipeline — flagged honestly rather than silently working around it.
- **Fourth Reader preface:** *"It can not be presumed that every pupil has at hand all the works of reference necessary... hence all the aids that seem requisite... have been given. Brief notices concerning the various authors represented have been inserted; the more difficult words have been defined... and short explanatory notes have been given wherever required for a full understanding of the text."* — explicitly **selective** ("more difficult," "wherever required"), not exhaustive — a deliberate shift in method from the earlier books.

### 1b. Per-lesson vocabulary/spelling lists (which words are pre-taught before each lesson)

- **Primer / First Reader:** two-line head-of-lesson block — line 1 = new WHOLE WORDS, line 2 = new LETTERS/phonic elements, e.g. Primer Lesson I: `a and cat rat` / `a e d n r t`; Lesson VI: `dog Rab fat Nat's` / `o b g`. Counts stay ~4-9 items/lesson throughout, consistent with the preface's "about six" claim. This IS phonics apparatus (decoding) but the WORD half of it is a genuine pre-teach vocabulary list independent of the phonics half.
- **Second Reader:** head-of-lesson list of whole words with syllabification + accent/diacritical marks only (no bare letters), e.g. Lesson I: `news'paper cold or'der seem through / stock'ings chat sto'ry light Har'ry / branch'es kiss burns Mrs. e vents' / an oth'er Mr. stool lamp mends` — 20 items pre-taught before "EVENING AT HOME," a ~340-word passage. This is the item our cleaner strips (confirmed, Part 2 below).
- **Third Reader:** absent from this transcription (see caveat above) despite the preface's claim.
- **Fourth Reader:** absent — no head-of-lesson list at all. Vocabulary support has moved entirely to end-of-lesson selective **DEFINITIONS**, keyed by paragraph number, e.g. after "Perseverance": *"4. En-tan'gled, twisted in, disordered. 5. As-sist'-ance, help, aid. Dis-en-gaged, cleared, set free. 6. Grass'plot, a space covered with grass. Dig'ni-ty, majestic manner..."* Only words judged likely-to-be-misunderstood are glossed, not every new word.

### 1c. Comprehension questions after lessons

Present, in useful form, **only in the Fourth Reader** (43 `EXERCISES.--` blocks found across ~89 lessons; Primer/First/Second/Third have none). No answer key is ever given — questions are meant for the teacher to pose orally and judge the answer live. Representative real examples, spanning the range of question types found:

- Clean argument-structure ("who/what did X to Y"): *"What did Ned like? What did he not like?"* / *"What did the frost say? What did he do to the mountain? The trees? The lake?"* / *"How did Robinson Crusoe make a house? Of what did he make a chair and table? How did he obtain boards?"* / *"Whom did she ask to help her?"* (Jenny/Andrew lesson) — these reduce directly to agent-verb-object / agent-verb-recipient role-filler checks.
- Recall/retell (open-ended, no single short answer): *"Relate the story of the jeweler and his diamonds."* / *"Repeat the boys' story of the sleigh ride. The teacher's story."*
- Vocabulary / figurative-meaning: *"What is meant by 'dancing eyes' in the second stanza?"* / *"Can you define tomorrow?"* / *"What is meant by the phrase 'to apply himself'... 'a generous manhood'... 'expansion of soul'... 'good will'"* (this last block, from a lesson late in the book, shows the QUESTIONS themselves escalating toward abstract/figurative interpretation as the book progresses — an intra-book difficulty ramp, not just inter-book).
- Opinion/moral judgment: *"Is it right to take revenge on those who injure us?"* / *"Do you think such a man would follow the Golden Rule?"*
- Expository/general-knowledge (in the book's nonfiction lessons — coffee, giraffes, lions, wild horses): *"What country first supplied coffee? How did the plant come to be grown in other countries?"* — factual recall against expository content, not narrative argument-structure.

Rough categorization across all ~43 blocks (hand-tallied, not automated — treat as approximate): **~25-35%** clean single-answer argument-structure questions; **~15%** open retell/summary (no single short answer); **~20%** vocabulary/figurative-meaning; **~20-25%** opinion/moral/judgment; **~10-15%** expository general-knowledge; a small residual (<2%) is meta/mechanical (e.g. punctuation-mark questions).

### 1d. Review/repetition structure

- **Primer:** explicit `LESSON V.--REVIEW`, `X`, `XV`, `XX`, `XXV`, `XXX`, `XXXV`, `XL`, `XLV` — a **mechanical fixed interval: every 5th lesson**, each one a cumulative recombination of vocabulary from the preceding four lessons (no new words introduced).
- **First Reader:** `LESSON IX. REVIEW`, `XVI`, `XXVII`, `XXXIII`, `XLVI` — irregular gaps (8, 7, 11, 6, 13 lessons) — review is content-driven, not fixed-interval, once vocabulary volume grows.
- **Second/Third/Fourth Readers:** no explicit `REVIEW`-labeled lessons at all. Reinforcement becomes **implicit**, carried by recurring named characters and recurring vocabulary across ordinary lessons rather than a dedicated cumulative-recap lesson (this matches the already-staged corpus stats: 124/157/291 recurring names across the 2nd/3rd/4th readers, growing with book length).

### 1e. Concrete introduction-order / escalation numbers (measured directly from the corpus's own metadata, cross-checked against the staging note)

| Book | new word-TYPES (cumulative-book) | growth ratio vs prior book | words/passage | mean sentence length | apparatus present |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Primer | 453 | -- | 67 | 10.2-10.5 | word+letter list, review every 5 lessons |

The **~1.7-2.2x new-vocabulary-type growth per grade-step** is a concrete, empirically-derived pacing ratio from a text corpus that was deliberately hand-graded by working 19th-century teachers for exactly this purpose — a real number to weigh against the companion pedagogy scan's more abstract Hirsch/Fountas-Pinnell "sequence by knowledge-dependency" recommendation.

## Part 2 — Raw-vs-cleaned diff (confirms the cleaning-pipeline hypothesis, mixed result)

Direct diff performed on disk (not inferred):

- **Grade-1 corpus (Primer, First Reader)** — `data/corpora/graded_readers_grade1/cleaned/*.clean.txt`: the cleaner strips PG boilerplate/license/preface/alphabet-chart front matter (104 and 166 lines respectively) but **KEEPS the per-lesson word+letter lists inline** in the cleaned narrative stream (verified: Primer `# LESSON 1` in the cleaned file still shows `a and cat rat` / `a e d n r t` before the sentences, byte-for-byte the same as raw).
- **Graded corpus (2nd/3rd/4th)** — `data/corpora/graded_readers_graded/cleaned/*.clean.txt`, cleaned via `clean_gutenberg.py` per the staging note (`notes/graded_reader_corpus_staging_mcguffey_2nd_3rd_4th_wild_text_composition_2026-07-18.md`), which explicitly documents stripping "per-lesson DEFINITIONS/EXERCISES/NOTES apparatus + phonics vocab rows + running headers": **confirmed by diff.** Second Reader Lesson I's raw head-of-lesson list (`news'paper cold or'der seem through...`) is **absent** from the cleaned file, which opens directly on `EVENING AT HOME. / It is winter...`. Fourth Reader: zero `DEFINITIONS`/`EXERCISES` string matches anywhere in the 280KB cleaned file, versus 43 `EXERCISES` + ~35 `DEFINITIONS` blocks in the 388KB raw file.

**Practical implication for the teacher (not a substrate-ingestion change):** the two cleaning pipelines are inconsistent (grade-1 keeps the list inline, graded strips it entirely), and neither currently extracts the apparatus into a reusable form. Per the USER's reframe — the substrate does not need this apparatus in its reading stream — the correct move is **not** to change what the substrate reads, but to extract the apparatus (word lists, definitions, exercises, review-lesson markers) into a **separate per-book/per-lesson sidecar** (e.g. `data/corpora/graded_readers_graded/apparatus/<book>.apparatus.json`, keyed by lesson number) for the teacher's own use in curriculum planning and comprehension-check authoring, leaving the narrative-only cleaned corpus untouched for the substrate. This is a cheap, low-risk, purely-additive extraction (no re-cleaning of existing staged data required) — flagged as a buildable next step, not yet done.

## Part 3 — Distilled strategies for the TEACHER (ranked; deflated; novel-synthesis P<=0.50)

Reframed per USER: these are recommendations for how the Director should structure the substrate's curriculum, pacing, corrections, and comprehension-checks — not claims about what the substrate itself should read.

1. **Vocabulary pre-grounding curriculum, with two concrete numbers** — P(useful as a starting pacing policy for corpus/curriculum design) ~0.45. (a) Rate-limit new-term introduction per curriculum unit to a small, fixed budget at the novice stage — McGuffey's own stated figure is ~6 new lexical items per lesson; treat this as a starting anchor for "how many new grounded terms per ingestion chunk is safe," not a hard constant. (b) Guarantee 100% forward-coverage: never pre-teach/flag a term that doesn't occur in the immediately following material (First Reader's explicit design rule) — directly actionable as a corpus-curation check. (c) Dedup by lemma, not surface form, when counting "new" vocabulary (Second Reader's derivative-omission rule) — already implicit in most tokenization but worth an explicit audit. Ties directly to and sharpens (does not duplicate) lever #2 in the companion pedagogy scan (curriculum ordering by knowledge-dependency).

2. **Fade exhaustive pre-teaching -> selective glossing + active comprehension-checking as competence grows** — P(this arc is a valid model for the substrate's OWN curriculum staging, i.e., early corpus tiers get exhaustive term-grounding, later tiers get selective/contextual grounding plus a comprehension check instead) ~0.40, deflated for cross-domain (human classroom vs. machine training-tier) translation uncertainty. This is the single cleanest NEW structural finding from primary-source mining (the secondary lit-scan's Fountas-Pinnell/GRR levers named the *general* principle abstractly; this gives it a concrete four-stage historical instance: exhaustive word-list -> syllabified word-list -> apparatus gap (3rd Reader, arguably a "bridge" grade) -> selective end-of-lesson definitions + comprehension questions).

3. **Automate the cheap/low-level extraction step to near-ceiling fluency before layering a harder step on top** — P(directly informs sequencing of substrate-build priorities) ~0.35. Transliterating the Second Reader's "diacritical-mastery unlocks working independently of the teacher" principle: the general form is real and matches the project's own already-observed pattern (structural signals work / semantic signals fail, per `[[project_reading_axis_structural_beats_semantic_2cgs_reader_0557_2026-07-19]]`) — get the cheap, reliable layer (NER/coref/structural extraction) to saturation BEFORE spending build effort on the harder layer that depends on it (discourse/semantic reasoning), rather than developing both simultaneously. This is a scheduling/prioritization heuristic for the teacher, not a new mechanism.

4. **Multi-method-with-diagnostic-switching, not one fixed pipeline** — P(applicable as a design stance, already informally practiced) ~0.30. The First Reader's "Suggestions to Teachers" names FOUR interchangeable/combinable teaching methods and instructs the teacher to switch or combine based on what the specific pupil needs. Weak/soft lever — mostly a retrospective validation that combining structural + other signal types (already the project's practice) has 19th-century pedagogical precedent, not a new build item.

5. **Cumulative spaced review as the correction/consolidation mechanism, in place of per-error diagnosis** — P(informs how the teacher schedules re-exposure) ~0.30. IMPORTANT GAP, flagged honestly: the books give explicit teacher guidance for correcting *decoding/articulation* errors (out of scope) but **no explicit guidance for correcting comprehension errors** — the closest analog is the fixed-interval REVIEW lesson (Primer: every 5th lesson) which re-exposes recent vocabulary/content wholesale rather than diagnosing and patching a specific misunderstanding. This is a real absence in the source method, not a missed extraction — worth naming so it isn't silently assumed to exist. The recommendation for the teacher: since the substrate (unlike a human pupil) CAN be diagnostically probed cheaply and losslessly, prefer the modern comprehension-monitoring/coherence-gate approach (already built, per the companion pedagogy scan's strongest convergence finding) over reviving the blunter spaced-review-only mechanism — the historical method's cadence (fixed-interval) is worth noting as a fallback/backstop schedule, not a replacement for targeted error-checking.

6. **Comprehension questions as an independent, human-authored eval/reference set** (the parser-supervision angle — kept as ONE output among several, not top-ranked, per the reframe) — P(the Fourth Reader's ~43 EXERCISES blocks are usable as a cheap supplementary eval axis, if the teacher hand-derives answer keys) ~0.30, deflated hard: coverage is narrow (only the Fourth Reader carries this apparatus at all — zero in the other four books), no answer key is ever given (the teacher must hand-derive expected answers by re-reading each lesson, real labor), and only ~25-35% of the ~150-200 total questions are clean single-answer argument-structure checks that map to a role-filler eval; the rest are open retell, vocabulary/figurative, opinion, or expository-general-knowledge and do not reduce to a role-filler check at all. Honest assessment: this is a real but small, narrow, labor-gated resource — worth extracting into the apparatus sidecar (Part 2) as a reference/triangulation set for the ~43 Fourth-Reader lessons specifically, not a wholesale replacement for or addition to the existing single-annotator gold, and not a training signal (no volume, no answer keys, uneven question quality).

## Honest filter: what will NOT transfer

- **All diacritical-marks / articulation / elocution / pronunciation apparatus** (present in every book's front matter and per-lesson word lists) — explicitly out of scope per task framing: words arrive pre-tokenized, there is no audio/oral-reading layer. Flagged as present-but-not-transferable, not laundered into a false lever.
- **The moral/religious lesson content itself** (Lord's Prayer, Ten Commandments, temperance tracts, "Golden Rule" framing, Sermon on the Mount) — archaic 19th-century didactic content; the STRUCTURAL apparatus around it (definitions/questions/review) is what's extracted here, not the doctrine.
- **Live teacher judgment calls** ("carefully drilled... insisting upon exact execution," "much is left to the teacher" per the PG transcriber's own note) — requires a live human sensing a live learner in real time; no computational analog, consistent with the companion pedagogy scan's identical finding on Gradual Release of Responsibility.
- **The Third Reader's preface-promised apparatus** — does not actually exist in this digitization (see Part 1a caveat); do not build against a description that isn't backed by the artifact on disk.

## Cheap decisive test

Two bounded, low-effort checks before any build decision, both analysis-only:

1. **Apparatus-sidecar extraction pilot (Part 2 recommendation).** Extract the Fourth Reader's 43 DEFINITIONS/EXERCISES blocks plus all five books' vocabulary lists and review-lesson markers into one structured sidecar file (JSON, keyed by book+lesson). This is pure parsing effort against text already on disk — no new data, no model change. **HARD-PASS:** extraction is mechanical and clean (regex/structural parse handles >90% of blocks without manual correction) -> cheap enough to also do for the other three staged McGuffey-parallel readers (Baldwin/Elson/Beacon) when the ladder widens. **HARD-FAIL:** apparatus format is too irregular/OCR-noisy to parse reliably -> the apparatus stays a manual-reference resource (this note), not a structured artifact; still fine, just downgrades convenience, not value.
2. **Fourth-Reader EXERCISES as eval-triangulation micro-test.** Hand-derive answer keys for a small sample (~10 of the 43 blocks, the clean argument-structure subset) and score current reader output against them as a third, independent check alongside existing gold. **HARD-PASS:** scores correlate with existing single-annotator gold precision on the same lessons (if those lessons overlap the current eval set) -> validates the apparatus as a legitimate, if narrow, complementary eval source, worth the labor to extend to all 43. **HARD-FAIL:** no correlation, or the question format doesn't score cleanly against role-filler outputs even for the "clean" subset -> the apparatus stays a teacher-reference/qualitative resource only, not a scoring axis; still an honest, useful negative.

## Falsifiable predictions

- **Prediction A (vocabulary pacing).** If the ~6-new-items/lesson and ~1.7-2.2x/grade pacing ratios are a real, transferable curriculum-design signal (not just an artifact of 19th-century classroom constraints), applying them to reorder or rate-limit the substrate's own corpus/term-grounding ingestion should show measurable improvement on a later-document comprehension/compounding metric, holding total exposure constant. **HARD-PASS:** positive effect at conventional significance. **HARD-FAIL:** no effect -> matches the companion pedagogy scan's Prediction B, which already flags a possible convergent null on curriculum-ordering transfer from human-classroom literature to machine exposure-ordering; a second null from an independent (primary-source, not secondary-lit) angle would be a real, worth-flagging convergent negative.
- **Prediction B (fade-scaffolding arc).** If the exhaustive-pre-teach -> selective-gloss-plus-comprehension-check arc (Part 3, lever #2) is real and not just an artifact of these five particular books, the same arc should appear in at least one of the deferred parallel graded-reader ladders (Baldwin/Elson/Beacon, already staged as PD and assessed-not-yet-ingested per the staging note) when/if they are examined. **HARD-PASS:** the same fade-pattern (exhaustive vocab list in early grades, replaced by selective definitions + comprehension questions in later grades) recurs independently in a different author's series. **HARD-FAIL:** the pattern is McGuffey-specific (an idiosyncratic choice of this one series' editors) -> weakens confidence the arc reflects a real pedagogical principle rather than one author's house style.
