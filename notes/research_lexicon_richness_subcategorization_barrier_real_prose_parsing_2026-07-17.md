# Research: is lexicon richness (subcategorization + selectional preference + sense) the barrier to glass-box real-prose parsing, and is it distinct from foundation-size?

Date: 2026-07-17. Filed by: research (Sonnet, 3 parallel Sonnet lit-scan sub-agents + synthesis). Trigger: the proven glass-box syntactic scaffold (parse/generate/compositionally-generalize on toy SVO sentences, per the 07-16 word-grounding drill) and the 07-16 lexicon-learning drill (word-form -> foundation-concept mapping) both leave open whether the NEXT barrier for real prose is a small/ungrounded LEXICON specifically -- i.e. does real-prose parsing need each word's combinatorial potential (subcategorization frame, selectional preferences on its arguments, sense) specified richly, the way a human's ~40-60k-word mental lexicon is, and is that a different resource than "more world facts" (foundation size)?

Generic terms only used in all three lit-scan queries (lexicalist parsing, garden-path verb bias, head-lexicalized PCFG, CCG supertagging, HPSG/LFG valence, selectional restrictions, VerbNet/FrameNet/PropBank/WordNet) -- no substrate-novel mechanism names, configs, or numbers went off-platform.

---

## (a) HEADLINE

**Yes, lexicon richness is a real, load-bearing, and DISTINCT barrier from general world-knowledge/foundation size -- but the literature also contains a sharp, well-documented warning against over-attributing parsing failure to "not enough lexicon" when a chunk of it is actually under-specified GRAMMAR (constituent/category granularity).** Three independent literatures converge on the positive claim: (1) human psycholinguistics shows the parser leans on verb-specific subcategorization/argument-structure bias essentially immediately, word-by-word (MacDonald/Trueswell lexicalist-constraint tradition), with real quantitative richness per verb (COMLEX's 92 subcategorization features; VALEX's 6,397 verbs x 163 frame types); (2) lexicalized statistical parsing produced the largest historical accuracy jump in the field (unlexicalized ~70-75% F1 -> lexicalized ~88-90% F1, Collins/Charniac), and CCG supertagging treats rich per-word lexical categories as doing "almost" all the disambiguation work; (3) lexicalist grammar formalisms (HPSG, LFG, CCG) are EXPLICITLY engineered so the lexicon carries almost all combinatorial/argument-structure information, with a small, general, largely content-free rule component (the LinGO ERG: ~35,000 lexical entries vs. 225 syntactic rules, a ~150:1 ratio). Separately, and just as important: **Klein & Manning (2003) showed that a large fraction of the historical "lexicalization win" was actually a mis-specified-grammar artifact** -- an unlexicalized PCFG with better linguistically-motivated category splitting (no lexical statistics at all) reached 86.36% F1, nearly matching early lexicalized parsers. This means the lexicon-richness barrier and a separate grammar/category-granularity barrier are commonly conflated in the historical record, and our own glass-box build needs to test them SEPARATELY rather than assume "add subcat frames" is the whole fix. On the distinctness-from-foundation-size question: subcategorization + selectional-preference information is a bounded, taxonomically-organized, symbolic resource (VerbNet ~270-300 verb classes, FrameNet ~1,224 frames / 13,500 lexical units, PropBank ~4,221 verbs / 5,607 rolesets, WordNet ~117k synsets) that is qualitatively different in kind from open-ended world/commonsense-fact ingestion (the CSKG-scale foundation-build thread) -- it is a shallow, closed combinatorial-potential-plus-semantic-TYPE resource, not a store of individual facts. It is buildable glass-box TODAY because VerbNet/FrameNet/PropBank/WordNet already exist as free, symbolic, non-LLM resources -- this is a lookup-table integration problem, not a generation problem, in sharp contrast to the foundation-build thread's core finding that general knowledge is a GENERATION problem.

---

## (b) Cheap decisive test

Three-part test, cheapest-first, designed to separate "lexicon richness" from "grammar/category granularity" from "foundation/world-fact size" as three distinct levers rather than one blob:

1. **Coverage-audit (no code, ~1hr):** Take a representative sample of real prose (e.g. early-reader corpus already staged per the curriculum note, or a public children's-book / simple-Wikipedia sample). Tag every verb TOKEN against VerbNet-class + PropBank-roleset coverage. Compute (i) % of verb tokens covered by the ~270-300 VerbNet classes / ~4,221 PropBank verbs, and (ii) of the covered tokens, what % of their argument-selectional needs are satisfiable by a WordNet-hypernym-level semantic-TYPE constraint alone (e.g. "eats" selects a +edible object) vs. requiring an actual instance-level world fact (e.g. "the boy ate the sandwich HE MADE EARLIER" -- needs episodic/world fact, not type).
2. **Three-arm glass-box parse test (build required):** Using the already-proven role-filler scaffold, build a small VerbNet/COMLEX-derived subcat+selectional symbolic lookup table for the ~100-300 highest-frequency English verbs (Zipf-dominant, covers the bulk of tokens). Construct a held-out set of classic structural-ambiguity sentences (PP-attachment, reduced-relative-clause "horse raced past the barn"-style, verb-frame-bias sentences from the Trueswell verb-bias literature). Run three arms:
   - **ARM NO-LEXICON:** scaffold + generic/undifferentiated category grammar, no subcat table (current baseline).
   - **ARM LEXICON-ONLY:** scaffold + subcat/selectional lookup table, generic grammar unchanged (no category refinement).
   - **ARM LEXICON+GRAMMAR:** scaffold + subcat/selectional lookup table + linguistically-motivated category splitting (Klein & Manning-style: distinguish transitive/intransitive VP types, finite/nonfinite, etc.) in the existing grammar.
3. **Compare deltas** across arms to isolate the lexicon-specific contribution from the grammar-refinement contribution, mirroring the historical Collins-vs-Klein&Manning decomposition the field never cleanly ran in one experiment.

## (c) Falsifiable predictions (HARD-PASS / HARD-FAIL)

**Prediction 1 -- lexicon richness is necessary but the historical "lexicalization win" partly conflates lexicon and grammar-granularity; our own build will show the same conflation unless tested with 3 arms.**
HARD-PASS: ARM LEXICON-ONLY improves structural-ambiguity resolution accuracy by >=15 percentage points over ARM NO-LEXICON (confirming lexicon richness materially helps), AND ARM LEXICON+GRAMMAR improves by >=1.5x the ARM LEXICON-ONLY delta (confirming grammar/category refinement is a separate, additional, non-subsumed lever -- reproducing the Klein & Manning finding on our own substrate).
HARD-FAIL: ARM LEXICON-ONLY improves by <5 points (our parsing failures are not lexicon-shaped at all -- would falsify the headline), OR ARM LEXICON-ONLY captures >=90% of the full ARM LEXICON+GRAMMAR gain (grammar refinement would be negligible, contradicting the well-replicated Klein & Manning transfer and meaning the historical caution doesn't apply here).
P estimate: **P=0.38** (deflated per lit-scan calibration; the qualitative direction is well-precedented in two independent, high-confidence literatures -- MacDonald/Trueswell psycholinguistics and Collins/Klein&Manning computational parsing -- but the specific 3-arm decomposition, run on our own glass-box scaffold with VerbNet-derived tables rather than treebank-trained statistics, has never been attempted and could interact with scaffold-specific idiosyncrasies not present in either source literature).

**Prediction 2 -- subcategorization/selectional richness is a bounded, closed, TYPE-level resource that is separable from open-ended world-fact/foundation size for the large majority of real-prose disambiguation needs.**
HARD-PASS: >=90% of verb tokens in a representative general-prose sample are covered by VerbNet-class + PropBank-roleset symbolic resources, AND of those, argument-selectional resolution is satisfiable via WordNet-hypernym-level semantic-TYPE constraints alone (no instance-level world fact needed) in >=80% of cases -- meaning lexicon-richness accounts for the large majority of the disambiguation burden and is genuinely separable from the foundation-size question.
HARD-FAIL: <60% coverage, OR type-level selectional constraints resolve <50% of covered cases without requiring an instance-level world fact -- either would mean the "lexicon-richness barrier" and "foundation-size barrier" are NOT cleanly separable in real prose; they collapse into one bottleneck, and the distinct-buildable-slice framing in the headline would be wrong.
P estimate: **P=0.40** (deflated; VerbNet/PropBank/WordNet coverage-of-common-verbs numbers are well-documented at the TYPE level -- Zipf-dominance of a few hundred verbs across most tokens is a robust corpus-linguistics fact -- but the specific claim about what FRACTION of real disambiguation needs stop at semantic-type vs. require instance facts has not been directly measured in the literature found this cycle; it is inferred from the existence and apparent sufficiency of type-based selectional-preference research, not a directly cited statistic).

**Prediction 3 -- subcategorization + selectional-preference information can be encoded and consumed entirely glass-box (symbolic lookup table + role-filler binding), with no dense/hidden embedding, exactly mirroring the lexicon-table architecture already specified in the 07-16 word-grounding drill.**
HARD-PASS: the VerbNet/COMLEX-derived subcat+selectional table for the top ~100-300 verbs, expressed purely as (verb -> ranked list of (frame, argument-role-tags, WordNet-hypernym-type-constraint)) and consumed by the existing role-filler scaffold via simple lookup + type-compatibility check, achieves the Prediction-1 HARD-PASS bar without introducing any dense/learned/opaque component.
HARD-FAIL: the symbolic lookup is insufficient to resolve the test-set ambiguities without falling back to a similarity/embedding-style soft match that cannot be fully inspected per-decision (i.e., the glass-box constraint itself breaks under real-prose load).
P estimate: **P=0.45** (deflated but the least novel-synthesis-capped of the three predictions -- this is closest to "faithfully port an existing resource format," not new mechanism invention; VerbNet/FrameNet/PropBank were designed from the start to be machine-readable symbolic lookup structures, so the engineering risk is table-construction effort and format-mapping, not a new theoretical claim).

**Composite headline P: P=0.40** (average of the three, all independently deflated per lit-scan calibration; capped below the novel-synthesis ceiling of 0.50 since Prediction 2's "type-level suffices for most disambiguation" claim is inferred rather than directly measured in any source found this cycle).

## (d) Cross-thread synthesis

- **Directly extends and refines the 07-16 word-grounding-lexicon drill, but answers a DIFFERENT question.** That drill (`research_word_grounding_lexicon_structure_content_unification_2026-07-16.md`) solved word-FORM -> foundation-CONCEPT mapping (which concept does "dog" point to) and role-filler binding as the structure-content join. This drill is upstream of that: it asks what makes the PARSE STRUCTURE ITSELF correct for real (non-toy) sentences -- i.e. before you can bind "dog" into the object-role filler slot, the parser has to correctly decide THAT "dog" occupies the object-role slot, which for real prose (garden paths, PP-attachment, verb-specific frame variation) requires per-verb subcategorization information the toy SVO probe never needed (every toy sentence had exactly one unambiguous parse). The two lexicons are complementary, not redundant: a SEMANTIC lexicon (word-form -> concept) and a SYNTACTIC lexicon (word-form -> combinatorial potential / subcat frame). VerbNet is notable because it already fuses both: each class pairs shared syntactic frames WITH shared thematic roles and selectional restrictions -- i.e. VerbNet is architecturally the single resource that could serve both lexicons at once, which the 07-16 drill did not identify (it cited VerbNet only in passing, via the T3-Phase-A FrameNet ingest reference in a testbed hand-off, not as the subcategorization-lexicon source).
- **Answers the framing question directly: distinct from foundation-size, but not perfectly orthogonal.** The PIVOT thread (`project_PIVOT_build_ideal_knowledge_foundation_from_existing_tools_USER_AUTHORIZED_2026-07-14`) frames general-knowledge ingestion as a GENERATION problem (re-ingesting full CSKG only reaches 5.7% density -- the hard part is generating/curating facts, not storing them). Subcategorization/selectional lexicons are the opposite shape: VerbNet/FrameNet/PropBank/WordNet are FINISHED, bounded, freely available symbolic resources -- this is an INTEGRATION problem (map an existing table into our scaffold's role-filler format), not a generation problem. That is the sharpest actionable distinction this drill surfaces: the lexicon-richness barrier is cheap and largely already solved by existing linguistic resources; the foundation-size barrier is expensive and still open. Prediction 2 is the honest hedge -- selectional preferences DO reach toward world-knowledge at the margins (an instance-level fact like "sandwich HE MADE EARLIER" needs episodic memory, not a type constraint), so the two barriers share a boundary rather than being hermetically separate, but the bulk of real-prose disambiguation should resolve at the TYPE level per the literature's own use of WordNet-hypernym generalization for selectional preference.
- **The Klein & Manning caution is the most important new finding this drill adds that neither prior arc had, and it is a genuine risk to the current plan.** If the glass-box scaffold's category set is still coarse/flat (per the 07-16 SVO-scaffold description, likely true -- it was built to prove binding-algebra correctness, not linguistic category granularity), then simply bolting a subcat lexicon onto it risks reproducing the historical over-attribution error: some of any observed gain from "adding the lexicon" could actually be grammar-granularity gain masquerading as lexicon gain, exactly as it was in the Collins-vs-Klein&Manning history. The 3-arm decisive test in section (b) is designed specifically to not repeat that 20-year-old field-level confusion on our own substrate.
- **Corroborates the "recreate structure-content factorization" thrust** (`project_focus_recreate_brain_factorization_structure_content_two_thrusts_USER_2026-07-14`) with a further factorization layer: not just structure (syntax) vs. content (semantics), but WITHIN the lexicon itself, syntactic combinatorial potential (subcat frame) vs. semantic content (concept/selectional type) are stored as separate but jointly-indexed fields per lexical entry -- exactly how VerbNet, HPSG signs, and LFG lexical entries are actually built (a SUBCAT/valence feature and a semantic/selectional feature living on the same lexical object). This is a validated design pattern to copy directly, not a novel proposal.
- **Sharpens, and partially re-routes, the same-day ranked-barriers synthesis** (`research_glassbox_reading_synthesis_ranked_barriers_2026-07-17.md`, filed earlier today), which ranked five barriers as (1) core-syntax/construction coverage [empirically confirmed dominant via a real HARD_FAIL cell], (2) lexicon quality/meaning-attachment, (3) prediction, (4) working-memory, (5) foundation-size, and named barrier #2 specifically as the SEMANTIC word-form-to-concept attachment gap (the 07-16 lexicon-learning note's Prediction 2/3). This drill's finding implies barrier #2 is not one thing but at least two: a SEMANTIC lexicon (word->concept, what the 07-16 note and today's synthesis already scoped) and a SYNTACTIC lexicon (word->subcat-frame/argument-structure, this drill). Critically, the syntactic half is architecturally closer to barrier #1 than to barrier #2 as ranked -- subcategorization frames are exactly the information that resolves the kind of construction/attachment ambiguity (reduced relatives, PP-attachment, clause-type selection) that caused today's ClausIE-class cell's HARD_FAIL (58.6% unhandled sentences). A verb's subcat frame tells the parser WHICH clause type to expect, which is a construction-coverage lever, not merely a meaning-precision lever. Practical implication: when the barrier-#1 fix (adopt a published clause-typology toolchain) is implemented, it should be paired with the barrier-#1.5-in-practice subcat/selectional table from this drill, since per-verb valence information is likely to reduce residual construction-ambiguity errors beyond what a clause-typology grammar achieves on its own -- this drill's anchor 2 (three-arm test, see hand-off) is a direct, cheap way to measure that interaction rather than assume it.
- **Corroborates the "brain baseline first" discipline** (`feedback_nail_the_brain_baseline_first_then_shore_up_real_weaknesses_USER_2026-07-16`): the psycholinguistic lane confirms the human parser is NOT running a clean two-stage syntax-then-semantics pipeline -- it is lexically-driven essentially immediately (MacDonald/Trueswell), which argues our glass-box design should likewise fuse subcat-driven structural prediction into the parse step itself (online, word-by-word), not as a post-hoc reranking filter -- a concrete architectural cue for how to wire the lookup table into the existing scaffold, not just what data to put in it.

## (e) Substrate-product implications

1. **Build a syntactic (subcategorization) lexicon table as a companion to, and reusing the same lookup-table architecture as, the 07-16 semantic lexicon table.** Format: `verb -> ranked list of (VerbNet-class-id, subcat-frame, argument-role-tags, WordNet-hypernym-type-constraint-per-role, frequency-weight)`. Fully inspectable, fully symbolic, directly derivable from VerbNet + COMLEX + PropBank without any LLM call -- this is table construction/format-mapping, not new-mechanism invention (Prediction 3's low novel-synthesis risk).
2. **Do NOT treat "lexicon richness" and "foundation/world-knowledge size" as the same problem or the same budget.** They are different resources with different acquisition costs: the subcat/selectional lexicon is CHEAP (existing free resources, bounded scope, a few hundred high-frequency verbs cover the bulk of real-prose tokens per Zipf); the general-knowledge foundation is EXPENSIVE (generation problem, per PIVOT). Prioritize the lexicon build first -- it is nearly free capability, and per Prediction 2 likely resolves the majority of real-prose structural-ambiguity cases without waiting on foundation scale-up.
3. **Test grammar/category granularity SEPARATELY from lexicon richness before concluding either one is "the" barrier.** Per the Klein & Manning caution, budget the 3-arm test (section b) rather than a single "add the lexicon and see" experiment -- a 2-arm test cannot distinguish which lever produced any observed gain, repeating a documented historical confusion.
4. **VerbNet is the single highest-leverage resource to ingest first** (not FrameNet or PropBank alone) because it is the only one of the four that natively pairs syntactic frames WITH selectional/thematic information in the same class entry -- it can seed BOTH the syntactic lexicon (this drill) and strengthen the semantic lexicon (07-16 drill) from one ingest pass. FrameNet/PropBank/WordNet are valuable secondary/backfill resources (FrameNet for frame-semantic role vocabulary beyond Levin classes; PropBank for large-verb-count coverage and roleset granularity; WordNet for the hypernym backbone selectional preferences generalize over).
5. **Wire subcat-driven structural prediction into the parse step itself, not as a post-hoc filter.** The psycholinguistic finding that lexical/verb information is used essentially immediately, word-by-word, is a concrete architectural cue: as each verb-phasor is bound during parsing, the scaffold should immediately consult its subcat-frame table to narrow the expected role-slots for upcoming words, mirroring online lexical-guidance rather than building a full parse tree and reranking afterward.
6. **Flag honestly:** Prediction 2 (type-level selectional constraints suffice for the bulk of real-prose cases) is the one genuinely under-cited claim in this drill -- it is inferred from the existence and design of type-based selectional-preference research (Resnik, WordNet-based generalization), not a directly measured fraction from any single source found this cycle. Treat it as the most falsifiable and least certain of the three predictions; the coverage-audit in section (b) part 1 is cheap enough to run before committing further build effort.
