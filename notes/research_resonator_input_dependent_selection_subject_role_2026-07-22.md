## HEADLINE

**Resonator networks (Frady/Kent/Olshausen/Sommer 2020) are the RIGHT MECHANISM CLASS but the WRONG SCALE for this specific capability, and this substrate's own most recent same-family attempt just HARD-FAILED with 95% non-convergence — a real caution, not a disqualification.** The correct, right-sized answer is the SAME underlying operation resonator networks use (iterative energy-descent settling = repeated bilinear compatibility-scoring + nonlinear renormalization), applied to a small, closed candidate set (2-5 nouns per sentence) rather than resonator's large-unknown-search-space regime. This substrate already has the primitive built and validated (`hdlab/iterative_attractor.py`, ATT1, HARD_PASS precedent) — it needs one extension (a second, agreement-compatibility term added to the settling energy) to become the missing mechanism. Two literature threads converge independently on this same shape: the classic PDP constraint-satisfaction literature (McClelland & Kawamoto 1986) and the variable-binding-problem literature (Hummel & Holyoak's LISA / Shastri & Ajjanagadde's SHRUTI — mutual-inhibition competition among role-binding units). **The load-bearing honest caveat, present in every branch of this drill:** every literature source that achieved genuine RULE-LEARNING (not rule-EXECUTION) switched to gradient training (St. John & McClelland 1990 abandoned M&K's hand-set energy settling for backprop specifically to get learned constraints). What the glass-box mechanisms below can do is COMBINE multiple hand-specified compatibility signals into a globally consistent, input-dependent decision that a single linear pass cannot express — they do not, by themselves, discover which signals matter from unlabeled data. That has to be supplied (fairly or not) as one of the settling terms, and this note is explicit about where that line sits.

## 1. Does a resonator network do "which filler occupies the subject role" — and at what scale does that framing actually apply?

**Mechanically, yes — resonator networks are designed exactly for "unbind without a known key."** Given a composite vector formed by binding an unknown ROLE to an unknown FILLER (and possibly superposed with other such pairs), a resonator iteratively estimates each factor by unbinding-and-cleaning-up conditioned on current estimates of the others, until the joint estimate is self-consistent. This is genuinely input-dependent (the settling trajectory is a function of the specific composite's content, unlike a fixed linear map applied identically to every input) and fixed-algebra/glass-box (iteration count, codebooks, and the cleanup nonlinearity are all fixed and inspectable; no gradient training).

**But the founding papers' whole value proposition is searching LARGE, initially-unknown joint (role, filler) spaces cheaply** — their reported gains (10x-10^6x over brute-force/optimization baselines, per this substrate's own prior drill `research_resonator_decode_capacity_ceiling_crux_v2_2026-07-10.md`) matter when the combinatorial search space `M^F` is large. For subject-selection, the candidate set is tiny: 2-5 nouns per sentence, 1-2 roles in play (subject vs the attractor). `M^F` here is under 100 — several orders of magnitude below the ~1.2x10^5 limit-cycle-onset threshold Kent et al. report, and nowhere near where a full alternating-projection factorizer earns its keep. Using the literal Frady/Kent/Olshausen machine here is architecturally over-scaled: it is not wrong, but the leverage that makes resonator networks worth their convergence risk (large search spaces) is absent.

**A fresh, directly-relevant caution from this substrate's own disk (not the literature):** `data/exp_crux_engine_v2_resonator_decode_v1_smoke/metrics.json` — this exact mechanism family, wired for a KG relation-path factorization task (14,541 entities), just landed `HARD_FAIL_A_SNR_FLOOR`: `nonconvergence_rate=0.95`, resonator-gated recall (`res_gated_recall@10=0.058`) BELOW both single-shot unbind (`0.175`) and a naive population-relative-frequency baseline (`POP_RELFREQ hits@1=0.25` vs resonator's `0.042`). This is a smoke-scale (n=1 seed) result on a much larger, semantically-correlated codebook than subject-selection would ever use, so it does not directly transfer — but it is honest, current, on-substrate evidence that "just wire in a resonator" is not a proven-easy lever here, and the predicted risk from the prior drill (codebook correlation, an open literature gap) appears to have materialized. Report this plainly rather than treat resonator-network-proper as a low-risk default.

**Verdict on this section:** resonator networks are the right MECHANISM CLASS (iterative, content-dependent, glass-box factorization) but the wrong INSTANTIATION for a 2-5-candidate, single-sentence problem. Keep the full resonator in reserve for a genuinely large-candidate-set variant of the same question (e.g., discourse-level antecedent resolution over many referents), where its comparative advantage actually applies. For subject-selection, the right-sized version is Section 2.

## 2. The right-sized mechanism: small-scale iterative attractor settling (already built), and why it is exactly what a fixed linear readout lacks

**Why a fixed linear readout ties at majority (structural diagnosis, this drill's own synthesis).** A fixed linear map `W` applied once to the bound superposition computes a single linear functional of the whole bundle. It cannot express "pick whichever candidate satisfies compatibility-condition-C(this specific instance)" because that requires a term that MULTIPLIES together (candidate content) x (verb/agreement content) — a bilinear interaction, not a linear one. This is the same class of limitation as a single-layer perceptron failing XOR, or a linear map failing to implement query-key attention (which needs `Q·Kᵀ`, a bilinear form, before any nonlinearity). A linear map's best available fallback under this constraint is exactly the input-independent majority class — which is the observed symptom (~0.63 ties on the hard subset), not a coincidence.

**The mechanism that supplies the missing bilinear interaction is already built on this substrate: `hdlab/iterative_attractor.py` (ATT1).** Its update rule — `state_{t+1} = renormalize(softmax(temp * (state_t @ codebook.T)) @ codebook)` — is precisely a per-candidate compatibility SCORE (`state · candidate`, bilinear) followed by a softmax nonlinearity and weighted recombination. This is the modern-Hopfield/attention-equivalent operation (Ramsauer et al. 2020, "Hopfield Networks is All You Need," verified this cycle: the continuous Hopfield update `ξ_new = X·softmax(β X^T ξ)` is mathematically the same operation as one step of transformer attention `softmax(QK^T/√d)V`). ATT1 already carries an ACF/HARD_PASS-class precedent on this substrate (recall_at_1 lift over argmax baseline in its designed noise regime) and cites exactly this correspondence (Krotov-Hopfield 2016, Ramsauer 2021, Saxena-Bartlett 2024 "VSA Finite State Machines in Attractor Neural Networks").

**Important nuance surfaced by the lit-scan, not to be glossed over:** Ramsauer et al.'s result is that a single modern-Hopfield/attention step is a ONE-SHOT retrieval (this is explicitly why it maps cleanly onto a single, non-iterative attention layer) — classical discrete Hopfield networks need MULTIPLE iterations to reach an energy minimum, and it is the multi-step iteration (not the one-shot version) that accumulates evidence from more than one compatibility signal. For subject-selection, the design needs at minimum two joint terms (position-prior + agreement-compatibility, possibly a third for hierarchical-embedding depth) — this argues for a small number of settling iterations (matching ATT1's existing 8-step budget), not a single softmax pass, if more than one constraint must be reconciled.

**Two independent literature threads converge on this exact shape, from different eras and different motivations:**

- **McClelland & Kawamoto (1986), "Mechanisms of sentence processing: assigning roles to constituents"** (PDP Vol. 2) — the classic demonstration that role-assignment under ambiguous surface cues is solved by mutual-constraint-satisfaction settling combining multiple weak cues (semantic microfeatures, animacy, syntactic slot) jointly until a globally consistent labeling is reached. **Honest limit, directly on-point for fairness:** the constraint weights in M&K were hand-designed, not Hebbian-derived from unlabeled statistics — Lachter & Bever's (1988) contemporaneous critique is that the hand-coded microfeature representations are "ad hoc and biased," making the task easier by construction than an honest test would allow. This is the SAME "smuggled answer" risk this session's structure-induction drill (`research_glassbox_structure_induction_hierarchy_from_linear_2026-07-22.md`) already flagged for a different mechanism — it recurs here and must be guarded against explicitly (see Section 4).
- **Hummel & Holyoak's LISA (and Shastri & Ajjanagadde's SHRUTI)**, the variable-binding-problem literature: role-binding units for competing candidate fillers MUTUALLY INHIBIT, and noise-driven competition resolves which filler wins a role — an iterative, Hebbian/settling dynamic, not gradient-trained, that performs genuinely content-dependent role assignment rather than fixed-key lookup. Known, stated limitation: temporal-synchrony binding is capacity-limited and considered impractical at scale — but our candidate sets are exactly the small scale where this does not bind.
- A concrete, very recent (2024) engineering realization of the SAME unification was surfaced this cycle: **"Self-Attention Based Semantic Decomposition in VSA" (arXiv:2403.13218)** reformulates resonator-network iterative unbinding as a Hopfield-style attention/energy-minimization update and reports materially improved convergence — independent confirmation that "resonator settling" and "iterated attention/Hopfield update" are the same underlying operation, not two competing mechanisms to choose between.

**Recommended concrete build (cheapest, most directly reusable, right-sized):** extend ATT1's existing softmax-attractor energy with a second additive term — an agreement-compatibility score computed via `bind(candidate_number_vec, verb_number_vec)` similarity (a coincidence-detector construction well precedented in the HRR/VSA literature for exactly this XOR-like match-detection role) — alongside the existing position-prior term, and let the settling process (2-8 iterations, matching ATT1's current budget) converge on the candidate that is jointly consistent with both signals. This reuses 100% of an already-validated primitive; the new code is the second energy term and its relative weight (which may be hand-set at a plausible value, or calibrated with a cheap scalar fit reusing the substrate's already-validated structured-perceptron machinery — see Section 4 for why this weight-calibration choice matters for the fairness question).

## 4. The fairness gradient — this drill's most important calibration point

Every candidate mechanism above sits somewhere on a spectrum from "discovers the compatibility signal from raw content" to "is handed the compatibility signal as a pre-computed feature." This matters because a HARD-PASS built on the wrong end of that spectrum would be a cosmetic win, not a real one — the same trap this session's other threads (structure-induction note, HP2 brain-check note) have already had to guard against explicitly.

- **Weakest (fastest, least informative):** a structured-perceptron token-classifier fed a pre-computed "candidate-number matches verb-number" binary feature. The classifier only has to learn "select where this feature = 1" — nearly all the work is in the feature engineering, not the mechanism. Cheapest, highest raw accuracy, weakest claim.
- **Middle:** McClelland & Kawamoto-style settling with HAND-SET relative weights on the compatibility terms (position-prior vs agreement-compatibility) — the settling PROCESS is genuine (multiple terms jointly and iteratively reconciled, which a linear map cannot do), but which terms matter and how much they matter is still hand-specified, exactly the Lachter & Bever critique of M&K.
- **Strongest (most informative, most novel-synthesis risk):** the agreement-compatibility SCORE itself is computed via bind+similarity directly from raw number/person feature vectors (no hand-labeled match/mismatch indicator), and only the ITERATION DYNAMICS (not the comparison itself) is fixed/hand-specified — closer to the LISA/SHRUTI mutual-inhibition picture, and closer to what would count as the substrate discovering the relevant relational structure rather than being told it.

**Recommendation: build and report at the MIDDLE tier as primary (Section 2's ATT1 extension), explicitly measure and report where on this spectrum the compatibility term sits, and do NOT claim the strongest tier's interpretive credit unless the compatibility score is demonstrably NOT a disguised hand-labeled indicator.** This mirrors the "weak hierarchical-capacity scaffold vs smuggled answer" distinction the structure-induction note already established for a different mechanism (ABL/ADIOS vs DMV/CCM) — the same discipline transfers directly here.

## Falsifiable predictions

**HARD-PASS:** ARM_SETTLE held-out accuracy on the attractor subset clears the fixed-linear-readout arm's ~0.63 by a wide, non-noise margin (>= 15 percentage points, matching the GO-scoping note's own bar), AND beats both baselines (i)/(ii) above, AND a content-dependence discriminator fires correctly (scrambling which candidate carries which number feature, while preserving positions, should flip ARM_SETTLE's answer accordingly — if it does not, the mechanism is silently still keying on position, not on the agreement term).

**HARD-FAIL:** ARM_SETTLE ties or underperforms the existing fixed-linear-readout arm (~0.63) — meaning the added bilinear compatibility term did not supply the missing capability, and either (a) the settling dynamics are not actually using the new term (implementation bug, check first) or (b) this mechanism class genuinely does not clear the bar, redirecting to the discrete MST/Eisner route (Section 3) or the token-classification route (Section 3, at the acknowledged lower-fairness tier) as the next-tried mechanism, not a program-level dead end.

**MIDDLE:** ARM_SETTLE beats the fixed-linear-readout tie but by less than 15pp, or beats one baseline but not both — partial signal; the settling PROCESS helps some but the specific compatibility term or iteration budget likely needs tuning before a clean verdict either way.
