# Research: definite-description -> named-antecedent resolution — Centering/focus vs. salience vs. a real world-knowledge ceiling

**Date:** 2026-09-07. **Filed by:** research (4 parallel Sonnet lit-scan lanes + Opus synthesis). **Trigger:** free-form topic statement (no routing file) — the live mechanism binds a definite person-role-noun ("the doctor") to the most-salient (ACT-R base-level activation) OR most-recent gender-compatible named-person card, scoring only ~7% precision on modern GUM (18 genres). Question: wrong brain mechanism (fixable) or genuine ceiling?

**Method:** 4 parallel Sonnet lit-scan lanes (Centering mechanics; Grosz-Sidner focus/genre; Poesio-Vieira anaphoricity corpus statistics; Stanford deterministic-sieve nominal precision), each instructed to WebSearch public academic literature only, cite author/year/venue, flag inference vs. direct-citation, and report exact numbers. Cross-referenced against this project's OWN prior measured work: `notes/problems/expand_the_clean_semantic_memory_foundation_with_world_knowledge_via_the_consolidation_gate/SOLVED.md` (a 2026-09 measurement on this exact corpus — GUM common-noun coreference, n=2855 anaphoric-common mentions), `notes/research_coreference_hobbs_centering_resolver_2026-07-16.md`, `notes/T2b_the_coref_HARD_FAIL_DRILLED_...2026-08-21.md`, and `notes/step2_pronoun_coref_centering_role_prominence_design_2026-08-02.md`.

## (a) HEADLINE

**Both — but not in the proportion the framing implies.** The literature does NOT support "raw recency" as the wrong mechanism relative to Cb/topic-tracking — in English, grammatical-role-ranked Cb-tracking and simple recency are reported as *statistically indistinguishable* for antecedent selection (Poesio et al. 2004, GNOME corpus); they diverge only for transition-*classification*, and a literal textbook Centering (BFP) implementation has been reported to score *worse* than plain recency on pronoun antecedent selection (Tetreault 2001: BFP 59.4%/46.4% vs. recency 60.0%/60.0%, news/fiction). So "swap recency for real Cb-tracking" is not, by itself, a well-evidenced fix.

What the literature DOES identify as almost certainly the dominant, fixable defect is upstream of cue-choice entirely: **the mechanism as described never asks "is this definite description even anaphoric?"** — it always resolves to *some* named-person card. Three independent corpus studies (Poesio & Vieira 1998, WSJ, verified primary source; Poesio/Vieira/Teufel 1997 re-tabulation; Fraurud 1990, Swedish, secondary cite; Ng & Cardie 2002, MUC-6/7, verified primary source, common-nouns specifically) converge on **roughly 70-78% of definite descriptions in running text being non-anaphoric or requiring only bridging/world knowledge, not identity-anaphora to an explicit prior mention.** A resolver with no anaphoricity gate is, by these base rates alone, wrong on the large majority of its attempts before the selection cue is even evaluated.

Below that, for the specific residual subtask the user actually cares about — common-noun role-description resolving to a **named** entity (Poesio & Vieira's "associative"/this project's own "name_bridge" category) — **two independent measurements 16+ years and two very different corpora apart converge tightly on a genuine, non-discourse-recoverable ceiling: Raghunathan et al. 2010 found 85% (17/20 sampled) of common-noun recall errors on 1990s MUC-6 news required "semantic/world knowledge" (e.g., knowing "Gitano" IS "the company"); this project's own 2026 GUM measurement (SOLVED.md, name_bridge subset, n covers 10.1% of all anaphoric-common mentions) found in-text apposition/is-a signal resolves only 19.1% of name_bridge cases, leaving 80.9% requiring an external world-knowledge fact.** That 81-85% convergence, from unrelated methods/eras/genres, is about as strong as this kind of estimate gets — it is a real ceiling for ANY text-internal mechanism, Centering included, not an artifact of using the wrong cue.

## (b) Cheap decisive test

Do NOT rebuild the resolver around "proper Centering" first — the literature does not predict that alone would move the needle much (see Poesio et al. 2004, Tetreault 2001 above). Instead, stratify the existing 7%-precision result by TWO orthogonal variables that the literature says matter far more:

1. **Anaphoricity stratum.** Using Hawkins' 1978 taxonomy (as operationalized by Poesio & Vieira 1998/PVT97), hand- or gold-tag a sample of the failing GUM cases into: (i) genuinely anaphoric to a prior identical/synonymous mention, (ii) bridging/associative (a real prior mention of a *related* entity, e.g. "a hospital ... the doctor"), (iii) larger-situation/generic/first-mention (no resolvable antecedent exists at all — correct behavior is ABSTAIN). Report current-mechanism precision separately on stratum (i)+(ii) only, vs. on the full unfiltered set.
2. **In-text predication stratum.** Within the "name_bridge" cases specifically (common-noun -> named-person), separately flag cases with an explicit in-text apposition or copula/predicate-nominative construction ("Elizabeth, a doctor," / "Elizabeth is the doctor") vs. cases with no such construction anywhere in the preceding discourse.

**HARD-PASS:** precision on stratum (i)+(ii) with an in-text predication cue present is >= 60% (this project's own GUM instrument already measured the type-licensing-filter mechanism at 0.6998 on the *aggregate* anaphoric-common task, and Vieira & Poesio 2000's staged rule-based system reached 76-83% precision on definite-description resolution generally once gated — 60% is a deflated, achievable bar for the narrower name-bridge-with-predication slice) AND precision on cases with NO in-text predication cue is materially lower (i.e., the split itself is diagnostic, confirming predication-detection — not the salience cue — is the lever).
**HARD-FAIL:** precision stays near 7% even on the gated, predication-cue-present stratum — this would falsify the "missing anaphoricity gate / missing apposition detector" diagnosis and force a harder look at whether the candidate pool (gender-compatible named-person cards) itself is mis-specified (e.g., wrong candidate set, not wrong cue).

**MIDDLE otherwise:** report which stratum moved and by how much; that identifies whether the gate, the predication detector, or neither is carrying the fix.

## (c) Falsifiable predictions (HARD-PASS / HARD-FAIL)

**Prediction A — an explicit anaphoricity gate (abstain on larger-situation/generic/first-mention definites) is the single largest fixable lever, larger than any change to the selection cue.**
P estimate: **P=0.50** (capped at the novel-synthesis ceiling; the base-rate evidence for the *size* of the non-anaphoric population is direct-citation-strong — Poesio & Vieira 1998 verified primary source, 48.37% larger-situation/unfamiliar + Ng & Cardie 2002 verified primary source, 77-78% of common nouns specifically non-anaphoric — but whether THIS project's specific 7%-precision failure is dominated by this factor specifically, vs. some other implementation defect, is not directly measured yet, hence the cap.)
HARD-PASS: gating out the (measured or estimated) non-anaphoric ~70-78% of attempts raises precision on the remaining gated subset by >= 3x over the unfiltered 7% (i.e., to >= 21%).
HARD-FAIL: gated-subset precision stays within 2 points of 7% — would mean the non-anaphoric population is not actually where the mechanism is failing, and the defect is elsewhere (e.g., wrong candidate pool, or the "salient/recent gender-compatible card" selection itself is broken even among genuinely-anaphoric cases).

**Prediction B — grammatical-role-weighted Cf ranking (subject > object > other, BFP 1987) outperforms un-role-weighted recency/ACT-R-activation, but only modestly.**
P estimate: **P=0.35** (deflated; Poesio et al. 2004's GNOME finding that role-ranking and linear-order/recency are statistically indistinguishable for antecedent selection in English argues AGAINST a large gain here, but the user's specific mechanism has no role signal at all — unlike GNOME's baseline, which implicitly tracks grammatical position — so some gain is plausible; this project's own `step2_pronoun_coref_centering_role_prominence_design` note independently flagged the identical missing-role-signal gap for the pronoun case, not yet measured for common-noun.)
HARD-PASS: adding subject/agent-role weighting to the salience score improves precision by >= 5 points on the genuinely-anaphoric-with-competing-same-type-candidates subset (the only subset where role-ranking can matter at all).
HARD-FAIL: <1 point change — would corroborate Poesio et al. 2004's finding that in fixed-word-order English text, role-ranking is redundant with recency and not worth the added machinery.

**Prediction C — an explicit apposition/predicate-nominative ("precise constructs") detector recovers a meaningful, bounded slice of the name_bridge subtype, consistent with the Stanford sieve's ~90-96% per-fire precision and this project's own 19.1% GUM in-text-is-a coverage measurement — but the remaining ~80% is a genuine, non-recoverable-from-text ceiling requiring a world-knowledge KB.**
P estimate: **P=0.50** (capped; this is the strongest-evidenced prediction in this note — TWO independent numbers, Raghunathan et al. 2010's 85% and this project's own SOLVED.md 80.9%, converge within 4 points of each other across 16+ years and unrelated corpora/methods — but capped at the novel-synthesis ceiling per calibration discipline since the *exact* percentage for THIS project's person-role-noun-specific slice, as opposed to the general name_bridge category, has not itself been separately measured.)
HARD-PASS: an apposition/copula detector applied to the failing 93% raises overall precision by a bounded, measurable amount consistent with ~19% coverage (i.e., an absolute lift in the range of +3 to +15 points depending on how much of the 93% miss-pool actually contains an in-text predication cue) AND a follow-up world-knowledge-KB lookup (entity-type/role fact, e.g. Wikidata P31) is required to close more than that.
HARD-FAIL: the apposition detector recovers <1 point of absolute precision — would mean this corpus's failing cases have almost no in-text predication cues at all (a genre-density fact worth knowing in itself), and/or that the remaining ceiling is even larger/harder than 80-85%.

## (e) Substrate-product implications

1. **Build the anaphoricity gate first, not a new selection cue.** Per Prediction A, the base-rate evidence (Poesio & Vieira 1998, verified: 48.37% larger-situation/unfamiliar; Ng & Cardie 2002, verified: 77-78% of common nouns non-anaphoric) says most of the 93% failure mass is likely attempts on definites that were never resolvable to begin with. Correct behavior on these is ABSTAIN (zero triple), exactly the invariant this project already enforces elsewhere for pronoun coreference (per `research_coreference_hobbs_centering_resolver_2026-07-16.md`'s HARD-FAIL bars on false-confidence guessing). A cheap first gate: does the mention's head noun appear (identically or as a WordNet hypernym/hyponym — reusing the already-built `hdlab/typed_spokes.py` C5 is-a spoke) anywhere in the preceding discourse window at all? If not, it is very likely non-anaphoric/generic and should abstain rather than force-bind to the nearest gender-compatible card.
2. **Add an apposition/predicate-nominative detector as a second, additive, cheap, well-precedented stage** — this is exactly the Stanford sieve's "precise constructs" sieve (Raghunathan et al. 2010; Lee et al. 2013), reported at ~90-96% precision where it fires, and this project's OWN GUM instrument already measures its in-text analog (is-a/apposition coverage) at 19.1% of the name_bridge subtype. This is a bounded, well-understood, glass-box, no-KB-required win — build it before touching the KB question.
3. **Do NOT expect a "proper Centering" rebuild (Cb/Cf machinery replacing recency) to be the dominant fix.** Poesio et al. 2004 (GNOME) and Tetreault 2001 both independently report that in English, grammatical-role-ranked Cb-tracking and plain recency are close-to-equivalent for antecedent SELECTION (they diverge mainly on transition-TYPE classification, which is not the task here). This project's own SOLVED.md reached the identical architectural conclusion independently ("recency-primary... our architecture is the dominant/default account"). Budget any role-weighting addition (Prediction B) as a small, cheap refinement on top of recency, not a rearchitecture.
4. **The remaining ~80-85% of name_bridge cases is a genuine, convergent, cross-validated ceiling for any text-internal mechanism** (Raghunathan et al. 2010's 85% and this project's own 80.9%, independently derived, 16+ years apart). This is NOT a Centering/salience/discourse-mechanism problem at all — it requires an entity-type/role fact from a world-knowledge source (e.g. Wikidata P31/instance-of, DBpedia InstanceOf, or a curated role-kinship KB), admitted the way this project's existing p11/consolidation-gate typed-spoke architecture already admits offline-curated world knowledge (is-a C5, part-whole C6 spokes). The SOLVED.md already names this as "the next FOUNDATION acquisition, not a prototype" — this note independently corroborates that call from the external literature and should be read as confirming, not just repeating, it.
5. **Genre composition of the eval set matters independent of the mechanism.** GUM's 18-genre mix structurally depresses aggregate precision relative to a single-genre narrative corpus (OntoGUM: ~2x F1 spread between narrative/conversational and expository/instructional genres, external, direct citation). When reporting a headline precision number, stratify by genre-class (narrative-like vs. expository-like) so a genuine mechanism improvement is not masked or inflated by genre mix.
6. **Any decisive test of these predictions must be adequately powered and properly controlled**, per the T2b lesson: n in the hundreds, a shuffle/scramble control matched on the identical population, and full parameter sweeps run to their knee before reporting a value.

## TLDR

I checked what the science says about how people figure out who "the doctor" refers to, and compared it against a result already sitting on disk from this project's own testing on modern text (18 different genres, hundreds of documents). Two clear findings.

First: the literature says recency (who was mentioned most recently) is already a very good stand-in for "discourse topic" in English — swapping in a fancier topic-tracking mechanism by itself is not likely to be the big fix. This project's own prior test agrees: recency alone already beat every simpler baseline tried.

Second, and this is the important one: roughly seven out of ten "the ___" phrases in ordinary writing are NOT actually pointing back at something said earlier at all — they're new information, or a generic reference, or point at something only loosely related. A mechanism that always tries to link every one of them to a specific earlier person will be wrong most of the time just from that, regardless of how good its picking rule is. That is very likely the single biggest reason the current 7%-correct number is so low.

Below that, for the specific narrow case being asked about — a role-word like "the doctor" that should point to a NAMED person — there is a second, harder problem, and here two completely independent measurements (one from a 1990s news-text study, one from this project's own 2026 test on modern multi-genre text, run 16 years apart on different text) land within four percentage points of each other: about eight times out of ten, resolving that link genuinely requires knowing an outside fact (that a particular named person holds that role) that is not stated anywhere in the passage. That is a real wall, not a discourse-tracking problem, and no amount of better topic-tracking closes it — it needs a small factual lookup source instead.

**Bottom line, in cost terms:** the cheap, well-precedented fix (teaching the system to first ask "does this phrase even refer back to someone," then to notice when the text directly says "X, a doctor,") should recover a meaningful chunk of the current miss rate at low build cost. Beyond that, roughly 80% of the hardest remaining cases will not budge without adding a small curated fact-lookup source — a different kind of work, not a smarter reading mechanism.
