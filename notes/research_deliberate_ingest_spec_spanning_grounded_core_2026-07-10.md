## HEADLINE

1. **A dense + perceptually-grounded ALIGNED pair is achievable, but only as a deliberate COMPOSE, and the composing principle is not "density" -- it is DIMENSIONAL SPAN.** No single off-the-shelf corpus is simultaneously dense, general-domain, and perceptually grounded (confirmed again this drill). But the literature independently converges on a small, well-characterized "grounded core" that is span-complete by construction: the dictionary **grounding kernel / minimal grounding set** (Vincent-Lamarre, Blondin Massé, Harnad et al.) intersected with **Lancaster sensorimotor norms** (11 perceptual+action dimensions) and **Wierzbicka/Goddard NSM semantic primes+molecules** (65 primes + ~50 molecules, the only known cross-linguistically universal covering of ABSTRACT/relational/logical meaning). This triple composition is span-complete in a way no named-entity KG (FB15k-237, CoDEx, Hetionet, Wikidata) can be, no matter how dense.
2. **The grounded core to ingest first = MGS/Kernel words (highest-frequency, earliest-AoA, structurally load-bearing by feedback-vertex-set) UNIONED with the 65 NSM primes + ~50 molecules, each carrying a full multi-channel grounded-attribute vector** (Lancaster 11-dim sensorimotor + Brysbaert concreteness + Kuperman AoA + Warriner valence/arousal/dominance) **and cross-source entity-resolved via CSKG's existing ConceptNet/WordNet/Wikidata-CS/FrameNet merge** (2.2M nodes, 6M edges, 251,517 `mw:SameAs` links already computed by USC-ISI -- this is the entity-resolution engine we do not have to rebuild).
3. **The single biggest alignment risk is not overlap-percentage -- it is picking a DENSE-BUT-NARROW spine and mistaking density for readiness.** FB15k-237 (37.4 avg-deg, clears our floor) and Hetionet (47.9 avg-deg, clears our floor) are both dense enough for KG-inference, and both are catastrophically bad grounded cores: you cannot ground "sadness," "multiplication," "rough texture," or "because" by correlating to film-award facts or drug-disease edges. **Density != spanning.** The corollary risk: even the good composition (MGS+NSM+sensorimotor) has UNVERIFIED local density at the core-member level and UNVERIFIED grounding-reach beyond the core -- both are now the pre-registered cheap decisive tests below, not assumed.

## Brain-first framing (lead with biology, per standing discipline)

Children do not ground language via encyclopedic named-entity facts. They ground it via (a) a small sensorimotor-experienced core -- body, caregivers, food, basic motion/action, spatial/temporal deixis -- acquired through direct perception and action, and (b) an early-emerging, cross-linguistically universal set of relational/logical function words (more, not, want, good/bad, same, big/small, because, if) that let the REST of meaning bootstrap via structural/metaphorical mapping onto the concrete core, not via more facts. Two independent literatures converge on the same empirical claim:

- **Developmental psycholinguistics:** early productive vocabulary is dominated by concrete words; abstract-word share grows from <10% of known words at age 4 to >40% by age 12, peaking in acquisition rate around age 7-8. Age-of-acquisition and concreteness are directly coupled -- later-acquired word meanings are systematically more abstract (Harvest/USask "Development of Abstract Word Meanings"; Nature Sci Rep "Abstractness emerges progressively over the second year of life"; Frontiers "Development of Abstract Word Knowledge"). This is a STRICT concrete-first, core-outward curriculum, never abstract-first.
- **Computational lexicography (independently, via graph theory on dictionaries, not developmental data):** the "grounding kernel" of a dictionary -- the minimal self-referential closure of definitions (a feedback-vertex-set computation) -- and the smaller, non-unique "minimal grounding set" (MGS/MinSet) within it, are BOTH shown empirically to consist of words that are more frequent AND lower in age-of-acquisition than the rest of the dictionary (Vincent-Lamarre, Blondin Massé, Marcotte, Ollivier, Harnad et al., "The Latent Structure of Dictionaries," Top. Cogn. Sci. 2016, arXiv:1411.0129; Blondin Massé et al., "How Is Meaning Grounded in Dictionary Definitions?" ACL W08-2003 / arXiv:0806.3710). The MGS is reported at roughly 1% of a dictionary's word count and roughly 15% of the Kernel's size (the Kernel itself on the order of ~10% of the dictionary) -- i.e. for a ~40-50k-word English norm-covered vocabulary, a core on the order of several hundred words, not thousands. (Caveat: I could not pull a clean full-text re-derivation of the exact percentage in this session -- one WebFetch of the raw PDF likely produced OCR/binary-garbage-influenced numbers that partially conflict with the search-snippet-sourced figures; I report the search-snippet-sourced 1%/15%/10% figures as the more reliable reading and flag the discrepancy rather than silently picking one.)
- **The two literatures were derived independently (one from child language corpora, one from pure graph structure on adult dictionaries) and agree**: the words that are structurally load-bearing for definability are the same words children learn first and use most. This is the strongest available empirical anchor for "what to ingest first."
- **Harnad's own original framing is the direct precedent for the Director's "correlate to what's already grounded" principle**: in the dual-code account, ONLY the kernel/MGS members need direct sensorimotor grounding (category induction from perception/action); everything else is grounded INDIRECTLY, via symbolic recombination of already-grounded terms in its definition. A term whose definition cannot be reduced, through some chain, to kernel members has no path to grounding -- it stays a floating symbol. This is exactly the substrate mechanism the Director is describing, sourced to a 35-year-old, still-active cognitive-science research program, not a novel claim.
- **Abstract-concept grounding is not disconfirmed by lacking direct sensorimotor content -- it is graded and relational.** "Boundaries to grounding abstract concepts" (PMC6015828) and "Ultimate Grounding of Abstract Concepts: A Graded Account" (J. Cognition, PMC9400652) both argue for a two-layer account: a perceptual layer grounding concrete concepts directly, and a relational/linguistic layer that grounds abstract concepts BY STRUCTURAL MAPPING onto concrete concepts (the "journey" schema lending its structure to "problem solving," i.e. conceptual metaphor / structure-mapping) -- this is the mechanism by which grounding "radiates outward," not simple graph adjacency. A companion empirical test, "A test of indirect grounding of abstract concepts using multimodal distributional semantics" (PMC9577286), operationalizes exactly this radiate-from-concrete-neighbors mechanism computationally, which is a direct precedent for the "grounding-reach" acceptance test specified below.

### CSKG as the entity-resolution shortcut (new this drill)

CSKG ("CSKG: The CommonSense Knowledge Graph," Ilievski, Szekely, Zhang; arXiv:2012.11490, github.com/usc-isi-i2/cskg) already merges **ConceptNet, ATOMIC, FrameNet, Roget, Visual Genome, Wikidata-CS, and WordNet** into one consolidated graph: 2.2M nodes, 6M edges, with 251,517 `mw:SameAs` cross-source identity links and 45,659 `fn:HasLexicalUnit` links already computed and published. This is directly the "cross-source entity resolution to link CN/WN/FN/Wikidata views of one concept" mechanics asked for in Q3 -- **it does not need to be rebuilt**, only adopted as the base identity layer and then re-densified/re-filtered around the grounded core. Its own aggregate density (6M/2.2M ~ avg-deg 5.5) is still below our floor graph-wide, for the same reason as full ConceptNet -- but it is the best available PRE-RESOLVED substrate to extract a dense, span-complete core FROM, rather than aligning CN/WN/Wikidata by hand.

## 2. The SPANNING-BASIS requirement (PRIMARY criterion, per Director course-correction)

**The claim, stated precisely:** a future concept grounds only by projecting onto (correlating with) dimensions already present and already grounded in the core. If the core's dimensional coverage has a gap -- no channel encodes texture, no channel encodes quantity, no channel encodes causal/logical structure -- then any future concept whose meaning lives PRIMARILY on that missing dimension has nothing to correlate against, and stays a floating symbol regardless of how densely it is otherwise connected. This is a restatement, in graph/correlation language, of Harnad's dual-code account (direct grounding for the kernel/MGS, indirect symbolic-recombination grounding for everything else, contingent on reducibility to the kernel) -- so the spanning-basis requirement is not a novel claim this drill invents; it is 35 years of active cognitive-science literature, newly mapped onto a co-ingest/correlation mechanism.

**Proposed basis (four channel families, each covering a different primitive dimension that no single norm set covers alone):**

1. **Sensorimotor axes** -- Lancaster Sensorimotor Norms (Lynott & Connell et al., Behav. Res. Methods 2020, PMC7280349): 11 dimensions across 6 perceptual modalities (visual, auditory, gustatory, olfactory, haptic, interoceptive) and 5 action effectors (hand/arm, foot/leg, mouth/throat, head, torso), 39,707 words covered. Vision dominates numerically but the multi-effector action dimensions are the ONLY published source with effector-specific action grounding, distinct from pure perception. Covers: physical, sensory, embodied-action concepts.
2. **Semantic primes + molecules (NSM)** -- Wierzbicka & Goddard's Natural Semantic Metalanguage: 65 semantic primes cross-linguistically validated in 30+ languages (e.g. GOOD, BAD, DO, HAPPEN, WANT, THINK, BECAUSE, IF, NOT, SAME, PART, KIND, BIG, SMALL, TIME, PLACE, MOVE, TOUCH) plus ~50 universal semantic molecules (MAN, WOMAN, CHILD, FIRE, WATER, HAND, MOUTH, LONG, ROUND, etc.). This is the ONLY known basis that explicitly covers logical/relational/abstract meaning (causal connectives, quantifiers, mental-state predicates) that sensorimotor norms structurally cannot reach -- exactly the "sadness/multiplication/because" gap the Director flagged. Small, closed list (~115 words total); expect very high overlap with the MGS/kernel (these are near-definitionally the highest-frequency, most basic words in any language).
3. **Affect/valence-arousal-dominance + concreteness + age-of-acquisition scalars** -- Warriner et al. (VAD, ~14k words), Brysbaert et al. concreteness (~40k words), Kuperman et al. AoA (~30k words). Already integrated in this session's `exp_grounding_multiattribute_fusion_v1` pipeline (concreteness, aoa, valence/arousal/dominance channels were tested; valence/arousal/dominance were pruned by the independence gate in favor of visual/haptic/interoceptive/olfactory, per the 2026-07-10 12:33 ship log above -- worth re-checking whether that pruning still holds once NSM/kernel-restricted ingest changes the node population).
4. **The empirical grounding kernel / MGS itself** -- not a dimension, but the STRUCTURAL selection criterion for WHICH words the above three channel families are attached to first: words that are simultaneously (a) high-frequency, (b) low-AoA, (c) feedback-vertex-set load-bearing (removing them breaks the dictionary's definitional closure) are exactly the words guaranteed to have the highest correlation-reach to the rest of the vocabulary once grounded.

**Anti-pattern, stated explicitly for the record:** FB15k-237, CoDEx, Hetionet, raw Wikidata-by-degree -- ALL of these are dense-but-narrow. They span named-entity/factual/biomedical relational space extremely well and span almost none of {sensory quality, affect, quantity-as-concept (not quantity-as-fact), logical/causal connective, basic action}. Picking the densest corpus as the "grounded core" without checking dimensional span would silently reproduce this session's earlier finding (grounding works as an MM but doesn't chain) for a different, non-obvious reason: not because grounding doesn't propagate, but because the core never had the dimension the ungrounded concept needed to project onto in the first place. This is a DIFFERENT failure mode than the density-floor failure mode identified earlier this session, and the two must not be conflated when interpreting a future negative result.

## 3. The grounding-reach test (core-acceptance gate, operationalized)

**Purpose:** make "does the core span enough dimensions" a measurable, falsifiable property of a specific candidate core, not an assumption.

**Procedure:**
1. Build a **stratified diverse probe set** of 200-500 concepts, deliberately sampled OUTSIDE the grounded core, across at minimum 6 domains chosen to stress different primitive dimensions: (a) physical/object (e.g. "gravel," "sponge"), (b) emotional/affective (e.g. "melancholy," "relief"), (c) mathematical/quantitative (e.g. "multiplication," "ratio," "infinity"), (d) social/institutional (e.g. "contract," "debt," "citizenship"), (e) temporal/aspectual (e.g. "duration," "interruption," "eventually"), (f) purely relational/logical (e.g. "because," "unless," "whereas" -- note several of these may already BE NSM primes, in which case substitute their less-primitive cousins, e.g. "consequently," "notwithstanding"). Sampling should deliberately include domains the chosen core's channels do NOT explicitly cover, to stress-test span rather than confirm it.
2. For each probe concept, measure whether it has a **positive-margin grounded correlation** to the core, using the SAME discriminator machinery already built and validated this session for the multi-attribute grounding-fusion FULL run (gap/lift over baseline, cv across seeds, leave-one-seed-out stability, high-vs-low-degree strata, beats-single-channel margin, redundancy/scramble controls) -- i.e. this is not a new metric to invent, it is the existing MM-grounding discriminator re-applied to a deliberately adversarial, domain-diverse probe population instead of a random or convenience sample.
3. **HARD-PASS (core is span-adequate):** >=70% of probe concepts (deflated target; the fusion FULL run this session cleared 5 of 7 gates, so 70% is a comparable, non-inflated bar) show a positive-margin grounded correlation, AND failures are NOT concentrated in a single domain (a scattered ~30% failure rate is consistent with "some concepts are always harder," a domain-concentrated failure is not).
4. **HARD-FAIL, case A (missing dimension):** failures cluster heavily in one or two of the 6 domains (e.g. all mathematical/quantitative probes fail) while other domains pass comfortably. Diagnosis: the core lacks a channel for that dimension -- ACTION: add a channel (e.g. for mathematical concepts, a symbolic-math primitive set or Wikidata-quantity-as-CONCEPT rather than as-fact; for social/institutional concepts, a legal/economic relation-type donor analogous to ATOMIC's social relations) and re-run the test. This is a structural, falsifiable expand-the-core signal, not a mechanism failure.
5. **HARD-FAIL, case B (mechanism bottleneck, not core coverage):** failures are roughly UNIFORM across all 6 domains regardless of core composition changes. Diagnosis: this reproduces this session's `exp_grounding_rung2_loop_closer_v1` finding (grounding informs attribute-level meaning but does not, by itself, transfer to relational inference) -- the bottleneck is the decoder/inference mechanism, not the core's dimensional span, and further core-expansion work should be paused in favor of mechanism work.

This test is the honest, falsifiable version of "is the core a spanning basis" -- it replaces the impossible-to-verify-in-advance claim "this core spans meaning" with a measurable pass/fail on a specific, pre-registered, adversarially-diverse probe population.

## Curriculum / ingest-then-consolidate mechanics (Q2 + Q3, folded together)

**Sequencing, brain-grounded (concrete-before-abstract, core-outward, never abstract-first):**

1. **Shell 0 (the core):** MGS/Kernel-selected words (highest-frequency, lowest-AoA, feedback-vertex-set load-bearing) UNIONED with the 65 NSM primes + ~50 molecules. Each Shell-0 member ingested WITH its full multi-channel grounded-attribute vector already attached (sensorimotor 11-dim + concreteness + AoA + VAD where available) -- ground-at-ingest, not ground-after-the-fact. Cross-source identity resolved via CSKG's existing `mw:SameAs`/`fn:HasLexicalUnit` links, not re-derived.
2. **Consolidate Shell 0** with the diffusion-with-restart consolidation engine BEFORE adding Shell 1. This mirrors the developmental finding that abstract vocabulary share grows slowly and monotonically from a stable concrete base (10% at age 4 -> 40% at age 12) -- never a flood-fill.
3. **Shell k (k=1,2,3...):** BFS-expand outward from the grounded frontier via ConceptNet/CSKG relational edges (or dictionary-definitional edges, per the Blondin Massé "radiates outward" mechanism), one shell at a time. Ground-at-ingest for any Shell-k member that itself has norm coverage (many will, since Lancaster/Brysbaert/Kuperman/Warriner each cover 30-40k words -- far more than the core alone).
4. **Verify-at-ingest, per shell:** run the grounding-reach test (Section 3) restricted to the newly-added shell's members before consolidating the NEXT shell. A shell with a HARD-FAIL (case A) triggers a channel-expansion detour before continuing outward; a HARD-FAIL (case B) triggers a full pause on core-expansion.
5. **Firewalled held-out split:** reuse CoDEx's engineered low-leakage methodology (hard negatives, type-consistent sampling) for the relational read-off eval, per the prior corpus-survey note. ADDITIONALLY hold out an entire probe population (the Section-3 diverse probe set) that is NEVER ingested/consolidated during core-building -- these are reserved specifically to test whether concepts encountered LATER in the ingest stream latch onto the coordinate system the core establishes. Selection objective for what counts as a "good" ingest batch at any shell = inferential capacity / bridge-edge count / relation-type diversity (does this shell add NEW ways to connect existing grounded material) -- NOT raw triple count or confidence-volume, per the standing discipline already in force for this project.

## Substrate-product implications

If built and the grounding-reach test clears HARD-PASS: the substrate gets a genuine MEANING COORDINATE SYSTEM -- a small, verified-spanning set of grounded primitives such that any future ingested concept's "grounded representation" is literally its measured correlation/projection onto that basis, not a separate disconnected attribute lookup. This is the structural difference between "we bolted sensory norms onto some nodes" (this session's fusion/rung-2b work) and "the substrate has a coordinate system new knowledge is expressed in" (the Director's framing). It does NOT, by itself, resolve the previously-identified decoder/mechanism gap (grounding-doesn't-chain-to-relational-inference) -- a span-complete core makes the INPUT fair for that mechanism work, it is not a substitute for it.

Compute this subgraph's own k-core / avg-degree, restricted to THESE nodes and their edges among each other (not the whole graph). **HARD-PASS:** local avg-degree among core members >= 20-25 (does not need to hit the full 37 floor, since the core is meant to be a small, richly-interconnected coordinate basis, not the whole relational spine) -- proceed to attribute-graft + grounding-reach probe. **HARD-FAIL:** local avg-degree collapses to the same low regime as the graph-wide average (~2.6-5.5) -- meaning even the highest-frequency, most basic words are NOT densely interconnected in ConceptNet/CSKG's own graph structure, which would be a surprising and important negative result (implying ConceptNet's assertion-collection process under-connects exactly the words that should be best-connected) and would force the relational-spine choice back toward a purpose-built small dense graph over just the core vocabulary rather than extracting from an existing resource.
