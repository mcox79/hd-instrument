# Research: grammar-construction resources for role assignment (2026-07-20)

Director synthesis of a 3-way parallel lit-scan (Construction Grammar/Constructicon inventories; Levin alternations + VerbNet + descriptive grammars; preposition semantics + UD dependency-to-role linking rules), dispatched off the USER's walkthrough of 10 who-is-affected sentences resolved by explicit GRAMMAR rules (word order, passive-flip, load/spray alternation, before-connective, preposition semantics), not grounding/simulation. Builds on and credited: `notes/research_plausibility_web_engineering_resources_adoptable_foundation_2026-07-20.md` (VerbNet/PropBank/FrameNet as SEMANTIC role-label resources), `notes/research_affectedness_decomposition_typelevel_lookup_vs_perinstance_2026-07-20.md` (type-level-lookup vs per-instance-residual split on the SEMANTIC axis). This drill is a DIFFERENT, orthogonal axis: STRUCTURAL role assignment — given a parse, which syntactic slot maps to which role, independent of semantic plausibility. Deflated throughout per lit-scan-calibration; novel-synthesis P capped at 0.50.

## HEADLINE

**The USER's hypothesis is directionally correct and stronger than the affectedness-decomposition drill's semantic-axis findings: a STRUCTURAL role-assignment layer is genuinely available, genuinely machine-readable, and genuinely different in kind from the thematic-fit/distributional reader already tried and bounded at 0.557 -- but it exists only as a STACK of three separate resources (VerbNet's per-alternation static frames + UD's `obl:agent`/proto-role linking rules + preposition-sense lexicons), not as one off-the-shelf construction-role-assigner, and one of the USER's five example constructions (the "before" connective) has NO resource coverage in anything found this session.** VerbNet does not compute alternation logic (no generative "container->holistic-Patient" rule) -- it hand-authors a SEPARATE STATIC FRAME per syntactic variant, which is still adoptable as a lookup keyed on full syntactic-frame-pattern, not verb alone. UD's passive-agent-demotion relation (`obl:agent`) is a confirmed, named, standard mechanism -- the cleanest single win in the whole catalog, essentially free given a correct dependency parse. FrameNet's Constructicon is the best single inventory of Goldberg-style form-meaning pairings in the abstract but has stalled since ~2016 and its coverage of the concrete alternation-frame-lookup case is unconfirmed.

## Cheap decisive test

Build a construction/frame-matcher: UD dependency parse -> `nsubj`/`obj`/`obl`/`obl:agent`/`nsubj:pass` relations, each `obl` disambiguated by its preposition via a preposition-sense lookup (by=agent-of-passive/with=instrument-or-comitative), matched against VerbNet's per-class SYNTACTIC FRAME list (not just its role-name list) to resolve alternation cases (load/spray, dative, causative/inchoative). Run on the USER's original 10 sentences plus a matched held-out set covering the same 5 construction types (word-order default, passive-flip, locative alternation, dative alternation, before/after-connective-separated events). Score EXACT role-assignment accuracy, with connective-separated sentences scored as a SEPARATE bucket (expected near-floor, since no resource here covers discourse connectives -- this is itself a control).

**Design gate (per feedback-experiment-design-gate):**
1. Real baseline = the existing thematic-fit/distributional reader (bounded 0.557 per prior work), run on the identical items.
2. Discriminator can-fail: the frame-matcher must be able to score WORSE than baseline on any bucket where the matcher can't resolve a frame (e.g. two frames are both grammatically well-formed and only semantics picks between them -- must show as a genuine miss, not silently default to the right answer).
3. Difficulty on: include genuinely alternating pairs (same verb, two different syntactic frames, e.g. "loaded hay onto the truck" / "loaded the truck with hay") so the matcher must actually distinguish frames, not just default to a single fixed role template per verb.
4. One variable differs: only the ADDITION of the construction/frame layer (as a filter/prior, or standalone), same eval items, same gold labels.

**Arms:** (a) thematic-fit baseline alone; (b) construction/frame-layer alone (UD parse + VerbNet frame-match + preposition-sense lookup); (c) construction-layer as a hard PRIOR/filter, thematic-fit breaks remaining ties; (d) scrambled-frame control (random syntactic-frame-to-role mapping -- must not beat chance, sanity check for eval-harness leakage).

## Falsifiable predictions

**HARD-PASS:** on the non-connective subset (4 of the 5 construction types: word-order, passive-flip, locative alternation, dative alternation), the construction/frame layer (b) or combined (c) resolves role assignment at >=0.90 accuracy -- because these are claimed to be near-deterministic given correct parse+frame-match, this should look like a ceiling effect, not a graded statistical win like thematic-fit's 0.557. AND it does not require semantic/thematic-fit information to pick between competing frames on this subset (if it does, the "form alone determines meaning" premise has silently collapsed back into needing plausibility, which would falsify the separability claim).

**HARD-FAIL:** accuracy on the non-connective subset is <0.75, OR the matcher requires semantic disambiguation to choose among multiple grammatically-valid frame matches on >20% of items (meaning VerbNet's static per-alternation frames don't actually disambiguate the way the USER's grammar-rule examples suggest -- the frame boundary is fuzzier than the book examples imply), OR VerbNet's SELRESTRS/frame coverage is too incomplete across common verbs for the lookup to fire on most real sentences (this is the same coverage-completeness risk flagged unverified in the affectedness-decomposition drill, now applied to the SYNTACTIC-frame side rather than the SELRESTRS-type side).

**Must-fail control:** the connective-separated ("before") bucket and the scrambled-frame control must both land at or near chance -- if the connective bucket unexpectedly scores well, some other signal (likely surface heuristics leaking through the UD parse) is doing the work, not the claimed resource.

## Cross-thread synthesis

### 1. Construction Grammar / Constructicon (lit-scan 1)

Goldberg's canonical argument-structure constructions (ditransitive "X causes Y to receive Z", caused-motion "X causes Y to move Z", resultative, conative, way-construction) are FORM->MEANING pairings that license role assignment independent of, or supplementing, the verb's own lexical semantics (the canonical "sneezed the napkin off the table" case). The **Berkeley FrameNet Constructicon** ("Beta Constructicon", Fillmore/Lee-Goldman/Rhomieux; Baker & Ellsworth LREC 2012, aclanthology.org/L12-1319) is the most complete machine-readable general-ASC inventory: ~73-75 constructions, XML, downloadable via FrameNet data releases + NLTK API (Schneider & Wooters, arXiv:1703.07438), with construction elements explicitly linked to FrameNet frame roles -- genuine form->role encoding, not just a named list. Two caveats: (1) coverage has been flat since ~2016 (stalled, not actively growing); (2) whether it has a DEDICATED entry for the locative (load/spray) alternation specifically was not confirmed -- flagged unverified. **Fluid Construction Grammar** (Steels et al.) is the most mechanistically complete formalism (an actual bidirectional parser doing form<->meaning unification with role binding) but has no single canonical downloadable general-English grammar -- FCG grammars are hand-built per project/domain. Dunietz's causal constructicon (CMU, Causeway/DeepCx, BECAUSE corpus) is the right METHODOLOGY (corpus-built constructicon -> lookup) but wrong SCOPE (causal relations only). SBCG has only a small non-maintained proof-of-concept implementation (github.com/RuiPChaves/SBCG).

### 2. Levin alternations + VerbNet (lit-scan 2) -- the decisive finding

Direct inspection of live VerbNet class pages (spray-9.7, put-9.1, give-13.1, break-45.1) confirms: **VerbNet does NOT encode a general, computable alternation-triggering RULE** (e.g. "if object=container then Patient-holistic"). Instead its builders hand-authored a SEPARATE, STATIC frame entry per syntactic variant of each alternating verb class -- "NP V NP PP.destination" and "NP V NP PP" (locative-alternation frame) are two independent lookup entries with independently-specified role-to-syntax mappings, both using the same role names (Theme, Destination) without a distinct "holistic-Patient" relabeling. The USER's question "do we use the alternation rules for role assignment, or just the role labels" has a precise answer: **neither purely** -- VerbNet gives pre-computed PER-FRAME role labels, keyed on the FULL syntactic frame pattern (not the verb alone), which functions adoptably as a lookup table for exactly the cases the USER named, but does not generalize to novel alternations via a shared rule the way Goldberg's prose account implies. The holistic/totally-affected SEMANTIC entailment of the locative alternation (object = completely affected) is largely unformalized in VerbNet's predicates -- present only implicitly in which frame is matched. No full machine-readable digitization of Levin's ~80-alternation catalog exists; the closest partial resource is **LaVA/FAVA** (5 of ~80 alternations, ~515 verbs, ~9,413 generated example sentences, arXiv:2209.04811) -- useful as a small held-out generalization test, not a comprehensive source. **CGELBank** (github.com/nert-nlp/cgel; arXiv:2210.00394, arXiv:2305.17347) is a genuine computational derivative of Huddleston & Pullum's CGEL but its own annotation manual states passive/raising/control are "addressed in CGEL, but not made explicit in trees" -- no passive-role-flip rule extraction exists there yet. No comparable derivative was found for Quirk et al. (flagged unverified-absent, not confirmed-absent).

### 3. Preposition semantics + UD linking rules (lit-scan 3) -- the cleanest single win

**UD's `obl:agent` relation** (universaldependencies.org/u/dep/obl-agent.html) is a confirmed, standard, NAMED dependency relation specifically for passive-agent demotion ("X was tutored by Y" -> Y attaches as `obl:agent`, X as `nsubj:pass`) -- this is the single cleanest, most directly machine-readable rule found in the entire scan: given a correct UD parse, the passive-flip the USER described is essentially free, no additional resource needed beyond the parser itself. Caveat: UD's own documentation flags agent-vs-instrument ambiguity in oblique/instrumental marking as a known cross-treebank inconsistency. **The Preposition Project (TPP) / PDEP** (Litkowski et al., arXiv:2104.08922, clres.com) is the most complete sense-level preposition resource (43,129 sense-tagged instances tied to FrameNet, inheritance hierarchy of preposition-sense primitives) -- designed for exactly this role-labeling purpose, but the exact granularity of role labels (does it literally distinguish by=agent-of-passive from by=locative-proximity as machine-readable tags) could not be confirmed this session (source pages 403-blocked). **PredPatt + Reisinger et al.'s Semantic Proto-Roles** (2015, TACL 3:475, aclanthology.org/Q15-1034; White et al. 2016 UDS, D16-1177) is the standard published rule-based UD-to-thematic-role linking system in the NLP literature -- it operationalizes Dowty's (1991) Proto-Agent/Proto-Patient as scalar properties (volition, causation, change-of-state) computed over UD-parsed predicate-argument structure, handling passive/alternation implicitly via the property-based (not categorical) design rather than hand-coded exceptions.

### 4. What's missing -- the "before" gap

None of the three scans surfaced a resource for discourse-connective sense-tagging (the USER's "before cleanly separates the two events" case). This is a genuine, confirmed gap in this session's coverage, not a finding that no such resource exists -- the standard NLP resource for this (Penn Discourse TreeBank / PDTB-style connective-sense annotation) is known from general background but was NOT verified or drilled this cycle. **Flag as next-drill candidate**, adjacent to the `coding-theory`/`inference` fields already in the field-advisor matrix but new to this specific thread.

### 5. Synthesis with the semantic-axis drill (affectedness-decomposition, same day)

The affectedness-decomposition note split "who is affected" into a TYPE-level lookup (verb-class predicts semantic type of affected argument -- VerbNet SELRESTRS, PropBank Arg1, ATOMIC effects) and a per-instance residual (near-chance on maximally-ambiguous items, Chersoni et al. 53%). That entire analysis operates AFTER a syntactic parse already exists and assumes the reader already knows which NP fills which syntactic slot -- it is about picking the right FILLER given a role slot. This drill is upstream and orthogonal: it is about determining the role slot itself from FORM (word order, voice, alternation frame, preposition) BEFORE any semantic-plausibility computation is needed. The two problems compose, not compete: structural role-assignment (this drill) should in principle resolve role labels for the majority of ordinary sentences from grammar alone (canonical-sentoid / good-enough-parsing literature, e.g. Bever 1970's NVN heuristic and Ferreira's good-enough-parsing studies, both already-established psycholinguistic background, suggest people rely on structural cues as a strong prior in most sentences) -- semantic thematic-fit and type-lookup should only be load-bearing on the residual where structure underdetermines the answer (ambiguous PP-attachment, genuinely reversible active-voice sentences with no case-marking cue, garden-path resolution). If the reader's 0.557 bound was measured WITHOUT first extracting and exploiting this structural layer, that bound is not informative about the ceiling of a combined structural+semantic reader -- this is the single most actionable implication of this drill and should be checked against the actual reader pipeline before further semantic-axis work.

## Substrate-product implications

- If HARD-PASS: a genuinely CHEAP, glass-box, build-time-only addition -- three static resources (VerbNet frame list, a UD parser's own `obl:agent`/`nsubj:pass`/`obl` output, and a preposition-sense lookup seeded from TPP/PDEP or hand-curated for the small closed class of role-marking prepositions: by/with/onto/into/from/to) stacked as a pre-filter ahead of any thematic-fit computation. No new architecture, no runtime LLM.
- **Substrate binding proposal** (extends the E6 idea from `notes/research_drill_nl_understanding_universal_unlock_3x_2026-06-11.md`, refined by this drill's VerbNet finding): store one hypervector per (verb-class, syntactic-frame) pair -- NOT one per verb-class alone -- since VerbNet's own resolution of the alternation is at the frame level, not the verb-class level. The parse-derived query is built by binding each filled syntactic slot (subject/object/oblique) to its dependency-relation tag AND, for obliques, to the specific preposition lexeme (so "PP-with" binds differently from "PP-onto", giving the query vector itself the discriminating information needed to select between the locative alternation's two frames without any additional rule). Passive voice is encoded as part of the same key (voice-marker bound into the query) so "NP1 was V-en by NP2" naturally binds to a DIFFERENT stored frame-vector than the active form, with the passive frame's role assignment (patient=NP1, agent=NP2-via-obl:agent) already correct BY CONSTRUCTION of which frame-vector it matches -- no separate voice-detection-then-flip step required. Role-slot extraction after the best-matching frame is found is a standard unbind operation. This reuses the existing bind/unbind/argmax-cosine machinery; the new piece is the FRAME-KEYED (not verb-keyed) construction inventory itself, sourced from VerbNet's frame list rather than invented.
- If HARD-FAIL (VerbNet frame coverage too sparse, or frame-selection itself requires semantic disambiguation on a meaningful fraction of items): this would mean the USER's "explicit grammar rules are the load-bearing available part" hypothesis is TRUE IN PRINCIPLE (the resources exist, are machine-readable, are glass-box) but bounded in the same way the semantic axis was -- coverage/completeness-limited rather than architecture-limited. Still diagnostic progress: it would mean the next investment is CURATING/EXTENDING the frame-pattern coverage (a data-engineering task on top of VerbNet, analogous to how LaVA/FAVA extended Levin's alternation catalog), not abandoning the structural-layer idea.
- Either outcome does not touch or require reopening the semantic-axis closures (29375's per-instance text-derivation null, the affectedness-decomposition type/instance split) -- this is a genuinely separate, upstream mechanism.

## P (deflated per lit-scan-calibration; novel-synthesis capped at 0.50)

- **P(the VerbNet-frame + UD-obl:agent + preposition-lookup stack, taken together, correctly resolves role assignment on the USER's 4 non-connective construction types at or near ceiling, i.e. HARD-PASS band fires): 0.45** (novel synthesis, capped under 0.50). Built from: UD's `obl:agent` mechanism is confirmed and should be close to free (high sub-confidence ~0.75 on that piece alone); VerbNet's per-alternation frames are confirmed to exist as separate lookup entries (~0.70 sub-confidence they discriminate correctly when matched); but frame-list coverage/completeness across common verbs is unverified and independently flagged as a risk in the semantic-axis sibling drill for the analogous SELRESTRS case, and no end-to-end test of the STACKED system exists anywhere in the literature found -- this is an assembly nobody has published, not a replicated result.
- **P(the "before"/connective construction has zero coverage in resources found this session, i.e. that gap is real and not an artifact of search terms): 0.80** (high confidence -- three independent scans with different search angles all missed it; consistent with connectives being a genuinely different resource family (discourse-level, not argument-structure-level) that wasn't in scope for any of the three dispatched angles).
- **P(a construction/frame-based structural role-assigner is a genuinely different, additive mechanism from the tried thematic-fit reader -- not redundant with it): 0.55** (the LINGUISTIC distinction between structural/form-driven role assignment and semantic/plausibility-driven role assignment is well-established and not really in question; the uncertainty is entirely about the SUBSTRATE side -- whether the existing reader pipeline already exploits parse structure in a way that makes this partially redundant. This drill cannot verify that without inspecting the reader's actual feature set; flagged as the single most important thing to check before banking this finding as a fresh lever.)
- **P(VerbNet's per-class frame coverage is complete enough across common verbs for this to fire on a majority of ordinary sentences without needing new frame authoring): 0.40** (unverified; consistent with the parallel, independently-derived 0.40 estimate in the affectedness-decomposition note for SELRESTRS completeness -- the same underlying resource, same completeness risk, now on the syntactic-frame side).
