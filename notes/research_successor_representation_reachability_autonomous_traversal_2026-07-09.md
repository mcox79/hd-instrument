# Research: successor-representation reachability signal for autonomous multi-hop hop-selection

**Filed by:** research sub-agent. **Trigger:** two landed negatives on autonomous subgoal derivation, both via proximity heuristics: (1) `grounding_multihop_autonomous_subgoal_greedy_v1` — greedy goal-cosine (aim continuously at the distant goal) landed MIDDLE_BAND_CG_PARTIAL, reach@2=0.181 (0.363x supplied-waypoint ceiling 0.499); (2) landmark/hub-node routing (per `[[research-landmark-subgoal-hub-routing-autonomous-traversal]]`) landed HARD_FAIL, reach@2=0.111 — WORSE than plain greedy, because a nearby high-degree hub is not necessarily on the path toward a SPECIFIC goal. **Diagnosis under test:** proximity (to goal, or to a generically-important hub) is the wrong signal category; the missing ingredient is REACHABILITY — a multi-step, goal-conditioned "does this neighbor actually lead toward G" score. **Method:** 3 parallel Sonnet lit-scan sub-agents — (1) hippocampal successor representation (SR) as predictive map; (2) ML successor features / goal-conditioned value / closed-form resolvent computation; (3) graph-mining resolvent methods (personalized PageRank / random-walk-with-restart) and numerical feasibility at KB-graph scale. Generic math/neuro/graph-theory terms only in external queries, no substrate-specific mechanism names or numbers, per query-privacy discipline.

## HEADLINE

**All 3 angles converge cleanly, and — unusually for this research thread — the convergence includes a directly-published, name-brand mathematical equivalence, not just a structural/cross-domain analogy: the hippocampal successor representation (SR), goal-conditioned value functions, and personalized PageRank / random-walk-with-restart (RWR) in graph mining are THE SAME MATHEMATICAL OBJECT — a resolvent M = (I - gamma*T)^-1 (Dayan 1993; explicit isomorphism stated in Millidge, "Equivalence of Personalized PageRank and Successor Representations," arXiv 2512.24722). This single fact explains BOTH prior failures at once, not just the second one:**

- **Greedy goal-cosine failed because it is a single-step, static-embedding-distance signal** — it has no mechanism for "does this neighbor's real, multi-step future actually pass through the goal," only "does this neighbor look like the goal right now." SR/resolvent scoring is explicitly and categorically NOT a distance metric (Dayan 1993: M(s,s') = expected discounted future occupancy of s' from s, under the actual transition dynamics) — this is the field's own stated reason SR was invented: to replace static similarity with dynamics-aware multi-step prediction.
- **Landmark/hub routing failed because degree/centrality-based hub selection is GOAL-AGNOSTIC** — a hub is "generally well-connected," which says nothing about whether it is well-connected specifically TOWARD the current goal. The personalized-vs-global PageRank distinction in the graph-mining literature is exactly this: global PageRank (source-agnostic, i.e. structurally a hub/degree-centrality score) ranks nodes by general importance; PERSONALIZED PageRank is rooted at a specific seed/target and is goal-conditioned by construction. Hub-routing used the goal-agnostic variant; SR/resolvent scoring is inherently the goal-conditioned variant.

**The proposed fix is therefore not "yet another heuristic to try" but the literature's own explanation for why the two prior attempts sit on opposite sides of the one axis (single-step-static vs. multi-step-dynamic; goal-agnostic vs. goal-conditioned) that actually matters, with SR/resolvent scoring being the one point in that 2x2 space nobody has tried yet (multi-step AND goal-conditioned).**

## The brain mechanism (hippocampal SR as predictive map)

1. **Dayan (1993, Neural Computation)** — original formalism: M(s,s') = expected discounted number of future visits to s' from s under policy pi. This is a matrix, not a scalar distance; it is (a) multi-step (aggregates a full discounted trajectory distribution, not a one-hop relation) and (b) policy-dependent (defined relative to actual transition dynamics, not raw embedding geometry). This is the foundational reason SR differs in KIND from a proximity heuristic, not just in degree.
2. **Stachenfeld, Botvinick & Gershman (2017, Nat Neurosci), "The hippocampus as a predictive map"** — place-cell population activity approximates rows of M; grid cells encode a compressed spectral basis for M (grid periodicity = SR-matrix eigenvectors, eigenvalue magnitude sets spatial scale). **de Cothi & Barry (2020/2022, Current Biology), "Predictive maps in rats and humans for spatial navigation"** — direct behavioral test: both species' trajectories in a cluttered, obstacle-laden maze are best matched by SR-based agents, not vector-to-goal or model-free caching agents. This is direct evidence that biological navigation switches to SR-like computation specifically in the regime where naive proximity should fail (cluttered/bottlenecked spaces) — the exact regime diagnosed in the KB-graph traversal problem.
3. **Why SR differs from proximity, concretely**: V(s) = M(s,.) . R; with reward concentrated at goal G, V(s) approx M(s,G) — expected future occupancy of G. A neighbor can be embedding-close to G yet structurally disconnected from any path that continues toward G (low M(s',G) despite high raw similarity); conversely a neighbor that looks "farther" in embedding space but sits on the real corridor toward G will have high M(s',G). Direct grounding: **PLOS Biology (2025), goal-directed navigation strategies** explicitly contrasts "vector-based" (direction/Euclidean) strategies against "state-transition-based" (graph/SR-like) strategies — vector-based dominates in open fields (73.8% of choices) but participants SWITCH to transition-based strategies specifically in cluttered/obstacle environments, i.e. exactly where proximity heuristics are known to fail.
4. **Russek, Momennejad, Botvinick, Gershman & Daw (2017, PLOS Comp Bio)** — model-based/model-free reconciliation via TD-on-SR; reward-revaluation experiments are the standard behavioral proof that SR-value is NOT cached distance (instant correct valuation of a newly-placed reward without re-traversal is impossible for a pure distance cache, possible for SR . reward). **Honest caveat surfaced by this same paper**: classical SR-TD FAILS at transition-revaluation (detour/blocked-path tasks) — if the graph structure changes, SR must be relearned via further experience, it does not instantly re-derive the new best route. This is a real limitation for a system where the transition structure is not stationary, but is NOT a live concern for the KB-graph case (the graph is static and fully known), where closed-form recomputation replaces TD-relearning entirely.
5. **Discount-factor smearing is explicitly discussed, not hand-waved**: Momennejad (2020, Curr Opin Behav Sci) frames predictive representations as needing MULTIPLE timescales because a single high gamma trades away local/order structure for long-horizon reachability. Piray & Daw (2021) add that hippocampus may compute SR for a fixed default/diffusive policy (not the literally-optimal policy) — a deliberate bias-variance choice, generalizing better at the cost of exact optimality.

## The ML / closed-form mechanism (the load-bearing computational result)

1. **Successor features (Barreto et al., NeurIPS 2017) + Generalized Policy Improvement** generalize tabular SR to feature space and goal-set reuse — but for a single, discrete, known goal node, this generalization is mathematical overkill: tabular SR/M[:,goal] already gives the exact answer. UVFA (Schaul et al. 2015) / goal-conditioned Q / HER (Andrychowicz et al. 2017) formalize V(s,g) as a LEARNED function approximator for the case where the transition model is unknown or the goal space is too large to tabulate. **Both frameworks solve a harder problem (unknown dynamics) than the KB-graph case has** — a fully-known, finite graph with a discrete goal node is the degenerate/easy corner of the UVFA generalization, where learning is replaceable outright by direct linear algebra.
2. **The closed-form question, directly confirmed**: the field's own literature explicitly distinguishes "model-based SR" (MB-SR, exact inversion when T is known) from the default TD-learned SR assumed throughout most of the RL literature (T unknown, must be learned). One source states plainly that "the SR can be calculated directly from the transition matrix... [direct inversion is] the expensive fallback when T happens to be known" — TD-learning is a workaround for unknown dynamics, not an assumption that has to be carried into a fully-known-graph setting like a KB.
3. **SR = Personalized PageRank, explicitly published**: Millidge, "Equivalence of Personalized PageRank and Successor Representations" (arXiv 2512.24722) states the isomorphism directly, identifying PPR's teleportation coefficient alpha with SR's discount gamma and M = (I - alpha*P)^-1. A second, independent paper (Avrachenkov et al., arXiv 2605.00532, verified via directly-quoted abstract) establishes that MDP policy evaluation equals PageRank of a time-reversed chain, discount<->teleportation, reward<->restart distribution — an independent confirmation of the same underlying identity from a different author group.
4. **Computation cost — the most load-bearing finding for buildability**: single-TARGET reachability (one column of the resolvent, i.e. exactly what's needed here: "which of my neighbors leads toward THIS goal") is a NAMED, separately-optimized algorithm family in graph mining — **backward push** (Fujiwara et al., "Personalized PageRank to a Target Node" + "Revisited"). This is the dual of the better-known single-source forward-push (Andersen, Chung & Lang 2006, FOCS) and modern accelerations (FORA, SpeedPPR) that answer approximate single-source/single-target PPR queries on **billion-edge graphs in under 1 second**. This regime is far above the KB graph's scale.
5. **Feasibility at n~4,000-5,000 nodes, degree ~tens**: at this scale, BOTH extremes are cheap — full dense O(n^3) inversion is roughly 1-5 seconds on ordinary hardware (inference from BLAS throughput scaling, no direct benchmark found at this exact n), and a single sparse linear solve (CG/BiCGSTAB) or truncated Neumann series is comfortably sub-second (the local-push literature's target regime is 2-5 orders of magnitude larger than this). **The KB graph is small enough that the approximate/local-push machinery built for web-scale graphs is not even necessary — a simple, exact sparse solve or truncated power-iteration suffices.**
6. **Truncated Neumann series gives a literal, principled hop-budget interpretation**: M_k = sum_{t=0}^{k} gamma^t T^t is exactly a weighted sum of k-hop walk counts; truncating at k terms IS choosing a lookahead horizon, with geometric convergence rate ~gamma^k. Small gamma (0.5-0.75) needs single-digit-to-~10 terms; gamma approaching 0.9-0.95+ needs tens-to-hundreds of terms for tight convergence (standard PageRank power-iteration convergence-rate literature, log(tolerance)/log(gamma)).
7. **Degeneracy at gamma->1, confirmed from multiple independent angles**: as alpha->0 (gamma->1), the resolvent converges toward the graph's GLOBAL stationary distribution — which is goal-AGNOSTIC by construction, and rank orderings become "extremely sensitive to small alpha perturbations" (documented rank-reversal across the full ordering space). This is not classical numerical ill-conditioning so much as loss of MEANING (the walk "forgets" its target/seed) — directly the same failure mode as landmark/hub routing (goal-agnostic global centrality), reached via a different mechanism (gamma pushed too high) rather than being built-in from the start.
8. **Community structure interacts with this**: mixing-time literature confirms strong community/cluster structure slows mixing relative to expander-like graphs — meaning a KB graph with real community/bottleneck structure should RETAIN target-specificity longer (at a given gamma) than a near-expander graph, where the resolvent decays toward the goal-agnostic stationary distribution fastest. This is inference from adjacent mixing-time results, not a directly cited comparison, but it directly mirrors — a THIRD independent confirmation of — the landmark drill's own flagged, never-yet-checked risk: an expander-like KB graph with no real bottleneck/community structure would defeat BOTH landmark-routing AND high-gamma SR/resolvent scoring for the same underlying reason.

## Convergent mechanism (>=2 angles agree; here all 3 agree)

- **SR/resolvent reachability is categorically different from proximity because it is (a) multi-step (walks the real transition structure forward many steps, not a static embedding distance) and (b) goal-conditioned (computed specifically for the target node, not a generic centrality/importance score).** Brain (Dayan 1993 definition + Stachenfeld 2017 grounding + de Cothi/Barry cluttered-maze behavioral test + PLOS Biology 2025 vector-vs-transition strategy switch) + ML (Barreto/UVFA framing the SR-collapse as the closed-form special case) + graph-mining (personalized-vs-global-PageRank distinction, explicitly naming why generic centrality/hub scores are goal-agnostic) all converge on this exact two-axis distinction.
- **The closed-form resolvent is computable exactly, cheaply, with no learning required, when the transition structure is known** — ML angle (field's own "MB-SR" terminology) + graph-mining angle (backward-push single-target algorithms, billion-node-scale precedent) converge cleanly; this is the strongest, least-hedged claim across all 3 scans.
- **High-gamma (long-horizon) degeneracy toward a goal-agnostic global signal is a real, literature-documented risk, not a hypothetical** — brain angle (Momennejad multi-scale-SR motivation, Piray & Daw default-policy bias-variance) + graph-mining angle (alpha->1 stationary-distribution convergence, rank-reversal sensitivity) converge independently on the same qualitative failure mode.
- **Honest non-convergence, reported rather than papered over**: no source in any of the 3 scans directly tests the exact three-way comparison this drill needs (SR/resolvent-selection vs. greedy-cosine vs. hub/landmark-routing, on a general non-spatial KB-graph traversal task). The spatial-navigation literature's SR-vs-vector-strategy comparisons and the graph-mining literature's PPR-vs-degree-centrality framing are both directly adjacent and mechanistically on-point, but the specific three-way empirical horse race is an extrapolation bridging two literatures, not a verbatim result anywhere.

## ONE concrete substrate realization

**Closed-form (or truncated-Neumann-series) resolvent scoring over the known KB transition matrix, substituted in place of the cosine-to-goal score inside the SAME already-certified local-neighborhood-scoping + argmax loop used for both prior attempts — zero new retrieval primitive, one new scoring function plus one tunable hyperparameter (gamma):**

1. **One-time transition-matrix construction (reuses the existing local-neighborhood-scoping primitive verbatim):** for every node, its true edge/neighbor set (already retrievable) IS the sparse transition matrix T (row-normalize to make it row-stochastic, or leave as a weighted adjacency and normalize inside the solve). No new lookup mechanism — this is exactly the same neighbor-set data the greedy-cosine and landmark drills already used, just packaged as a matrix instead of queried one node at a time.
2. **Per-query, per-goal reachability column (one sparse linear solve, not a full inverse):** given final goal G, solve (I - gamma*T) x = e_G for x via conjugate-gradient/BiCGSTAB, OR compute the truncated Neumann series x_k = sum_{t=0}^{k} gamma^t T^t e_G via k sparse matrix-vector products (each term = literally one more hop of lookahead — this maps directly onto the existing hop-budget concept already used in the traversal harness). Either method yields x[v] = M[v, G] for every node v in one pass, at negligible cost for n~4,440 nodes per the literature's own billion-node precedent.
3. **Hop-selection (same argmax loop, new score):** at current node C, take C's TRUE local neighbor set (existing primitive, unchanged), score each candidate v by x[v] = M[v,G] instead of cosine(v,G), argmax. Optionally add the same generate-and-verify safety check already proposed in the sibling drill (reject a step if M[v,G] does not exceed M[C,G], i.e. the step must genuinely increase expected goal-occupancy, not just look locally attractive).
4. **Gamma as the one new, cheap-to-sweep knob neither prior attempt had:** run a small offline gamma sweep (e.g. 0.5, 0.7, 0.85, 0.95) against the SAME pre-registered diagnostic the landmark drill proposed but never ran — KB graph degree distribution + a rough mixing-time/community-structure check — to pick the gamma that keeps target-specificity in the regime this KB graph actually has, rather than assuming one gamma works everywhere. This tunable escape valve is the single biggest structural advantage over hub/landmark routing, which had no equivalent knob to turn when the "nearby-important-node" signal turned out to be goal-agnostic.

This is structurally the graph-mining backward-push / personalized-PageRank-to-a-target pattern, transplanted onto the KB graph's existing local-neighborhood-scoping primitive, and it is simultaneously the closed-form, no-learning-required realization of the hippocampal SR/predictive-map mechanism (justified as a "known-world model" special case rather than the usual TD-learned case, since the graph is static and already fully known).

## Honest risk section

1. **SR/resolvent smearing at high gamma, or on a large/expander-like graph, degrades toward the SAME goal-agnostic failure mode that sank hub-routing** — this is not a new risk invented for this drill, it is the third independent confirmation (after the greedy-cosine tie-degeneracy finding and the landmark drill's own expander-graph caveat) that the KB graph's actual degree distribution / community structure is the single most load-bearing unresolved unknown in this entire research thread, and it has now been flagged three times without being checked once. **This should be the very first action before building anything** — the same cheap diagnostic proposed (and skipped) in the landmark drill.
2. **SR-to-a-specific-goal-node vs. SR-to-a-goal-SET**: the literature (backward-push algorithms, single-target PPR) is built and well-precedented for the single-target case used in all prior drills in this thread (one specific goal node G). If a future product requirement needs "reach ANY of several acceptable goal nodes," the natural extension is a sum or max over per-goal columns (M[v,G1], M[v,G2], ...) — mathematically straightforward but NOT the case tested by any cited source here; flagged honestly as untested rather than assumed to generalize for free.
3. **Compute cost at n=4,440+**: the literature gives strong asymptotic and billion-node-scale precedent that both a single sparse solve and even a full dense inverse are cheap at this scale, but no source gives a directly-cited wall-clock number at exactly this n — this is inference from complexity scaling and general BLAS/HPC throughput facts, not a measured benchmark. The one-time cost of constructing the transition matrix T from the KB graph's real edge structure is a genuine (if minor) engineering step, not literally free, and should be timed on the actual graph rather than assumed.
4. **Classical SR-TD's slow-to-adapt-under-changed-dynamics limitation is not a live concern here** (the KB graph is static and fully known, so closed-form recomputation replaces TD-relearning entirely) but is worth naming explicitly so this proposal is not confused with the usual biological/TD-learned SR literature, which carries that limitation and this proposal does not.
5. **No literature runs the exact three-way empirical comparison this drill's discriminator needs.** The convergence here is stronger than the prior two drills in this sequence (a genuine name-brand mathematical equivalence, not just a structural analogy), but it is still, ultimately, an extrapolation from spatial-navigation and graph-mining literatures onto a KB-graph traversal task that neither literature was built to test directly.

## Intuitive summary

**What we did:** two earlier attempts to have the substrate figure out its own next step toward a distant goal both fell short — aiming straight at the goal worked a little, and trying to route through "well-connected hub" locations worked WORSE. We asked: what's actually missing? We looked at how real brains represent "where a path actually leads" (a decades-old idea from hippocampus research called the successor representation, literally meaning "a map of where you'll probably end up, not just where things currently look close"), how modern AI goal-reaching systems formalize the same idea, and — this turned out to be the most useful angle — how internet search-engine and social-network researchers solve the near-identical problem of scoring "how related is this webpage/person to that one," which has a well-known name: personalized PageRank / random-walk-with-restart.

**What we found:** these three fields turn out to be doing EXACTLY the same math under different names — a genuinely published paper states this equivalence directly, not just "sort of similar." And that math cleanly explains both of our earlier failures at once. Aiming straight at the goal failed because it only looks at how similar things seem right now, not where a path actually goes over many steps. Routing through hubs failed because "well-connected in general" says nothing about whether a hub is well-connected specifically TOWARD the goal you currently care about — it's the wrong kind of importance. The math we found (the "successor representation" / "personalized PageRank") is the one thing that is BOTH multi-step-aware (like a real path, not a snapshot) AND specifically goal-conditioned (computed for THIS destination, not generic importance) — exactly the combination missing from both prior attempts.

**The plan we're proposing:** since our knowledge map's connections are already fully known (we're not learning them from scratch the way an animal or a game-playing AI has to), we can compute this "which real neighbor actually leads toward the goal" score directly with ordinary linear algebra — no training, no new learned component — and it's cheap enough on a knowledge base our size that internet-search researchers solve vastly bigger versions of this exact problem in well under a second. We reuse the same "look at real neighbors, pick the best one" logic already built and proven for the hint-given case, just swap in this better-grounded score.

**Why it matters, honestly:** this is the strongest-converging idea in this whole line of investigation so far, because it doesn't just propose a third guess — it actually explains why the first two guesses failed for two different, specific reasons. But there's a real, checkable risk that has now come up THREE separate times across three different investigations and still has not been checked: if our knowledge map turns out to be more like a uniformly-tangled web than a map with real neighborhoods and bottlenecks, this fix (like the hub-routing fix before it) could still come up short — so the very first thing to do, before building anything, is finally check what kind of map we actually have.
