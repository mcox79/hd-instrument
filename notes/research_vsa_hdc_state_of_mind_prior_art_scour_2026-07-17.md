## (1) Cognitive architectures: how do they hold + update a running bound state?

**Eliasmith's Semantic Pointer Architecture (SPA) / Spaun** (Eliasmith et al. 2012, *Science* 338:1202-1207; Eliasmith 2013, *How to Build a Brain*) is the deepest, most directly relevant precedent found. The mechanism, in enough detail to build from:

- **Hold/update**: a **gated neural integrator** — a recurrent population tuned (via the Neural Engineering Framework) to approximately integrate its input over time, with an explicit gate signal controlling whether new input is summed in or the current value is frozen. Voelker & Eliasmith's **doubly-latched integrator** sharpens this into a synchronous-register-like primitive (two separate latch signals: one gates whether input affects the store at all, one gates whether the store's value is allowed to change) — shown to be as powerful as a multi-head deterministic pushdown automaton when combined with cleanup memories. This is a genuinely reusable primitive for our Tier-0 "true focus" pointer: a cheap, controllably-latched register, not a resonator-decoded item.
- **Build compound structure**: circular convolution binds role to filler; superposition adds multiple bindings into one scene/proposition vector — exactly Plate's HRR algebra (see section 3).
- **Query/retrieve**: approximate circular correlation (the inverse) unbinds, producing a noisy result cleaned up via an **associative/cleanup memory** (nearest-neighbor decode against known semantic pointers).
- **Cognitive control/routing**: a spiking basal-ganglia model (Stewart, Choo & Eliasmith 2010) selects among candidate condition-action rules over current buffer contents and opens/closes gates between cortical "buffer" populations — this is literally a production-system implemented by gating WM buffers, and is the mechanism to look at if the WSM needs an explicit "what gets promoted/routed where" control layer beyond simple thresholded discontinuity detection.
- **Sequence-specific extension — Choo's (2010) Ordinal Serial Encoding (OSE) model**: builds a memory trace additively, `M ← M + (item_i ⊛ position_p)`, decoded via correlation-with-position-cue + cleanup, with already-retrieved items suppressed to force non-repeating recall. Reproduces human **primacy and recency effects** on serial digit recall (a genuine, tested match to human data, not just a capacity claim). This is the DIRECT reusable mechanism for "when in the discourse was this added" — the exact structural analog of a Tier-2→Tier-3 timestamp tag, already neurally implemented and already validated against human serial-position curves.
- **Nengo n-back (Gosmann & Eliasmith 2015)**: maintains a rolling n-item window via chained convolution (position = convolution depth) plus a dot-product "compare" module — a working reusable pattern for "does the current item match something N steps back," relevant to our own coreference/Cb-matching design.
- **Gayler's MAP framework** (Gayler 2003, ICCS/ASCS; Gayler & Levy on analogical mapping) supplies an algebraically cleaner alternative binding operator: elementwise multiplication on bipolar vectors, which is its own inverse (unbind = bind again, no separate approximate-inverse step needed), with permutation used to prevent spurious matches from multiplication's commutativity. Gayler's contribution is architectural/algebraic (answering Jackendoff's binding-problem challenges), not an empirically-tested WM circuit — no serial-recall or capacity-curve experiment was found attributed to Gayler himself. **Design tradeoff worth carrying forward**: HRR/convolution needs a cleanup step to unbind (lossy but the vocabulary check gives an automatic confidence signal); MAP/multiply is exact-inverse but needs permutation-based "quoting" to avoid structural confusions.
- **ACT-R hybridized with HRR — Holographic Declarative Memory (HDM)** (Kelly, Kwok & West 2015, ICCM; Kelly, Arora, West & Reitter 2020, *Cognitive Science* 44(11):e12904; Ray & Dancy 2025, arXiv:2508.15630): replaces ACT-R's symbolic chunk store with a single superposed HRR trace; retrieval strength becomes cosine/dot-product similarity instead of discrete symbolic matching. **The fan effect (more facts sharing an entity → slower/less accurate retrieval) emerges directly from superposition crosstalk, with no hand-coded interference parameter** — an independent, cross-community confirmation that crosstalk-as-capacity-limit reproduces graded human-like degradation rather than a hard cutoff. Ray & Dancy (2025) add a time-encoding mechanism (oscillator-like functions bound via convolution to slot-value pairs, `m_t ← m_t + T̃ ⊛ Σ(slot ⊛ value)`) — structurally identical to Choo's OSE update rule, applied to declarative-memory chunk storage rather than a dedicated WM buffer, i.e. a second independent lineage converging on the same "convolve-with-a-position/time-vector-and-accumulate" mechanism for tagging when something entered memory.
- **Soar + VSA/HRR**: no results found. Confirmed genuine gap, not a search-term failure (multiple phrasings tried).

## (2) Semantic memory from reading: how do BEAGLE / Random Indexing accumulate a running representation, and what does it capture?

**BEAGLE** (Jones & Mewhort 2007, *Psychological Review* 114(1):1-37): each word has a fixed random **signal vector** (its unchanging "identity") and a learned **memory vector**, built by (a) summing OTHER words' signal vectors from the same sentence into it (unordered "gist" — bundling), and (b) circularly convolving n-gram signal vectors around a reserved **placeholder vector Φ** anchored at the target word's position, superposed into the SAME composite vector (order information, invertible via correlation to predict "what fills this slot"). It reproduces semantic similarity/synonymy (beats 300-dim LSA on a TOEFL-style synonym test), typicality judgments, semantic/associative priming (including mediated priming), and sentence-completion/cloze-probability effects — all without hand-coded grammar. **Explicit, literature-documented limitation, load-bearing for our purposes**: BEAGLE conflates order-sensitivity with unordered gist in one blended vector and does NOT preserve role-filler bindings as retrievable structure — Recchia et al. (2015) show directly that "bird eats worms" and "bird eats wings" are rated equally plausible by this class of model, i.e. positional/statistical order flavor, not compositional proposition structure. **BEAGLE is a static, batch-trained corpus-level lexicon, not a running per-passage discourse state** — this is confirmed explicitly in the lit-scan as a genuine, unfilled gap (no published "BEAGLE + online per-sentence discourse layer" variant was found).

**Random Indexing** (Kanerva, Kristoferson & Holst 2000; Sahlgren 2005): assigns each context (document/window) a fixed sparse random ternary index vector, then accumulates a word's context vector by simple addition as the corpus is scanned once — this INVERTS LSA's order of operations (accumulate-then-optionally-reduce vs. build-full-matrix-then-reduce), making it genuinely incremental: similarity is meaningful after a few examples, dimensionality is fixed in advance, and no huge intermediate co-occurrence matrix is ever built. Empirically comparable to LSA on TOEFL synonym tests (~64-72% vs LSA's ~64.4%, vs human ~64.5%) and text categorization. Like BEAGLE, this is a construction method for a STATIC word-space lexicon, not a discourse-instance tracker — Sahlgren flags the incrementality as an advantage but does not build a running-state model from it.

**Recchia, Sahlgren, Kanerva & Jones (2015)** is the key bridging paper: compares circular convolution (BEAGLE's order operator) against **random permutation** as an alternative, cheaper order-binding operator. Convolution is O(k log k) (FFT-based) per n-gram; permutation is O(k) — measured as **>70x faster at d=2048**, and this compute wall is explicitly WHY the original BEAGLE was never scaled past small corpora. At full 418M-token Wikipedia scale, only the permutation-based model could even run, and it substantially outperformed the small-corpus convolution baseline (synonym accuracy ~0.46 → ~0.63; similarity correlation ~0.10 → ~0.32-0.35) — evidence that **scale matters more than the specific binding operator** once you can afford to run at scale, but permutation is what BUYS you the scale in the first place. This is a directly actionable, quantified engineering lesson for any of our own order-encoding choices intended to run over a large foundation.

**Kwantes (2005) / Jamieson, Avery, Johns & Jones (2018, *Computational Brain & Behavior*) — instance-based semantic memory (CSM / "instance theory")**: an architecturally distinct alternative worth flagging — rather than pre-compressing everything into one running summary vector, meaning is CONSTRUCTED AT RETRIEVAL TIME from a store of raw episodic instance traces (MINERVA2-style). This is the closest literature analog to a genuinely "running/on-the-fly" semantic-construction model, though it sidesteps rather than extends BEAGLE's composite-vector formalism, and was not found explicitly cast as a discourse-tracking mechanism. Worth keeping as a fallback architecture pattern if our own consolidate-as-you-go approach proves lossy: store raw, construct-on-query instead of compress-on-ingest.

## (3) Structured state + querying: encode a scene, unbind to query, factorize when the search space is combinatorial, and what's the actual capacity math

**Plate's HRR** (Plate 1995, *IEEE TNN* 6(3):623-641; Plate 2003 book): binds role⊛filler via circular convolution, superposes multiple bindings into one fixed-width scene vector, recurses by treating a whole bound-and-superposed trace as itself a filler for higher-level nesting (his "chunking" mechanism — directly relevant if the WSM ever needs nested scenes-within-scenes, e.g. embedded clauses). Query = circular correlation (approximate inverse) + cleanup-memory nearest-neighbor decode. Capacity: crosstalk-noise SNR falls off as the number of superposed pairs grows; a cross-source citation states an approximately linear D-vs-capacity relationship with "quite large" asymptotic constants (~128 dimensions per unit of capacity slope) — i.e. costly, not cheap, to buy more bound pairs via dimensionality alone. A modern, more precisely quantified comparison (**Schlegel, Neubert & Protzel, arXiv:2001.11797**) gives concrete numbers: FHRR (closest analog to continuous HRR) needs **~330 dimensions to bundle 15 items at 99% accuracy**; combining bundling WITH binding (i.e. actual role-filler scenes, not bare item-bundling — the Plate/HRR use case) pushes dimensionality requirements higher still (sparse architectures need up to 44% more dimensions for the same reliability). **This is a directly reusable, standardized metric format** (dimension-vs-item-count-at-99%-accuracy curves, per binding-architecture) that our own capacity claims should be reported in, for direct comparability to the published VSA literature, rather than an ad hoc "N/16-32 ceiling" framing.

**Kanerva's HD data structures** (Kanerva 2009, *Cognitive Computation* 1:139-159): bundling (addition/majority-vote) = unordered SET; binding+permutation = ORDERED SEQUENCE (`S = i_0 + ρ(i_1) + ρ²(i_2) + ...`, decoded by applying ρ⁻ᵏ then cleanup); a STACK is a special-case sequence where a fixed "shift" permutation re-permutes the whole superposed representation on every push so the top element always sits in a canonical decodable slot. **This stack construction is a direct, ready-made mechanism to consider for the WSM's own Tier-0/1/2/3 paging** — push/pop of discourse segments implemented as exactly this kind of permutation-based rotating superposition, rather than inventing a new paging primitive from scratch.

**Resonator networks** (Frady, Kent, Olshausen & Sommer 2020, *Neural Computation* 32(12), two parts; arXiv:1906.11684): solve the problem of FACTORIZING a bound composite vector back into its constituent codebook entries when the combinatorial search space is too large to search directly — exactly the decode problem our own Tier-2 bundled-scene retrieval faces. Mechanism: iterative alternating projection — unbind all OTHER current factor estimates from the composite, project the residual onto each factor's codebook subspace (Hopfield-style outer-product or least-squares weights), clean up with a nonlinearity, repeat to convergence. Quantitative results: stability requires **D_f/N ≤ 0.056** (codebook size per factor relative to vector dimension); beats six benchmark optimization methods by **~2 orders of magnitude** in "operational capacity" (≥99% accuracy within ≤0.001×total-search-space iterations); operational capacity scales roughly **quadratically in N** (e.g. F=3 factors at N=5000 reaches ~10⁸ factorizations solved reliably). **This is a strong, concrete upgrade candidate over a naive direct-unbind+nearest-neighbor-cleanup decode for our own multi-role scene retrieval** — worth a direct head-to-head test (Prediction 1 below) against whatever decode our existing resonator-focus-lever work is currently using, since the D_f/N ≤ 0.056 threshold gives a formal number to check our own F=3-4 sweet-spot claim against.

**VSA working-memory-capacity analyses (Cowan's-4 analog)**: no paper was found that explicitly sets out to reproduce Cowan's ~4-item human WM limit as a VSA result — this specific framing appears to be an implicit/analogical connection in review prose, not a dedicated empirical target (confirmed gap). The underlying quantitative machinery to DO this ourselves is mature and directly reusable: Frady, Kleyko & Sommer's SNR law `s = √(N/M)` for M items bundled in N dimensions, and the more general logarithmic relation between required SNR and vocabulary size; Hsin/Cummins et al.'s (arXiv:2301.10352) formal big-O capacity theorems, including one specifically for k-way role-bindings (capacity cost grows roughly exponentially in `k log k` with binding ARITY — directly relevant to how many roles our own scene-vectors should hold before the theory itself says retrieval degrades).

## (4) Discourse/situation models in VSA/HDC specifically — the honest gap-check

**Confirmed, exhaustively-searched gap.** No paper connects HDC/VSA to Centering Theory, Grosz-Sidner discourse structure, Kintsch's Construction-Integration model, or Zwaan's event-indexing model — direct searches for each combination returned the two literatures as disjoint, with WebSearch's own synthesis explicitly confirming no citation bridge exists. No VSA/HDC paper encodes a multi-sentence story as an accumulating vector and queries it (no bAbI-style HDC work found); the one closely-adjacent paper, **Laube & Eliasmith (2024), "QAVSA," RepL4NLP-2024 (ACL Anthology)**, uses VSA to encode a STATIC knowledge-graph for augmenting an LM on multiple-choice QA — reasoning over pre-loaded KG triples, not a representation accumulated sentence-by-sentence from a story, and notably shows Eliasmith's own group is still active in exactly this space as of 2024 (worth periodically re-checking for follow-on work). No permutation/convolution order-vector work extends past local (2-5 word) windows to event-sequence/plot-level ordering.

The single most useful NON-vectorized precedent found: **Grosz & Sidner (1986, *Computational Linguistics* 12(3):175-204), "Attention, Intentions, and the Structure of Discourse"** — discourse structure = linguistic structure + intentional structure + **attentional state**, the last modeled as a **stack of focus spaces**, one per discourse segment, with explicit push/pop operations. This is structurally the closest existing account of "a separate tier tracking what's currently salient, distinct from the larger memory store" — exactly our own Tier-0/1/2/3 intuition, just never cast in vectors. Grosz's own 1999 follow-up (*Computational Linguistics* 25(4)) flags a known limitation: plain stacks don't cleanly explain interruption/pronominal behavior at segment boundaries, motivating cache-based alternatives — worth reading before finalizing our own tier-transition rules, since we may hit the same edge case. **Cardona-Rivera, Cassell, Ware & Young (2012), "Indexter," Workshop on Computational Models of Narrative** is a direct, fully worked SYMBOLIC (non-vector) implementation of Zwaan & Radvansky's five-dimension event-indexing model built on causal-link narrative plans, used to predict event salience/memorability — this is the clearest existing formalization available for a VSA reimplementation, and already partially informs today's earlier biology-side note's Tier-2 design.

This is the actual, narrow, honestly-claimable novelty, and it should be framed exactly that narrowly in any product narrative (per the standing no-papers/product-only discipline): the value is a glass-box, self-growing discourse tracker assembled from proven parts, not a new kind of vector algebra.

4. **The Grosz-Sidner stack-of-focus-spaces gives a concrete, cheap DESIGN AUDIT to run before committing further engineering to the Tier 0-3 structure** (Prediction 4) — a half-day literature cross-check, not a cell, that could catch a structural gap (the interruption/pronominal edge case Grosz's own 1999 paper flags) before it's discovered the expensive way during implementation.
5. **Sequencing**: per today's earlier note and the ranked-barriers synthesis, the WSM remains a later-stage build (after parser/lexicon progress) — this note does not change that sequencing, it only sharpens WHICH mechanisms should be reused when the WSM build starts, and flags one cheap pre-build audit (Prediction 4) worth doing now, since it costs no compute.

## (i) Ranked list of mechanisms to BORROW into the state-of-mind assembly

1. **Grosz & Sidner (1986) stack-of-focus-spaces** — structural precedent for the overall Tier-0/1/2/3 paging design; run the cheap design-audit (Prediction 4) before finalizing tier-transition rules.
2. **Eliasmith/Voelker's doubly-latched gated integrator** — Tier-0 "true focus" register: a controllably-latched, O(1) pointer, not a resonator-decoded item.
3. **Choo's Ordinal Serial Encoding (item ⊛ position accumulation)** / **Ray & Dancy's oscillator-time-binding** (same update-rule family) — Tier-2 "when was this added to the discourse" tagging; already validated against human primacy/recency curves in one lineage and a fan-effect-adjacent context in the other.
4. **Kanerva's permutation-based stack construction** (shift-permute-then-bundle) — the ready-made mechanism for Tier 0-3 push/pop, avoiding a bespoke paging primitive.
5. **Resonator networks (Frady/Kent/Olshausen/Sommer)** — iterative factor-decode upgrade candidate over naive unbind+cleanup for Tier-2 scene retrieval; comes with a formal stability threshold (D_f/N ≤ 0.056) to check our own resonator-sweet-spot claims against.
6. **Recchia et al.'s permutation-over-convolution engineering result** — cheapest actionable lesson: use permutation, not circular convolution, for any order-encoding that needs to scale.
7. **ACT-R Holographic Declarative Memory's fan-effect test (Kelly et al.)** — an off-the-shelf, zero-hand-tuned-parameter external validation paradigm for our own crosstalk/capacity story.
8. **Cardona-Rivera's Indexter** — the clearest existing SYMBOLIC operationalization of Zwaan's 5-dimension event-indexing model, ready for direct vectorization.
9. **Kwantes/Jamieson's instance-based (construct-at-retrieval-time) semantic memory** — a fallback architecture pattern if consolidate-as-you-go proves lossy.

## (ii) Honest novel-vs-precedented statement

**Well-precedented (borrow, don't reinvent)**: the core algebra (convolution/multiplication bind, addition bundle, permutation order); gated/latched WM registers with basal-ganglia-style routing; item-position sequence accumulation; iterative resonator-based factorization; permutation-vs-convolution scaling tradeoffs; crosstalk-driven graceful capacity degradation (independently confirmed via the ACT-R fan effect); incremental/online corpus-level accumulation (Random Indexing); instance-based query-time construction as an alternative to precompression.

**Genuinely novel for us (confirmed by exhaustive, multi-angle search, not merely unsearched)**: (1) any multi-sentence discourse/situation-model state in HD/VSA vectors at all — no connection to Centering Theory, Grosz-Sidner, Kintsch-CI, or Zwaan's event-indexing exists in this literature; (2) a nested multi-tier (true-focus / attentional-focus / activated-situation-model / durable-store) architecture specifically cast in vectors; (3) using a prediction-error/discontinuity signal as a VSA-native event-boundary/consolidation trigger; (4) a system that grows its OWN foundation from reading AND simultaneously does discourse-level tracking — all VSA semantic-memory precedent (BEAGLE, Random Indexing, QAVSA) is either a static batch-trained corpus lexicon or a static pre-loaded knowledge graph, never both self-growing and discourse-tracking at once.

## (iii) Test/metric to adopt from prior art

- **ACT-R fan-effect paradigm** (interference vs. entity fan-in, no hand-tuned parameter) — adopt directly for validating Tier-2/3 crosstalk behavior (Prediction 3).
- **Spaun's serial-position curve (primacy/recency)** — adopt as the shape-check for Tier-0/1 update/decay behavior.
- **Resonator networks' "operational capacity" metric** (≥99% accuracy within ≤0.001×M iterations) — adopt as the standardized way to report Tier-2 decode capacity claims, replacing ad hoc framing.
- **Recchia et al.'s small-vs-full-scale head-to-head comparison methodology** — adopt for deciding convolution vs. permutation at whatever scale our own foundation eventually reaches.
- **Schlegel/Neubert/Protzel's dimension-vs-item-count-at-99%-accuracy curve** — adopt as the standard capacity-reporting format, directly comparable across VSA architectures.
- **Cardona-Rivera's Indexter event-salience/memorability prediction test** — adopt as an external behavioral check for the Tier-2 discontinuity-trigger design (today's earlier note's Prediction 2).
