# Research: register-general verb-argument attachment — the human mechanism, and an implementable spec

## HEADLINE

**The human parser does NOT use lexical/corpus co-occurrence statistics to decide WHO the subject and object of a verb are. It uses a small stack of categorical, closed-inventory, largely register-invariant mechanisms — (1) a verb's CATEGORICAL valency (how many argument slots it has, and their coarse semantic type — near-universal, stable across centuries and even across language families), (2) the CLOSED-CLASS/function-word system (determiners, prepositions, complementizers, auxiliaries, case/agreement morphology — empirically the most register/era-stable layer of English, independently confirmed by 60+ years of authorship-attribution stylometry), and (3) a small set of structural DEFAULT-LINKING rules (agent->subject, theme->object, word-order/position) — that together determine which attachments are even LEGAL, before any frequency-based preference is consulted at all.** Corpus-frequency-like statistics (verb-bias, "main-clause bias," thematic-fit norms) are real and measurably used, but only as a **downstream tie-breaker among options the categorical layer has already licensed** — in the one place this is precisely quantified (McRae, Spivey-Knowlton & Tanenhaus 1998), the fitted weights split roughly **structural/frequency-prior ≈0.51, semantic plausibility ≈0.37, item-specific lexical-tense frequency ≈0.12** for one specific ambiguity type, i.e. even where a frequency effect is real it is a minority contributor. And critically, even that residual frequency-based layer is **not the "train once, freeze, apply" mechanism a modern statistical parser uses**: Fine, Jaeger, Farmer & Qian (2013, *PLoS ONE*) show people **recalibrate syntactic expectations within tens of sentences to the local statistics of the text/register currently being read** — the human "prior" is a fast-adapting, locally-re-estimated signal, not an imported lifetime corpus frequency table. This is the direct mechanistic explanation for why a human reader does not need register-specific retraining and a modern statistical dependency parser does: the human's primary attachment mechanism (valency + closed-class + default-linking) was never corpus-fit in the first place, and the part that IS frequency-sensitive re-fits itself online from the very text being read. Direct causal evidence that content-word lexical statistics are dispensable for correct attachment: Jabberwocky/nonce-word sentences are parsed with the **identical ERP signature** (early negativity + unattenuated P600) as real sentences (Hahne & Jescheniak 2001) purely from closed-class scaffolding + morphology + word order, and grammatical-but-semantically-anomalous sentences ("colorless green ideas sleep furiously"-style) engage the brain's language network at **~97% of normal-sentence strength**, while destroying word order (backward sentences) costs **~55%** — structure, not lexical meaning, is what the machinery actually depends on (Kauf et al. 2024).

## Cheap decisive test

Build the staged mechanism-spec pipeline below (categorical-valency + closed-class-scaffold + default-linking, with a locally-adapted frequency tie-breaker only as a last resort) and run a 4-condition register-shift battery, gold-labeled for subject/object/oblique attachment:
- (a) modern news/Wikipedia-register baseline (where the existing modern-corpus-trained statistical parser is known to work well — the "real baseline" already on hand from the substrate's current pipeline);
- (b) 19th-century literary prose sample (e.g., a Dickens/Melville/Austen excerpt — matches the user's stated failure case);
- (c) legal/technical-register sample (contract or statute language — dense subordination, nominalizations, unusual closed-class density);
- (d) a constructed Jabberwocky-style battery: real closed-class words + real inflectional morphology + real word order, with NONCE open-class content words substituted into KNOWN argument-structure frames (transitive, ditransitive, passive) — this isolates whether the pipeline's attachment decisions depend on any lexical content at all.

**Arms:** (1) existing modern-statistical-parser baseline, unmodified, run on all four conditions (expected: strong on (a), collapses on (b)/(c), and is undefined/meaningless on (d) since there is no lexical entry for nonce words at all); (2) the new valency+closed-class+default-linking pipeline, full version (includes the ACT-R-style cue-retrieval residual-ambiguity stage and the locally-adapted frequency tie-breaker); (3) an ABLATION of (2) with the frequency tie-breaker stage removed (categorical layer only) — isolates how much of cross-register robustness comes from the categorical backbone alone vs. the adaptive statistical layer; (4) a **scrambled-closed-class control**: same texts, but determiners/prepositions/complementizers/auxiliaries randomly shuffled among their token positions while content words and word order are held fixed — this must catastrophically degrade arm (2)/(3) if the closed-class scaffold is genuinely load-bearing (a positive control against silently defaulting via some other unaccounted signal, per the standing discipline that a can-fail control is mandatory).

**One variable differs per comparison:** (2) vs (1) isolates mechanism-class (categorical/structural vs. corpus-statistical); (2) vs (3) isolates whether the adaptive frequency layer adds anything beyond the categorical backbone, or whether the backbone alone already carries most of the register-general accuracy (the literature above predicts the backbone should dominate); (2)/(3) vs (4) is the must-fail sanity control.

## Falsifiable predictions

**HARD-PASS (validates the categorical-backbone claim as the primary register-general mechanism):**
- Arm (2)/(3) accuracy on subject/object/oblique assignment stays within **~5-10 accuracy points** across conditions (a)->(b)->(c) (CI-separated bands, not point estimates), while arm (1) (modern statistical baseline) drops by **>=20-30 points** from (a) to (b)/(c) — reproducing, in a controlled in-substrate test, the exact out-of-domain collapse the user has already observed informally.
- Arm (2)/(3) on the Jabberwocky battery (d) achieves **>=80% correct subject/object assignment** using only closed-class scaffold + morphology + default word-order linking, with zero lexical-content lookup for the nonce words — directly testing the Hahne & Jescheniak / Kauf et al. finding that structure-assignment does not require lexical content.
- Arm (4) (scrambled closed-class) drops to **near-chance** (e.g., <40% on a task where random guessing among 2-3 candidate NPs would score 33-50%) on arms (2)/(3) — confirming the closed-class scaffold is actually doing the work claimed, not being silently bypassed.
- Arm (3) (categorical-only ablation) captures **most** (e.g., >=80%) of arm (2)'s cross-register robustness advantage over arm (1) — confirming the literature's claim that valency+closed-class, not the frequency-adaptation layer, is the primary register-general driver, with the adaptive layer as a secondary refinement.

**HARD-FAIL (falsifies the categorical-backbone claim, or the specific staged pipeline as proposed):**
- Arm (2)/(3) ALSO collapses by a comparable magnitude to arm (1) on (b)/(c) (e.g., >=15-20 point drop) — this would mean valency+closed-class information alone is insufficient for register transfer, and some other unaccounted factor (e.g., still-needed broad lexical coverage of archaic/legal vocabulary for the valency LOOKUP itself, or genuinely register-shifted closed-class usage patterns not captured by the historical-stability literature) is actually gating performance. This is a real, informative negative — it would redirect effort toward lexicon-coverage engineering rather than mechanism redesign.
- Arm (4) does NOT catastrophically degrade (stays within ~10 points of arm (2)/(3)'s un-scrambled performance) — meaning the pipeline is silently relying on some other signal (e.g., leaked word-order-only heuristics, or accidental content-word pattern matching) rather than genuine closed-class parsing, invalidating the claimed mechanism even if the accuracy numbers looked good.
- Jabberwocky-battery (d) accuracy is **<50%** (indistinguishable from a word-order-only default heuristic, or from chance) — this would falsify the strong claim that closed-class+morphology+word-order alone (without any lexical semantics) is sufficient for human-level attachment, and would mean some minimal lexical/semantic scaffolding is unexpectedly load-bearing even for nonce-word sentences.
- Arm (3) (categorical-only) underperforms arm (2) (full pipeline with adaptive frequency tie-breaker) by a **large** margin (e.g., >15 points) on ANY of (a)/(b)/(c) — this would mean the frequency-adaptation layer is doing more real work than the literature's ~0.12-0.37 weight estimates suggest, and the categorical backbone is not sufficient on its own; the substrate-product priority would then need to shift toward building the online-recalibration mechanism (Fine et al. 2013's finding) as a first-class component, not a minor refinement.

## Cross-thread synthesis (per numbered literature area, with citations)

### 1. Constraint-based comprehension — what constraints, weighted how?

MacDonald, Pearlmutter & Seidenberg (1994, *Psychological Review* 101:676-703) frame ambiguity resolution as ordinary lexical retrieval: a word's stored argument-structure/subcategorization alternatives are activated in parallel with strength set by relative frequency ("Full Lexical Representation Hypothesis" — put as much structure as possible in the lexicon; parsing = retrieval + joining). Trueswell (1996, *J. Memory & Language* 35:566-585) gives the sharpest quantified test: verb-specific participle-frequency (from Francis & Kucera 1982 corpus counts) predicts garden-path magnitude almost perfectly — high-participle-frequency verbs show a **141ms** garden-path effect eliminated entirely (Fs<1), low-participle-frequency verbs retain it. Trueswell, Tanenhaus & Garnsey (1994, *J. Memory & Language* 33:285-318) show the effect is graded, not categorical (continuous Patient-typicality ratings correlate with reading time, R=-.51 to -.56), and immediate (first-pass reading times). McRae, Spivey-Knowlton & Tanenhaus (1998, *J. Memory & Language* 38:283-312) supply the **only fully quantified weighting scheme found in this literature**: six named constraints (initial-NP thematic fit; verb tense/voice bias; a corpus-derived main-clause-bias prior, 92%/8%; by-bias .8/.2; the-bias .875/.125; agent-NP thematic fit) combined in a competition-integration (normalized-recurrence) network, with weights **fit once on off-line data and then reused unmodified** to predict on-line reading times (R²-quality fit; **main-clause bias≈.51, thematic fit≈.37, verb tense/voice≈.12**, normalized). Critically, an explicit two-stage/delayed-semantics variant of the identical architecture fit reliably WORSE (t(48)=195.97, p<.0001), and even a minimal ~10-25ms delay of semantic information measurably worsened fit — there is **no evidence anywhere in this literature for a structure-first, semantics-delayed staging**; combination is parallel and graded throughout. Tanenhaus and colleagues' visual-world eyetracking work (Tanenhaus, Spivey-Knowlton, Eberhard & Sedivy 1995, *Science* 268:1632-1634) shows referential/scene context can override even a strong verb-specific lexical bias essentially immediately, confirming multiple constraint types are integrated continuously, not in a privileged temporal window.

**Register-invariance verdict for this area:** the register-DEPENDENT ingredient is explicit and named: verb-specific tense/participle bias and the corpus-derived main-clause-bias prior are, BY CONSTRUCTION, statistics over a specific language sample — corpus-linguistics work on verb-subcategorization-frequency differences across genre (business-news vs. conversational vs. fiction corpora) independently confirms these numbers shift across register. Thematic-fit/plausibility is comparatively more stable (event/world knowledge) but not perfectly invariant either.

### 2. Verb valency/subcategorization projection — register-invariant? primary driver?

Vosse & Kempen (2000, *Cognition* 75:105-143) — the Unification Space model — stores each word's licensed argument/complement slots as a lexical frame (three-tier tree: Root Node + Foot Nodes for functional slots like Subject/Head/Direct Object). Ambiguity = multiple candidate unification links competing for the same node, resolved by **lateral (competitive) inhibition**, with frequency/recency entering only as **entry-activation and activation-decay parameters that bias which of the independently-licensed candidates wins** — they do not create new attachment possibilities. Hagoort's Memory-Unification-Control model (Hagoort 2005, *TICS* 9:416-423; 2013, *Frontiers in Psychology* 4:416) is architecturally the same claim with neurobiological localization: verb argument frames (subj/hd/dobj/mod slots) are stored in the lexicon (posterior temporal cortex, "Memory") and combined online by a **domain-general, word-frequency-independent unification operation** in left inferior frontal gyrus ("Unification") — explicitly built on Vosse & Kempen's mechanism (cited as ref. 6). Levin & Rappaport Hovav (1995, *Unaccusativity*) state explicit, general linking rules ("Immediate Cause Linking Rule," "Directed Change Linking Rule," "Existence Linking Rule") mapping event-structure decomposition to syntactic position, claimed to hold across English, Dutch, Italian, Hebrew, Russian. Baker's Uniformity of Theta Assignment Hypothesis (Baker 1988; extended 1996/97) reduces this to a fixed structural template — agent=higher-VP-spec, theme=lower-VP-spec, goal/path/location=lower-VP-complement — argued to hold not just in English but in ergative languages (Dyirbal, Greenlandic), "primary object" languages (Mohawk, Sesotho), and nonconfigurational languages (Warlpiri) once surface Case-driven movement is factored out. Perlmutter & Postal's (1984) Universal Alignment Hypothesis is the founding statement of this whole research program.

Trueswell, Tanenhaus & Kello (1993, *JEP:LMC* 19:528-553) supply the crucial categorical/graded split: what varies gradedly by frequency is **which of several already-licensed frames is preferred** (e.g., "forgot" vs. "hoped" both categorically license NP-object and S-complement, but differ in which is the default first guess); what is NOT shown to be graded is **whether a frame is in the verb's inventory at all** — that is treated throughout this literature as a listed, binary lexical fact, the same status as part-of-speech. Diachronic/typological evidence converges on exactly this categorical/graded split showing up as a stability/instability split: Tsunoda's (1985) cross-linguistic transitivity hierarchy shows verbs at the "core" (resultative change-of-state: kill/break/bend; perception: see/hear) end are transitive in essentially every language surveyed, while peripheral 2-argument classes (pursuit, feeling, ability) show real cross-linguistic variation. Fillmore (1970, "The Grammar of Hitting and Breaking") shows categorical alternation-participation (break causativizes, hit categorically does not: *"the window hit"* is ungrammatical) is a stable lexical-semantic fact, not a frequency effect. Barðdal's comparative reconstruction of oblique-subject argument-structure constructions traces some semantic-class-to-structure mappings to Proto-Indo-European — a ~6000-year stability claim. The single most directly quantified register-specific case study found (Denison, "Argument structure," in *One Language, Two Grammars?*) traces a real, measured British-English-specific surface-mapping shift for "substitute" (old-as-object patterns: 18.5% British BNC tokens vs. 3.5% American ANC tokens) to a specific register-contact event (football/soccer commentary after the 1966/7 substitution rule) — but explicitly, **the verb's categorical valency (a two-participant exchange predicate) never changed; only which participant surfaces as direct object vs. oblique shifted, and only in the specific register where the change originated.**

**Register-invariance verdict for this area:** categorical valency (arity + coarse semantic role inventory) is the most strongly register/era-invariant ingredient found anywhere in this whole 7-part scan — stable enough to survive translation across unrelated language families, and where change is measured (Denison), it is confined to the surface-mapping layer, never the categorical arity. Graded frame-preference (which licensed frame is the default first guess) is the one part of valency knowledge that is genuinely frequency-derived and hence register-sensitive — directly parallel to the register-dependent ingredient identified in Area 1.

### 3. Cue-based retrieval + similarity-based interference — what cues, what weights?

Lewis & Vasishth (2005, *Cognitive Science* 29:375-419) give the field's most explicit formal mechanism: total activation `A_i = B_i + Σ_j W_j·S_ji`, with **`W_j = G/j`** — the total available goal-activation is divided EQUALLY across however many retrieval cues are active, a flat linear scheme with **no built-in privilege of structural over semantic cue type**. Interference is `S_ji = S - ln(fan_j)` — a pure function of cue-overlap/discriminability, agnostic to whether the overlapping feature is syntactic or semantic. All five reading-time datasets modeled were fit with ACT-R DEFAULT parameters (decay=.5, S=1.5, both reused from unrelated cognitive-psychology memory experiments, not fit to any linguistic corpus) plus a single shared scaling constant (R²=.76-.91) — i.e., **the retrieval algorithm itself carries no corpus-specific tuning at all.** The authors explicitly note (§7.2.2.2) the published base model implements only syntactic/structural cues as a scope choice, not an architectural claim that semantic cues are excluded or weighted less — "Van Dyke (2002) provided direct evidence for interference effects as a function of semantic cue overlap." Van Dyke & McElree (2006, *J. Memory & Language* 55:157-166) directly demonstrate semantic/selectional-fit features function as genuine retrieval cues (extraneous memory-load words matching a verb's selectional preferences slow reading at that verb, despite zero syntactic relationship). Van Dyke (2007, *JEP:LMC* 33:407-430) supplies the one real asymmetry in this literature, and it is **temporal, not weight-based**: syntactic-feature interference is detectable in the EARLIEST online reading measures, semantic-feature interference shows up LATER — plausibly reflecting when each information type becomes computable from the input, not a hard-wired priority.

**Named cue list (compiled across this literature):** grammatical role/structural position, gap/filler and embedded-clause features, morphological agreement (number/gender/person — from the adjacent agreement-attraction literature), animacy/semantic-selectional fit, recency/positional distance (via base-level activation decay).

**Register-invariance verdict for this area:** the retrieval ALGORITHM (content-addressable matching + fan-interference + activation decay, with fixed non-corpus-derived parameters) is maximally register-invariant — it is domain-general working-memory machinery, not a linguistic-frequency model. What is NOT invariant is the FEATURE CONTENT it operates over: structural/closed-class cues (case, agreement, gap-features, phrase category) are comparatively register-stable; semantic-selectional cues depend on lexical entries whose typical fillers can be register-specific (archaic senses, obsolete professions, legal terms-of-art) — a lexicon with thin coverage of a register's vocabulary degrades the semantic cues even though the retrieval mechanism itself is unaffected.

### 4. Clause-bounded incremental parsing — segmentation and within-clause attachment

Frazier's (1978; Frazier & Fodor 1978, *Cognition* 6:291-325) two-stage Sausage Machine defines Minimal Attachment and Late Closure as operating on **syntactic-category information alone**, in an informationally-encapsulated first-pass module — Mitchell (1987) confirmed this first-pass stage attends only to major grammatical category, with argument-structure/frequency information available only to the later "Monitor" stage. Frazier & Clifton's (1996, *Construal*, MIT Press) major revision restricts this: **primary relations** (a verb's obligatory/subcategorized arguments) are attached immediately by the syntax-only algorithm and are governed by Minimal Attachment/Late Closure; **non-primary relations** (adjuncts) are only loosely "associated" with the current thematic-processing domain and resolved later via discourse/semantic principles. This is a direct, explicit precedent for treating verb-argument attachment as the register-invariant, structurally-driven core, and adjunct attachment as the softer, semantically-negotiated residue.

Kimball (1973, *Cognition* 2:15-47) — the single most directly on-point citation for clause-boundary detection — states seven principles including **Principle of New Nodes**: a new clausal node is signaled by a **function word** (complementizer "that," relative pronoun "who/which/that," subordinating conjunction "because/since/although/when"), not by any property of the open-class content that follows. **Principle of Two Sentences** bounds how many clauses can remain simultaneously open (a working-memory constraint, direct ancestor of Gibson's storage cost); **Principle of Early Closure** and the "They knew (that) the girl was in the closet" contrast show closed-class presence/absence directly controls when a clause is judged closed; **Principle of Processing** has each clause flushed to semantic interpretation as soon as it closes syntactically (clause-by-clause processing, not sentence-final).

Gibson's Dependency Locality Theory (1998, *Cognition* 68; 2000) posits **storage cost** (working-memory burden of holding incomplete syntactic predictions — a bounded stack of open, category-typed predictions, directly descended from Kimball's Two-Sentences principle) and **integration cost** (scales with the number of new intervening discourse referents — new NPs and finite/tensed verbs — since a dependency's head/dependent was last active), explaining subject- vs. object-relative-clause complexity asymmetries. New clause boundaries (introduced by a finite verb, complementizer, or relative pronoun) are exactly the events that create new discourse referents and drive up cost.

Abney (1991, in Berwick, Abney & Tenny eds., *Principle-Based Parsing*) formalizes the two-stage chunk architecture directly relevant to Ask #2: **the chunker** (stage 1) identifies chunk (NP/PP/VP) boundaries using ONLY closed-class + POS-category templates — it does not need to know which specific content word fills a slot, only its category and the function words framing it (grounded in Gee & Grosjean 1983's finding that spontaneous-speech pausing/prosody aligns with exactly these chunk units, i.e. chunks are argued to be a real production/perception unit, not a computational convenience); **the attacher** (stage 2) resolves inter-chunk attachment (PP-attachment, clause-attachment) using lexical/semantic preferences.

**Register-invariance verdict for this area:** clause-boundary and chunk-boundary detection is the cleanest, most explicitly-formalized register-invariant mechanism in the entire scan — it is triggered by a small, closed set of function-word/category cues, independent of open-class lexical identity, matching exactly the register-stable "scaffold" property the target system needs.

### 5. Closed-class/function-word system as a register-stable structural cue

Bradley, Garrett & Zurif (1980, in Caplan ed., *Biological Studies of Mental Processes*, MIT Press) report closed-class lexical decisions showing no word-frequency sensitivity (unlike open-class words) and no cross-class interference, interpreted as evidence for a specialized, structurally-dedicated closed-class access route, with agrammatic Broca's aphasics specifically losing this route (a double-dissociation signature). **Honest caveat:** the frequency-insensitivity finding proved non-replicable in several follow-ups (e.g., Gordon & Caramazza 1982) and weakened the strong dual-route framing. Friederici (1985, *Cognition* 19:133-166) independently found processing-level distinctions between vocabulary types (open-class vs. closed-class vs. closed-class sub-functions) in normals vs. agrammatics. Neville, Mills & Lawson (1992, *Cerebral Cortex* 2:244-258) report the **N280**, a left-anterior negativity larger/earlier for closed-class than open-class words — feeding directly into Friederici's later three-phase model (2002, *TICS* 6:78-84): **Phase 1 (~100-300ms)** builds initial phrase structure from syntactic-category information alone (the ELAN component, ~150-200ms, elicited by word-category violations), preceding lexical-semantic integration (Phase 2, N400) and reanalysis (Phase 3, P600). **Contested:** Steinhauer & Drury (2012, *Brain and Language* 120:135-162) argue many published ELAN effects are artifacts of asymmetric experimental designs (pre-target-word confounds), and Fromont, Steinhauer & Royle (2020, *PLOS ONE* 15:e0229169), using a confound-controlled balanced design, found **no early (100-300ms) anterior negativity** for pure syntactic-category violations — instead an N400 appeared for ALL anomaly types including pure category violations, arguing against strict syntax-first staging. **The dispute is about the specific ERP component and strict temporal-priority claim, not about whether the brain computes grammatical category from closed-class/form-based cues largely independent of full lexical semantics — both camps agree on that; they disagree on whether it happens in a strictly earlier encapsulated stage (Friederici) or in parallel with semantic access (Fromont et al.).**

**Independent, non-psycholinguistic confirmation of register-stability (stylometry):** Mosteller & Wallace (1963, *JASA* 58:275-309, the Federalist Papers study) built the founding result of quantitative authorship attribution specifically on the premise that a small set of common function words (articles, prepositions, conjunctions) are **stylistically neutral and topic-independent**, unlike content vocabulary which tracks subject matter. Burrows' Delta (Burrows 2002, *Literary and Linguistic Computing* 17:267-287) and its many variants (Hoover's, Eder's, Cosine Delta) formalize this further: z-score-normalized frequency profiles of the MOST FREQUENT words in a corpus — overwhelmingly closed-class items — serve as a stable per-author/per-register stylistic fingerprint precisely because they are largely orthogonal to topic/content. Grammaticalization/diachronic-corpus literature (using resources like COHA) confirms the core English closed-class inventory (basic determiners, personal/relative pronouns, core prepositions, coordinating conjunctions, modal/auxiliary verbs) shows "continuity and remarkable diachronic stability across centuries," with change concentrated in slow grammaticalization drift at the margins (e.g., "notwithstanding" shifting from conjunction toward adposition) rather than wholesale turnover — categorically different from open-class vocabulary's rapid genre/era-driven churn.

**Register-invariance verdict for this area:** strongly confirmed from FOUR independent methodologies (psycholinguistic parsing theory, neuropsychological lesion/dissociation evidence, ERP, and 60+ years of stylometric practice) that closed-class items are both mechanistically privileged in parsing AND empirically the most register/era-stable statistical layer of English — the same property doing double duty as a parsing mechanism and a stability guarantee.

### 6. Precision-weighting of cues (Competition Model + predictive coding)

Bates & MacWhinney's Competition Model formalizes **cue validity = availability × reliability** (MacWhinney & Bates 1989) — an input-statistic computed over a language sample, by construction NOT an architectural constant. MacWhinney, Bates & Kliegl (1984, *J. Verbal Learning & Verbal Behavior* 23:127-150) is the field's clearest existence-proof that cue weighting is a language-specific (and by extension, register-specific) LEARNED parameter, not a universal setting: English speakers rely overwhelmingly on word order (a first-noun-actor default), German speakers weight agreement and animacy jointly over word order, Italian speakers weight agreement extremely heavily — each pattern tracking that cue's actual availability/reliability in that language's input distribution. Kuperberg & Jaeger (2016, *Language, Cognition and Neuroscience* 31:32-59) supply the predictive-coding/precision-weighting VOCABULARY for why re-weighting should happen dynamically ("estimate the reliability of both her prior knowledge as well as new input... and use these estimates to modulate the degree to which she updates her beliefs... 'weight' prediction error"), explicitly linking this to flexible adaptation to "the demands of a given situation" — but this is a **theoretical/programmatic synthesis, not a worked-out quantitative mechanism** (unlike McRae et al.'s or MacWhinney et al.'s fitted numbers) — treat as a hypothesis the theory endorses, not a demonstrated result, per this drill's own honest calibration.

**The load-bearing empirical bridge:** Fine, Jaeger, Farmer & Qian (2013, *PLoS ONE* 8:e77661) close the gap between theory and data: self-paced reading experiments show that when a garden-path structure is made artificially frequent within a short exposure block, readers' difficulty for it **decreases within the block**, and difficulty for the previously-dominant structure correspondingly **increases** — i.e., syntactic expectations (verb-bias/subcategorization-like priors) are **recalibrated within tens of sentences to the LOCAL statistics of a novel input stream** (the authors' own framing: comprehenders "rapidly adapt to the syntactic statistics of novel linguistic environments, e.g., speakers or genres"). This is the single most directly load-bearing citation in the whole scan for the user's actual engineering problem: it is empirical proof that humans do not carry a frozen, register-specific corpus prior into text — they continuously re-estimate cue reliability from the text currently being read, which is functionally equivalent to "no register-specific retraining needed" because the adaptation happens online, from the very document being processed.

**Register-invariance verdict for this area:** the Competition Model's `validity = availability × reliability` formalism explains WHY cue weighting differs cross-linguistically (and by extension, could differ cross-register) — cue weights are learned statistics, not universal constants. But Fine et al. (2013) shows the human system does not need a PRE-LEARNED register-specific weighting at all — it recomputes validity/reliability on the fly from local input, which is the actual mechanism that makes register-shift painless for a human reader within the first several sentences of an unfamiliar register.

### 7. Jabberwocky/nonce-word parsing — does attachment depend on lexical co-occurrence stats?

Hahne & Jescheniak (2001, *Cognitive Brain Research* 11:199-212) is the single most directly decisive citation for the crisp question posed: a 2×2 design crossing syntactic well-formedness with real-word vs. Jabberwocky (real function words + real inflectional morphology + nonce content words) sentences found phrase-structure/word-category violations elicit an **early anterior negativity followed by a P600 in BOTH real-word and Jabberwocky sentences**, with the **Jabberwocky P600 not attenuated relative to the real-sentence P600** — i.e., morphosyntactic parsing runs on FORM (morphology + function-word/positional context) alone, identically whether or not the content words carry real lexical meaning. Grammatical Jabberwocky sentences produce no N400 for the nonce placeholders (nothing to lexically integrate); syntactically-violated sentences of either type show no N400 at the violation point (syntax appears to gate semantic integration). Authors' explicit conclusion: "syntactic processes are independent of the presence of lexical-semantic information."

Berko (1958, the classic "wug test") shows productive, rule-based inflectional morphology (plural -s, past -ed, progressive -ing) is applied to entirely novel word-forms based on phonological shape alone — the direct mechanism behind parsing "distims" as 3sg-present, "slithy" as adjectival (-y suffix), "gyre"/"gimble" as bare verb forms after the auxiliary "did." Mintz (2003, *Cognition* 90:91-117; cross-linguistically replicated by Chemla, Mintz, Bernal & Christophe 2009, *J. Child Language* 36:1017-1050) formalizes "frequent frames" — two-word closed-class-anchored templates ("the ___ is," "did ___ and ___") that reliably predict grammatical category of the intervening word in child-directed speech corpora — a quantitative model of exactly the mechanism Jabberwocky/gostak sentences exploit. Braine's (1963) classic artificial-grammar-learning work shows learners are extremely sensitive to (and sometimes over-rely on) absolute word-order POSITION when inducing grammatical categories — the cue that lets "the gostak distims the doshes" assign gostak=subject (pre-verbal) and doshes=object (post-verbal) from English's fixed SVO order alone. Naigles (1990, *J. Child Language* 17:357-374) shows the acquisition-literature mirror image: 25-month-olds infer a novel verb's meaning FROM its sentential argument frame ("The duck is gorping the bunny" -> causative two-participant reading), not vice versa — syntax constrains interpretation of an unknown word, never the reverse.

At the level of aggregate neural engagement (a different, magnitude-of-response question, not an attachment-correctness question): Fedorenko, Scott et al. (2016, *PNAS* 113:E6256-E6262, ECoG) find the "gradual buildup" signature of incremental sentence composition occurs ONLY for real sentences, with Jabberwocky > nonword-lists but < word-lists — i.e., pure structure elevates engagement over nothing at all, but real lexical content contributes more to the AGGREGATE response magnitude than structure alone. Kauf, Kim, Lee et al. (2024, bioRxiv 2024.06.21.599332) is the cleanest modern empirical analog of the Chomsky "colorless green ideas" thought experiment: grammatical-but-semantically-anomalous sentences (real words, implausible combinations, exactly Chomsky's logic) engage the language network at **~97% of intact-sentence strength (Δ=-0.08, n.s.)**, Jabberwocky (nonce content words) shows a more substantial reduction, and **backward-word-order sentences (word order destroyed) drop sharply (Δ=-0.55, significant)** — i.e., the network's engagement magnitude tracks syntactic PARSABILITY far more than semantic plausibility. These two findings (magnitude-of-engagement vs. attachment-correctness) measure genuinely different things and must not be conflated: the ERP data (Hahne & Jescheniak) says structure-assignment for nonce words is computed correctly and with the same machinery as real words; the fMRI/ECoG data says the AMOUNT of aggregate neural engagement additionally benefits from real lexical content, which is a separate finding about processing "richness," not about whether attachment itself needs corpus statistics for the specific open-class item.

**Ranking of what does the real work for nonce-word attachment (per this literature):** (1)=(tied) closed-class/function-word context (Mintz's frequent frames, quantitatively validated; available pre-linguistically per Valian & Coulson 1988/Gerken & McIntosh 1993; neurally indistinguishable from real-word processing per Hahne & Jescheniak) and word-order/position (Braine 1963 — inseparable from closed-class cues in a fixed-order language, since function words largely define the relevant positions); (3) morphological/inflectional cues (real, Berko-demonstrated, but supplementary — many Jabberwocky examples parse correctly even with minimal derivational morphology, from closed-class+order cues alone); (4) the parser DOES still perform a minimal category-assignment inference for every nonce word (it must decide "gostak" is a noun), but this is a **generative, type-level, closed-class-anchored inference** ("things after 'the' and before a tensed verb are typically subject NPs"), never a token-level corpus/co-occurrence statistic for the specific nonce item, because no such statistic can exist.

**Colorless-green-ideas note:** Chomsky's (1957, *Syntactic Structures*) example is a thought experiment, not itself an experiment; there is no classic "literal-sentence" ERP study, but Kauf et al. (2024) is a direct, modern, explicitly-Chomsky-motivated empirical realization of the same logic (grammatical + semantically-anomalous vs. real, vs. word-order-destroyed), and it comes down decisively on the autonomy-of-syntax side (structure dominates over meaning for engaging the sentence-processing machinery).

## Register-invariant vs. register-dependent signals — the mechanism spec (implementable, ordered pipeline)

Synthesizing all seven areas into a single staged spec. **Order matters** — earlier stages act as HARD FILTERS on grammatical legality (categorical, not gradable); later stages act as SOFT COMPETITION among options the filters have already licensed (graded, weighted). This is the key structural distinction the whole literature converges on and that a flat arc-factored/discriminative dependency parser collapses: legality and preference are NOT the same computation, and only preference (the smaller, later contributor) is corpus-frequency-shaped.

**Stage 0 — Closed-class scaffold pass (Abney chunking + Kimball clause segmentation).** Scan for determiners, prepositions, complementizers, relative pronouns, auxiliaries, coordinating/subordinating conjunctions. Segment into clauses: open a new clause node at each complementizer/relativizer/subordinator or new finite verb (Kimball's Principle of New Nodes); track a bounded stack of open clause-predictions (Kimball's Two Sentences / Gibson's storage cost). Chunk each clause into NP/PP/VP spans using ONLY closed-class + POS-category templates (Abney) — content-word identity is irrelevant at this stage. **Register-invariant by construction**: small closed inventory, independently confirmed stable across centuries (grammaticalization/COHA evidence) and across genre (stylometric practice).

**Stage 1 — Morphological category assignment (per open-class token).** Apply productive inflectional rules (Berko wug-test mechanism: -s/-ed/-ing/-ly and other closed, rule-governed affixes) plus positional/frame cues (Mintz frequent-frames: slot between "the" and a finite verb = noun; slot after an auxiliary = bare verb) to assign a provisional part-of-speech to EVERY open-class token, known or nonce — no lexical-frequency lookup required for this step, only the closed-class scaffold from Stage 0 plus morphological form.

**Stage 2 — Categorical valency projection.** For each finite verb identified within a clause (Stage 0), project its CATEGORICAL argument slots from a valency lexicon (VerbNet-class-style, frame-keyed per the prior `research_grammar_construction_resources_for_role_assignment_2026-07-20.md` finding: subject always; direct object iff the verb class is transitive; recipient iff ditransitive; oblique iff the verb requires a specific PP-complement). For genuinely unknown/nonce verbs (no lexicon entry), default to the canonical transitive frame (subject + object) per Baker's UTAH coarse template, since that is linguistically the unmarked/default mapping. **Register-invariant**: categorical arity/role-inventory is the most strongly stable ingredient found in this whole scan (survives translation across language families per Baker; survives centuries per Barðdal/Denison, with change confined to the surface-mapping layer, never the categorical fact).

**Stage 3 — Structural default-linking (word order + UTAH).** Assign the Stage-2 slots to the clause's candidate NPs by default linking order: pre-verbal NP -> subject/agent slot; first post-verbal NP -> object/theme slot; PP arguments -> oblique slots keyed by the specific preposition (reusing the prior note's UD `obl:agent`/preposition-sense finding: "by" under passive voice = agent, "to"/"for" = recipient, etc.). **Register-invariant**: this is the language-wide default (English is a fixed-word-order language; the default mapping is a grammatical fact of English, not a corpus statistic), and it is exactly the cue Braine (1963)/the Jabberwocky literature shows suffices for nonce-word attachment.

**Stage 4 — Voice/case-marking override (still closed-class-driven).** Detect passive voice from auxiliary "be" + past-participle morphology + "by"-PP; flip the Stage-3 default linking accordingly (surface subject = patient/theme, "by"-oblique = agent). This is itself closed-class/morphology-driven, hence register-invariant, not a corpus-statistical override.

**Stage 5 — Cue-based retrieval binding for residual ambiguity (ACT-R).** Where Stages 0-4 leave more than one grammatically-legal candidate (e.g., PP-attachment ambiguity, relative-clause attachment, long-distance filler-gap dependencies), bind slots to specific NP tokens via content-addressable retrieval keyed on structural cues (clause-membership, agreement features, gap/filler features) PLUS semantic/selectional cues (animacy, thematic-fit-if-available) as secondary cues, combined with roughly equal weighting per active cue (Lewis & Vasishth's `W_j=G/j`, no built-in structural-over-semantic privilege), with similarity-based interference degrading retrieval when cues fail to discriminate. This stage is register-invariant in its ALGORITHM (fixed, non-corpus-derived retrieval dynamics); the semantic-cue CONTENT it draws on is only as good as lexical coverage of the target register's vocabulary.

**Stage 6 — Locally-adapted frequency tie-break (last resort only).** If Stages 0-5 still leave genuine residual ambiguity, apply a graded preference (verb-bias-like, thematic-fit-like) — but critically, this prior must be an ONLINE, INCREMENTAL, LOCALLY-RE-ESTIMATED counter over the document/register currently being read (per Fine et al. 2013's empirical finding), starting from a near-uniform/maximally-uncertain prior rather than a frozen modern-corpus-trained distribution, using something like the Competition Model's `validity = availability × reliability` recomputed on local text statistics as they accumulate within the current document. **This is the ONE stage where corpus-like statistics enter at all, and even here the human mechanism is explicitly NOT "train once on an external corpus, freeze, apply" — it is "start uncertain, adapt fast, locally."**

**Combination rule across the whole pipeline:** NOT a single flat weighted sum over all cue types simultaneously (which is what would make the whole system as register-fragile as any statistical parser). Instead: Stages 0-4 act as **hard grammaticality filters** — they eliminate illegal candidates outright because they encode categorical/structural facts, not preferences (this is the load-bearing structural difference from a discriminative arc-scoring parser, which treats everything as a single graded scoring function with no categorical/legality tier). Only among the candidates surviving Stages 0-4 does Stage 5's graded competitive-activation process (lateral inhibition per Vosse & Kempen/Hagoort MUC, or ACT-R activation per Lewis & Vasishth) resolve the winner, with Stage 6's locally-adapted statistics entering only as the final, lowest-priority tie-break.

## Crisp answer to the key question

**Does the brain use lexical/corpus co-occurrence statistics for ATTACHMENT, or grammar+valency+closed-class rules?** Both are used, but in a specific and decomposable way that is fundamentally unlike a modern statistical/discriminative dependency parser: **attachment LEGALITY** (which structures are even possible for a given verb and its candidate arguments) is controlled entirely by grammar + categorical valency + closed-class structure — categorical, register-invariant, and (per McRae et al.'s one fully-quantified case) responsible for the majority of the fitted weight even where frequency effects are measurable (structural/frequency-prior ≈0.51 vs. item-specific lexical frequency ≈0.12 in that one construction). **Attachment PREFERENCE among multiple already-legal options** is influenced by frequency-like statistics (verb-bias, thematic fit, main-clause-bias), but this is a minority, downstream contributor, and — the decisive point for the register-generalization question — even this residual statistical layer is not a frozen, externally-pretrained corpus prior: Fine et al. (2013) demonstrate humans re-estimate it online, within tens of sentences, from the very text/register being read. A modern statistical dependency parser conflates legality and preference into one flat, corpus-fit scoring function with no categorical tier and no online-adaptation mechanism — which is exactly why it collapses out-of-domain, while a human reader (running mostly on the categorical/closed-class backbone, with only a small, fast-adapting statistical residue) does not.

## TLDR

We looked in the science literature for how people manage to read old-fashioned or unfamiliar writing styles and still correctly figure out who did what to whom in a sentence, even though today's computer sentence-parsers get confused when the writing style changes. The answer: people are not relying on having seen a lot of matching example sentences before (the thing that breaks when the style changes) -- they are relying on a small, mostly-fixed rulebook (which words are little "grammar glue" words like "the," "of," "was," "that"; how many people or things a given action-word needs; simple word-order defaults like "the doer usually comes before the verb"). That rulebook barely changes across centuries of English -- it is proven so stable that it is literally the tool used to detect forged historical documents by their author's writing style. People do use frequency/experience as a small tie-breaker when the rulebook alone leaves two readings equally possible, but even that tie-breaker gets re-learned on the fly from the very page being read, within a few sentences -- not carried in frozen from a lifetime of reading modern text. This gives us a concrete build plan: put the fixed rulebook first (cheap, no training needed, and should carry most of the weight), add a small memory-based tie-breaker for genuinely ambiguous cases, and if any frequency guessing is needed at all, make it re-learn itself from the current document rather than being pre-loaded from modern text.
