## Item 1: the cortex's teacher-free meaning-learning mechanism (lit-scan verdict)

Ground truth from the dedicated lit-scan, HIGH confidence: **plain co-occurrence / Hebbian pairing / slowness / temporal prediction, taken alone, does NOT produce discriminative codes -- it produces blurred, merged (invariant) codes.** This holds across every sub-literature checked, with no counter-example found:
- Foldiak's (1991, *Neural Computation* 3(2):194-200) trace rule is explicitly an invariance/merging mechanism -- it binds units across a transformation sequence, collapsing within-class variation, not separating between-class variation. Foldiak's own follow-on (with Schraudolph, NeurIPS 1991, "Competitive Anti-Hebbian Learning of Invariants") had to ADD competition/anti-Hebbian terms to stop different classes collapsing into one unit.
- Wiskott-Sejnowski Slow Feature Analysis (2002, *Neural Computation* 14(4):715-770) only avoids collapse because of its **built-in decorrelation/orthogonality constraint** among extracted features -- slowness alone would converge every unit onto the single slowest signal; the discriminative content comes from the constraint, not the slowness objective.
- Predictive coding (Rao-Ballard lineage; Millidge et al., arXiv:1608.06277; 2022 "Dynamic Predictive Coding" preprint) supplies a genuine self-generated supervisory signal (prediction error), but the discriminative sharpening again depends on an added compression/error-gating step, not on raw next-step prediction alone.
- Pehlevan & Chklovskii's "correlation game" (arXiv:1704.00646) is the cleanest formal statement: **Hebbian excitation maximizes correlation WITH inputs; anti-Hebbian inhibition decorrelates units FROM EACH OTHER** -- both terms are required, and synaptic competition (winner keeps strongest-correlated inputs) plus lateral inhibition is what turns a blurred Hebbian assembly into factorized, distinct detectors. Barlow's redundancy-reduction (1961) and Olshausen-Field sparse coding (1996) are the classical version of the same two-term requirement.
- On the language side, Levy & Goldberg (2014, "Neural Word Embedding as Implicit Matrix Factorization") show skip-gram-with-negative-sampling implicitly factorizes a **shifted-PMI** matrix -- negative sampling is doing the anti-Hebbian/repulsive work; raw co-occurrence counts alone are explicitly NOT what gets fit. Firth (1957) and Harris (1954) themselves are shown to diverge under closer reading (Perrone & Tesconi, arXiv:2205.07750) -- the folk "co-occurrence = meaning" reading of the distributional hypothesis is not, on its own, the mechanistic story either.

MEDIUM confidence only on which single formalism (information-bottleneck vs. redundancy-reduction vs. contrastive-estimation) is "the" unifying frame -- this remains a genuinely active, somewhat fragmented literature, not one settled theorem. Treat the "acting together" synthesis (co-occurrence/attraction PLUS competition/repulsion, jointly, as the actual mechanism) as the correct read, not an oversimplification in either direction.

## Item 2: graph/relational self-supervision methods, with a concrete two-view solution (lit-scan verdict)

Ranked by graceful degradation toward low degree (this is the answer to the 07-08 drill's Rank 3 unsolved gap: "our encoder has no two-view construction mechanism"):

1. **Encoder-perturbation views (SimGRACE, Xia et al. 2022, arXiv:2202.03104) -- BEST, fully degree-agnostic.** Perturbs the GNN encoder's own PARAMETERS (not the graph, not the input) to create two views for a standard NT-Xent contrastive loss; zero data augmentation, zero graph-structure requirement. This is the direct graph-domain analog of SimCSE's (Gao et al. 2021, arXiv:2104.08821) dropout-only views, and it works identically at degree 0 or 1 by construction, since it never touches the graph at all. **This is the concrete, previously-missing answer to Rank 3's two-view gap** -- it needs nothing from the KB's density, so it is entirely unblocked by the density question above.
2. **Random-walk-context views (node2vec: Grover-Leskovec 2016; DeepWalk: Perozzi et al. 2014).** Degrade smoothly, not catastrophically -- low-degree nodes produce low-diversity, repetitive walks (documented degree-imbalance in "Degree-Based Random Walk," arXiv:2110.13627) but no hard floor. Not the recommended primary mechanism here (SimGRACE dominates it on both simplicity and degree-robustness), but a viable fallback.
3. **Relational-neighbor resampling (two independent random subsets of a node's own KB neighbors/triples).** No paper found does this exact construction as its own named method (closest analogs: GraphSAGE fixed-fanout neighbor sampling; "Bootstrap Latents of Nodes and Neighbors," arXiv:2408.05087, for a different objective). Has a **hard analytic floor at degree >= 2** (a degree-1 node cannot form two non-empty disjoint subsets) -- this is a logical necessity, not merely an empirically-observed degradation. Given the reconciliation above, the main concept-KB (median degree 2) marginally clears this floor for roughly half its nodes; the ConceptNet subgraph (median 6, ~99% >= 5) clears it comfortably and is the right primary training ground for this specific method.

Cross-checked against 2 (GRACE: Zhu et al. 2020, arXiv:2006.04131; BGRL: Thakoor et al. 2021, arXiv:2102.06514; DGI: Velickovic et al. 2018, arXiv:1809.10341) -- all three are, in aggregate, benchmarked on Citeseer (avg degree ~2.7-2.84), which is now close to (main KB) or below (ConceptNet subgraph) the densified KB's own numbers. This is real, if not degree-stratified, published precedent -- a materially different position than the 07-04 drill's finding of "below every tested floor in every family." Tail-GNN (Liu et al. 2021, KDD) and meta-tail2vec (Liu et al. 2020, CIKM) operationalize "tail/low-degree" as degree <= 5 by convention -- the ConceptNet subgraph (median 6, ~99% >= 5) is now, by this field's own definition, predominantly a HEAD-node graph, not a tail-node graph. Confidence on this whole item: LOW-MEDIUM -- aggregate benchmarks touch this range but no paper performs a genuine per-node degree-stratified ablation down to degree 1-2, so the boundary behavior is inferred, not directly measured.

## Item 3: does relational/co-occurrence learning structurally avoid BGE's crowding problem? (lit-scan verdict -- the important honest caveat)

**Two distinct claims exist in the literature and must not be conflated (this is the single most important nuance from this lit-scan):**

- **Claim A (well-established, medium-high confidence): objectives with an EXPLICIT repulsive/uniformity term structurally resist collapse, independent of any sparsity constraint.** Wang & Isola (2020, arXiv:2005.10242) prove this cleanly -- decomposing contrastive loss into alignment + uniformity, showing alignment alone has a degenerate (collapsed) global optimum, and the uniformity term (an explicit geometric/entropy-maximizing pressure over pairwise in-batch structure) is what prevents it. This is the direct mechanism behind why the "representation degeneration problem" (BERT/embedding anisotropy, mean-cosine ~0.99, exactly the crowding failure mode this whole thread is trying to escape) arises specifically from objectives that lack any explicit repulsive term (softmax/next-token, single-target regression, plain distillation) -- confirming the mechanism, not just describing the symptom.
- **Claim B (unestablished, low confidence): locality or relational/co-occurrence structure ALONE, absent an explicit repulsive term, is sufficient to avoid collapse.** No literature found supports this. Dentate-gyrus pattern separation and population-sparseness/lateral-inhibition literatures DO show decorrelation emerging from co-occurrence-adjacent processes, but only when COUPLED to a sparsity/reconstruction objective or an architecture-level expansion+inhibition mechanism -- these are objective- or architecture-specific mechanisms, not a free-standing consequence of "locality" or "relationalness" per se. Information-limiting correlations (Moreno-Bote et al. 2014) is a genuinely separate literature (noise-correlation caps on population information), not directly connected to the collapse/crowding question.

**Honest verdict: relational/co-occurrence learning does NOT have a structural, locality-derived reason to avoid crowding that teacher-distillation lacks.** Both suffer the identical failure mode (regression toward the nearest single-target/majority-correlation attractor) unless an explicit repulsion term is present. What changes when the attraction signal switches from "match BGE" to "match KB graph neighbors" is only the SOURCE of the positive-pair signal, not the presence or absence of the repulsion requirement. **This is a deflationary, not an inflationary, finding relative to a naive "graph objectives are automatically less crowded because they are relational" hope** -- and it is the correct, disciplined read (avoids the "brain automatically solves this" framing the project's own ground rules warn against).

## Item 4: minimal teacher-free objective to build+test first, ranked, with a cheap decisive test

Given items 1-3, the correct framing is: **all three candidates below still need the SAME explicit repulsion term; what differs is the source of the positive-pair (attraction) signal and how gracefully each degrades against the (still imperfectly dense) KB graph.** None of the three is a genuine wholesale alternative to the hybrid-distillation family from the 07-08 drill -- each is either a generalization or a concrete unblocking of that drill's own named candidates.

### Rank 1 -- Batch-computed repulsion term (VICReg/Barlow-style covariance penalty), now shown teacher-independent [TOP BET, unchanged position from 07-08 but independently reconfirmed]

**Mechanism.** Identical math to the 07-08 drill's Rank 1 (off-diagonal covariance-Frobenius penalty over a batch of student embeddings, `c(Z) = (1/d) sum_{i!=j} [C(Z)]_ij^2`, optional VICReg variance floor). What this drill adds: **three independent lit-scans this cycle (Pehlevan-Chklovskii's anti-Hebbian term, Barlow's redundancy-reduction, Wang-Isola's uniformity term) converge on this being THE brain-and-math-grounded repulsion mechanism, not merely an ML engineering trick bolted onto distillation.** Critically, this term is computed purely on the student's own batch of embeddings -- it is **fully degree-agnostic**, meaning it is completely unaffected by the KB density question either way (does not need the graph, does not care whether nodes are degree 0 or degree 1000). It can be attached to a distillation-anchored attraction signal (as in 07-08) OR to a graph-relational attraction signal (Rank 3 below) with zero change to this term itself.

**Effort:** LOW (one loss term, already partially validated in the 07-08 drill's own falsifiable-prediction design).

**P_deflated = 0.35** (raw ~0.55-0.60, the strongest convergent evidence of any candidate across two research drills and three independent framings; deflated -0.20-0.25 because no paper directly measures "graph-relational-attraction + explicit-repulsion exceeds a frozen teacher's own separation," this remains a novel synthesis of independently-proven pieces, not a directly-tested combination).

### Rank 2 -- SimGRACE-style encoder-perturbation contrastive views [the concrete, previously-missing solution to Rank 3's two-view gap]

**Mechanism.** Two forward passes through the SAME encoder with independently perturbed parameters (weight-space noise, not dropout, not graph augmentation), standard NT-Xent loss between the two resulting embeddings of the same entity. Per item 2 above, this is fully degree-agnostic (needs zero graph richness) and is the most direct, literature-grounded (SimGRACE arXiv:2202.03104; SimCSE arXiv:2104.08821 in the adjacent text domain) instantiation of the two-view construction the 07-08 drill flagged as unsolved.

**Effort:** LOW-MED (encoder weight-perturbation + a second forward pass; no new data pipeline, no augmentation engineering).

**Argument for exceeding parity:** because it needs zero graph structure, it is the most ROBUST of the three candidates to any future KB-density surprise (including the possibility that the "median 2 / median 6" figures above are themselves later revised) -- this robustness is itself a reason to prioritize it as a hedge alongside Rank 1, even though it is ranked below Rank 1 on raw mechanism-convergence strength.

**P_deflated = 0.28** (raw ~0.45 from SimCSE/SimGRACE direct precedent in adjacent domains; deflated -0.17 because neither SimCSE nor SimGRACE has been tested on a KB-relational-entity-embedding task at our specific scale, and the "does perturbation-view diversity provide as rich an attraction signal as a real semantic-neighbor pair" question is unaddressed in the literature found).

### Rank 3 -- Relational-neighbor contrastive, scoped to the ConceptNet subgraph specifically (NOT the whole 1.28M-entity KB) [graph-native, most degree-fragile]

**Mechanism.** Two independent random subsets of an entity's KB-relational neighbors/triples as the two "views," standard InfoNCE loss against in-batch negatives, PLUS the Rank 1 repulsion term (per item 3's finding, this or an equivalent explicit repulsion term is not optional). Per the degree-floor analysis in item 2, this should be scoped to the 133,306-node ConceptNet subgraph (median degree 6, ~99% >= 5), where the degree>=2 mathematical floor is comfortably cleared and the field's own graph-SSL benchmarks (Citeseer/Cora/Pubmed, avg 2.7-4.5) are matched or exceeded -- NOT applied uniformly across the main 1.28M-entity concept KB, where median degree 2 only marginally clears the floor for about half of nodes and sits below every directly-tested graph-SSL benchmark degree.

**Effort:** MED (degree-aware positive-pair sampling logic, KB-neighbor-subset extraction, best implemented as a ConceptNet-subgraph-scoped pilot rather than a whole-KB pass).

**Argument for exceeding parity:** this is the most "authentically KB-native" of the three (it literally uses the relational graph structure, the Firth/Harris distributional-hypothesis analog on real relational data, not a synthetic augmentation), but it is also the one most exposed to residual density gaps outside its scoped subgraph, and to the item-2 finding that no paper has directly degree-stratified graph-SSL performance down to degree 1-2 -- it is the least de-risked of the three even though it is the most "on-brand" for a KB-native teacher-free objective.

**P_deflated = 0.20** (raw ~0.35 from real, if aggregate-only, graph-SSL precedent now matched by the ConceptNet subgraph's numbers; deflated -0.15 for the missing degree-stratified evidence and the scoping requirement, which limits it to a fraction of the KB rather than being a uniform, whole-graph objective).

### Main claim P_deflated (some combination of the above measurably exceeds BOTH the BGE-distillation ceiling on superposition recall AND the native-decorrelated-code's pointwise-fidelity weakness -- the decouple target)

**P_deflated = 0.30** (capped per novel-synthesis discipline; this is the correct target framing per the certified decouple law -- graph-relational attraction + explicit repulsion is a candidate STORE-side/superposition-side mechanism, not a full replacement for whatever the pointwise-fidelity/retrieval side still needs, which per the certified finding may still benefit from teacher-anchored or correlated signal).

**Honest answer to "is this a 5th independent candidate, a solved instantiation of Rank 3's gap, or a genuine alternative to the hybrid-distillation family":** it is **not** a 5th independent candidate, and it is **not** a genuine alternative to the whole hybrid-distillation family (item 3's finding rules that framing out -- the repulsion term is mandatory regardless of attraction-signal source, so "teacher-free" cannot mean "no repulsion term needed"). Rank 1 is a reconfirmation-and-generalization of the 07-08 drill's own Rank 1 (now shown to work with either a teacher-anchored or a graph-relational attraction signal). Rank 2 (SimGRACE) IS the concrete, previously-missing solution to Rank 3's two-view-construction gap. Rank 3 (relational-neighbor contrastive, ConceptNet-scoped) is a second, KB-native two-view construction method, complementary to Rank 2, narrower in scope (degree-gated), useful specifically because it uses REAL relational structure rather than pure encoder noise.

## Cheap decisive test (CPU-only, no GPU dispatch)

Small-scale CPU pilot on the ConceptNet 133,306-node subgraph (median degree 6, the one sub-graph with real graph-SSL literature precedent), subsampled to ~5,000-20,000 nodes for tractability:

1. Train a minimal shallow/linear projection head over existing cached surface-feature inputs (no new encoder architecture) using Rank 1's repulsion term (batch covariance penalty) plus Rank 3's relational-neighbor-resampled positive pairs (or Rank 2's encoder-perturbation views as a same-cost alternative arm) -- CPU-feasible, a few minutes per arm, no GPU needed.
2. Compare against a no-repulsion control: the CURRENT teacher-free objective (Hebbian/aggregative mean-pooling of relational context, no repulsion term at all) on the identical subsample.
3. Reuse already-built infrastructure from the substrate-self-mapping thread (modularity Z-score / cluster-quality against a degree-preserving random-rewire null) as the primary discriminator, PLUS the off-target mean-pairwise-cosine diagnostic already instrumented from the whitening-revival and 07-08 work.

**HARD-PASS:** modularity/silhouette Z-score against the degree-preserving null clears >= 2-3 sigma, AND off-target mean-pairwise-cosine is measurably lower (better separated) than the no-repulsion control by >= 0.03-0.05 margin, AND no dimensional collapse (per-dimension variance floor maintained, not e.g. < 1% of dimensions carrying > 90% of variance).

**HARD-FAIL:** Z-score against the null stays below 1 sigma, OR off-target cosine is statistically indistinguishable from or worse than the no-repulsion control (the repulsion term adding variance/spread without genuine discriminative structure -- the same "cosmetic uniformity" failure mode flagged in the 07-08 drill). This would mean even the newly-densified ConceptNet subgraph does not yet carry enough relational signal for a graph-native attraction+repulsion objective to produce real structure, and the correct action is to escalate back to the distillation-anchored Rank 1 (07-08) as the primary near-term build, treating the graph-native version as still gated behind further density growth specifically on THIS metric (not a blanket density number).

**MIDDLE-BAND:** separation improves measurably but does not clear 2 sigma, or clears only within a narrow margin -- worth one iteration on the repulsion-term weight before escalating or abandoning, same discipline as the 07-08 drill's own falsifiable-prediction structure.

## Intuitive summary

**What we asked:** can the substrate learn what concepts mean, on its own, purely from how its internal knowledge-graph facts relate to each other -- with no help from an outside AI model -- now that the knowledge graph has grown a lot bigger and richer than it was a few days ago?

**What we found:** the knowledge graph's growth is real and it does open a door that was previously closed, but only a specific, narrower door than "wean off the outside AI model entirely." A new, dense, well-connected slice of the graph (a 133,000-concept web of everyday-word relationships, where almost every concept now has at least 5 connections) is now, for the first time, big and rich enough to try real self-supervised learning methods from outside research on -- something the wider knowledge base as a whole (which grew from about 1.6 to about 2 connections per concept, on average) is still just short of. But we also found something important and a little deflating: simply switching from "copy an outside AI's answer" to "copy what your own knowledge-graph neighbors look like" does not, by itself, fix the crowding problem (many different concepts ending up looking too similar to each other). Every serious study we could find agrees that you also need an explicit, deliberate "push different things apart" ingredient, regardless of where the "pull similar things together" signal comes from. The brain does this too -- it does not just passively soak up co-occurrence, it actively separates by combining a "pull together" mechanism with a "push apart" mechanism, always both.

**Why it matters:** this means the plan is not "throw out the outside AI model, use the graph instead" -- that would just recreate the same crowding problem with a different signal. The right plan is "keep the push-apart ingredient we already identified as promising, and now ALSO give it a second, richer, brain-native source of the pull-together signal (the newly-dense concept graph), instead of relying only on the outside AI model for that." We also found a very cheap, concrete, no-outside-AI recipe for the two independent "views" of the same concept that this kind of self-teaching needs (borrowing an idea, called SimGRACE, that just wiggles the encoder's own internal settings slightly rather than needing rich external data) -- which solves a specific gap flagged in the previous research pass as unsolved.

**Where this leaves the project:** the currently-running experiment (distillation from the outside AI model plus a push-apart term) stays the right thing to keep running -- nothing here says stop it. What's newly buildable, cheaply, alongside it: (1) the same push-apart term, now understood as brain-grounded and independent of any teacher, (2) a simple "wiggle the encoder twice, compare" self-teaching trick that needs no rich data at all, and (3) a graph-native version scoped specifically to the new dense 133,000-concept sub-graph, not the whole knowledge base, since that's the one place the numbers actually support it today. A cheap, few-minutes, no-GPU-needed test is specified to decide, within one cycle, whether the graph-native version is worth building out further or should wait for the knowledge base to grow denser still.
