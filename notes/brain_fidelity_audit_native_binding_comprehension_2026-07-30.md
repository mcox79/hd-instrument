## HEADLINE

**NO, the pipeline is not full brain-foundational, and the honest answer has a real sting: the single component we centered the whole approach on (algebraic/phase VSA binding) is the SHAKIEST of the six, not the strongest.** The literature verdict is Marr-level-2 "computationally plausible, implementable on spiking hardware" (Eliasmith SPA/Nengo, Frady-Sommer resonator networks) riding on top of a neuroscience hypothesis (binding-by-synchrony/phase-coding) that is **actively contested, not settled**, with real 2023-2025 papers arguing rate-based/mixed-selectivity binding without any oscillation at all. This is a materially different claim than "brain-founded" — it means the binding mechanism is *neurally implementable*, not *neurally confirmed*. Two other components are worse: the encoder (confirmed non-fidelity, unchanged diagnosis) and role-key derivation by oracle-averaging (confirmed non-fidelity, no real analog beyond a loose prototype-formation parallel). One component we deliberately moved AWAY from (PBWM content-gated slot WM) turns out to answer a DIFFERENT brain function (temporal update-gating, not binding) than the one we replaced it for — so that move was not a binding-fidelity regression, but it does mean we may have silently dropped brain-faithful coverage of the maintenance/update axis while chasing the binding axis. Whitening/decorrelation status is unchanged from the prior note (PCA/ZCA-whitening is an engineering convenience; brain-faithful is sparse-expand + post-projection divisive-normalize).

**If forced to answer the USER's literal question in one line: the direction (algebraic binding generally, over a hand-rolled bag-of-tokens) is plausibly toward fidelity, but the SPECIFIC claim "this is how the brain binds" is not supported by settled neuroscience — it is one live hypothesis among several competing ones (mixed-selectivity/conjunctive coding is, per 2024 Neuron review literature, now regarded by many researchers as MORE biologically plausible than synchrony-based binding).** Report this to the USER plainly, per the instruction: the lit does not cleanly support FHRR/phase-binding as settled brain fact.

## Cheap decisive test

There is no single wet-lab-style cheap test that resolves a contested neuroscience debate — that is not something a substrate-side experiment can adjudicate. The decisive test available to us is a DIFFERENT one: an ABLATION comparing the two competing hypotheses' computational signatures on our own held-out generalization tasks —

- **Arm 1 (current):** FHRR phase/complex-multiplication binding (implemented).
- **Arm 2 (mixed-selectivity control):** replace bind/unbind with a random nonlinear conjunctive projection (elementwise product of role and filler passed through a fixed random nonlinear expansion, à la Rigotti/Fusi mixed-selectivity population code), with linear-readout decode instead of algebraic unbind.

Measure on the SAME held-out role-generalization benchmarks already in use (`exp_vsa_native_bind_zeroshot_role_v1.py` lineage, `exp_cross_slot_relational_binding_v1/v2.py`): zero-shot role generalization accuracy, capacity (number of simultaneously superposed bindings before crosstalk floor), and computational cost. This does not settle "what the brain does" (open question) but DOES tell us, for OUR substrate, whether the two competing-hypothesis mechanisms differ in capability — which is the actionable question regardless of which one is more brain-true.

## Falsifiable predictions

**HARD-PASS (justifies keeping FHRR as primary):** Arm 1 (FHRR) matches or exceeds Arm 2 (mixed-selectivity control) on zero-shot role generalization AND capacity-before-crosstalk, at comparable or lower compute cost. This would mean the choice, whatever its neuroscience status, is not costing us capability relative to the alternative the literature says is *more* biologically credentialed.

**HARD-FAIL (should trigger reconsidering primary reliance on FHRR):** Arm 2 (mixed-selectivity conjunctive coding) meaningfully beats Arm 1 on zero-shot generalization (>=0.10 absolute) OR capacity-before-crosstalk, while ALSO being the more literature-credentialed mechanism (per the lit-scan below) — that combination would mean we picked the less-supported AND lower-capability option, which is the worst-case outcome and would warrant an actual architecture pivot, not just a footnote.

**MIDDLE-BAND (most likely, deflated P=0.45):** the two arms perform comparably (within noise) on held-out generalization — meaning the choice between them is underdetermined by OUR current benchmarks, and the deciding factor should be engineering cost (FHRR's O(N log N) FFT bind/unbind vs. mixed-selectivity's larger expansion width) rather than a brain-fidelity tiebreak neither hypothesis clearly wins.

## Cross-thread synthesis — per-component verdicts

### 1. Native algebraic binding (FHRR/VSA) — THE load-bearing component

**Brain analog claimed:** binding-by-synchrony / oscillatory phase coding (Singer, Engel, Fries).
**Our impl:** `hdlab/binding.py` — FHRR elementwise complex multiplication for bind, conjugate multiply for unbind; HRR circular convolution (FFT) as the real-valued sibling.
**Lit verdict (CITED, sub-agent A):** binding-by-synchrony is contested, not settled. Classic disconfirmation cited (Shadlen & Movshon 1999, *Neuron*, "Synchrony Unbound") plus 2023 (ScienceDirect) and 2025 (Springer) papers arguing assemblies bind via rate co-modulation with NO oscillation/synchronization required, explicitly challenging synchrony as a *general* theory. Meanwhile mixed/conjunctive selectivity (Rigotti et al. 2013 *Nature*; Fusi et al., 2024 *Neuron* review "Mixed selectivity: Cellular computations for complexity") is now widely regarded as a credentialed, arguably MORE biologically plausible alternative binding solution — the review literature explicitly frames precise spike synchronization as "unrealistically precise" relative to population-code alternatives.
**Separately (CITED):** Eliasmith's Semantic Pointer Architecture / Nengo (Blouw et al. 2016 *Cognitive Science*; PLOS ONE 2016) and Frady & Sommer's resonator networks (*Neural Computation* 2020; *Nature Machine Intelligence* 2024) DO implement HRR/VSA-style bind/unbind on spiking neuron populations — this establishes computational FEASIBILITY on brain-like (spiking) hardware, which is a real and useful result, but it is not independent evidence that biological cortex actually performs the specific operation (elementwise complex multiplication / circular convolution). Eliasmith himself frames SPA as a unifying computational-level (Marr L2) theory, not a claim about literal biophysical implementation.
**Phase-coding specifically (REASONED, weak):** theta-gamma phase coding (Lisman & Jensen 2013, *Neuron*, "The Theta-Gamma Neural Code") is a real, well-established oscillatory code — but for DISCRETE slot-ordering/sequencing in working memory, not for continuous-phase elementwise complex multiplication. No paper found mapping FHRR's conjugate-unbind operation onto any measured neural phase-coding mechanism; that specific bridge is an analogy VSA researchers draw, not a literature-derived result.
**FIDELITY RATING: MEDIUM.** Marr-L2 computational plausibility with genuine spiking implementation work (real, citable), riding on a contested biological hypothesis (synchrony/phase binding) that a competing, arguably better-credentialed hypothesis (mixed selectivity) directly challenges. NOT "unfounded" (there is real neuroscience engagement, unlike, say, oracle-averaged role keys below) but also NOT settled brain fact — the honest label is **hypothesis-pending-VET**, one live candidate among at least two serious competitors.
**Is the gap fixable-detail or fundamental?** Neither, cleanly — it's an OPEN SCIENTIFIC QUESTION, not a bug in our implementation. The actionable move is the ablation above (Arm 1 vs Arm 2), which sidesteps needing to resolve the neuroscience debate ourselves.

### 2. Superposition / composition (bundle multiple bindings, retrieve via noisy dot-product + cleanup memory)

**Brain analog claimed:** population/distributed coding, superposition of representations.
**Lit verdict (sub-agent B):** CITED — Plate 1995 HRR math confirms the algorithm exactly as we implement it (additive bundling, capacity ~linear in N, crosstalk noise requiring cleanup). CITED — Fusi/Rigotti mixed-selectivity work shows a genuine capacity-vs-dimensionality analog in real PFC populations (more random high-dimensional nonlinear mixing -> more linear-readout capacity, interference-limited). REASONED gap (sub-agent's own flag): cortex is not shown to perform literal vector ADDITION of role-filler pairs into one fixed-width code with dot-product-cleanup readout — mixed selectivity achieves its capacity via nonlinear random EXPANSION (kernel-trick-like), not via superposition-then-unbind of previously bound pairs.
**FIDELITY RATING: MEDIUM.** The CAPACITY/CROSSTALK TRADEOFF has a genuine neural parallel (this is the useful, honest part). The specific RETRIEVAL MECHANISM (additive superposition + dot-product cleanup memory) is a VSA/cognitive-architecture construct without direct single-unit evidence of that literal operation — same flavor of gap as binding above, one level down.
**Gap type:** fixable-detail-leaning — the abstract principle (capacity limited by dimensionality + interference) transfers; the exact algebra is a reasonable proxy, not a fundamental mismatch.

### 3. The encoder (feed-forward, bidirectional-MLM, stateless)

**Brain analog:** recurrent, forward-predictive, stateful cortical processing.
**Lit verdict (sub-agent C, actively looked for counterevidence):** the STATEFUL/RECURRENT axis is well-supported with low controversy (CITED: PMC7205463 recurrent PFC/frontoparietal persistent-activity dynamics; PMC3518874 WM capacity gating sentence argument-retrieval; a 2026 bioRxiv paper on activity-silent WM reactivation during hierarchical language processing, flagged as unreplicated/preprint). The PREDICTIVE-CODING axis is well-established in vision (Rao & Ballard 1999, foundational, heavily replicated) with a standard but not independently-nailed-down extension to language (Friston free-energy framework; treated as REASONED-by-analogy in this scan). The BIDIRECTIONAL-vs-FORWARD-CAUSAL axis specifically is the **shakiest leg, with genuine counterevidence found**: Schrimpf et al. 2021 (PNAS, CITED) and a 2025 paper (arXiv:2510.03156, CITED) support causal/unidirectional models predicting brain activity better than bidirectional ones; but methodologically serious 2025 counterevidence (biorxiv 2025.03.09.642245 "Illusions of Alignment"; arXiv:2605.14025 "Prediction Scores Are Not Enough") argues the "GPT is uniquely brain-like" finding was partly an artifact of last-token activation-extraction methodology that specifically disadvantages bidirectional models, and that with better methods bidirectional models are NOT consistently outperformed.
**FIDELITY RATING: LOW (confirmed non-fidelity), with one sub-claim softened.** Stateless-vs-stateful is a clean, confirmed non-fidelity — the worst-supported part of our encoder is NOT having persistent state/recurrence at all, not specifically its bidirectionality. Recommend softening "bidirectional=bad" to "non-recurrent/non-predictive=bad" — the safer, better-supported framing is that the brain does forward-predictive, STATEFUL processing, without that necessarily requiring every layer be architecturally acausal-free.
**Gap type: FUNDAMENTAL, not fixable-detail.** This matches the standing ledger diagnosis (component_brain_fidelity_ledger.md row 1/2, UNFAITHFUL) — confirmed again independently this cycle, with the added nuance that the fix target should be phrased as "recurrent + stateful + predictive" rather than over-indexing on "must not be bidirectional."

### 4. Whitening / decorrelation

Unchanged from `notes/brain_whitening_decorrelation_pattern_separation_fidelity_2026-07-30.md` (reused per KB-check, not re-derived this cycle): brain decorrelation is NOT a global linear whitening transform (no PCA/ZCA-analog operation found anywhere in real neural circuits across that prior scan). It is local/online subtractive removal of an expected component PLUS divisive gain control PLUS sparse expansion recoding. **FIDELITY RATING: LOW for PCA/ZCA-whitening specifically; the fix direction (sparse-expand -> post-projection divisive-normalize, no separate whitening stage) is HIGH fidelity and already characterized with a concrete pending experiment (Variant A in that note).** Any move toward global/row-wise scale-normalization (mentioned in the task prompt as "the fix we're making") is a lateral move within the same non-faithful family (still a form of the wrong global-linear-operation class) UNLESS it is specifically the post-projection per-code divisive normalization the prior note recommends — confirm which variant is actually being implemented before claiming progress toward fidelity.
**Gap type: fixable-detail.** The prior note already names the concrete brain-faithful replacement; this is an execution/sequencing question, not an open scientific one.

### 5. Content-gated learned WM (PBWM) — moved away from, for binding

**Brain analog:** O'Reilly & Frank PBWN (prefrontal + basal ganglia gating).
**Lit verdict (sub-agent B, CITED):** O'Reilly & Frank 2006 (*Neural Computation*, "Making Working Memory Work") and Chatham & Badre 2015 ("Multiple gates on working memory") both describe BG gating as controlling **WHEN** PFC slot content is updated/protected (a temporal update-control operation, learned via dopaminergic RL over D1/D2 pathways) operating on content that is assumed already bound elsewhere in cortex. No evidence found (explicit negative-result flag from the sub-agent, deflated as a limited-scan absence-of-evidence claim) that BG gating itself performs binding — i.e., conjoining role and filler features into a composite code.
**VERDICT ON THE MOVE:** moving away from content-gated-slot-WM toward algebraic binding was **NOT a fidelity regression for the binding step specifically** — PBWM was never a brain model of binding in the first place; it's a model of a functionally distinct operation (maintenance/update gating). BUT this reframes the earlier move: it means we may have unintentionally ALSO dropped a genuinely brain-faithful maintenance/update mechanism (PBWM-style gating) while solving a different problem (binding), rather than deliberately deciding to defer it. Per the standing ledger, row 6 (WORKING MEMORY/active maintenance) is still marked ABSENT — this lit-scan CONFIRMS that gap is real and distinct from the binding gap, and that PBWM/BG-gating is the correct, well-cited target to fill it, not a discarded alternative to binding.
**FIDELITY RATING: the move itself is fidelity-neutral-to-positive for binding; the underlying maintenance/update gap (separate component) remains a genuine, well-cited-as-absent hole.**
**Gap type: the binding-reframe is a fixable-detail (relabeling); the maintenance/update absence is FUNDAMENTAL and unaddressed** (matches ledger row 6, "ABSENT (the worst gap)").

### 6. Role-key derivation by oracle-averaging over labeled instances

**Brain analog claimed (implicitly):** schema/role representation formation.
**Lit verdict (sub-agent D, CITED):** real precedent exists only as an ANALYSIS technique (fMRI/MVPA condition-averaging across trials sharing a label — Frankland & Greene decode agent/patient structure this way, but that's an experimenter-side tool applied to brain data, not a brain mechanism) and a loose parallel in prototype/centroid category learning (Posner & Keele) — but even there, the "label" is something the learner acquires incrementally through trial-by-trial experience, never a batch average computed with privileged ground-truth access the learner never had. The literature's actual candidate mechanisms for role/schema acquisition are (a) Smolensky Tensor Product Representations, where role vectors are gradient-learned jointly with fillers against a reconstruction objective (CITED, arXiv:1812.08718), (b) distributional/statistical role induction from word order and co-occurrence without explicit labels (CITED), (c) Gentner structure-mapping via comparison/alignment across instances (CITED), (d) Tse et al. schema consolidation via gradual, repeated, label-free experience (CITED, Science 2007).
**FIDELITY RATING: LOW (confirmed non-fidelity).** This is legitimate as an UPPER-BOUND PROBE (measuring "how good could role-general binding be if the role representation itself were solved") but has no real mechanistic analog — it should never be reported or treated as evidence the substrate has learned roles the way a brain does.
**Gap type: FUNDAMENTAL, but with a concretely-named fix.** The lit converges on gradient-learned TPR-style role vectors (optimized jointly with fillers against prediction/reconstruction error) as the brain-faithful replacement target — this is buildable, not an open scientific question like item 1.

## Substrate-product implications

This is a sequencing/priority signal, not a publication angle. Rank order of what actually needs fixing, by (fundamentality x current confidence it's wrong):

1. **Role-key derivation (oracle-averaging) — FUNDAMENTAL, fix is well-specified (gradient-learned TPR-style roles).** Currently a pure upper-bound probe masquerading, if unflagged, as a real capability. Any reported "zero-shot role generalization" number that used oracle-averaged keys should be captioned as a ceiling-probe result, not a comprehension-capability result, until replaced.
2. **Working memory / maintenance-update gating — FUNDAMENTAL, ABSENT, well-specified fix (PBWM-style BG/PFC gating), and now CONFIRMED DISTINCT from the binding work already done** — meaning the binding progress does not substitute for this gap at all; it answers a different question. This is the single most under-addressed component relative to how well-cited its brain-faithful target is.
3. **Encoder statefulness/recurrence — FUNDAMENTAL, confirmed again, softened framing (recurrent+stateful+predictive over strict non-bidirectionality).** No new action beyond the existing ledger item; this cycle adds nuance, not urgency change.
4. **Native binding mechanism itself — OPEN QUESTION, not a confirmed gap.** The right move is the Arm1-vs-Arm2 ablation above, not a pivot away from FHRR on faith. Continuing to build on FHRR is defensible (it is implementable, cited, in active use in the field) as long as we stop describing it as "the" brain mechanism and start describing it as one credentialed-but-contested hypothesis, with mixed-selectivity/conjunctive coding as the live alternative worth testing cheaply.
5. **Whitening — fixable-detail, fix already named in the prior note; verify which variant is actually being shipped.**

The single most brain-foundational next move, by fundamentality x well-specified-fix x currently-unaddressed: **build the gradient-learned (TPR-style) role-key mechanism to replace oracle-averaging** — it directly removes the least-defensible component in the audit (item 6), has a concretely named literature target (Smolensky TPR, joint gradient learning against reconstruction/prediction error), and is a prerequisite for making the binding-mechanism ablation (item 4) a fair test at all — right now any binding-mechanism comparison is confounded by an admittedly non-faithful role-key construction sitting upstream of it.

## Substrate-product implications — direct answer to "full brain-foundational?"

**NO.** Two components are confirmed non-fidelity with no real defense (encoder statelessness, oracle-averaged role keys). One component (native binding) is Marr-L2-plausible but rests on a contested, not-settled neuroscience hypothesis — treat as hypothesis-pending-VET, not fact. One component (superposition/bundling) has a real capacity-tradeoff analog but an unconfirmed literal-mechanism claim. One component (whitening) has an already-diagnosed fix in flight. One component (WM/maintenance) is confirmed ABSENT and was reframed, not solved, by the binding work. The lit does NOT cleanly support "FHRR/phase-binding = how the brain binds" as settled fact — flagging this plainly per the instruction: if this becomes load-bearing to a downstream claim, that claim should be captioned "computationally plausible, not neuroscience-confirmed."
