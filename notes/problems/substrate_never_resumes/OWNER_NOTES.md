substrate_never_resumes — REFUTED. The system really does forget everything each run (confirmed), and the plan hoped that letting it remember would fix its habit of deciding many unrelated words mean the same thing. I wired the "remember" switch and measured it: remembering does not help. Starting from a saved memory, a fresh read of the same material teaches it almost nothing new — 9 new word-meanings versus 168 from a blank start — because it already "knows" the common words, and the genuinely new ones don't match anything in the saved memory closely enough to stick. The meanings it does assign are no more correct than random, either way. The reason: its idea of "meaning" is really "what words appeared nearby in the same batch of text," which doesn't carry across runs. So this was the wrong lever — worth wiring so knowledge can accumulate, but it will not fix the reading. That points back at the reader's meaning channel as the real problem. Wiring proven both ways; measured on 3 seeds with a clean snapshot built on current code; exact code change handed off for integration. Follow-up problem I'd propose creating (insight for the main thread)
There's a genuinely new, file-worthy finding buried in this refutation that no existing brief names. I'd suggest a new problem, tentatively grounding_does_not_transfer_across_reads.

The observation, as one measured number. A word read now matches the words read alongside it 98% of the time, and matches words learned in an earlier read 0% of the time — at the same 0.45 similarity gate, across all 3 seeds (161/164 vs 0/164). The reader assigns meaning by matching a word to its same-batch neighbours, and that representation is not comparable to anything built in a different batch.

Why this is new, not a duplicate. The existing meaning-channel brief measures whether an asset can rank related words in isolation (rho ≈ 0.32). That is a within-snapshot correlation. It does not measure the property a reader-over-time actually needs: can a meaning assigned today be checked against, or built on, what was learned before? My work shows that property is currently zero, and nothing on the board measures it. It's also the unifying explanation for two refutations at once — flat_store (better store didn't help) and substrate_never_resumes (memory didn't help) both failed for the same reason: the representation doesn't survive the read that produced it, so the system structurally cannot build on itself, regardless of how storage or persistence is wired.

What the brief's success criterion should be (this is the part worth stealing): not "the asset ranks related words," but "grounding a word against prior knowledge works as well as grounding it against same-batch neighbours" — i.e., close the 0.00-vs-0.98 cross-batch gap, measured on the real grounding decision against the ConceptNet gold, CI-separated over the co-occurrence floor. That reframes reader_meaning_channel from an isolation metric into a transfer test, which is the thing that actually matters and the thing currently failing.

Why it's tractable now. My substrate_resume work leaves the exact rig to measure it: the identical-text A/B driver, the clean cross-read snapshot, and the gold-precision harness with the RANDOM_ANCHOR floor. A first deliverable could be purely diagnostic — characterize the cross-batch cosine distribution against the 0.45 gate and answer "is there any representation (external embeddings, the meaning asset, a normalized context code) for which cross-read matching is non-zero?" — before anyone commits to a build.
