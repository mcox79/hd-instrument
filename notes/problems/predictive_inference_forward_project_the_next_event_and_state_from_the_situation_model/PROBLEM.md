## THE PROBLEM IN PLAIN LANGUAGE

When you read a story you are always a step ahead of the page. You don't just remember what has happened -- you constantly guess what happens next, and you feel a little jolt when the story surprises you. If a character is hungry and walks into a kitchen, you expect them to open the fridge; if a glass is knocked off a table, you already see it breaking before you read that it did. That running "what comes next?" is not decoration -- it is how reading feels effortless, how you notice when something is off, and how you follow a plot toward its point.

Our reader has the opposite habit. It carefully records everything that has happened -- who did what, where, when, why, what each character wants, believes, and feels -- but it only ever looks BACKWARD. It never once asks "so what happens next?" There is no place in the reader that turns the current situation into a guess about the coming event or state. Build that forward guess: from the situation as it stands right now, plus what the characters are trying to do and what usually follows from what, predict the next event -- and, crucially, know HOW SURE it is, so it commits when the story strongly points one way and holds back when it genuinely could go either way. Prove, on modern test stories, that this forward guess beats simply betting on the most common thing, that it collapses when you scramble the story order (so it is really USING the story, not a frequency trick), and that when it says it is confident it is more often right.

## WHY THIS ONE

The situation-model REPRESENTATION is now largely built: the reader tracks who/what/when/where/why plus per-agent goals, belief, affect, and state, all live and default-on. The next frontier is INFERENCE over that representation, and FORWARD PREDICTION is the most foundational case of all, because the brain's entire comprehension architecture is predictive: it is the same top-down generate-and-check loop that produces the N400, that segments experience into events, and that (in the goal-directed case) anticipates a character's next move. Everything the reader already does is the BACKWARD half of that loop (encode the input); this problem builds the FORWARD half (project the next input) that the loop needs to be a loop at all. It is high-leverage because it reuses proven registers rather than building new representation -- a PARTIAL forward primitive already exists (`predict_surprisal`, a word/argument-level N400 surprisal that is default-ON) and the goal, causal, event-type, and successor-representation organs supply the rest; the value is in the FORWARD PROJECTION over EVENTS, which does not exist anywhere on disk. It also unlocks a whole family of downstream reasoning (anticipation, surprise-driven segmentation, "what will X do", plot expectation) that all bottom out in this one computation. A rigorous negative is equally valuable: it would localize whether the ceiling is the registers' extraction quality, the coarseness of the event representation, or the projection rule itself -- which tells the assembly what to fix next.

## HOW THE BRAIN DOES THIS (the opening move -- PINNED vs OUR-INVENTION)

- **PINNED -- comprehension is PREDICTIVE CODING: the brain constantly generates top-down predictions of upcoming input and codes the ERROR (Rao & Ballard 1999; Friston active inference).** Higher levels predict the next state, the mismatch (prediction error) is the signal that propagates up, and the whole thing is PRECISION-WEIGHTED -- predictions are trusted in proportion to their reliability (defer when uncertain). This is the frame: a forward generative pass over the situation model, an error against what actually arrives, weighted by precision. The BACKWARD half is what the reader already does; this problem is the FORWARD generative pass.
- **PINNED -- language readers PRE-ACTIVATE upcoming words/events, and the N400 is the lexical/semantic prediction error (Kutas & Federmeier 2011); the P600 is structural revision.** Prediction is at the level of MEANING/features, not word-form (Nieuwland et al. 2018). The substrate already computes the word/argument-level N400 (`predict_surprisal`) -- the missing level is the EVENT/STATE prediction that discourse comprehension runs.
- **PINNED -- discourse comprehension actively predicts the NEAR FUTURE at the EVENT level and updates at prediction-error spikes (Event Segmentation Theory: Zacks, Speer & Reynolds 2007; Reynolds, Zacks & Braver 2007).** The event model is a running forward predictor of the next event; when its prediction fails, an event boundary is posted and the model is updated. The substrate has the BACKWARD boundary detector (`n400_coherence_monitor`, graded content prediction error + EST relative-threshold gate) but not the FORWARD next-event generator that it is the error signal FOR.
- **PINNED -- the forward prediction is generated from the CURRENT situation model + the agent's GOALS (goal-directed anticipation) + the CAUSAL / SCRIPT structure (what typically follows).** Readers anticipate the event that advances an active goal (Zwaan & Radvansky 1998 event-indexing; goal is one of the five situation-model indices) and the event that a script/causal chain makes typical (Schank & Abelson scripts; the hippocampal successor representation as a predictive map, Stachenfeld/Behrens; Momennejad 2018). Three PINNED sources, all already on the substrate: `sm.wants`/goal hierarchy (goal), `sm.causal_links`/`event_type` transitions (causal/script), `successor_representation` (learned transition map).
- **THE MECHANISM TO BUILD (the forward projection):** at story-time t, from the situation-model state, generate a GRADED distribution over the next event/state -- (a) goal-directed: the event that advances an OPEN superordinate goal (`goal_hierarchy_graph.open_superordinate`); (b) causal/script: the event the causal chain / event-type transition ontology (`event_type` MENTAL_TRIGGER->MENTAL_OUTCOME etc.) / the successor-representation transition matrix makes most likely; (c) scored as competition over candidate continuations, reusing `graded_competition` (additive cue support -> softmax posterior; the difficulty currency is the maintained distribution's normalized ENTROPY). The PRECISION is the concentration of that distribution (low entropy = confident = commit; high entropy = defer), reusing the predictive-reader precision-weighting rather than a fixed constant. Keep the projection glass-box and hand-auditable (a named composition over the live registers), NOT a learned continuation model.
- **OUR-INVENTION-UNDER-TEST (sweep, do NOT adopt as truth):** the event-prediction READ-OUT format (predict the next event PREDICATE? the next PATIENT/object? a right-vs-wrong CONTINUATION discrimination? a next-STATE transition on `sm` world-state?); how the three cue sources (goal / causal-script / successor) are WEIGHTED and combined (Competition-Model cue validities -- sweep, do not fix); the candidate-continuation generator (from the gold's own options vs a lexical-neighbourhood proposal set); the precision -> commit/defer threshold; the prediction HORIZON (immediate next event vs multi-step). These are parameters/architecture choices -- sweep them, name each PINNED-vs-INVENTED.
