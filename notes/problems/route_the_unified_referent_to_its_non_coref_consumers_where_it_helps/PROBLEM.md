## 1. THE PROBLEM IN PLAIN LANGUAGE
A good reader keeps ONE shared record per character -- "Elizabeth", "the young woman", and "she" all update the same
mental card. We built that shared record and it works, but we plugged it into the one place it does NOT help: the reader's
already-strong, separately-tuned way of guessing who "he" or "she" means. That guesser is so well tuned that the shared
record actually gets in its way -- three separate careful tests agree, and even a PERFECT shared record still loses there.
But the SAME tests show the shared record clearly HELPS the reader's OTHER jobs -- the ones that do NOT have that tuned
guesser: filing a fact under the right named person, attaching a feeling to the right character, and keeping straight which
"the man" is which. Those jobs are still working off their own, weaker, separate bookkeeping. The job here is to run the
shared record to THOSE jobs instead -- and prove, on modern test text, that each one it touches gets better, that a
scrambled version of the shared record falls apart (so we know the real grouping is doing the work), and that the "who is
he/she" guesser is left exactly as it was, untouched.
