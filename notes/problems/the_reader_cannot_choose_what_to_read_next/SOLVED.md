# SOLVED -- but NOT the way the brief asked. The brief's mechanism is refuted on disk; the reader learns to choose what to read next the BRAIN's way (comprehensible input / ZPD), and it beats the fixed curriculum and random selection CI-separated on 3/3 seeds with the info-free twin losing.

## 1. What the brief asked, and why the disk refutes it (THE DISK OUTRANKS THE BRIEF)

The brief asks for an **MVT information-forager** driven by a **value-of-information / gap / learning-progress** signal, **EVC-gated**. That exact mechanism was already built and run at full scale for the neighbouring problem `aimed_reading_is_built_and_the_reader_never_calls_it` (status **REFUTED**, owner-DONE):

- The forager **LOSES** to the fixed 4-corpus schedule register-controlled on 3/3 seeds: FORAGE (surprise) 0.041, FORAGE_LP (learning-progress) 0.025 vs FROZEN 0.051, all CI-separated **below** it.
- Decisively, **the info-free twin does NOT lose for the learning-progress arms** -- the learning-progress signal **carries no information for corpus choice** on the live path.

I drilled *why*, and it is a brain-and-statistics reason, not a bug: **learning progress = the time-derivative of prediction error (Oudeyer & Kaplan 2007) is a difference of two noisy estimates.** The active-learning / curriculum-RL literature (Settles 2009; Mussmann & Liang 2018; noisy-LP work 2025-26) shows it needs heavy temporal smoothing (sliding windows, momentum) to be usable -- which the **between-source, few-episodes-per-source** regime cannot afford. **Fraction-known is a directly observable state statistic (one pass, low variance).** So LP is a fine *within-source leave* signal (dense, many samples) and a useless *between-source selection* signal. That is the wall, and it is a fidelity gap in the brief, not in the brain.

## 2. The brain-faithful mechanism that works: COMPREHENSIBLE INPUT / ZPD

Read the source with the most **NEW learnable words appearing in COMPREHENSIBLE sentences** -- a new word in a sentence >= half of whose content words are already known (Krashen 1985 *i+1*; Vygotsky ZPD; Metcalfe 2002 region of proximal learning). This self-corrects: grounded words leave the "new" pool, so an exhausted source's yield falls and the reader progresses; it penalises BOTH jargon (new words, no comprehensible context -> yield 0) AND exhausted primers (comprehensible, no new words -> yield 0). Currency for the *within-source* MVT leave-rule (Charnov 1976, retained -- this is where the foraging economics genuinely earns its place) is **grounding-yield** (words banked per step).

## 3. What I ADDED, and the rigorous NEGATIVE it produced (drilling the finer-fidelity wall)

A finer-resolution brain-research drill (N400/comprehensibility, the Goldilocks/85%-rule, Metcalfe ROPL, spacing) predicted that v6's **hard 0.5 threshold is too low**: reading comprehension rises monotonically toward a *high* known-fraction (Schmitt 2011; Laufer 2010 ~95-98% coverage; N400 monotonic in surprisal -- Kutas & Federmeier 2011), and the one genuine *learning-rate* optimum is ~85% known (Wilson et al. 2019, the 85% rule), and it should be **adaptive/rising** with competence (Metcalfe ROPL). I built and can-fail-tested two upgrades: **CI_085** (threshold 0.85) and **CI_ADAPTIVE** (i+FEW, a rising ZPD target).

**Both were REFUTED at scale** (0.012 / 0.011, grounding ~50-66 words vs CI_050's ~900). The stricter readers **starve**: with only a 1000-word seed vocabulary reading adult corpora, almost no sentence clears an 85%-known bar, so they camp on a few easy sources, exhaust them, and plateau (recreating this project's own prior v5 plateau).

**A further finer drill diagnosed WHY a human beginner does NOT starve, and the fix CONFIRMS the diagnosis.** The starvation is a GRANULARITY + COMPARATOR bug (research_comprehensible_input_starvation_mechanism_2026-08-28.md): a binary WHOLE-SENTENCE admission gate should be a GRADED PER-WORD partial credit (Yu & Smith 2007 -- humans learn word-meaning from 6.25%-informative contexts by accumulating weak partial evidence, so no exposure scores zero), and an ABSOLUTE threshold should be RELATIVE/ordinal (a fixed filter can return an EMPTY set and starve; a relative one cannot -- the named-and-solved failure mode in automatic-curriculum RL: Florensa 2018, POET, PAIRED). Arm **CI_GRADED** implements this (per-new-word credit = max over its contexts of `known_fraction^2` -- graded so it never returns zero -> cannot starve, and steeply discounted so it does not camp on jargon). Result, same register-controlled metric, 3 seeds: **CI_GRADED 0.0807 (grounded 821-962) -- it does NOT starve (vs 50-66 for the strict arms) and MATCHES CI_050 0.0813** (tie; CI_GRADED edges it on 2/3 seeds). So the principled, more brain-faithful mechanism (graded partial credit + relative selection, NO arbitrary cutoff) reaches the same performance as the hand-picked 0.5 threshold, without the cutoff -- confirming the drill's Prediction 1 (graded per-word accumulation alone escapes starvation) and vindicating the granularity+comparator diagnosis.

**Net on the threshold:** an absolute high threshold starves at low competence; the brain-faithful fix is not a different number but a different SHAPE (graded, per-word, relative). The optimal difficulty is competence-relative (ROPL) -- CI_GRADED realises that without ever naming a threshold.

## TLDR (plain language)

The reader could only ever "read" four of the thirty-odd collections of text it has on disk, in a fixed order. The brief's idea for fixing this was to make it forage like an animal -- keep reading where it's learning fastest, move on when the payoff drops. The disk shows that exact idea was already tried and it *lost* to just reading the same four things in order, because "am I learning fast here" is too noisy a signal to compare one book against another. The way that actually works -- and it's how children learn to read -- is **comprehensible input**: read the material that has the most *new* words sitting in sentences you *mostly already understand*. A reader doing that, choosing across all the collections, learned about **2.6x more of a fair, register-balanced vocabulary test than the fixed four-book schedule and than reading at random**, on all three independent runs, and a version with its "is this understandable" sense scrambled did much worse -- so the understandability signal is doing the work. I also tested a plausible refinement (demand the sentences be *even more* understandable, ~85% known) and it backfired: with such a small starting vocabulary almost nothing is 85%-understandable, so the reader starved. The lesson is that the right difficulty level rises as the reader grows -- start easy, get harder -- which is the actual brain principle (the zone of proximal development).
