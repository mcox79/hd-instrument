## 1. THE PROBLEM IN PLAIN LANGUAGE

Our meaning representations are good at telling which words are **RELATED** (dog-leash, coffee-cup) and bad at
telling which words are **ALIKE IN KIND** (dog-wolf, coffee-tea). Those are two genuinely different senses of
"meaning," and the brain has **two separate systems** for them: an **associative-relatedness** system (broad-context,
LIFG/pMTG) and a **feature-similarity** system (correlational / distinctive-feature, the ATL amodal hub). **We have
built the first and not the second.** Worse, the brain **switches** between them depending on the question (IFG
semantic control, a task-gated multiplicative gain) -- and we have no switch: a fixed blend of our channels HELPS
relatedness but HURTS similarity. So on any test that asks for "alike in kind" (which most taxonomic meaning tests
do), our system reads as broken when it is actually doing the *other* job well. **Build the missing feature-similarity
system brain-faithfully -- from GROUNDING and from STRUCTURED LOCAL context (a tight window / syntactic-thematic
slots), not from a better read-out format -- and build the SEMANTIC-CONTROL gate that selects the right system per
task.**

## 3. HOW THE BRAIN DOES THIS (frame + discipline)

**PINNED (the dissociation, and the control op-class):** the brain has two similarity systems. The **ATL amodal hub**
computes **FEATURE/correlational similarity** weighted toward DISTINCTIVE features (Rogers & McClelland; Patterson,
Nestor & Rogers 2007) -- this is the "alike in kind" axis. A distributed **LIFG/pMTG** system computes **ASSOCIATIVE/
thematic relatedness** from BROAD context. The two use different context: feature-similarity is carried by LOCAL /
STRUCTURED context (syntactic slots, thematic roles, a narrow window -- Levy & Goldberg 2014's functional-vs-topical
result); relatedness by broad context. **SEMANTIC CONTROL = the IFG (with pMTG) applies a TASK-GATED, roughly
MULTIPLICATIVE gain** that selects the currently-relevant system / features (Thompson-Schill 1997; Jefferies &
Lambon Ralph controlled-semantic-cognition framework). This is the audit's THIN "semantic control" deviation, now
with a measured need.
**OUR-INVENTION-UNDER-TEST:** the exact gain function of the gate, the distinctive-feature weighting, and the
structured-context encoder details (window width, whether to use dependency slots vs a raw narrow window).
**COPY the COMPUTATION** (distinctive-feature-weighted similarity; narrow/structured context; task-gated
multiplicative selection). **SWEEP the PARAMETER** (window width, gain temperature, feature-weighting exponent).
