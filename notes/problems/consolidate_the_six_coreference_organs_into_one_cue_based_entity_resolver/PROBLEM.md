## 1. THE PROBLEM IN PLAIN LANGUAGE
When the reader works out that "she", "the doctor", and "Dr. Chen" all point to the same person, the brain does it with ONE machine: it uses whatever hints are available (the word's type, its name, how recently the person was mentioned) to reach into memory and pull back the best-matching earlier mention. We built that ONE machine as SIX separate parts — one for pronouns-by-type, one for names, one for definite common nouns, one for a knowledge-based guess, and so on — each re-implementing the same "use cues to retrieve the best antecedent" step with a different cue. That is not how the brain is organized, and it is the biggest example of us building one brain system as many parts. This problem is to fold those six back into one resolver whose different cues are just settings, and prove the reader behaves identically through the change.
