## 1. THE PROBLEM IN PLAIN LANGUAGE
A good reader explains WHY things happen by knowing, specifically, that THIS action produces THAT state. Our reader has the *machinery* to reason about that (a structured, intervenable world-model that can ask "if this hadn't happened, would that still hold?") and it WORKS wherever it has specific-enough knowledge — the goal slice, where an explicit goal statement + the means-end hub make the causal link specific. But everywhere else it only has GENERIC knowledge ("be happy", "have money") that fits too many candidates, so it picks the cause no better than chance. Three separate deep investigations all hit this exact wall. The missing piece is the KNOWLEDGE, at the right granularity, acquired the way the brain acquires it — from a lifetime of DIRECTED CAUSAL TESTIMONY in language ("the glass broke BECAUSE it fell", "IF she hadn't watered it, it WOULD have died", "stress CAUSES headaches") — mined at scale, consolidated, and applied to the specific story being read.
