## The exact signal-loss diagnosis (the owner's central question, answered with numbers)
Aggregate CoNLL hid the phenomenon, so I measured the hard population and **decomposed every failure by mechanism** (`--signalloss`): **66% were candidate-set misses, and 79% of those were because the true antecedent's most recent mention was a PRONOUN** ("i" 93, "you" 84, "my" 37, "himself" 29…) that the resolver **skipped entirely** — no record for a description to bind to. **The exact difference from the brain:** the brain keeps one entity per character and binds pronouns *into* it in real time; we threw them away. Fixing it (pronoun-into-entity) dropped `gold_entity_absent` 505→170 and raised the candidate-set ceiling 0.34→0.40 exactly as predicted.

## TLDR (plain language)
When a story keeps renaming a character — "the master", "the old man", "the stranger" — linking them was almost never working (4 in 100 of the hardest cases). I found exactly why: most misses were because the character's most recent mention was a pronoun ("he", "I", "himself"), and the system threw pronouns away, so there was no "who" on file for the new phrase. People don't do that — "he", "the old man", and the name are one mental card. I added that missing brain-like step (put pronouns on the card), a "who's in the spotlight now" tracker, and — crucially — I let it use the reader's own character-tracking (which descriptions refer to the same person in *this* story). Together these took the hardest cases from ~4-in-100 to ~43-in-100 (63% of the way to the best-possible ceiling), lifted overall character-linking by a clean, statistically-solid margin with no harm to name-linking, and improved a downstream task (resolving "her father" to the right person). The fixed dictionary of social roles was a small piece (~2%); the real wins were the brain-like mechanisms — and, exactly as expected, the biggest jump came from using the reader's real understanding rather than a rough stand-in.
