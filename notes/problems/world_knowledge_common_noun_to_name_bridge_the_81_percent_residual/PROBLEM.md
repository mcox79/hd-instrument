## 1. THE PROBLEM IN PLAIN LANGUAGE
Readers routinely link a description back to a named character — "the artist" is Zurbarán, "the old man" is Pyotr — even when the text never said "the artist Zurbarán." Our reader can only do this the ~1-in-5 times the link is stated in the text itself; the other ~4-in-5 need knowledge ABOUT the person that isn't on the page. We tried the obvious knowledge source — a database of people's occupations — and it barely helped (about 1 hit in 16), because in real narrative people get referred back to by their RELATION to others, their ROLE in the current situation, their family tie, their age or gender — not by their catalogued job. The job here is to find the RIGHT kind of world-knowledge (the kind the brain actually stores about people and uses to re-identify them) and show it closes this gap, or to prove precisely which kind of knowledge the gap needs.
