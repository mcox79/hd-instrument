## HEADLINE

Both operations are built on **PINNED** primitives (Right-hand Head Rule for compounds, DP-possessed-noun-as-head for genitives, a real neurally-measured phrase-closure/bracket operation in IFG+STS). The place fidelity is **weakest** is the *engineering scaffolding* around those primitives: (a) treating head-identification and role-assignment as two cleanly-sequential stages is an **OUR-INVENTION simplification** — the strongest neuro/behavioral evidence favors tight interleaving, not staging; (b) the case cue's near-silence on canonical-active full-NP objects is not a gap in your implementation, it is **exactly what the Competition Model / eADM predict**, because English morphological case is structurally absent from full NPs (case marks only a closed pronoun class). Build both; expect the chunker to carry the measured gain and the case cue to be legitimately near-silent on the bulk of the corpus, with its real signal concentrated in a small, identifiable subset (pronoun objects, non-canonical order, wh/relative clauses).

## PART 1 — NP-head identification: is it a faithful copy of a PINNED operation?

### 1a. The head-finding rule itself — PINNED, with known and named exceptions

- **Right-hand Head Rule (Williams 1981, *Linguistic Inquiry* 12(2), "On the notions 'lexically related' and 'head of a word'")** — **PINNED.** Standard generative statement: for English N-N compounds and derived words, the rightmost morphological head projects category/features ("kitchen table" → head = table). This is the uncontested default account across the field.
  - **Known exception classes (PINNED to exist, NOT quantified):** exocentric V-N "cutthroat" compounds (*pickpocket, killjoy, scarecrow, spitfire*) and bahuvrihi A-N compounds (*redneck, egghead*) have **no lexical head at all** — the compound denotes an entity outside the compound's own reference (Bauer, "English Exocentric Compounds"; Zwicky's "cutthroat compound" coinage). Dvandva/coordinate compounds (*passer-by*-type, Sanskrit-tradition dvandvas) are argued to be **co-headed**, not right-headed.
  - **No corpus-based exception rate exists in the literature we could retrieve.** A cross-linguistic study (*Linguistics Vanguard* 2019, De Gruyter, DOI: lingvan-2018-0033) defends RHR as "viable" only as a **strong default**, with exceptions treated as systematically conditioned (phonological in Romance, syntactic in Germanic) — not as an exceptionless universal. Any exception-list/lexicon your chunker uses is therefore **OUR-INVENTION-under-test** (hand-curated, uncalibrated against a corpus base rate) — flag it as such, don't claim it as "the measured exception rate."

- **Genitive/possessive DP headedness (Abney 1987, MIT PhD dissertation, "The English Noun Phrase in its Sentential Aspect")** — **PINNED, effectively undisputed** at the level you need it: in "the blacksmith's hammer," the **possessed noun (hammer) is the lexical head of the NP**, the possessor ("the blacksmith's") sits in specifier position. Cross-linguistically motivated (Szabolcsi 1987, Hungarian possessor agreement). Grounding: Chomsky 1970 "Remarks on Nominalization" + Jackendoff 1977 *X-bar Syntax* (MIT Press) — origin of X-bar headedness as a structural primitive.
  - There IS a live theory-internal debate about whether D or N heads the **outer** DP projection (Bruhn, "Little NP Hypothesis," CLA 2017) — but this is orthogonal to your use case: it does not touch whether the possessed noun is the head of the NP itself, which is undisputed. Don't let this contested margin worry you; it's not the claim you're relying on.

- **Universality of headedness across frameworks** (context, not load-bearing for your build): PINNED in X-bar theory, HPSG (Head Feature Principle, Pollard & Sag 1994), and Dependency Grammar (definitional). Weaker/CONTESTED in LFG (violable tendency, not a hard universal) and explicitly REJECTED by Construction Grammar (Croft's Radical CxG denies strict head-driven selection). This doesn't affect English NP/compound/genitive headedness specifically, which sits in the PINNED core all frameworks agree on.

**Verdict 1a:** "rightmost noun in a compound (RHR); possessed noun in a genitive (DP-headedness)" is a faithful copy of a PINNED rule, not a convenient heuristic invented for this project. The exception list you build to patch RHR's known failure modes (exocentric/bahuvrihi compounds) is OUR-INVENTION and should be labeled as a hand-curated patch, not a brain-derived rule.

### 1b. Does head-identification happen BEFORE role assignment? — the weak link

- **Neural evidence for a real, measurable constituent-building operation — PINNED that the operation exists:**
  - **Nelson et al. 2017, PNAS 114:E3669–E3678** (ECoG) — high-gamma activity ramps per incoming word then **drops sharply at phrase closure**, tracking "number of open nodes" — a genuine **stack push/pop, Merge/bracket-closure signature**. Localizes to anterior+posterior STS, IFG pars triangularis/orbitalis, TPJ, dorsal/midline frontal cortex (left-hemisphere-dominant). Model fits favor **bottom-up and left-corner** parsers over strict top-down at these sites.
  - **Ding et al. 2016, Nature Neuroscience 19:158–164** (MEG) — cortical entrainment tracks phrase- and sentence-level periodicity from syntactic/semantic structure alone (no prosodic cue), perisylvian temporal(-frontal) cortex — phrase-boundary/chunk tracking, but the signature itself is **operation-agnostic** (consistent with, not proof of, any one bracket-closure mechanism).
  - **Pallier, Devauchelle & Dehaene 2011, PNAS 108:2522–2527** (fMRI) — six left ROIs (aSTS, pSTS, IFGtri, IFGorb, TP, TPJ) show **logarithmic** activation growth with constituent size (2–12 words). pSTS/IFGtri/IFGorb respond even to jabberwocky (pure syntactic composition, structure without meaning) — direct evidence that **structure-building is dissociable from meaning/role computation** as a neural population-level signal. aSTS/TP need real words and only engage for larger (6+ word) constituents (semantic integration layered on top of, not prior to, structure).
  - Reading across these three: there is a real, PINNED, neurally-localized "close this constituent / commit to a bracket" operation (IFG + STS), and it is measurably dissociable from meaning-driven processing. This is strong support for building a discrete "chunk closes → head available" event in your assigner.

- **Incremental parsing architecture that best fits this signature — left-corner, not top-down or bottom-up:**
  - **Resnik 1992** (COLING, "Left-Corner Parsing and Psychological Plausibility") + **Abney & Johnson 1991** (*J. Psycholinguistic Research* 20:233–250) — left-corner parsing predicts a mother category from its leftmost daughter bottom-up, then attaches remaining daughters top-down; this uniquely matches the human difficulty asymmetry (center-embedding costly / right-branching cheap) that neither pure strategy predicts alone. **Head-timing consequence: in head-initial constructions, the head becomes available as soon as it is scanned and immediately licenses the mother category** — i.e., for VO English, the verb's argument structure becomes available essentially immediately, well before its NP arguments are fully parsed. Modern convergent support: Kuribayashi et al. (*Neurobiology of Language*, PMC11025653) — left-corner-derived surprisal best localizes the syntactic-composition brain signal versus top-down/bottom-up alternatives.
  - Left-corner parsing has a known fidelity limitation for **head-final** constructions (the head is deferred until it arrives late) — not directly your English case, but worth knowing if you ever port this to a head-final construction (e.g., some subordinate-clause or nominalization patterns).

- **The staging question itself — NOT directly evidenced; best characterized as concurrent, not sequential:**
  - **Christiansen & Chater 2016** (*Behavioral and Brain Sciences*, "The Now-or-Never bottleneck") — memory-bounded "chunk-and-pass": rapid compression into local chunks under fleeting-memory pressure. The theory does **not** stage head-identification separately from role computation — a chunk (head + its local dependents) is formed as **one operation**, recoded "Right-First-Time" via heavy prediction. This argues AGAINST a clean two-stage pipeline and FOR concurrent head+role computation within a chunk.
  - **Frazier & Rayner 1982** (*Cognitive Psychology* 14:178–210, Minimal Attachment/Late Closure) — the parser commits structurally to the simplest tree immediately, independent of plausibility; but the structural commit (which licenses the head) and the associated default role assignment appear to happen **in the same processing cycle**, not as separately measured, temporally offset stages.
  - **Ferreira & Patson 2007** (*Language and Linguistics Compass* 1:71–83, "good enough" processing) — thematic roles are assigned via shallow NVN/plausibility heuristics **in parallel with**, not gated on, complete/correct structural (head) analysis. Garden-path misassigned roles measurably **linger** even after syntactic reanalysis has corrected the structure — direct evidence of partial decoupling between structure-building and role-assignment, i.e., role assignment does NOT wait for head-identification to fully complete.
  - **No citation in this scan directly evidences a strict "head-identification-complete, THEN role-assignment-begins" pipeline.** That ordering is an inference from combining structure-first Minimal Attachment with grammatical theory (heads license argument roles) — not itself a measured timing result.

**Verdict 1b (the honest fidelity call):** The RULE for finding a head (RHR / DP-headedness) is PINNED. The EXISTENCE of a discrete, neurally-localized chunk/bracket-closure event (IFG+STS) that plausibly gates head availability is PINNED (Nelson 2017, Ding 2016, Pallier 2011). But **implementing "chunk → extract head → THEN assign role" as two cleanly sequential stages in code is an OUR-INVENTION simplification of a process that the strongest evidence (Now-or-Never bottleneck, good-enough processing, Minimal Attachment) argues is tightly interleaved/concurrent, not strictly staged.** This is a fine, low-cost engineering approximation — a symbolic glass-box system can legitimately implement in two sequential code steps what the brain does in one fused/concurrent operation, as long as you don't claim the sequencing itself is neurally pinned. Say so explicitly in your writeup.

## PART 2 — Morphological case as a role-assignment cue

### 2a. eADM: case is an EARLY, first-pass prominence cue — not a late repair cue

- **Bornkessel & Schlesewsky 2006, Psychological Review 113(4):787–821** — the canonical eADM paper. Defines a three-phase architecture:
  - **P1** — constituent structure building (no relational interpretation yet; this is the Part-1 chunking stage above).
  - **P2** — "compute prominence" + "compute linking": actor/undergoer role assignment via a cross-linguistically-motivated cue set — **case, animacy, word order, person, definiteness — computed IN PARALLEL, within the N400 latency window.**
  - **P3** — generalized mapping, integrating P2 output with plausibility/discourse; **cross-source CONFLICT (not case per se) is what triggers P600.**
  - **Case is explicitly a P2 (early, first-pass) cue, not a P3 (late, reanalysis-only) cue.**
- **Bornkessel-Schlesewsky & Schlesewsky 2009, Language and Linguistics Compass 3(1):19–58** — actor identification proceeds "quickly and unambiguously," verb-independently, from prominence cues computed together; formalized as a ranked, per-language-weighted prominence scale.
- **Bornkessel-Schlesewsky & Schlesewsky 2013, Brain & Language 125:60–76** — dorsal-ventral synthesis; P2 prominence/linking reframed as ventral-stream, time-independent "unification" — consistent with parallel, not serial-reanalysis, cue integration.
- **"How actors become attractors" (Bornkessel-Schlesewsky et al.)** — actor assignment as attractor-basin dynamics: case (language-specific) and animacy (domain-general) jointly shape the depth/steepness of the actor attractor — simultaneous graded cues, not sequential gating.

**ERP signatures** (case-conflict specific, not a single universal component):
- **Frisch & Schlesewsky 2001, NeuroReport 12** — German double-nominative constructions: **N400** when both competing subjects are animate (role-competition cost), **no N400** when animacy already disambiguates; **P600 occurs regardless of animacy**. This dissociates N400 (=thematic-hierarchizing/role-competition cost, P2-level) from P600 (=structural reanalysis cost, P3-level).
- **Bornkessel, Schlesewsky & Friederici 2002, Cognition 85** — "grammar overrides frequency": case-marked object-initial orders elicit ERP effects tracking grammatical case category, not corpus frequency — supports case as an early, rule-governed cue, not a late strategic one.
- **Bornkessel & Schlesewsky 2006 scrambling studies** — biphasic **N400–P600** at the first NP in object-initial orders regardless of which cue (case or animacy) disambiguates.
- **Cross-linguistic case-violation ERPs are NOT a single universal signature**: Hindi ergative violations → N400–P600 biphasic; Basque → mixed (biphasic in one study, isolated P600 in Díaz et al. 2012); Punjabi → P600-only; Icelandic → dissociates by verb class × case combination. The pattern tracks whether the violation creates thematic/role ambiguity (→ N400) versus pure structural illformedness (→ P600) — not a fixed component-per-cue mapping.

**VERDICT 2a — EARLY.** Case is a first-pass Phase-2 prominence/actor-identification cue in the eADM, weighted by language-specific grammaticalization degree, computed in parallel with animacy/word order — most directly supported by Bornkessel & Schlesewsky (2006). This is **PINNED as the eADM's explicit position** (not a contested reading of it). Gate your case cue as a **soft, early, parallel cue alongside position** — NOT as a late revision-only check. This also means: don't build it as "check case only when position is ambiguous" — that would be a Competition-Model-inconsistent serial architecture. Compute both cues together and let them combine (weighted sum/product), per the existing cue-integrator design in `graded_role_assigner`.

### 2b. eADM vs Competition Model — one real mechanistic divergence worth knowing

The lit-scan surfaced a genuine disagreement you should be aware of when you pick the combination rule: **eADM** holds that a sole argument is **always** initially considered for the actor role by default regardless of cue quality, unless a better competitor exists (soft, gradient competition). **Competition Model** (MacWhinney/Bates) allows a strong undergoer cue (e.g., unambiguous accusative case) to **exclude** an argument from actor candidacy outright (harder, more categorical). Your existing architecture is explicitly a Competition-Model cue integrator (per the problem brief) — that's a legitimate, PINNED-alternative choice, just note you are following Competition Model over eADM on this one point of disagreement, not eADM throughout.

### 2c. Cue validity for case IN ENGLISH — qualitative confirmation, no retrievable number

- **MacWhinney, Bates & Kliegl 1984, J. Verbal Learning & Verbal Behavior 23(2):127–150** — defining cross-linguistic cue-validity study (word order, agreement, animacy, stress manipulated). Qualitative finding: **English speakers rely overwhelmingly on word order** (first-noun-as-agent in NVN; second-noun in VNN/NNV); German splits between agreement and animacy; Italian relies heavily on agreement. **We could NOT retrieve specific numeric cue-validity coefficients for English/German/Italian** (paywalled) — only the qualitative ranking, corroborated across secondary sources. Do not quote a specific number for this paper; only the ranking.
- **Bates & MacWhinney 1989, in *The Crosslinguistic Study of Sentence Processing*, Cambridge UP, ch. 1** — the formal definition: **cue validity = availability × reliability**, where availability = fraction of input tokens where the cue is present, reliability = fraction of those occasions the cue gives the correct role.
- **Kempe & MacWhinney 1999, Language and Cognitive Processes** (Russian vs. German) — case availability is measurably lower in German than Russian due to syncretism. The same logic extends further to English, where case survives only on a closed pronoun class (he/him, she/her, they/them, who/whom) — full lexical NPs carry **zero** case morphology in English. This is a structural fact about English morphology, not an empirical uncertainty: **case availability on full-NP direct objects is mechanically zero**, independent of any experiment.
- Qualitative Competition-Model characterization (multiple secondary sources, consistent framing): case is a **high-reliability, low-availability** cue wherever it exists cross-linguistically, contrasted with word order as **always-available-but-not-maximally-reliable**. This maps directly onto English.

**VERDICT 2c — CONFIRMED (structurally, not just empirically):** English case cue validity = high reliability when present × near-zero availability outside pronouns. On canonical-active clauses with full-NP direct objects (the bulk of the cleaned 19c gold), the case cue **cannot fire at all** — this is not "redundant," it's **structurally absent** (the object simply carries no case morphology to read). It becomes present-and-then-redundant-with-position specifically on **pronoun objects in canonical SVO order** (case and position agree). It becomes **decisive** only where case is present AND word order is non-canonical or ambiguating.

### 2d. Where case actually earns its keep — the decisive regime

- **MacDonald 1994, Language and Cognitive Processes 9** — case-marked relative pronouns (who/whom, he/him) **immediately disambiguate** NP/S reduced-relative-clause ambiguity (the "horse raced past the barn" paradigm, Bever), **eliminating** the standard garden-path effect when the embedded NP is a case-marked pronoun rather than a full NP. This is the clearest, most direct English evidence of case being load-bearing.
- Explicit acknowledged gap (noted via Colin Phillips' summary in the scan): "whom" is rare in speech/writing, so it's genuinely unclear whether comprehenders reliably commit to the case cue even in constructions where it's grammatically available — flag this as an open uncertainty, not a settled fact, for modern English. **19c prose likely preserves "whom" usage more robustly than modern English (per your problem brief's own framing) — this is where you'd expect the case cue's measured contribution to concentrate.**
- German evidence (not directly transferable, but structurally analogous and load-bearing for the *mechanism*, not the *magnitude*): **Schlesewsky, Fanselow, Kliegl & Krems 2000** — case conflict is a stronger, faster reanalysis trigger than agreement/number conflict for German subject-object ambiguity; **Meng & Bader 2000** (*Language and Speech* 43(1)) — agreement-based disambiguation produces a MORE robust garden-path than case-based disambiguation, i.e., case resolves ambiguity fast and (relatively) cheaply when present.
- **No English eye-tracking/self-paced-reading study was found that directly pits pronoun case against word order** the way the German literature does — this is a genuine literature gap, not a retrieval failure on our part (confirmed absent across multiple searches). Standard-English nominative-in-object-position / accusative-in-preverbal-position sentences are simply **ungrammatical** rather than garden-path-inducing, since pronoun case is structurally tied to position in the modern language — which is itself informative: it means canonical modern English essentially never presents the parser with a genuine case/position conflict on pronouns. 19c prose, with more literary/inverted constructions and more consistent "whom," is a better hunting ground for genuine conflict cases than modern English.

**Named decisive regime (quotable):** wh-relative-clause/wh-question case marking (who/whom) and non-canonical/inverted/topicalized pronoun-object order. Everywhere else in canonical-active English, case is either absent (full NPs) or redundant-with-position (canonical pronouns).

## PART 3 — Honest fidelity check / the one-paragraph verdict

**Quotable verdict:** For who-did-what SELECTION on canonical-active English clauses with full-NP direct objects — the population that dominates the cleaned 19c gold — the live lever is structural head-identification (NP-head chunking via the Right-hand Head Rule for compounds and DP-headedness for genitives, both PINNED, near-consensus operations grounded in a real, neurally-localized IFG/STS bracket-closure signal), while morphological case is a genuinely EARLY, first-pass prominence cue in the brain's actual mechanism (eADM Phase 2, Bornkessel & Schlesewsky 2006) that is **structurally unavailable** on full-NP objects (English case marks only a closed pronoun class) and, even where present on canonical-order pronouns, is **redundant** with word order (the two cues agree). This is not an implementation gap — it is exactly what the Competition Model's own architecture predicts (cue validity = availability × reliability; MacWhinney, Bates & Kliegl 1984): English is a word-order-dominant language precisely because its case cue has near-zero availability outside pronouns, so word order correctly dominates as the default cue, and case can only earn its keep in the low-frequency regime where it's both present and could diverge from position — pronoun objects, non-canonical/inverted order, and wh-relative-clause "who/whom" marking (MacDonald 1994). A near-silent case cue on the bulk of the corpus, with its measured contribution concentrated in that named subset, is the theory's own prediction confirmed, not a located failure.

**The deeper operation you would be missing, if you stopped at "rightmost-noun rule":** the neuro evidence (Nelson 2017, Ding 2016, Pallier 2011) says the real brain operation is a **left-corner, memory-bounded, chunk-and-pass incremental parser** with a discrete bracket-closure/stack-pop event (localized to IFG pars triangularis/orbitalis + STS) that is **tightly interleaved with, not strictly prior to**, prominence/role computation (Now-or-Never bottleneck; good-enough processing). Building "chunk closes → extract head via RHR/DP-rule → then assign role" as two sequential code stages is a faithful, low-cost engineering approximation of PINNED sub-operations (the head rule, the bracket-closure event), but the **sequencing itself** is OUR-INVENTION — say so in your BRAIN_FOUNDATIONAL_AUDIT entry rather than claiming the two-stage pipeline itself is neurally pinned.

## Cheap decisive test (run BEFORE building the full case-cue wiring)

Before wiring the case cue into `graded_role_assigner`, measure on the held-out cleaned 19c gold: **what fraction of direct objects are pronouns (he/him/she/her/they/them/who/whom) vs. full NPs, and within the pronoun subset, what fraction sit in non-canonical order or wh/relative-clause position.** This is a pure corpus stat, costs nothing to build, and directly predicts the case cue's *ceiling* contribution before any implementation effort: if pronoun-objects are (say) <8% of the gold, the case cue's maximum possible average lift is mechanically bounded near zero regardless of how well you implement it — consistent with, not contradicted by, the theory above. This test converts "case cue gave ~0 lift" from an ambiguous outcome into an interpretable, pre-registered one.

## Falsifiable predictions

**HARD-PASS (mechanism-level, testable on your own build):**
1. NP-head chunker (RHR + DP-headedness) replicates/extends the already-measured +0.043 CI-separated lift over the 0.918 position floor on held-out data, with a shuffled-chunk-boundary twin losing CI-separated.
2. Case cue shows a measurably higher (ideally CI-separated positive) marginal lift on the **pronoun-object subset** than on the **full-NP-object subset** — because only pronouns can carry the cue at all (structural availability difference, not a tuning difference).
3. Case cue's largest lift concentrates in the **non-canonical-order / wh-relative-clause subset** (even if that subset is small) — consistent with MacDonald 1994 and the eADM's own prediction that case is decisive precisely where word order can't disambiguate.

**HARD-FAIL / diagnostic negatives (name these before you look, per calibration discipline):**
1. Chunker gain fails to replicate on held-out data (reverses or vanishes) — a real fidelity ceiling on RHR/DP-headedness generalizing to 19c compound/genitive patterns; report and stop chasing, don't re-derive.
2. Case cue shows a LARGE CI-separated lift on the **full-NP-object subset** — structurally implausible (English full NPs carry no case morphology), almost certainly a leakage/confound in the cue's implementation, not a real case-processing effect. Treat as a bug signal, not a win.
3. Case cue shows **zero or negative** lift even on the pronoun-object, non-canonical-order subset where the literature predicts it should be decisive — this is a genuine, honest located negative on case's marginal value in this specific corpus. Per the problem's own bar, a rigorous located negative here (case implemented faithfully per eADM/Competition-Model, contributes nothing measurable in this register) is a FULL PASS — do not keep re-tuning the cue weight chasing a result the theory itself says should be small.

## Substrate-product implications

A correctly-scoped case cue (soft, early, parallel with position, near-silent on full-NP objects by design) is a small, bounded, low-risk addition to the reader's role assignor — it should not regress canonical modern/19c performance (it structurally can't fire on the objects that dominate both), and its value proposition is specifically in edge-case robustness (wh-questions, relative clauses, inverted/literary constructions) rather than average-case lift. This matches the problem's own framing (a "clean, bounded win," not a north-star capability). The chunker is the larger, already-partially-measured lever; the case cue's contribution should be reported and interpreted at the subset level (pronoun vs. full-NP, canonical vs. non-canonical), not as a single pooled average, or a real small effect will wash out and look like a false negative.
