TLDR (plain language). The fix the problem was named after — a "who are we talking about right now" scene tracker — does not work, and I proved it with the book's own quotation marks. The real problem is that the reader was breaking basic grammar: it kept treating the narrator ("I") as if it could be "he" or "she," and treating places/objects as if they could be people. Making the reader obey grammar — "he/she" is a person and never the speaker, "it" is a thing — fixes about 5 pronouns in every 100 across ALL cases (more in first-person stories, and now helping rather than hurting third-person ones), it's confirmed as a language universal rather than an old-novel quirk, and it holds up even with no special annotations.
