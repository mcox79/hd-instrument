Date: 2026-08-30. Type: literature / mechanism drill (NO experiments run). Grounds the measured
result in `SOLVED.md` (participant-exclusion lifts the anti-typical coref residual +0.079 CI-sep,
recall 1.000, biggest in 1st-person narration +0.137; the focus-STACK was refuted by a perfect-

## BOTTOM LINE (calibrated)

**The literature CONFIRMS the mechanism is brain-foundational, and if anything it is MORE pinned
than I expected.** Grammatical person is not a soft, late, gradient preference the way the brief
feared it might be — across four independent literatures (theoretical, ERP, typological, memory-
retrieval) person is the phi-feature most tightly bound to the representation of the discourse
PARTICIPANTS (speaker/hearer), and the one most RESISTANT to the interference that lets a wrong
antecedent intrude. The adversarial finding the brief said "would be most valuable" — that person-
agreement is NOT hard/early — **did not hold.** It failed in the direction that strengthens the
result.

**One honest correction to the brief's framing** (Q1): "readers CATEGORICALLY never entertain a
1st/2nd-person entity" is the right claim at the GRAMMAR (competence) level but slightly too strong
at the PROCESSING (performance) level — human retrieval is graded best-match, not a hard gate. The
correct statement: person↔non-person disjointness is a truth-conditional fact of the grammar
(Benveniste), realized in processing as an unusually strong, discourse-anchored, interference-
resistant cue. For a lever that operates on the candidate SET (eligibility), a hard EXCLUSION is the
faithful operationalization — it mirrors competence, and the measured recall 1.000 (participant-is-
gold ~1/200) confirms the corpus almost never violates it. **Exclusion is correct; "downweight" would
be the less faithful choice.**

## Q1 — Is person-feature agreement an obligatory, early, hard constraint? Is it stronger than gender? [MOST LOAD-BEARING]

**PINNED — person is a distinct, discourse-anchored, early constraint, qualitatively different from
number and gender.**

- **Grammar / competence (the strongest single point).** Benveniste (1966): 1st/2nd person are the
  "persons" of the speech act (speaker/addressee); 3rd person is the "non-person" — it refers to
  *what is talked about*. A 3rd-person anaphor and a discourse participant are DISJOINT in reference
  by definition, barring quotation/logophoricity. So a participant is not a *low-probability*
  antecedent for "he/she/they" — it is an *impossible* one. This is why the right operation is
  EXCLUSION (set-eligibility), not re-ranking. **PINNED.**

- **ERP — person is processed differently and is discourse-linked.** Mancini et al. (2011), Spanish
  agreement violations: person violations elicit an **N400** ("seldom reported for agreement
  mismatches") plus an **early frontal P600**, whereas number elicits a left-anterior negativity +
  posterior P600. The N400/frontal-P600 for person is interpreted as **"discourse-related integration
  difficulties"** — specifically an "interruption of the establishment of interpretive relations
  ... the association of morphosyntactic person marking with the representation of the discourse
  participant (speaker/hearer)." "The Attractions of Agreement: Why Person Is Different" (PMC6540680)
  and "Processing Differences Between Person and Number" (PMC6382692) formalize this: both features
  share a phi-checking mechanism but differ in **feature-mapping — number maps to cardinality,
  person maps to the DISCOURSE.** **PINNED** (caveat: this ERP work is mostly subject-VERB agreement,
  not pronoun-antecedent anaphora — see calibration).

- **Memory-retrieval mechanism — person is a retrieval CUE (graded), but the interference-resistant
  one.** Cue-based retrieval (Van Dyke & McElree 2006/2011; Parker & Van Dyke 2019, "Cue
  Combinatorics in Memory Retrieval for Anaphora"): an anaphor uses person/number/gender/animacy/
  syntactic-position as cues to retrieve the best-matching antecedent; retrieval is content-
  addressable and interference-prone (cue overload, similarity-based interference). So at the
  processing level it is a strong graded cue, **not a literal hard filter**. BUT the interference
  ("agreement attraction") that lets a feature-mismatching distractor intrude is reported
  overwhelmingly for NUMBER (plural attractors); person is notably resistant — the "Why Person Is
  Different" line is exactly that person does not attract the way number does. **This reinforces the
  lever: person is the feature LEAST likely to admit a wrong antecedent.** **PINNED.**

- **Is person stronger/earlier than GENDER specifically?** Partly. Gender is accessed early (at
  lexical access; gender mismatch yields a larger P600 than number — Chinese ERP work). Person is a
  *different kind* of effect (discourse-interpretive N400) rather than strictly "earlier." So "person
  > gender in strength/earliness" is **SPECULATIVE / not cleanly evidenced** as a single ordering.
  What IS pinned: person is discourse-anchored in a way gender is not, and — decisively for this
  result — the SOLVED doc's own causal test already showed GENDER is a non-lever here (freshly-named
  gold has no prior gendered pronoun), while person is. The literatures agree: person is the sturdier,
  more categorical, discourse-bound constraint.

**Verdict Q1:** the mechanism is pinned. The brief's fear (person is soft → reframe) is refuted;
person is the hardest phi-feature and the one explicitly tied to speaker/hearer representation.

## Q2 — Is the deixis-vs-anaphora / participant-vs-entity split neurally & cognitively real?

**PINNED at the theoretical/cognitive level; SUPPORTED (not proven for the exact linguistic feature)
at the neural level.**

- **Theory.** Benveniste (person vs non-person, above). Bühler's deictic field (origo = I-here-now)
  vs symbolic field; Levinson (1983) deixis — I/you are indexicals whose referent is fixed by the
  utterance's participant roles, categorically distinct from anaphors whose referent is fixed by prior
  text. Grosz & Sidner (1986) attentional state = a stack of focus spaces (global) + centering
  (local) holding the salient ENTITIES; the discourse PARTICIPANTS are the agents doing the
  attending, modeled separately from the focus-space referents. (Precise: G&S's formal object is the
  3rd-person focus space; the *participant* track is Benveniste/Levinson's contribution layered on
  top — cite G&S for "focus space," Benveniste/Levinson for "participants are a separate track.")
  **PINNED.**

- **Neural.** Self-referential "I" processing engages **medial prefrontal cortex / cortical midline
  structures** (Northoff; Ruby & Decety); 1st- vs 3rd-person perspective engage dissociable networks
  (mPFC/self vs TPJ, temporal pole, and hippocampus/visual for 3rd-person observer perspective —
  fMRI moral-judgment and narrative-comprehension studies). Addressee ("you") processing is the
  domain of "second-person neuroscience" (Schilbach et al. 2013) / social-interaction networks.
  3rd-person entity tracking in narrative recruits episodic/situation-model machinery (hippocampus,
  ATL, TPJ). So there ARE distinct representational substrates for self (mPFC), addressee (social/
  ToM), and 3rd-person entities (episodic). **SUPPORTED — caveat:** these map onto PERSPECTIVE / self-
  relevance, which is closely related to but not identical with the linguistic *person feature*; the
  neuro is corroborating, not a direct localization of "person agreement in anaphora." Flag as
  **SUPPORTED, not PINNED.**

**Verdict Q2:** the participant/entity split is real cognitively and has a plausible, multi-region
neural correlate; the exact claim "the brain tracks discourse participants on a separate track from
the anaphoric focus space" is well-motivated and partially localized.

## Q3 — Global person-feature vs per-utterance speaker/addressee tracking: what is faithful?

**PINNED: the coarse GLOBAL proxy is defensible AND is the robust operation; per-utterance tracking
is strictly more faithful but re-introduces the hard attribution problem. The literature directly
predicts "exclusion robust, attribution hard."**

- **The faithful distinction the brief raises is real.** Deictic shift: who "I"/"you" denote changes
  per utterance/turn (the person who is "I" becomes "you"); a maximally faithful model tracks
  speaker/addressee per quoted turn.

- **But EXCLUSION does not need attribution.** Excluding participant-type entities from a 3rd-person
  anaphor's pool requires only the PERSON-TYPE of a candidate entity (does it get referred to as
  I/we/you?), which is invariant across *who exactly* the participant is. Attribution (which character
  is the speaker of this quote) is a strictly harder, inference-heavy task.

- **The literature predicts exactly this asymmetry.** Quote/speaker-attribution work (arXiv 2023-24,
  "Improving Automatic Quotation Attribution in Literary Novels," "Distinguishing Fictional Voices,"
  SIG): explicit quotes (speech verb + named mention) are easy; **anaphoric and implicit quotes are
  hard**, requiring long-range coref + discourse-pattern inference; and **homodiegetic first-person
  narratives are "very challenging."** So the substrate's finding — `WorkingOverlay.resolve_deixis`
  does robust speaker/addressee EXCLUSION while its finer speaker-ATTRIBUTION was SCOPE_LIMITED — is
  exactly what the literature would predict. **PINNED.**

- **Where the global >=50% proxy is SOUND vs where it can fail.** SOUND for the MATRIX narrator/
  addressee — the dominant confound in 1st-person narration, which is precisely where the measured
  win is largest (+0.137). Its known FAILURE MODE is nested quotation: inside a quote, "I" denotes
  the quoted 3rd-person SPEAKER, not the narrator; if those quoted-"I" mentions are aggregated into a
  3rd-person character's cluster, the global proxy could wrongly exclude that character from OTHER
  pronouns' pools. The measured "neutral in 3rd-person narration, never hurts" is consistent — 3rd-
  person narration has no dominant matrix "I," so the filter rarely fires. **PINNED strength (matrix
  participant) + SPECULATIVE bounded failure (nested quoted-I).** This maps onto the linguistic
  EXCEPTIONS to person-disjointness: quotation, logophoricity, free indirect discourse — the ~1/200
  cases and the honest ceiling on recall.

**Verdict Q3:** keep exclusion GLOBAL and robust (it mirrors competence-level eligibility); layer
per-utterance speaker/addressee tracking ONLY inside detected quotation spans, where the substrate's
`resolve_deixis` already operates. Do NOT make the whole lever depend on attribution — that trades a
robust +0.079 for a hard problem.

## Q4 — Is the participant / 3rd-person distinction a language universal?

**PINNED — strongly universal. The lever should generalize beyond English / 19c prose (surface cues
are language-specific; the distinction is not).**

- Cysouw (2003, *The Paradigmatic Structure of Person Marking*): the person paradigm is built from
  speaker(1), addressee(2), other/non-participant(3) in every language; attested group combinations
  are derived from these three primitives. Silverstein (1976) person/animacy hierarchy: speech-act
  participants (1/2, "SAPs") rank above 3rd person universally and govern grammatical marking (case,
  agreement, direct/inverse systems). Benveniste: person vs non-person is definitional, not English-
  specific.
- Corroborating: many languages lack dedicated 3rd-person pronouns and use demonstratives instead —
  which *reinforces* that 3rd person is the anaphoric "non-person," not a true member of the person
  system. **PINNED.**
- Caveat (engineering): the DISTINCTION is universal; the SURFACE FORMS that realize 1st/2nd person
  are language-specific, so a per-language participant lexicon is needed — but the mechanism transfers.

## Q5 — Why would a chatty 1st-person narrator intrude as a distractor absent the person filter?

**PINNED — this is the strongest single confirmation that the mechanism is brain-foundational. The
self is the most salient/accessible entity; ONLY the person constraint keeps it out of the 3rd-person
anaphoric competition. Remove person (via permissive gender) and salience does exactly what salience
does — which is exactly the measured pollution.**

- Accessibility Theory (Ariel): the most accessible entities take the most reduced expressions
  (pronouns); speaker and addressee are permanently, maximally accessible — always in the context,
  high topicality. Givenness Hierarchy (Gundel, Hedberg & Zacharski 1993): speaker/addressee are
  prototypically **"in focus,"** the highest cognitive status. (Precise caveat: Gundel notes the
  hierarchy encodes MANNER, not degree, of accessibility — so cite it for "participants are in-focus,"
  not for a raw salience ranking.) Centering: the narrator "I" is the single most frequent AND most
  recent entity in 1st-person narration → dominates any recency/frequency Cf ranking.
- **Mechanistic prediction = the measured result.** A resolver ranking candidates by recency/
  frequency/salience, with a permissive agreement filter that ADMITS the participant (gender unknown
  ⇒ `_gn_compat` = True), will systematically rank the narrator at/near the top for ANY 3rd-person
  pronoun. The verbatim misses (Dracula "she"→picks "I"; Adrift "he"→picks "I"; Vanity Fair
  "her"→picks "we") are exactly this. The human parser AVOIDS the error because **person is enforced
  BEFORE salience competes** — categorically removing the participant from the pool. The permissive-
  gender arm is a parser that has salience but LACKS the person constraint, and it fails precisely
  where theory says it must. **The effect being biggest in 1st-person narration (+0.137) and neutral
  in 3rd-person narration is a clean theory-data match** (the self is the dominant salient entity only
  in 1st-person narration). **PINNED.**

## TLDR (plain language)

I checked the science behind the fix the reader just got. The fix — "the person telling the story
('I', 'we', 'you') is never who 'he'/'she'/'they' refers to" — is not a lucky trick; it is one of the
most solidly established facts in how humans process language. Linguists since the 1960s have treated
"I/you" (the people talking) as a fundamentally different kind of thing from "he/she/they" (the people
being talked about), and brain-wave studies show the brain reacts to a mismatched "person" in a
special, meaning-level way that it does not use for mismatched gender. This distinction shows up in
every language studied, so the fix should travel beyond old English novels. And the reason the reader
was making the mistake is exactly what the science predicts: the narrator ("I") is the single most
prominent character on the page, so any system that ranks candidates by prominence will grab the
narrator for every pronoun — UNLESS it first rules the narrator out on the grounds of "person," which
is precisely what people do automatically. The one place the science says our coarse version could
slip is inside quoted dialogue, where a quoted "I" belongs to a character rather than the narrator;
that is a known, bounded edge, and it is the piece a finer per-sentence speaker tracker would handle.
