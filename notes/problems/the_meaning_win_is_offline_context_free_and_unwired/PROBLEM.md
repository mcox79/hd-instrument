## 1. THE PROBLEM IN PLAIN LANGUAGE

For a long time we thought the "understand a word" step was broken, because on our home test plain
word-counting beat our meaning tools. We just proved that home test was unfair -- it was secretly
rewarding "pick the commonest partner," which is frequency, not meaning. When we make the test fair
(force a choice between the right partner and decoys that appear equally often), **our meaning signal --
the hands-on sensory "feel" of a word -- picks the right partner far above chance and above every
frequency method, on every run.** That is a real, first-of-its-kind win.

**But that win has two limits we have not touched, and they are the whole of this problem:**
1. **It is OFFLINE.** It was measured by handing the meaning tool two words in isolation and asking "are
   these related?" It has never been put back into the live reader that reads passages, and never shown
   to change what the reader actually understands.
2. **It is CONTEXT-FREE.** It judges a word by itself, with no sentence around it. But the brain does not
   do that -- it picks a word's meaning FROM the context it is sitting in ("bank" by a river vs "bank"
   that holds money). Our win happened without any of that.

**So the question is: does this win survive contact with real reading?** Wire the meaning signal into the
live reader, give it the sentence as context the way the brain does, and test whether it makes the reader
understand a passage better than counting does -- or whether the offline win evaporates the moment it has
to work in context.

## 3. HOW THE BRAIN DOES THIS (frame + discipline)

**PINNED (do not invent around it):** meaning selection is CONTEXT-DRIVEN. The most-frequent sense/associate
is a PRIOR that context overrides when the sentence demands it (lexical-ambiguity resolution -- Duffy &
Rayner reordered-access; the subordinate-bias effect: a subordinate meaning wins when the context supports
it, against frequency). The grounded meaning itself is cross-modal agreement at the anterior-temporal (ATL)
semantic hub over sensorimotor spokes (Patterson 2007; Lambon Ralph 2017) -- which is the grounded
sensorimotor channel this win rides. A brain-faithful read-out therefore (a) uses the current context to
select, and (b) lets the right partner win even when it is not the most frequent.

**OUR-INVENTION-UNDER-TEST:** exactly HOW context conditions the grounded read-out (which contextual
signal, how it re-weights or re-ranks the grounded candidates), and where in the read loop the sentence
context is retained and handed to the read-out. Copy the brain's OPERATION (context-appropriate selection),
sweep any parameter (window, weighting) rather than adopting a number.

**Corpus confound to hold in mind (owner, repeatedly): the reading corpus (McGuffey-derived) is ~200 years
old.** Context-conditioned selection tested only on archaic prose may under-read; the `the_own_metric`
solver already flagged "test whether a MODERN corpus revives the distributional spoke and the concreteness
crossover." If the context-conditioned read-out looks flat, ask whether corpus modernity is doing the work
before concluding the mechanism failed.
