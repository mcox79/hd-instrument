# The meaning lookup cannot inflect -- and lemmatising it inserts REAL meaning on inflected text

## SOLVER REVIEW placeholder (strategy session fills this at integration)

## What the disk said vs the brief

- **Line 165 confirmed** exactly as briefed: `return _table().get(word.lower())` -- raw exact-string
  lookup, 36,810-word table. `countries`/`released`/`animals` miss; `lemma_word` recovers them.
- **The coverage control reproduced** to the brief's figures (token 0.6035, type 0.1027, 235,876
  types, SimLex 100% pair coverage) before any delta was computed.
- **DISK BEATS BRIEF in one place:** the finding note says `women->woman` and `feet->foot` are
  "recovered by NEITHER method." They ARE recovered by `lemma_word` (its irregular table + WordNet
  morphy). Measured directly. So the lemmatiser is slightly BETTER than the brief credited.

## The problem the brief could not measure, and how it was made measurable

The brief **deflated itself** (correctly, under the other session's Test-4): the defect costs `0` on
every scorable instrument we own, because SimLex/SimVerb words are already dictionary forms, and
`read()` never consults the asset. **That is a statement about where the defect was LOOKED FOR, not
about whether it costs anything.** The defect only bites on INFLECTED text, so the fix is to build
the instrument where it bites:

- Take the meaning benchmarks (human similarity ratings on lemma pairs).
- Present each word in a REAL inflected surface form -- generated by an independent rule inflector,
  kept only if it (a) actually occurs in the reading corpus >= 3 times and (b) MISSES the raw table
  (the defect actually bites). Gold lemma is known by construction, independent of the lemmatiser.
- Score four lookup policies against human ratings (Spearman rho), fixed population, paired
  bootstrap: **RAW** (live policy), **LEMMA** (fix: retry `lemma_word` on a miss), **TWIN** (the
  mandated info-free control: retry a RANDOM covered word), **BASE** (oracle: look the gold lemma up
  directly = the asset's own ceiling on this sub-population).

## Two brain-foundational drills (the negatives)

1. **Is the SimLex-verb non-clear a lemmatiser failure?** No. The **BASE oracle clears the same floor
   ABOVE on all four populations** (SimLex-verb +0.203 [+0.013,+0.390]); LEMMA sits only ~0.017 rho
   below BASE, false_rate there is 0.0, and LEMMA approximately equals BASE. So the non-clear is
   **power at the asset's own marginal ceiling** (n=198), not the fix inserting wrong meaning.
2. **False-recovery hand-check.** 0/536 nouns; 5/700 SimVerb-test (0.71%). Four of the five
   (`raped->rape`, `taped->tape`, `hoping->hope`, `waging->wage`) are cases where MY synthetic
   inflector produced a non-doubled form that genuinely belongs to a different lemma, and `lemma_word`
   mapped it **correctly** -- in the live system those are right, not wrong. Only `lives->life` (a
   real life/live ambiguity resolved noun-first) is a genuine lemmatiser error, ~0.1%.

## Brain fidelity

**PINNED-BY-EVIDENCE:** the brain accesses meaning for an inflected word by stripping inflectional
morphology at the lexical interface BEFORE semantic access; the semantic hub is lemma-addressed
(masked morphological priming: `countries` primes `country` like an identity prime -- Rastle & Davis
2008; Marslen-Wilson et al. 1994; Taft & Forster 1975). Lemmatise-on-miss copies that operation
exactly. The known over-segmentation of that mechanism (corner->corn+er) predicts the residual false
recoveries; `lemma_word`'s `is_known_word` gate is our stand-in for the brain's later
semantic-coherence check.
**OURS-UNDER-TEST:** reusing the lemma's grounded vector unchanged for the inflected form (justified:
the brief's landed morphological-pair cosine 0.7605 vs -0.0131 floor), and WordNet morphy as the
decomposer.

## What was NOT established / what I would withdraw first

- This is **not a live-path result.** `read()` still does not consult the asset (the B5 adapter,
  priority 3, is unbuilt). This establishes the MEANING VALUE of the fix on a held-out task; it does
  not by itself move any `read()`-based number. The fix is necessary; wiring B5 is what makes it live.
- The inflected presentation is **synthetic** (benchmark pairs shown in real corpus surface forms);
  there is no naturally-occurring inflected-similarity benchmark. If a critic rejects synthetic
  inflection, the result to withdraw first is the absolute margin -- but the DIRECTION (LEMMA >> TWIN,
  LEMMA ~= BASE, RAW mute) is robust to it.
- The frequency floor is chosen in-sample (strongest channel), which makes it if anything too strong;
  this is the conservative direction.

## PROPOSED hdlab change (NOT landed -- strategy session owns hdlab/, board Q111)

`hdlab/grounded_similarity.py:165`, reusing the repo's own lemmatiser (no new dependency):

```python
def grounded_vector(word: str) -> Optional[torch.Tensor]:
    t = _table()
    v = t.get(word.lower())
    if v is not None:
        return v
    from hdlab.thematic_role_labeler import lemma_word   # already live on the reading path
    return t.get(lemma_word(word.lower()))
```

Why: it recovers the asset's real meaning on inflected running text (the +13.2 token-coverage points),
at a measured false-recovery cost of ~0.1% and a task cost within ~0.017 rho of a perfect lemmatiser.
It should be landed WITH the B5 read() adapter (priority 3); alone it changes nothing `read()` sees.

## KEY REALIZATIONS

- **The enabling move: build the instrument where the defect bites.** The brief said the cost was
  "0 on every instrument we own" -- true, because those instruments feed the lookup dictionary forms.
  Inflecting the benchmark to REAL corpus surface forms is what converted an unmeasurable coverage
  statistic into a CI-separated task result. (This is the same shape as the refuted `flat_store`, but
  the opposite outcome, because here the premise was measured directly rather than assumed.)
- **The BASE oracle separates "lemmatiser quality" from "asset ceiling."** Adding a direct-gold-lemma
  arm turned every negative into a diagnosis: LEMMA ~= BASE and false_rate ~= 0 => the fix is faithful;
  where it does not clear, BASE does not clear either => it is the asset, not the fix.
- **Run more floors than the one you are told to.** The mandated control was the scramble twin; adding
  orthographic and frequency floors caught that at low n a frequency confound eats much of the noun
  signal (the smoke's NOT_SEPARATED), and recomputing the floor on the full corpus flipped it to
  ABOVE -- the measurement-bar rule "recompute the floor on the item's own population" in action.
- **Hand-checking the false recoveries REDUCED the error attributable to the fix:** 4 of 5 were my
  inflector's collisions, not the lemmatiser's.

## TLDR (plain language)

Our meaning table knows "country" but not "countries", "release" but not "released" -- it matches
words letter-for-letter. Teaching it to strip word endings before giving up doesn't just let it
speak more often (that was already known); it lets it speak CORRECTLY. On a standard test of whether
word meanings match human judgement, feeding the table inflected words the way they appear in real
text and then stripping the endings recovers essentially all of the meaning the table would give for
the base word -- clearly better than random word-substitution or than string/frequency look-alikes,
and it holds up across three independent word sets. The occasional wrong strip (about 1 in 700, e.g.
treating "lives" as "life") is rare and mostly harmless. The one caveat: the reader still has to be
wired to consult this table at all (a separate job); this proves the fix is worth wiring.
