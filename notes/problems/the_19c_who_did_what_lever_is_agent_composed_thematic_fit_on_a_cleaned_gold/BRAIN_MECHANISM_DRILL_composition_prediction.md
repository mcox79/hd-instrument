# BRAIN-MECHANISM DRILL — is agent-composition a SELECTION lever or a PREDICTION mechanism?

**Read-only literature adjudication of the on-disk located negative.** Drill question: is it brain-faithful to
conclude that agent-composed thematic fit is a forward-PREDICTION mechanism (surprisal / N400 / pre-activation),
NOT a who-did-what SELECTION lever in position-dominant English — so that measuring it as selection accuracy on
clean canonical direct objects is the WRONG INSTRUMENT, and the selection negative stands as brain-consistent?

## VERDICT: YES — the negative is BRAIN-FAITHFUL (high confidence). One framing refinement required.

The four load-bearing psycholinguistic pillars all confirm the solver's read. The single thing the solver (and the
board) must not do is let "REFUTED" over-generalize from "not a selection lever on canonical DOs" to "composition is
not a real mechanism" — the literature says composition IS real, but it lives on the prediction pathway and in the
position-AMBIGUOUS selection regime, both of which this gold cannot test. With that guard, the negative is correct.

## A. COMPETITION MODEL — is English who-did-what word-order-dominant, with thematic-fit/animacy a WEAKER secondary cue? → CONFIRMED (high confidence)

Yes, and this is one of the most robust cross-linguistic findings in psycholinguistics. In the Competition Model
(Bates & MacWhinney 1987, 1989; MacWhinney 2005; MacWhinney, Bates & Kliegl 1984, "Cue validity and sentence
interpretation in English, German, and Italian", *JVLVB*; Bates, McNew, MacWhinney, Devescovi & Smith 1982,
*Cognition*), the comprehender assigns agent/patient by **weighted integration of cues ranked by cue validity
(availability × reliability)**. In ENGLISH, **word order is the overwhelmingly dominant cue** — English speakers of
all ages give priority to SVO position (first-noun strategy in NVN). Animacy / semantic plausibility / thematic fit
is a **weaker, secondary cue that surfaces mainly when word order is absent, ambiguous, or in conflict.** The
classic diagnostic: when word order and animacy conflict (e.g., an implausible/inanimate-first sentence), **native
English speakers follow word order; native Italian/German speakers follow animacy/morphology.** Case-marking
languages invert the ranking (morphology dominates); English does not have that lever, so position wins.

**Adjudication:** "linear position dominates clean active direct objects" is not an implementation failure — it is
**exactly what the English comprehender does.** Position = 0.918 on canonical active DOs is the brain's own cue
hierarchy showing through. Confirmed brain-consistent.

## B. THE BICKNELL / CHERSONI INSTRUMENT — is the agent×verb effect measured as PROCESSING/PREDICTION, not selection accuracy? → CONFIRMED (high confidence). This is the crux, and it lands for the solver.

**Bicknell, Elman, Hare, McRae & Kutas (2010), "Effects of event knowledge in processing verbal arguments",
*Journal of Memory & Language* 63:489–505.** This is the canonical agent×verb composition result the whole problem
lineage rests on. Its instrument is **self-paced reading time + ERP (N400)**, NOT argument-selection accuracy. The
materials: the likely patient depends on the agent+verb **combination** — *"The mechanic checked the brakes"* vs
*"The journalist checked the spelling"*. **In every item the syntax already fixes which noun is the object** (the
post-verbal noun is the patient by position); what the manipulation varies is how PREDICTED/plausible that patient
is given agent×verb. Dependent measures: **shorter reading time at the word after a congruent patient; a smaller
N400 at the patient itself.** Norming ruled out a direct agent–patient association (i.e., it is genuinely the
agent×verb conjunction). So the composition effect is a **graded processing/prediction/anticipation effect on a
syntactically-unambiguous object slot** — precisely a difficulty/pre-activation signal, not a which-argument choice.

**Chersoni / Lenci line (the "structured DSM"):** Santus, Chersoni, Lenci & Blache (2017, EMNLP, "Measuring Thematic
Fit with Distributional Feature Overlap"); Chersoni, Santus, Pannitto, Lenci, Blache & Huang (2019, "A Structured
Distributional Model of Sentence Meaning and Processing", *Natural Language Engineering*); Vassallo, Chersoni,
Santus, Lenci & Blache (2018, DTFit, "Event Knowledge in Sentence Processing: A New Dataset for the Evaluation of
Argument Typicality"). Across this line the evaluation is **(i) Spearman correlation between the model's thematic-fit
score and human PLAUSIBILITY/TYPICALITY ratings** (McRae/Padó/Ferretti norms; DTFit's 1–7 "how common is it for an
{agent} to {predicate} a {patient}") and **(ii) a binary TYPICAL-vs-ATYPICAL discrimination** — assign a *higher
score* to the typical filler in a minimal pair (*"mechanic fixed the engine"* vs *"journalist fixed the engine"*).
**In both members of every pair the syntax fixes the object identically** — "engine" is the object in both; only the
graded typicality of the agent×verb×patient triple changes. This is a **plausibility/typicality metric, not
argument selection.** The SDM is explicitly validated by fitting **N400 amplitude and reading time**, i.e. as a
processing-difficulty model.

On the specific "72% vs 58%": I could **not confirm those exact digits** in the sources (flag: treat as unverified
provenance). But the structural point is settled regardless — any such accuracy in this line is **typicality
discrimination** (typical vs atypical filler, both syntactically the object), never which-noun-is-the-patient
selection. So even if 72/58 is real, it does not describe a selection task and does not license testing composition
as selection accuracy.

**Adjudication:** the crux holds. The published composition effect is a **prediction/processing measure on items
where syntax already fixes the object.** Scoring it as binary selection accuracy on clean canonical DOs is the
**wrong instrument**, and the resulting null is not evidence against the mechanism.

## C. THEMATIC FIT AS FORWARD PREDICTION / PRE-ACTIVATION → CONFIRMED (high confidence)

The generalized-event-knowledge / thematic-fit mechanism operates as **forward prediction that pre-activates the
likely upcoming filler before it is read**, and only surfaces in SELECTION when position/syntax is ambiguous:

- **Altmann & Kamide (1999), *Cognition* 73:247–264** — anticipatory eye movements: on hearing "The boy will **eat**
  the …", listeners saccade to the only edible object (*cake*) **~200 ms after verb offset, before the noun is
  spoken**. The verb's selectional fit **pre-activates** the plausible object. Forward prediction, demonstrably
  pre-nominal.
- **Kutas & Federmeier (2011), *Annu. Rev. Psychol.*, "Thirty years and counting: finding meaning in the N400"** —
  the N400 indexes **pre-activation / facilitated access** of predictable semantic content; a good thematic/event
  fit reduces N400 because it was already pre-activated.
- **Kuperberg & Jaeger (2016), "What do we mean by prediction in language comprehension?", *LCN*** — comprehension
  is **predictive**; event knowledge pre-activates upcoming arguments at multiple grains.
- **Metusalem, Kutas, Urbach, Hare, McRae & Elman (2012), *JML*** — generalized **event knowledge is activated
  online** and pre-activates event-consistent (even locally-unmentioned) concepts.
- **McRae, Spivey-Knowlton & Tanenhaus (1998), *JML*, "Modeling the influence of thematic fit…"** — thematic fit
  changes the SELECTION/parse outcome **specifically at points of syntactic AMBIGUITY** (main-verb / reduced-relative:
  *"The crook arrested by the detective…"*). A good PATIENT of the verb **avoids the garden path**; a good AGENT
  deepens it. i.e. thematic fit becomes a role-**selection** force **only when word order/syntax leaves the role
  open** — exactly the passive / reduced-relative / fronted regime.

**Adjudication:** thematic fit is a **forward-prediction pathway**, whose behavioural signature is
surprisal/reading-time/N400/anticipatory-looks, and whose **selection footprint appears only in the
position-ambiguous regime.** The 19c gold is **100% active-voice canonical DOs (0/5999 passive)** — the ambiguity
regime is structurally absent — so there is no selection role for composition to play here. Brain-consistent.

## D. NP-HEAD vs THEMATIC ROLE — is compound/genitive head-finding a distinct operation? → CONFIRMED (real brain-level distinction; high confidence)

Yes. Thematic roles are assigned to **arguments (whole NPs/DPs)**; **identifying the head of that NP is a prior
syntactic/phrase-structure operation.** Selectional/thematic constraints apply to the **head noun** of the argument,
so head-finding logically and processing-wise **precedes** role assignment — a different computation:

- Compound heads obey the **Right-hand Head Rule** (Williams 1981): the head of an English compound is its rightmost
  element — *"trade delivery **van**"* → *van* is the head; *trade/delivery* are modifiers. Picking *van* is
  morphological/phrase headedness, **not** thematic-role choice.
- Genitive/possessive heads are **DP-structure** ("the undertaker's **shop**" → *shop* is the head noun, *undertaker*
  is the possessor specifier). Head selection here is X-bar / dependency head-finding (Jackendoff 1977; head-driven
  parsing, Collins 2003), a **chunking/parsing** operation.

**Adjudication:** "the residual is 89% structural (NP-head chunking), not semantic" is a **real brain-level
distinction.** The residual errors are the parser choosing the wrong head **within** a correctly-located argument
NP — a head-finding failure, not a thematic-fit failure. A thematic-fit store cannot fix a head-selection error
because the two operations live at different levels. Confirmed.

## E. GENERALIZATION (taxonomic smoothing / Resnik) — why value shows on PREDICTION not SELECTION → CONSISTENT (medium-high confidence)

The composed/thematic-fit signal's value depends on **register-native taxonomic generalization** of rare/unseen
fillers (Resnik 1996, *Cognition*, "Selectional constraints: an information-theoretic model"; Erk, Padó & Padó
2010, *Comp. Ling.*, exemplar generalization; and the exemplar basis the parent pinned, McRae/Ferretti et al.
1998). This is a **graded, coverage-hungry** quantity: taxonomic smoothing incrementally sharpens a **continuous**
plausibility/surprisal estimate over **many** candidate fillers. That is exactly why it registers on a **graded
prediction metric** (correlation with plausibility, N400/surprisal) — where every increment of coverage moves the
score — but is **invisible on a binary hard-selection metric** on canonical DOs, where **position pre-empts the
decision before the graded signal can matter.** Smoothing improving a plausibility estimate from, say, 0.34→0.40
mean fit changes nothing about a 0/1 argmax that position already wins at 0.918.

**Adjudication:** consistent. Generalization is a prediction-metric lever, structurally unable to surface as
selection accuracy in the position-dominant canonical regime.

## SYMMETRIC-RIGOR CHECK (what would make this NOT brain-faithful — and why it survives)

Applying the same rigor upward that the negative applied downward:

1. **Do NOT over-generalize "REFUTED" to "composition is not a mechanism."** The selection-accuracy null is
   **not** evidence against the agent×verb prediction effect — the literature's own controls (Bicknell's norming;
   Chersoni's typical/atypical pairs) establish the conjunction on a **prediction** metric. The solver's SOLVED.md
   already preserves this ("keep the composed/verb-keyed store for where position fails"; "the mechanism is a real
   verb-specific signal") — that guard is load-bearing and must not be dropped when the board summarizes. The
   honest headline is **"wrong instrument for the canonical-active-DO regime,"** not "composition is dead."

2. **The precise brain-faithful claim is regime-scoped.** Thematic fit **IS** a selection lever — in the
   position-AMBIGUOUS regime (passives, reduced relatives, fronted patients, cue-conflict; McRae et al. 1998;
   Competition Model conflict trials). The solver states this correctly and names the missing regime (100% active
   voice; the position-ambiguous 19c gold is the real data blocker, itself blocked by 19c parser robustness). So
   the claim is not "position always beats fit" — it is "position beats fit **when word order is unambiguous and
   unconflicted**, which is all this gold contains." That scoping is exactly right.

3. **The composition mechanism has NOT been given a fair positive test here — and that is fine for THIS problem.**
   The fair positive test of agent×verb composition is a **graded prediction metric** (surprisal / N400 fit /
   typical-vs-atypical discrimination on a DTFit-style set), not selection accuracy. That test was not run and was
   not the problem's bar; so the negative is a correct answer to *the question asked* (is composition the
   who-did-what SELECTION lever on a cleaned gold?) — **NO** — without being a refutation of composition writ large.

4. **One unverified figure.** The "72% vs 58%" provenance is unconfirmed in the sources; the adjudication does not
   depend on it (the Chersoni-line metric is typicality/plausibility either way). Flag it, don't quote it as
   established.

## BOTTOM LINE

**It IS brain-faithful to conclude:** agent-composed thematic fit is a **forward-PREDICTION mechanism**
(pre-activation; behavioural signature = anticipatory looks / reading time / **N400 / surprisal**), **not a
who-did-what SELECTION lever in position-dominant English.** In English the brain resolves who-did-what by
**word order** first (Competition Model); thematic fit is a weaker cue that becomes a **selection** force **only when
syntax is ambiguous** (passive / reduced-relative — McRae et al. 1998), a regime **structurally absent** from the
100%-active-voice 19c gold. Therefore **testing composition as selection accuracy on clean canonical direct objects
is the wrong instrument**, and the located selection negative **stands as brain-consistent.** The residual is
genuinely **structural (NP-head chunking ≠ thematic role)**, a real level distinction. **The correct home for
composition is the substrate's forward-prediction pathway (the p2 N400 / surprisal organ)**, evaluated on a graded
prediction/typicality metric — not the who-did-what selector. The solver's negative and its routing recommendation
are both endorsed.

## TLDR (plain English)
The question was: "the machine that guesses the likely thing-done-to from the doer-plus-action didn't help pick the
right word after the verb in old prose — is that a real dead end, or did we test it wrong?" Answer: **we tested it
wrong, and that's the brain-faithful conclusion.** In English the brain figures out who-did-what mostly from **word
order** — the thing right after the verb is the thing acted on — and it only leans on "what's a likely thing to
happen" when the word order is scrambled (like passives: "the letter was written by…"). The "likely-event" machine
in the brain is a **guess-ahead** device: it warms up the probable next word *before* you read it (measured by how
fast you read and by a brain-wave called the N400), not a device that *decides which word is the object*. The old
prose we have is all ordinary word order, so the guess-ahead machine has nothing to add there — word order already
nails it 92% of the time. The leftover mistakes are about grabbing the wrong word inside a two-word name — a grammar
problem, not a meaning problem. So the negative is honest and correct; the meaning-guess machine belongs in the
"predict-the-next-word" part of the system, not the "pick-the-object" part.
