## 1. THE PROBLEM IN PLAIN LANGUAGE
The reader has a "what word comes next" organ — the brain's anticipation signal (the N400). A just-finished investigation proved that combining the doer and the action to anticipate the likely next thing ("a blacksmith hammers ___") is a REAL, useful signal — but our organ can't use it, because the little 12-number meaning code it reads from is too coarse to tell the specific candidates apart. In a richer ~200-number meaning space the same idea works well — more than twice as good at anticipating the right word, already measured, with a reusable recipe in hand. So the job is to build that richer meaning space (a compact "hub" of word meaning, sharpened by how words are actually used) and the anticipation recipe on top of it, plug it into the live anticipation organ behind an off-by-default switch, and prove the improvement both on held-out text and running live — with the scrambled-control versions losing. The same richer meaning space is what the big north-star problem needs, so building it here delivers that foundation on a concrete, measurable task first.
