## 1. THE PROBLEM IN PLAIN LANGUAGE
For a word with a rare meaning, the reader now knows WHEN the common meaning is wrong and never wrecks the common cases (that half is solved). What it still cannot do reliably is name the SPECIFIC right rare meaning. The just-finished work proved this is not fixable by reading the answer off the word-web more cleverly, and — importantly — it does NOT need a run-time large language model: a small model that learns to predict meaning from ordinary reading, read out by "which meaning best reconstructs what I was expecting," already beats the old method and, unlike the memorizing version, actually generalizes to unseen documents. It was trained on a modest amount of text; the fix is to train it on far more (that big run is under way) and to train it to predict the event — who did what to whom — not just the next word. The job: build that scaled, self-supervised meaning-prediction model, prove it names the specific rare meaning better than the located-negative baseline on a strict unseen-document test, with a scrambled-context control failing.
