## 1. THE PROBLEM IN PLAIN LANGUAGE
Most words have more than one meaning. A good reader holds each of a word's possible meanings as a clearly DISTINCT idea, briefly considers them all, and then lets the surrounding sentence and story pick the one that fits -- the fitting meaning wins a short contest, the others fade. Our reader does neither part well. Its stored meanings are so alike that they blur into one another, and it has no fair contest for choosing between them -- so it gets the specific meaning right only about a third of the time. Two things need building, and they are how the brain actually does it. First, pull the stored meanings APART so they are genuinely distinct instead of near-copies. Second, let the wider story ACTIVELY push up the meaning that fits and push down the ones that do not, until one wins. Do this the brain's way, without training a big language model.

## 3. HOW THE BRAIN DOES THIS (the opening move -- PINNED)
- **Access is briefly EXHAUSTIVE, then context SELECTS.** Cross-modal priming (Swinney 1979; Tanenhaus, Leiman & Seidenberg 1979): all frequent senses activate within ~0-200ms, then context SUPPRESSES the irrelevant sense within ~200-400ms. Multiple-access -> selection, not lookup.
- **REORDERED ACCESS = weighted competition.** Duffy, Morris & Rayner (1988) subordinate-bias effect: the meaning-FREQUENCY prior and the CONTEXTUAL bias jointly settle it. Frequency is a resting bias, context adds activation (facilitatory, not subtractive) -- the additive reordered-access rule.
- **SUBSTRATE = a FIXED, well-SEPARATED semantic hub selected by a CONTROL network.** The Anterior Temporal Lobe hub-and-spoke (Lambon Ralph) stores concepts as high-dimensional, WELL-SEPARATED patterns learned slowly over a lifetime -- the ATL privileges DISTINCTIVE features (decorrelates the shared/common-mode axis; Patterson-Nestor-Rogers 2007). The context-appropriate aspect is selected by the SEMANTIC-CONTROL network (left IFG + posterior MTG; Jefferies & Lambon Ralph) which UP-weights context-relevant features and DOWN-weights irrelevant ones. Context PRE-ACTIVATES likely semantic features (the N400; Federmeier & Kutas). Settling is recurrent biased competition (McClelland) integrated at the DISCOURSE level (Kintsch construction-integration).
- **THE DECISIVE POINT.** The brain SELECTS among pre-existing senses over a fixed, well-separated hub, PRIMED by context. It does NOT re-encode the token with a learned model. So a trained contextual encoder is BARRED -- it copies a non-brain mechanism ("when the answer becomes train-a-model, STOP") and is separately a REFUTED located negative here. The two levers are (a) hub SEPARATION (decorrelate the near-collinear signatures toward a hub-like geometry) and (b) context-primed BIASED-COMPETITION selection, primed by the reader's DISCOURSE-level situation model rather than only the local sentence.
