## 1. THE PROBLEM IN PLAIN LANGUAGE
The grammar-reader under every ability was just made meaningfully better on modern text, and that work pinned down the two things holding it back now. First: attaching a chain of little linking words ("she put the key IN the box ON the shelf") to the right place — the reader needs this to know who received what and where things are, and it is the single biggest thing a better parser buys; on 200-year-old prose, nine of every ten "who did what" mistakes are exactly this. Second: reliably tagging each word's part of speech on old and literary writing, which the reader gets wrong far more often there and which then breaks everything downstream. The same work proved that no clever trick on the data we already have fixes either one — the reader has learned the modern-newswire style it was trained on and simply has not READ the old/literary style. The brain reads a register robustly because it has been EXPOSED to it, not because it has a fixed grammar. So the job is not more tuning: it is to get, or build, correctly-marked-up examples of the target style and train our own grammar-reader and tagger on them — or to show precisely which data is missing and why that blocks it.

## 2. WHY THIS ONE — the biggest measured parser lever, but gated on data
The parser is the cross-task ceiling, and the parent decomposed exactly where a BETTER parse still pays: NOT general who-did-what heads (position-driven, parse-robust — measured), but PP-CHAIN attachment (`ADP→object→verb`), which is the sole high-precision head demand AND the only measured parse ceiling (oracle-PP recovers path +0.177, location +0.103, source +0.096), AND is 93% of the 19c who-did-what failures. Above it sits UPOS as the universal floor (tagger noise directly caps every consumer; 19c verb-ID −0.10). Both are DATA-BOUNDED: the reader collapses on 19c because it has only modern gold. Modern annotated corpora (LitBank CoNLL, UD-EWT, and other literary/historical treebanks) are already on the shelf or acquirable; the substrate's own parser-training lab is built and proven. This is the shared upstream lever under BOTH the world-state register and the meaning channel, so it doubly compounds.

## 3. HOW THE BRAIN DOES THIS (the opening move)
PINNED: parsing is EXPERIENCE-BASED — the reader builds structure from the statistics of the register it has READ, not from a fixed universal grammar (Christiansen & Chater 2016 Now-or-Never; MacDonald 1994 constraint satisfaction; the brain adapts to register/genre by exposure, syntactic adaptation — Fine, Jaeger et al. 2013). A parse is CO-INFERRED with plausibility, not hard-committed (noisy-channel, Levy 2008 / Gibson 2013). PP-attachment specifically is resolved by lexical/selectional expectations learned from usage (does this verb take this PP as an argument, or is it a modifier of the noun?) — a statistics-of-the-register question, which is why gold target-register training is the faithful lever and self-training (bootstrapping off the model's own modern-biased guesses) is not. Mark each choice PINNED vs OUR-INVENTION: exposure-driven register statistics = PINNED; the specific training regime / feature set = OUR-INVENTION-under-test.
