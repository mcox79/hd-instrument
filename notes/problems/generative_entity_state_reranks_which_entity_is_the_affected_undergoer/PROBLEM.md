## 1. THE PROBLEM IN PLAIN LANGUAGE
When a story says someone "frightened him" or "the doctor examined her", and there are two people of the same gender already mentioned, grammar and recency get you part way — but often two candidates are equally allowed by grammar and equally recent. The brain then asks a deeper question: given what is HAPPENING in this event, whose SITUATION would actually change in a way that makes sense? It picks the person whose state-update is coherent with the event. We have just landed the grammar half (Principle B + role parallelism + salience); this problem builds the deeper half — a reader that tracks each character's evolving state and, for an ambiguous affected-entity decision, prefers the candidate whose state-change under the current event is the most coherent. Two of our solved problems independently pointed at exactly this as the only lever left for the hardest ~40-70%.
