TLDR (plain language). We check our brain-parts on examples chosen to flatter them; this job re-tested the flagged ones on real books they were never tuned for. Of 33 flagged, a third were already proven on big real data (false alarm), a third had already been shown to fail, and a third were only ever tested on made-up examples. We re-ran the most important of those on real corpora: the "smart memory" part shrinks ~15-fold (worth switching on only for busy characters); the "cause/enable/prevent" reader, the "event boundary" detector, and the "protect old memories" replay trick all fail on real text. Crucially, when they failed we found WHY and what would work instead — real causal reading is about which kinds of events tend to cause which (a pattern our data confirms carries the signal), and real memory competition is about two SIMILAR things confusing a cue, not one thing being busy. Those two "what would work instead" directions are the recommended next builds, but they're promising leads (~50/50), not finished. And the biggest fix is upstream: the part that reads events out of text in the first place is only ~1/3 accurate.
