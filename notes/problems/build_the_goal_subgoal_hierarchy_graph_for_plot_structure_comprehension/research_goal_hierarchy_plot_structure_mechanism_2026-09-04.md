## HEADLINE

Four independent literatures converge on a coherent, mostly-PINNED computational picture: a story's goal structure is a **recursive Goal→Attempt→Outcome tree** (story grammar) whose nodes sit inside a **causal-relatedness graph** (Trabasso), where **SALIENCE = causal degree + causal-chain membership** (not raw hierarchy depth), **edges from a goal to its subgoal are MOTIVATION (goal→attempt/subgoal) while a subgoal's OUTCOME reaches the next level up via ENABLEMENT of the superordinate ATTEMPT** (not directly satisfying the superordinate goal), and **reinstatement targets the most-recent-still-OPEN goal in the hierarchy, not the most-recently-mentioned goal** (a real dissociation from pure recency, primary-verified in kind though not in exact distance). The EXPLICIT/INFERENTIAL boundary is real and independently recognized across three unrelated formal traditions (Schank-Abelson plan-matching, Graesser-Singer-Trabasso automaticity classes, Baker-Tenenbaum Bayesian inverse planning) — this is your located negative, confirmed. A goal STACK (Grosz & Sidner) is the right ENGINEERING structure for subgoal *attachment* but is explicitly NOT validated, even by its own authors, as the brain's *reinstatement/activation* mechanism — use the stack for structural attachment, use graph-activation (not LIFO pop order) for salience/reinstatement readout. PFC dissociation evidence (Badre et al. 2009, genuine lesion double dissociation) supports "hierarchy is a real added capability" at the general-cognitive-control level, but there is **no direct narrative-specific test** — that specific bridge is CONTESTED/analogical, not proven.

P_deflated (lit-scan calibration penalty applied, 0.15-0.25 off raw confidence; novel-synthesis capped at 0.50): core structural claims (recursive tree + causal-network salience + M/E edge semantics) = **0.55** (multiply primary-replicated, own worked examples read); reinstatement-rule dissociation (most-recent-still-open vs recency) = **0.45** (primary citation/methodology verified, key quote is secondary-sourced-but-convergent, exact distance parameter unrecoverable); stack-vs-graph architecture recommendation = **0.50** (novel-synthesis cap — this is MY bridging inference across two literatures, not a single study); PFC-hierarchy-is-narrative-relevant = **0.30** (explicitly analogical, no direct test found after real search).

## Q1 — Trabasso & van den Broek (1985): what predicts recall/importance? [SALIENCE METRIC]

**Finding.** Two predictors dominate — **causal CONNECTIVITY** (raw count of causal edges, in+out) and **CAUSAL-CHAIN membership** (on the main opening→resolution chain vs. a causally-connected-but-dead-end node) — and they are correlated but not reducible to each other. Which one carries the *unique* variance is **corpus-dependent**, not a fixed law:
- Trabasso & Sperry (1985) — 6 oral-tradition folktales (Brown & Smiley 1977): connectivity did essentially all the unique predictive work (unique R²=.146, significant in 5/6 stories); chain-membership's unique contribution was ~0 (R²=.021, n.s. in all 6). Direct quote: *"connections uniquely accounted for significant proportions in five out of six stories. However, causal chain failed to account for any significant unique variance."*
- Trabasso & van den Broek (1985) — reanalysis of Omanson (1982b, 9 written stories) and Stein & Glenn (1979, 4 children's stories): the pattern **flips** — chain-membership carries the largest unique share (e.g. .19 of .65 total R² on the Omanson corpus) while connectivity's unique share shrinks to .04. The paper explicitly names the contrast with its own prior finding: *"the causal chain factor accounted for the majority of the variance while causal connectivity accounted for the smallest amount... This finding contrasts with that reported by Trabasso and Sperry (1985)."* Their own resolution: as the proportion of chain-events in a corpus rises (.21→.31→.41 across the three story sets), chain's unique variance rises and connectivity's falls — it tracks the *range of variation* in each predictor within that story set, not a universal weighting.
- Independent proposition-level replication: Fletcher & Bloom (1988), JML 27:235-244, 8 texts — both factors retain small-but-significant unique variance with heavy shared variance and no pathological multicollinearity; the authors state this "replicate[s] in detail the effects reported by Trabasso and van den Broek (1985)."
- **Hierarchy DEPTH does not survive once causal connectivity is controlled.** Trabasso & van den Broek (1985) itself, p.614 (primary-read): *"The 'hierarchical' status of the statement may depend upon [connectivity]... subordinate goals may... have more direct causal connections and be judged empirically as more important"* — i.e. a deeply subordinate goal can outrank a superordinate goal in importance if it is more connected; hierarchy per se does not set importance. A step-wise regression cited in the same passage found the critical-path/causal factor beat hierarchical level as a predictor even where hierarchy had some residual signal.
- **Recall vs. importance dissociate by degree, not kind**: in the Omanson-corpus breakdown, the causal factors' unique contribution *grows* moving from recall→summarization→importance-judgment (.13→.17→.31) while story-grammar *category*'s unique contribution shrinks (.10→.08→.04) — same underlying variables, shifting relative weight by task.

**Citation.** Trabasso, T., & Sperry, L. L. (1985). Causal relatedness and importance of story events. *J. Memory & Language*, 24(5), 595-611. Trabasso, T., & van den Broek, P. (1985). Causal thinking and the representation of narrative events. *J. Memory & Language*, 24(5), 612-630. Both **primary-verified** (original scanned pages read directly, including regression tables and worked quotes). Fletcher, C. R., & Bloom, C. P. (1988). Causal reasoning in the comprehension of simple narrative texts. *J. Memory & Language*, 27, 235-244 — **primary-verified** (full-text OCR read). Hierarchy-depth-null claim additionally sourced to van den Broek, P. (1988), *JML* 27, 1-22 — **secondary-only** (ScienceDirect blocked fetch), but corroborated by a primary-read aside in T&vdB (1985) p.614.

**Tension flagged.** Lane 2 (independently) surfaced a secondary-sourced claim from the *same* van den Broek (1988) paper that "importance of outcomes increased as they ascended in the hierarchy" — apparently contradicting the primary-verified "hierarchy is mediated by connectivity, not independent" reading above. Since the primary-verified source (T&vdB 1985 p.614) explicitly frames hierarchy's apparent effect as *confounded with* connectivity, and van den Broek (1988) itself is only secondary-sourced in both lanes, **resolve this tension by trusting the primary-read mediation account and treating "hierarchy has an independent direct effect" as the weaker, unverified claim** pending a primary read of van den Broek (1988).

**Verdict.** PINNED (3 independently-read primary sources converge) that {connectivity, chain-membership} are the two dominant, jointly-sufficient predictors of both recall and importance, with their *relative* weight being corpus-dependent. CONTESTED-leaning-refuted that hierarchy depth adds independent predictive power once causal factors are controlled (one primary-verified corroborating aside + one secondary-only paper in tension with it).

**What to copy / what to sweep.** COPY: salience = a function of (causal-degree, chain-membership-indicator), not a function of raw tree depth. Do NOT build "deeper subgoal = less salient" or "deeper subgoal = more salient" as a fixed rule — build salience as `w1*causal_degree + w2*on_chain_indicator` and SWEEP `w1:w2`, because the literature itself shows this ratio is not a universal constant — it shifts with story/corpus properties (density of chain-events). Do not use hierarchy-depth as a salience feature at all in the first-order model; if you want to test it, treat it as a candidate confound to explicitly partial out (regress out connectivity first, see if depth still explains variance) rather than a free feature, echoing the original papers' own analysis strategy.

## Q2 — Causal-relation taxonomy: exact GOAL↔SUBGOAL and OUTCOME↔GOAL edge semantics

**Finding.** The taxonomy (Warren, Nicholas & Trabasso 1979) has 4 causal relation types — **Motivation (M)**, **Psychological causation**, **Physical causation (Φ)**, **Enablement (E)** — plus 2 non-causal temporal relations usually dropped. Operational definitions and worked examples (Trabasso & Sperry 1985 pp.599-601; Trabasso & van den Broek 1985 pp.625-628 & Fig.4 — **both primary-read from original scans**, worked from the canonical "Father, His Son and Their Donkey" folktale and "The Tiger's Whisker"):

- **MOTIVATION = GOAL → ATTEMPT** (an already-formed goal motivates the action taken to pursue it): *"the subordinate goal of going to town... motivates the action... of continuing their journey."* M can also run **GOAL(super) → GOAL(sub)** directly in upfront-plan cases (goal-embedding, not triggered by failure) — e.g. the Tiger's Whisker story, where the lady's superordinate goal spawns a subordinate goal (playing music to befriend the tiger) as part of the initial plan, not as a repair.
- The REVERSE direction — an event/action giving rise to a *new* goal — is **PSYCHOLOGICAL causation, not Motivation**: *"the distinction between motivation and psychological causation is mainly on the basis of goal- versus nongoal-directed actions."* Example: tying the donkey's legs (an action) psychologically-causes an internal state ("not liking to be tied"). **So: Event/Outcome → new-Goal is a Ψ-edge; existing-Goal → Attempt/subgoal is an M-edge. These are different edge types, not the same relation read in two directions.**
- **ENABLEMENT** is the general "necessary-but-not-sufficient condition" relation (passes a counterfactual test, no causative verb/agent/state-change required) — reused across two contexts: (i) plain scene-setting (a bridge enabling a later fall), and (ii) critically, **the exact cross-level goal-hierarchy link**: *"Outcome (31) is successful and enables Action (22) which results in another successful Outcome (22) which finally enables Action (12) [which satisfies] Goal (11)."* I.e. the precise chain is **Outcome(subgoal) —Enables→ Attempt(superordinate) → Outcome(superordinate) = Goal(superordinate) satisfied.** A completed subgoal does **not** directly mark the superordinate goal satisfied — it enables the superordinate's *next attempt*, whose own outcome is what satisfies the goal. This is a genuine two-hop structural detail read directly off the primary source's own diagram, not a paraphrase.

**Citation.** Warren, Nicholas & Trabasso (1979), "Event chains and inferences in understanding narratives," in *New Directions in Discourse Processing* Vol. 2 — **secondary-mediated only** (pre-digital book chapter, unreachable; content imported via the two 1985 papers' verbatim citation of it). Trabasso & Sperry (1985) pp.599-601 and Trabasso & van den Broek (1985) pp.625-628/Fig.4 — **primary-verified**, worked examples and diagram read directly.

**Verdict.** PINNED for the M=Goal→Attempt(-or-subgoal) / Ψ=Event→new-Goal / E=Outcome(sub)→Attempt(super) semantics (drawn from two independently-read primary sources with matching worked examples). CONTESTED/weaker only for attributing exact original wording to the un-reachable 1979 source itself (normal citation-chain risk, not a substantive doubt).

**What to copy / what to sweep.** COPY exactly: (1) use TWO distinct edge types for goal-embedding depending on trigger — MOTIVATION for goal→subgoal (whether upfront-plan or failure-triggered), Ψ for event/outcome→newly-arising-goal (this is the "a bad thing happened, so now I want X" case, distinct from "I want X, so I'm trying Y"); (2) do NOT collapse "subgoal completed" directly into "superordinate goal satisfied" — insert the ENABLEMENT hop to the superordinate ATTEMPT node, then let that attempt's own outcome satisfy the goal. This is the single most concrete, copyable structural rule from this drill. SWEEP: nothing here is a free parameter — this is a fixed edge-semantics rule to implement, not a parameter to tune.

## Q3 — Story grammar / GPAO recursive episode structure

**Finding.** Canonical episode schema (Rumelhart 1975; Mandler & Johnson 1977; Stein & Glenn 1979 — converging across sources, minor notational variance in category count): **Setting → Initiating Event → Internal Response (incl. Goal) → (Plan) → Attempt → Consequence/Outcome → Reaction.** Recursive embedding is the standard reading across all three grammars: **a subgoal episode is spawned when (a) a preliminary step is required before the superordinate Attempt can be made, or (b) an Attempt at the superordinate Goal fails** — each spawned subgoal itself expands into a full Goal→Attempt→Outcome sub-episode, recursively, producing a **tree** (Mandler & Johnson's own framing: "a type of tree structure... adequate to describe... both single and multi-episode stories"). This is empirically real, not just descriptive convenience: Mandler & Johnson (1977) showed recall tracks the grammar's constituent structure (tested against Bartlett's "War of the Ghosts" + child/adult developmental data); Black & Bower (1980) found completed/successful goal-sequences recalled better than abandoned ones and superordinate actions recalled more than subordinate ones.

**Citation.** Rumelhart (1975); Mandler & Johnson (1977) *Cognitive Psychology* 9:111-151; Stein & Glenn (1979); Trabasso, Secco & van den Broek (1984); Lichtenstein & Brewer (1980) *Cognitive Psychology* 12:412-455 — bibliographic identity **verified** for all five (multiple independent indices); content **secondary-sourced/medium-confidence** for exact rewrite-rule wording (primary texts paywalled/unreadable scans), **high-confidence** for the general tree-structure and recursion claims (recurring near-verbatim across independent reviews, corroborated by the primary-read Trabasso 1985 papers' own use of the same G→A→O units).

**Verdict.** PINNED at the level of "recursive tree with two named subgoal-creation triggers, empirically tracked in recall" (multiply triangulated). Weaker/medium-confidence on exact original category counts and rewrite-rule notation (a real but non-load-bearing detail — textbooks disagree on 6 vs 7-8 categories, not on the recursive structure itself).

**What to copy / what to sweep.** COPY: the tree shape itself, AND — this is the useful, concrete, buildable detail — **two distinct subgoal-creation triggers to detect separately in text**: (i) a preliminary-step subgoal (planned upfront, no failure needed) vs. (ii) a repair subgoal (spawned specifically because the direct Attempt at the current level failed/was blocked). These likely correspond to different surface cues (planning language like "first she needed to..." vs. failure-then-new-attempt language like "that didn't work, so she tried..."). Don't build one generic "new subgoal detected" rule — sweep whether distinguishing the two triggers improves attachment accuracy over a single undifferentiated rule.

## Q4 — Suh & Trabasso (1993): reinstatement over a distance [REINSTATEMENT RULE]

**Finding.** Citation confirmed **PINNED**: Suh, S., & Trabasso, T. (1993). "Inferences during reading: Converging evidence from discourse analysis, talk-aloud protocols, and recognition priming." *J. Memory & Language*, 32, 279-300. (Verified independently via CiNii + multiple convergent citation records; not primary-PDF-read — paywalled.) Distinct companion piece, don't confuse: Trabasso, T., & Suh, S. (1993), *Discourse Processes* 16(1-2):3-34 (talk-aloud coding of 4 online operations: maintaining/retrieving/elaborating/explaining).

**Methodology** — three convergent methods (near-verbatim recurring phrase across independent sources, high confidence this reflects the actual abstract): (1) discourse/causal-network analysis of texts constructed to allow-or-block specific goal inferences; (2) talk-aloud protocols coded for goal-inference content; (3) recognition priming (probe recognition of goal-statement words/sentences) for converging online evidence of goal reactivation.

**The key dissociation (your (a)/(b)/(c) question).** A recurring, independently-corroborated phrase states inferences "depended upon whether a subgoal or action directly fit the plan of a superordinate goal, and upon which goal was the **most recent unsatisfied goal** in a hierarchical plan." Parse this carefully: this is **not** pure recency (whichever goal was most recently *mentioned* in the text, full stop — that's option (c), and the design appears built specifically to dissociate it) and it is **not** "always the single topmost goal of the whole story" either. It is: **among goals still open/unsatisfied, the one that is nearest/most proximate in the hierarchy** — your option **(b)**. The "Betty" worked example (found independently, recurring across sources): Goal1 (give mother a present) → Outcome1 (failed, couldn't buy anything) → several sentences later → Goal2 (decided to knit a sweater) — the research question is explicitly whether readers infer Goal2 is causally motivated by/reinstates the still-unsatisfied Goal1, testing this against a pure-surface-recency null.

**Distance quantification — genuine gap.** No exact clause/sentence-count for "how far" reinstatement reaches was recoverable from any open-web source after real search; this is disclosed as an honest gap, not fabricated.

**Citation.** Suh & Trabasso (1993) — **PINNED** citation/methodology/qualitative-direction (independently corroborated across ≥3 sources), but the exact quoted dissociation phrase and the distance metric are **secondary-sourced, medium-high confidence, not primary-PDF-verified**.

**Verdict.** PINNED-but-secondary-sourced for "reinstatement targets most-recent-STILL-OPEN goal, not raw recency" — this is the load-bearing finding for your build and it is corroborated by independent triangulation (Albrecht & O'Brien 1993 JEP:LMC 19:1061-1070, goal-context reinstatement/anomaly-detection when unsatisfied goals are violated even across intervening episodes — recalled/medium-confidence in lane 3, content-matched but title initially misattributed by the SOLVER's prompt, corrected here). UNVERIFIED on exact distance.

**What to copy / what to sweep.** COPY: reinstatement readout should be "most-recent-among-STILL-OPEN goals in the hierarchy," which is **exactly** what a status-filtered (active/satisfied/failed) register + most-recent-active readout already does — this VALIDATES your existing flat-register design as brain-faithful at the readout-rule level; the hierarchy composition adds the "in the hierarchy" scoping (skip to the nearest open ancestor, not just the nearest open goal anywhere in the flat list, when a subgoal completes). SWEEP: the reinstatement DISTANCE/decay parameter (how many intervening clauses/events before an open goal stops being eligible for reinstatement, if ever) — the literature gives no number, so this is an open parameter to tune against your own held-out comprehension-question accuracy, not a value to adopt from the science.

## Q5 — Grosz & Sidner (1986): intentional structure / focus stack

**Finding.** **Primary-verified, full text read (ACL Anthology J86-3001).** DOMINANCE relation (partial order: DSP1 contributes-to / DSP2 dominates DSP1) drives the stack: **PUSH** when a new DSP contributes to the immediately-preceding (or a higher, with intervening pops) DSP; **POP** occurs "if and only if" the segment's DSP has been satisfied, after which "the focus space for the interrupted segment will be at the top of the stack because its DSP is yet to be satisfied" — i.e. focus *returns* to the dominating purpose. This is exactly the push/pop mechanic the SOLVER hypothesized.

**Psychological-mechanism status — explicitly weak.** The authors themselves state "the attentional state component is not equivalent to cognitive state, but is only one of its components" and, as of 1986, "there have been no psychological studies of the consistency of recognition of section boundaries." Footnote 12 admits the stack model breaks for true interruptions: **"This boundary is clearly atypical of stacks. It suggests that ultimately the stack model is not quite what is needed. What structure should replace the stack remains unclear to us."** They bolted on an ad hoc "auxiliary stack" for flashbacks.

**Empirical status of the stack, decades later — genuinely unresolved.** Walker (1996) proposed a small-fixed-capacity "cache" alternative; Grosz & Gordon's (1999) rebuttal itself concludes "more empirical investigation is needed to determine the appropriate model," and cites mainstream working-memory findings (Kintsch & van Dijk 1978: shrinking a story-processing buffer from 4 propositions to 1 had **no effect** on recall/summarization fit; Ericsson & Kintsch 1995's long-term-working-memory theory: skilled comprehenders retrieve via organized long-term-memory structures, not a small buffer) that run **against** any small-capacity stack/cache model generally. No reading-time study directly testing LIFO-compliance vs. LIFO-violation in narrative goal-tracking was found after real search (explicit, enumerated absence).

**Centering Theory is a different theory, not a rebranding.** Grosz-Joshi-Weinstein Centering (1995) is a *local*, utterance-to-utterance backward-looking-center mechanism for pronoun/reference resolution, shifting every utterance — NOT the same as the *global*, whole-segment intentional-purpose stack. Grosz & Sidner (1986) themselves flag the risk of conflating them under the ambiguous word "topic."

**Citation.** Grosz, B.J., & Sidner, C.L. (1986). "Attention, Intentions, and the Structure of Discourse." *Computational Linguistics*, 12(3), 175-204 — **primary-verified**. Grosz & Gordon (1999), *Computational Linguistics* 25(4), 615-623 — **primary-verified**. Walker (1996), *Computational Linguistics* 22(2), 255-264 — citation verified, content secondary.

**Verdict.** PINNED as the exact CL/AI-planning-theoretic mechanism (push-on-dominance, pop-on-satisfaction, return-to-dominator). **CONTESTED/UNTESTED as a psychological/working-memory claim** — this is explicit, not just my read: the original authors decline to claim it, the field's own 25-years-later debate ends unresolved with both sides calling for more data, and the broader WM literature leans away from small-capacity-buffer models generally.

**What to copy / what to sweep.** COPY the DOMINANCE-based push rule as the STRUCTURAL ATTACHMENT mechanism — "attach a new subgoal to the most-recent still-open dominating goal of the same agent" is a clean, low-controversy engineering rule for deciding WHERE in the tree a newly-detected subgoal belongs (this is basically definitional, not a contested empirical claim). Do NOT copy strict LIFO pop-order as the REINSTATEMENT/salience-readout mechanism — for that, use the causal-network/graph-activation account (Q1, Q4), which is better empirically supported and already handles the exact failure case Grosz & Sidner admit their stack can't (non-adjacent, skip-level reinstatement). SWEEP: whether to allow "reach into the stack" (skip-level reinstatement past an intervening still-open goal, if evidence points to a *more* superordinate goal) vs. strict top-only — literature doesn't settle this, test both.

## Q6 — Explicit vs. inferential boundary [LOCATED NEGATIVE]

**Finding.** Confirmed as your located negative, from three independently-developed and non-reducible formal traditions:
- **Schank & Abelson (1977)** plan-based inference (mechanism verified via the near-identical 1975 IJCAI predecessor, primary-read): action→goal linking without a surface marker requires matching the observed action against a stored **plan library** ("planbox": a list of primitive acts + preconditions that achieve a goal). Worked example: "Willa was hungry. She took out the Michelin Guide." — sentence-level parsing alone predicts only ingestion-directed action; the link to the guidebook requires **stipulated stored world knowledge** ("the Michelin Guide lists restaurants"), not recoverable from the sentence containing the action.
- **Baker, Saxe & Tenenbaum (2009)**, *Cognition* 113(3):329-349 — **primary-verified**, full text read. Goal inference = Bayesian inversion of a rational-planning generative model: P(goal|actions) ∝ P(actions|goal)·P(goal), agents modeled as cost-minimizing planners in MDPs. Tested on **nonverbal, purely spatial maze-navigation agents** — this explicitly confirms the machinery is independent of, and not derivable from, text/sentence surface structure at all. Extended by Jara-Ettinger, Gweon, Schulz & Tenenbaum (2016), *TICS* 20(8):589-604 — **primary-verified** ("Naive Utility Calculus": joint cost/reward Bayesian inference).
- **Graesser, Singer & Trabasso (1994)**, *Psychological Review* 101(3):371-395 — direct quote recovered (p.390, via a course document quoting the primary text verbatim): causal-antecedent and **superordinate-goal** inferences are generated automatically on-line ("they explain why characters perform intentional actions"), while subordinate-goal/instrument inferences (the *how*) are not automatically generated on-line ("merely elaborate the details about how actions are executed"). Caveat: by GST94's own definition all their classes are "inferences" (beyond what's asserted) — an explicitly-marked goal is not itself one of their classes, it's just explicit content — so their automaticity axis maps onto, but isn't phrased identically to, your explicit/inferential framing; treat as strongly corroborating, not a verbatim restatement.

**Citation.** Schank & Abelson (1977) book itself not directly read (borrow-only); mechanism verified via Schank & Abelson (1975), *Proc. IJCAI-75* — **primary-verified**. Baker, Saxe & Tenenbaum (2009) — **primary-verified**. Jara-Ettinger et al. (2016) — **primary-verified**. Baker, Jara-Ettinger, Saxe & Tenenbaum (2017), *Nature Human Behaviour* 1:0064 — **recalled/secondary only** (paywalled), same family, weaker citation strength. Graesser, Singer & Trabasso (1994) — citation and one key quote **primary-verified via direct quotation**; the broader "13 inference classes" taxonomy is **secondary-sourced, medium-high confidence**.

**Verdict.** PINNED that a real explicit/inferential boundary is independently recognized by three unrelated research programs that do **not** reduce to one shared formal model (symbolic plan-matching vs. automaticity-tagged taxonomy vs. Bayesian generative-model inversion) — convergent-but-not-unified is itself informative: this is a robust phenomenon, not an artifact of one theory's framing.

**What to copy / what to sweep.** This is the clean stop-line for your build: explicit-marker extraction ("wanted to X"/"tried to Y"/"in order to Z") is a shallow, safely-in-scope lexical-syntactic operation. Linking a later, UNMARKED action to an earlier or unstated goal is a qualitatively different, heavier computation (plan-library matching, or a cost/utility generative model + Bayesian inversion over it) that consumes world/plan knowledge your structural graph builder does not have and should not fake with surface heuristics. Recommend: keep this explicitly out of scope as a separate, harder, named capability (a future "planning-inference module"), not a stretch-goal of the hierarchy-graph builder. Nothing here is a sweep parameter — it's a scope boundary to hold.

## Q7 — PFC hierarchical-control dissociation (is the hierarchy a real added capability?)

**Finding.** In the **abstract task-switching/rule-abstraction domain**, dissociation evidence is genuinely strong: Badre, Hoffman, Cooney & D'Esposito (2009), *Nat. Neurosci.* 12:515-522 — **primary-verified, full text read (PMC2990342)** — a real crossover **double dissociation**: 12 frontal-lesion patients sorted by lesion location (caudal = "feature-deficit," rostral = "dimension-deficit") across 4 nested orders of rule abstraction; deficits are asymmetrically nested (impairment at a level given impairment at a lower level = 91%, vs. only 76% the other way — a genuine hierarchy signature, not generic damage-severity). Converges with Koechlin, Ody & Kouneiher (2003), *Science* 302:1181-1185 (cascade model: rostral LPFC gated by temporal-episode context, caudal by immediate stimulus) and an independent-lab, independent-paradigm replication, Balaguer, Spiers, Hassabis & Summerfield (2016), *Neuron* 90(4):893-903 (naturalistic hierarchical subway-network planning, dorsomedial PFC scales with hierarchy-representation cost). Charron & Koechlin (2010), *Science* 328:360-363, shows **flat/concurrent (non-nested)** goals recruit a *different* organizing principle (bilateral hemispheric division of labor) than the serial rostro-caudal cascade used for nested goals — the same research program treats flat and hierarchical goal-tracking as mechanistically distinct phenomena (this specific bridge is the lane's own inference, flagged OUR-INFERENCE, not asserted by either paper).

**Important qualification**: Badre & D'Esposito's own 2009 *Nat. Rev. Neurosci.* review is explicitly nuanced, not a clean endorsement — it surveys 4 competing organizing frameworks (domain-general WM capacity, relational complexity, temporal organization, abstract hierarchy) without declaring a winner, and other work (monkey tracer studies) finds the anatomical connectivity does NOT follow a strict rostro-caudal scheme. Shallice & Burgess (1991) is a **weaker fit** than commonly assumed for a hierarchy-specific dissociation — their Six Elements deficit looks more like impaired delayed-intention reactivation under flat concurrent-task management than a means-end embedding-depth manipulation; do not cite it as clean hierarchy-vs-flat evidence.

**Narrative-specific test: NONE FOUND**, after a real, well-enumerated search (~10 query reformulations + 4 direct-fetch attempts). The closest narrative-fMRI finding, Speer, Zacks & Reynolds (2007), *Psychological Science* 18(5):449-455 — **verified** — shows narrative goal-*change* tracking (not embedding *depth*) dissociates from character-change tracking and implicates lateral PFC; it never manipulates hierarchy depth. A superficially similar "hierarchical narrative" literature (cortical timescale hierarchy — words→clauses→scenes, Hasson/Baldassano/Chen tradition) is confirmed to be a **different construct** (temporal/event nesting, not means-end goal-plan nesting) and should not be conflated despite keyword overlap. The closest embedding-depth analog is recursive Theory-of-Mind (Coricelli & Nagel 2009, *PNAS* 106(23):9163-9168, k-level strategic reasoning ~ medial PFC activity) — a related but distinct construct (epistemic embedding, not motivational/means-end embedding), and shows only graded/parametric, not double-dissociated, depth effects.

**Citation.** Badre, Hoffman, Cooney & D'Esposito (2009) — **primary-verified**. Koechlin, Ody & Kouneiher (2003) — verified via abstract. Balaguer et al. (2016), Charron & Koechlin (2010) — verified via convergent secondary sources. Speer, Zacks & Reynolds (2007) — verified via convergent secondary sources. Badre & D'Esposito (2009) review — verified for its qualified/multi-framework stance, not primary-quote-level.

## Falsifiable predictions (HARD-PASS / HARD-FAIL)

1. **Edge-semantics prediction (Q2)**: if the ENABLEMENT-hop rule (subgoal-outcome enables superordinate-attempt, not superordinate-goal directly) is implemented, comprehension questions of the form "did completing subgoal X finish goal Y" should show that the substrate correctly withholds "goal Y satisfied" until the intervening superordinate-attempt's own outcome is registered. HARD-PASS: this two-hop structure reduces false-positive premature-goal-satisfied errors vs. a naive one-hop "subgoal done -> goal done" rule, measured on a held-out annotated set. HARD-FAIL: no measurable difference (the two-hop distinction doesn't matter for this substrate's task-format) — in that case, simplify to one-hop and note the mismatch as a documented simplification, not a silent deviation.
2. **Salience concentration prediction (Q1)**: connectivity and chain-membership should show the SAME collinear-but-partially-independent pattern found in the human literature — i.e., a regression of held-out human-importance-proxy (or comprehension-question-frequency, as a cheap proxy) on both features should show substantial shared variance but nonzero unique variance for at least one of the two, on this substrate's own texts. HARD-FAIL: if one of the two features carries ALL the signal and the other is entirely redundant across every tested text (not just corpus-dependent as in the human literature), the substrate's causal-network extraction is likely missing one of the two constructs (e.g., failing to distinguish dead-end nodes from chain nodes at all) — a construction-bug hypothesis to check before concluding the feature is genuinely useless.
3. **Reinstatement distance prediction (Q4)**: since the literature gives no distance parameter, sweep it explicitly (e.g. 1, 3, 5, 10 intervening events) against comprehension accuracy. HARD-PASS: accuracy is non-monotonic with a clear interior optimum (evidence the parameter is real and tunable, matching the WM-resource-gated account in the adjacent causal-attribution drill's Event Horizon Model findings). HARD-FAIL: accuracy is flat or monotonically decreasing with no benefit from any nonzero distance allowance (evidence the "reinstate across distance" mechanism isn't earning its complexity for this substrate — in that case, prefer the simpler "only reinstate within-current-window" rule and cite this as a located boundary, not a failure).

## Substrate-product implications

In plain terms: right now the reader tracks each character's goals as a flat list with a status (open, done, failed) and picks the "most recently mentioned open goal" when it needs to explain why a character does something later. This research says that's the right SHAPE of readout (open-goals-only, most-recent-among-those), but it's missing the HIERARCHY scoping — when a small goal finishes, the reader should look for the nearest still-open BIGGER goal that the small goal was serving, not just the nearest open goal anywhere. The science also gives two concrete, buildable rules: (1) how important a story event is to remember/answer questions about tracks how many other events it's causally tied to AND whether it sits on the main chain from the story's start to its end — NOT how deeply buried it is in a plan; (2) finishing a small goal doesn't instantly finish the big goal it served — it should unlock the NEXT attempt at the big goal, and only that attempt's own result finishes the big goal. There is a firm, science-backed boundary on what NOT to build: figuring out a goal that was never stated in words (inferring "he must want money" from watching someone rob a bank, with no "wanted to" in the text) is a genuinely different, harder capability requiring stored plan/world knowledge — keep it as a separate future capability, not a stretch of this graph builder. Risk: the "which goal gets reinstated" rule is well-supported in kind but the exact "how far back" number was never published anywhere I could find — that number has to be tuned against our own tests, not copied from the science, and the general "hierarchy lives in more rostral brain regions" claim, while true for lab rule-switching tasks, has never actually been tested on story-reading specifically, so it should be cited as a supporting analogy, not a proven fact, in any downstream document.

## TLDR

We asked cognitive science exactly how people track a story character's nested goals (want money → so try to get a job → so try to write a resume) and how they know which finished or unfinished goal matters most. Four independent research traditions agree on a clear picture: an event matters for memory and importance because of how many other events it's tied to and whether it's part of the direct line from the story's start to its ending — not because of how deeply buried it is in a plan. When a small goal finishes, it should unlock the next try at the bigger goal, not instantly finish the bigger goal itself. When the reader needs to explain a new action, they reach back for the nearest goal that is still unfinished, not just whichever goal was mentioned most recently — which matches how our existing goal-tracker already works, a reassuring confirmation. There's also a clean, well-supported line in the sand: figuring out a goal that the text never actually states requires a different, much heavier kind of guessing (using general knowledge about how people plan) — that's fair to leave for a later, separate piece of work. One brain-imaging finding (damage to a specific frontal-brain region breaks nested planning but not simple planning) supports the idea that a goal hierarchy is a genuinely different, extra brain capability, not just the same simple goal-tracking done twice — though nobody has directly tested that specific idea using story-reading itself, so it's a reasonable analogy, not a proven fact.
