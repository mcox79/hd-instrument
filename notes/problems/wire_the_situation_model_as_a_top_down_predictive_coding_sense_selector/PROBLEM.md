## 1. THE PROBLEM IN PLAIN LANGUAGE
Many words have more than one meaning. Ours picks the meaning by letting activation spread through a fixed word-web and reading off whichever meaning lights up — a purely bottom-up, local guess. That works for the common meaning of a word but fails on the rare one, which is exactly the hard case ("the river BANK" when "money bank" is far more common). A large, careful effort just proved that letting the word-web GROW from reading does not fix this — and, more valuably, it proved WHY: to pick the rare meaning you need to understand the SITUATION the sentence is describing and let that understanding predict which meaning is coming, then be surprised (and override the common guess) when a word does not fit. The brain does exactly this — it runs a running model of the situation, predicts each next word's meaning, and its "surprise" signal (the N400) is that prediction being violated. We already have the running situation model, the surprise/override machinery, and the meaning-selection circuit built and tested separately. The job is to connect them: let the situation model predict each word's expected sense, and let a mismatch drive the override — then let understanding and word-meanings sharpen each other as reading continues.

## 3. HOW THE BRAIN DOES THIS (the opening move)
PINNED: sense selection is TOP-DOWN prediction from a hierarchical GENERATIVE model of the SITUATION (world knowledge, events/entities/discourse), not bottom-up co-occurrence. Predictive coding (Rao & Ballard 1999; Friston 2010): higher levels predict lower-level activity; the residual is prediction ERROR. The N400 is the SEMANTIC prediction error (Kutas & Federmeier 2011; Rabovsky, Hansen & McClelland 2018 — the N400 as change in a model's implicit semantic representation). Reordered access (Duffy/Rayner): all senses activate, ordered by dominance; context reorders — so the read is a dominance PRIOR that a reliable top-down prediction can OVERRIDE by inhibition (LIFG/pMTG semantic control; biased competition, Desimone-Duncan). The apparent circularity (you need comprehension to disambiguate, and disambiguation to comprehend) DISSOLVES by BOOTSTRAPPING: senses and comprehension co-develop through cross-situational reading (Yu & Smith 2007; Srinivasan) — start coarse, comprehend, disambiguate, re-carve granularity, iterate to a fixpoint.
