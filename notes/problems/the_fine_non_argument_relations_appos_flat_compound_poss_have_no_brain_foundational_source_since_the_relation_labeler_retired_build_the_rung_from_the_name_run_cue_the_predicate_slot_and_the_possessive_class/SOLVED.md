## 2. WHAT WAS BUILT -- the brain's mechanism, per relation, and why it is an ARM and not a new organ

None of these four relations needs a classifier, and none of them is a new computation for this substrate.

* **A NAME RUN ("Mary Smith") is not a relation at all.** It is ONE referring expression stored and retrieved
  as a unit -- Kripke 1980 rigid designation; Semenza 2006/2009 proper-name anomia localises multi-word proper
  names to the LEFT TEMPORAL POLE, a level ABOVE and fed by the category level. The UD `flat` arc is the
  residue of that chunking. Our organ for it is pri 118's span-level NAME-RUN cue
  (`hdlab.coref.span_caps_symbol`, learned online from the reader's own confident typings).
* **An APPOSITION ("Elizabeth, the doctor") is a REDUCED PREDICATION** -- the same construction as "Elizabeth
  is the doctor" with the copula absent (Pustet 2003 on copula optionality; Maienborn 2005 Kimian states;
  Bemis & Pylkkanen 2011 LATL property attribution). The organ that detects a predication is pri 110/117's
  PREDICATE SLOT. The comma is surface: pri 129 counted the gold and only 24 of 105 appositions carry one.
* **A POSSESSIVE ("her brother", "Mary's dog") is CASE MARKING** -- the Competition Model's strongest

## C. THE COPULA-LESS PREDICATION — what the brain's slot looks like for "Tom, the baker, ..."

**It is not the main clause's predicate slot at all, and that is why lead 1 as I first stated it was the wrong
shape.** An appositive is a SECONDARY, PARENTHETICAL predication. Potts 2005 (*The Logic of Conventional
Implicature*) shows supplements contribute an INDEPENDENT, not-at-issue proposition: *"Tom, the baker, left"*
asserts `left(Tom)` and, in a separate layer, `baker(Tom)`. Del Gobbo 2003 and Heringa 2011 derive the
appositive as a REDUCED COPULAR CLAUSE (*"Tom, (who is) the baker"*), and Pustet 2003 is why the copula's
absence is unremarkable: the copula carries tense, it is not the predication, and a majority of the world's
languages omit it in exactly this configuration. So the slot the brain opens is a **second predicate slot whose
subject is the anchor DP** — and a predication needs TWO referring expressions, which is the whole point.

**THE SMALLEST ARM THAT OPENS IT, and it is only expressible because of §B.** `zcop` failed because "could a
copula stand in this gap" is true between any nominal and any post-nominal nominal. The missing evidence is the
CHUNK BOUNDARY: does the second nominal open a determiner phrase of its own, or is it a member of the first
one's chunk? That is exactly what the NP chunker built in §B draws. The cue `dpjux` reads it, arc-free:
`dp2_comma` / `dp2_bare` (a determiner-opened chunk abutting a closed nominal chunk, with or without a comma),
`same_chunk` (a member of a run that started earlier), `no_det` (a bare second nominal), `no_left`, `na`.

## D. WHAT A DISCRIMINATIVE VERSION OF EACH REJECTED CUE WOULD NEED

### D.1 THE HUB TYPE CUE (an apposition is a type attribution)

**The distinction it must carry, and does not: TOKEN-IDENTITY versus TYPE-SUBSUMPTION.** The cue I built asks
*what kind is this nominal, and what kind is the one before it* — and every one of the four classes it has to
separate answers that question the same way. `person|PROPN|diff` is "Elizabeth, the doctor" (appos), "President
Obama" (flat), "company director" (compound) and "Mary and John" (conj) alike, which is why its most
appos-favouring value tops out at P(appos | value) = 0.186 while carrying 22 compound / 30 conj / 15 flat. The
question an apposition actually answers is not *what kind* but **does the second expression PICK OUT THE SAME
INDIVIDUAL as the first** — Potts 2005's supplement asserts an identity (`the baker = Tom`), a compound
*restricts* a kind, and a conjunct introduces a *second* individual. That is a referential relation between two
discourse files, not a relation between two lexical types.

**Does the reading supply it?** Partly, and not at this rung. The entity layer has exactly the machinery: the
ACT-R cue retrieval already asks "is there a file this description matches", and `crosstype_bridge`'s
descriptive-content boost is that question with a name on it. What the parse rung can contribute is the
*evidence* — number agreement between the two heads, the second DP's definiteness, whether the first is already
an open file — and those are cheap and available **once mentions carry their span** (phase 7's B), which is
precisely why definiteness read `bare` on 100% of mentions before today. So the discriminative version is not a
better type cue; it is the arrow reversed: **the entity layer proposing co-reference and the parse rung reading
the proposal**, which is lead 2 and belongs to pri 136's organ, not to this one.

**Cheap check I did run, and it is worse than I expected:** of the 654 type values, exactly **6 have `appos` as
their argmax class, and between them they carry 17 of the 870 gold appositions in the counts — 2.0%**. A
PERFECT argmax readout of this cue would recover two appositions in a hundred. The cue is not weak; it is
answering a different question, and the counts say so without any further run.

### D.2 THE ZERO-COPULA PREDICATE SLOT

**The distinction it must carry, and does not: ONE referring expression versus TWO.** `zcop` asks *could a
copula stand in this gap*, and the honest count says the account behind it is right — the value fires 11,877
times and lifts apposition 5.7× over its base rate (P(appos | v) = 0.0407 against 0.0071). What it cannot do is
tell "Tom, the baker" from "the terrorist group Hamas": a copula could stand between any nominal and any
post-nominal nominal, so its top class is `nmod` at 0.355 with `compound` at 0.213, and in an additive
competition that is a correlated second vote on a population the `crs` / `gap` / `dcat` cues already delimit —
the double-counting hazard this organ documented at cue set v2. **A predication needs two referring
expressions; a compound is one.** The missing evidence is therefore the CHUNK BOUNDARY: does the second nominal
open a determiner phrase of its own, or is it a member of the first one's chunk?

**Does the reading supply it? Yes — and only since phase 7.** The NP chunk is exactly what the span repair
builds, and it is arc-free, so it is available at this rung with no second parse. That is why C is not a
re-run of the same idea: `dpjux` conditions the same predication account on the chunk boundary the chunker now
draws, which is the one piece of evidence `zcop` had no access to. Measured below.

## PHASE 7 — KEY REALIZATIONS

1. **TWO ORGANS CAN EACH BE CORRECT AND THE CONTRACT BETWEEN THEM STILL BE BROKEN.** `parse_litbank_conll`
   puts `wtok_start` at the span start; `referent_per_np` puts it at the head. Neither file is wrong on its
   own terms, both pass their own witnesses, and the pair silently disables a default-ON organ on 100% of
   documents. **A field contract is not checked by any test that runs one module.**
2. **A DEGENERATE VALUE HIDES A DEFECT IN BOTH DIRECTIONS.** The one-token span made the extent consumers
   accidentally right AND the head-index consumers accidentally right; repairing it breaks the second set,
   which is how I found that `graded_role_assigner:2069` and `:2154` have been reading the determiner instead
   of the head on every multi-token GOLD mention since they were written.
3. **COUNT THE CUE BEFORE YOU BUILD THE TABLE.** The two-DP pre-check cost seconds with gold categories and
   answered the whole of C: the account is right (×25.9 lift), the class is 48% treebank convention, and the
   arithmetic bound follows without a 10-minute rebuild. I had been about to spend the rebuild first.
4. **MY OWN CUE HAD A DEAD BRANCH AND THE COUNT FOUND IT.** `same_chunk` could never fire (runs are disjoint,
   so the previous run can never overlap this one); fixing it moved 251 gold appositions into the bucket that
   explains them. *A cue with an unreachable value is a cue you have not read.*
