## 1. THE PROBLEM IN PLAIN LANGUAGE
Our word-tagger decides each word's part of speech by picking the single highest-scoring label and never looking back. Two costs: the confidence it reports is not a real probability (it is over-sure), and on unusual/old prose it commits to a wrong tag and can't recover. The brain never commits — it holds a graded, calibrated belief about "is this an event-predicate here" and lets word-category and sentence-structure settle each other JOINTLY. A prototype already showed that retraining the SAME tagger with a probability (likelihood) objective — a CRF, identical features and data, only the training goal changes — gives a calibrated belief that recovers old-prose verbs at 0.806 instead of 0.582, at no cost to modern. The job: make that calibrated tagger real, then go one step further and let the tagger and parser decode JOINTLY (each re-estimating the other), which the same drill showed helps modern and — if the parser is ALSO made register-robust — should lift old prose toward what a competent reader gets. It is one upstream fix that pays off twice: better verb detection AND a better parser.

## 3. HOW THE BRAIN DOES THIS (the opening move)
PINNED: lexical category is a GRADED, CALIBRATED belief continuously re-weighted by context (predictive coding; Kuperberg-Jaeger 2016), NOT a committed max-margin tag; category + structure settle JOINTLY (MacDonald 1994; Fromont/Steinhauer/Royle 2020 — N400+P600 additive, no syntax-first ELAN). The computational analogs: a likelihood-trained CRF gives the calibrated posterior (Lafferty 2001); joint POS+parse decoding re-estimates category from structure inside the search (Bohnet-Nivre 2012), with the KEY refinement the drill bought — a LIKELIHOOD/CRF objective, NOT the perceptron max-margin (which gives a degenerate saturated posterior). OUR-INVENTION-UNDER-TEST: the exact joint-decode architecture / beam / feature set + whether to reimplement CRF glass-box or take a dependency. One contested-evidence caveat (hold SPECULATIVE): prediction-as-preactivation has a failed replication (Nieuwland 2017/2018); the graded-vs-margin and generative-vs-discriminative divergences are PINNED.
