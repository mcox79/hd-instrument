## HEADLINE

**The core three-cue model is right and should not be replaced — but the pipeline has a real, literature-motivated architectural bug: it types the argument noun BEFORE scoring the verb sense, when the evidence says the two should be scored JOINTLY at the governor-dependent parse edge.** Four independent psycholinguistics threads (Hare/Elman/Tabaczynski/McRae 2009 noun-typicality shifting verb-sense choice; Altmann & Kamide 1999 anticipatory verb-driven restriction; McRae/Ferretti/Amyote 1997 verb-specific thematic-role typicality; Trueswell/Tanenhaus/Garnsey 1994 immediate interactive use of thematic fit) converge on "argument semantics and verb meaning constrain each other immediately and locally," but — flagged honestly — **no single study manipulates noun WORD-SENSE ambiguity (the WordNet-polysemy kind the solver is worried about, e.g. "room"=place vs. scope) as the thing driving verb-sense choice.** That specific joint-resolution claim is a synthesis extrapolation, not a directly-evidenced result. The concrete fix (Section 2) is implementable regardless: score `(verb_sense, noun_sense)` pairs jointly at the single dependency edge, not sequentially.

Two secondary, also-load-bearing findings: (1) the "softmax-of-cues = Bayesian posterior" language should be downgraded from proven-fact to aspirational-unless-calibrated — McClelland (2013)'s actual proof requires log-likelihood-calibrated weights and conditional independence between cues, and construction-fit/thematic-fit are probably NOT independent given a sense (both derive from the same event schema); (2) the coarse-grain/MFS-underspecification design is correct **specifically for polysemy clusters** (most WordNet multi-sense verbs) but is the WRONG model for true homonym splits, where the literature shows fast, hard, obligatory commitment — the design needs a homonymy exception path, not a uniform rule.

Per lit-scan calibration discipline (deflate 0.15-0.25 uncharted, cap novel-synthesis P≤0.50): **P_deflated = 0.45** for the joint-scoring recommendation (the single most important, most novel-synthesis-heavy change below); P≈0.60 for "core three-cue model stands, no replacement needed"; P≈0.55 for "coarse-grain-with-homonym-exception is the right granularity design."

## 1. Core model verification (Q1) — DOES NOT NEED REPLACEMENT, needs two supplements

**Verdict: faithful, keep it.** The anchor citations check out as real and correctly characterized, and the architecture (frequency prior + argument-structure construction + graded thematic fit, combined via parallel graded competition) is the mainstream computational-level account in the constraint-based-lexicalist (CBL) tradition:

- **MacDonald, Pearlmutter & Seidenberg (1994)**, "The lexical nature of syntactic ambiguity resolution," *Psychological Review* 101(4):676-703 (DOI 10.1037/0033-295X.101.4.676, verified via multiple converging index hits). Lexical and syntactic ambiguity resolved by the same parallel constraint-satisfaction mechanism, sourced from the same lexical representations — this is exactly the move of folding construction-fit and thematic-fit into one competition.
- **McRae, Spivey-Knowlton & Tanenhaus (1998)**, "Modeling the influence of thematic fit (and other constraints) in on-line sentence comprehension," *JML* 38:283-312 (verified title/venue/pages via ScienceDirect + two university-hosted PDFs). This is the paper that actually *implements* the graded competition — the **Competition-Integration model**, built on Spivey-Knowlton's **normalized recurrence algorithm**. Note for the solver's implementation: normalized recurrence is a **linear, divide-by-sum normalization**, not exponential softmax (flagged plausible-but-not-independently-confirmed this pass — worth a primary-source check before treating the distinction as load-bearing).
- **Duffy, Morris & Rayner (1988)** — confirmed, and correctly cited as the "reordered access model" source (subordinate-bias-effect: dominant readings fast regardless of context, subordinate readings fast only under strong biasing context, never fully free).

**Serious competing accounts, reported honestly:**

**(a) Predictive-coding/surprisal-only (Levy 2008 *Cognition* 106:1126-1177; Kuperberg & Jaeger 2016 *Language, Cognition and Neuroscience* 31(1):32-59, both confirmed real/current)** — **not a rival for this problem.** Surprisal theory characterizes *processing cost* as a function of a word's in-context probability; it is agnostic about *how that probability is computed*. It is a different level of description (Marr's algorithmic vs. computational split) from "how is the posterior over senses computed" — complementary, not competing.

**(b) Distributional/exemplar continuous-space retrieval — the genuinely strongest rival.** **Elman (2009)**, "On the meaning of words and dinosaur bones: Lexical knowledge without a lexicon," *Cognitive Science* (confirmed, Wiley/PubMed 19662108). Elman argues there is no stored discrete inventory of senses to select among at all — meaning is an emergent product of event/world knowledge interacting with weak lexical cues online. Corroborated by current work showing contextualized embeddings (BERT) encode human-like sense-relatedness judgments (arXiv 2010.13057). **This denies the premise of the solver's fixed-inventory competition model.** It should not replace the current design (which has to output discrete, actionable frame decisions for downstream modules), but it predicts exactly where the current design will structurally fail: **novel/blended senses not in the pre-enumerated inventory** — a continuous-space model degrades gracefully there; a fixed-inventory competition model cannot represent the answer at all. Flag as a known, permanent representational ceiling, not a bug to fix.

**(c) Graded/continuous sense boundaries (Rodd/Gaskell/Marslen-Wilson 2002 *JML*; Klepousniotou; Frisson 2009 *Language and Linguistics Compass* 3(1):111-127; Pustejovsky's Generative Lexicon/coercion)** — **refinement within the graded-competition family, not a rival architecture**, EXCEPT for one real gap: **genuine type-coercion** ("began the book" — no existing sense of "book" fits the direct-object slot of "began" at all; a *covert event* must be generated, e.g. "began [reading] the book"). Traxler/Pickering/McElree's eye-movement work shows a distinct **coercion cost** (type-clash detection → covert-event inference → incorporation) separate from both frequency effects and outright anomaly. **If the solver's model only ever re-weights a pre-enumerated sense inventory, it cannot handle true coercion — this is a real architectural gap**, not covered by combining the three existing cues more cleverly.

**Is "additive activation → softmax = FLMP/Bayesian posterior" actually proven?** Yes — precisely, via a full-text fetch of **McClelland, J.L. (2013), "Integrating probabilistic models of perception and interactive neural networks," *Frontiers in Psychology* 4:503** (highest-confidence citation in this whole scan; full paper read, not a snippet). The equivalence holds **only if**:
1. bias term for sense *i* = **log p(sense_i)** — the actual log-prior, not an arbitrary tuned bias;
2. weight from cue *j* to sense *i* = **log p(cue_j | sense_i)** — the actual log-likelihood, not a heuristic weight;
3. cues are **conditionally independent given the sense** (Naive-Bayes assumption, stated explicitly in the paper);
4. combination is additive **in log-space** before the nonlinearity.

**Implication for the solver: this is currently aspirational, not proven, for their specific implementation.** Argument-structure construction and thematic fit are almost certainly NOT conditionally independent given a sense — a verb sense's construction and its typical argument fillers are both downstream of the same underlying event schema, so naive additive combination risks the classic Naive-Bayes double-counting failure (correlated cues get over-weighted). Two options, either is fine: (i) calibrate cue weights to genuine log-likelihoods and empirically spot-check the independence assumption, or (ii) relax the language to "softmax over weighted cues, structurally isomorphic to but not proven equal to a Bayesian posterior" — the second is cheaper and honest.

## 2. THE WALL — joint verb-noun sense resolution (Q2) — THE MOST IMPORTANT SECTION

**Correction to a prior internal citation first:** the paper the solver's earlier lit-scan flagged as "Hare, McRae & Elman 2003, 'Sense and structure: Language as a graded constraint network'" has the wrong title. The real paper is **Hare, M., McRae, K., & Elman, J.L. (2003), "Sense and structure: Meaning as a determinant of verb subcategorization preferences," *Journal of Memory and Language* 48:281-303** (confirmed via ScienceDirect, McRae lab page, Elman's own CV — 4 independent listings converge on this title). Its actual claim is narrower than previously assumed: verb subcategorization-frame preferences are **sense-contingent** (e.g. "find"/locate-sense is DO-only; "find"/realize-sense is SC-biased), demonstrated via corpus stability analysis + online structural-expectation measures. **Its tested direction is context→sense→structure, not a competing-noun-sense design** — treat the earlier "bidirectional" framing as an overreach of what this specific paper shows.

**The paper that actually demonstrates the reverse direction (noun properties shifting verb-sense choice):** **Hare, Elman, Tabaczynski & McRae (2009), "The wind chilled the spectators, but the wine just chilled: Sense, structure, and sentence comprehension," *Cognitive Science* 33(4):610-628** (full text fetched via PMC2742476 — verified, not a snippet). Manipulates the **subject noun's** thematic fit (good-cause "the brick shattered..." vs. good-theme "the glass shattered...") and shows it shifts which verb sense (causative vs. inchoative) is selected online, measured at the post-verbal region: **29ms and 34-46ms self-paced-reading effects, p<.01/.02.** This is genuine evidence that noun typicality shifts verb-sense selection. But the authors' own framing is a **cascade** ("the nature of the grammatical subject influences the interpretation of the verb, which in turn influences structural expectancies"), not symmetric mutual optimization — no formal joint model is given in the paper.

**Supporting evidence for immediacy/interactivity (not sequencing):**
- **Altmann & Kamide (1999)**, *Cognition* 73(3):247-264 (confirmed, multiple sources) — verb selectional restrictions restrict the plausible-referent set via anticipatory eye movements **before the argument noun's acoustic onset**. Caveat: "cake" in their design isn't itself sense-ambiguous — this supports "verb-first anticipatory influence on noun *reference*," adjacent to but not a direct hit on noun *sense* disambiguation.
- **McRae, Ferretti & Amyote (1997)**, "Thematic roles as verb-specific concepts," *Language and Cognitive Processes* 12(2-3):137-176 (confirmed) — thematic roles are verb-specific, feature-based, graded-typicality concepts (human ratings like "how common is it for a monster to frighten someone"). This is the concrete resource type the joint scoring function below needs: a gradable **(verb, role, filler)** typicality signal, shown to be computed and used immediately online.
- **Trueswell, Tanenhaus & Garnsey (1994)**, "Semantic influences on parsing," *JML* 33:285-318 (full PDF fetched, verified) — direct evidence AGAINST syntax-first-then-semantic-filter, FOR **interactive, immediate joint use** of thematic-role fit with syntax. Reduced-relative-clause materials show real-time modulation by NP-verb thematic fit.

**The honest evidentiary gap (state this plainly to the owner):** none of the above studies manipulate NOUN *word-sense* ambiguity (a WordNet-polysemy-style split, e.g. "room" = place vs. scope/opportunity vs. artifact) as the independent variable driving verb-sense choice. The evidence is one level up — noun **typicality/thematic-fit**, not noun **lexical-sense selection** — driving verb interpretation. The specific claim "two co-occurring WordNet-style sense ambiguities mutually resolve each other" is a reasonable, literature-consistent extrapolation, not a directly demonstrated human result. Label it **PLAUSIBLE-BY-SYNTHESIS**, per the mandatory calibration discipline, not CONFIRMED.

**Does the MacDonald/Pearlmutter/Seidenberg (1994) constraint-satisfaction framework generalize for free to the two-word joint case?** Theoretically yes (nothing in the architecture is single-word-specific), but **not for free as an implementation** — MacDonald et al. never tested or implemented simultaneous two-word ambiguity where the compatibility signal itself depends on BOTH unresolved variables. This is a genuine engineering step, not just "apply the same framework."

**Concrete, implementable joint-scoring mechanism** (synthesized from McRae et al.'s per-(verb,role,filler) typicality framework + MacDonald et al.'s weighted-constraint-integration architecture + a real prior computational architecture — Chaplot, Bhattacharyya & Paranjape 2015 AAAI, "Unsupervised WSD Using Markov Random Field and Dependency Parser," which frames WSD as MAP inference over a WordNet-graph MRF **restricted to dependency-parse-adjacent word pairs** — i.e., exactly "couple the verb node to its direct-object node because the parse says they're governor/dependent," the solver's exact use case):

```
score(verb_sense_i, noun_sense_j) =
      w1 * construction_match(i, j)   # does noun_sense_j's semantic type satisfy
                                       # verb_sense_i's selectional restriction for THIS
                                       # grammatical relation (WordNet hypernym-chain
                                       # compatibility, graded not binary)
    + w2 * thematic_fit(i, j)         # McRae-style typicality; corpus co-occurrence/PMI
                                       # between noun-sense's hypernym class and verb-sense
                                       # is a defensible automated proxy (MODELER-CHOICE —
                                       # McRae et al. use human norms, not this proxy)
    + w3 * freq_prior(verb_sense_i)   # WordNet sense-rank / corpus sense frequency
    + w4 * freq_prior(noun_sense_j)
    + w5 * gloss_overlap(i, j)        # cheap extended-Lesk supplementary signal
```

then **argmax over the small (verb_senses × noun_senses) cross-product for that ONE dependency edge** — with typical WordNet polysemy counts this is tiny (e.g. 5×6=30 cells), so a single argmax matches the *locality* the literature actually evidences (Trueswell et al., McRae et al. — the constraint is local and immediate at the governor-dependent relation, not sentence-wide) without assuming a heavier mechanism no cited study demonstrates for the two-word case.

**Escalation path if >2 mutually-dependent ambiguous words are in one clause** (e.g. verb + direct object + a PP-attachment noun all interacting): don't extend the cross-product (combinatorially it stops being tractable) — use **relaxation labeling** (classical iterative mutual re-scoring to convergence: each word starts with a sense distribution, every round each word's distribution updates from weighted compatibility with neighbors' *current* distributions, repeat) or the Chaplot et al. MRF-MAP approach directly. If the solver ever wants a whole-sentence collective WSD engine without writing custom inference, **UKB (Agirre & Soroa 2009, EACL — personalized PageRank over a WordNet graph, GitHub: asoroa/ukb, still maintained)** is a real, off-the-shelf, joint/collective WSD system: inject all candidate synsets as seed nodes, run personalized PageRank, take highest-ranked synset per word — verb and noun senses disambiguate together automatically via shared WordNet-relational paths.

**Explicitly labeled MODELER-CHOICE (not literature-pinned):** the specific weights w1-w5 and additive combination; restricting jointness to the single governor-dependent edge rather than sentence-wide coupling (justified by locality evidence, not prescribed by any single study); WordNet hypernym-chain compatibility as an automated proxy for selectional restriction (the psych literature uses human-collected typicality norms); argmax-once (not iterating) sufficing for the 2-word case (driven by tractability, not evidence).

**Bottom line: YES, replace noun-first-then-verb sequential typing with joint scoring at the governor-dependent edge.** This is the single most important design change identified by this drill.

## 3. Granularity / representation (Q3) — coarse-default validated, WITH a homonymy exception

**Strong, convergent support for coarse/underspecified-by-default, verb-specific:**

- **Pickering & Frisson (2001)**, *JEP:LMC* 27:556-573 — the single most load-bearing citation for this question because it is **verb-specific** (most of the surrounding literature is noun-based): **homonymous verbs get fast (~250ms-class) obligatory commitment to one sense even in neutral context; polysemous verbs do not** — readers activate one underspecified shared meaning and resolve later via context, with dominance/frequency effects on polysemous verbs showing up LATER than on homonyms.
- **Frazier & Rayner (1990)**, "Taking on semantic commitments: Processing multiple meanings vs. multiple senses," *JML* 29:181-200 (the Minimal Semantic Commitment / MSC hypothesis — get the correct title into the deliverable, the solver's brief cited it without one). Homonyms: gaze-duration cost when later context favors the non-dominant meaning (a real commitment had been made and revised). Polysemes: **no comparable cost** in neutral context when later context specifies a subordinate sense — consistent with an underspecified shared representation, cashed out only when forced. One nuance worth flagging: MSC's claim is closer to "stay uncommitted/carry ambiguity forward" than "resolve to a specific point-estimate default (MFS)" — behaviorally similar in most cases but not identical; a paradigm specifically probing whether the system committed to a specific sense vs. stayed genuinely ambiguous could dissociate the two.
- **Frisson (2009)**, *Language and Linguistics Compass* 3(1):111-127 — the best available synthesis (full text could not be fetched this pass, search-snippet-verified only): "instead of accessing a specific sense, language users initially activate a word's meaning that is semantically underspecified. Context can then help to make this meaning more specific, if there is a need for it." Direct endorsement of demand-driven disambiguation.
- **Fishbein & Harris (2014)**, "Making sense of Kafka: Structural biases induce early sense commitment for metonyms," *JML* 76:94-112 — this is the best single citation to add: it **directly operationalizes the solver's proposed trigger rule** (construction discriminates → commit early; construction neutral → stay underspecified) as an actual empirical finding, not an assumption.

**The homonymy/polysemy distinction is critical and must be added to the design, not just the citation list:**

- **Klepousniotou (2002)**, *Brain and Language* — priming evidence: polysemous senses **prime** each other (facilitation, consistent with one shared/core representation); homonymous meanings **inhibit** one another (consistent with separate, competing lexical entries).
- **Klepousniotou, Pike, Steinhauer & Gracco (2012)**, *Brain and Language* 123(1) — EEG/N400: for homonyms, **only the dominant meaning** shows a reduced N400 (real winner-take-most selection); for polysemes, **both dominant and subordinate senses** show reduced N400 (shared core representation covering the whole cluster).

Most WordNet multi-sense verbs (the "leave" case the solver opened with) are **polysemy clusters** (all senses departure-adjacent), not homonym splits — so coarse-default-with-underspecification is right for the dominant case. **But the design needs an explicit homonymy exception path**: where a verb's WordNet senses really do split into unrelated meaning clusters (rarer, but real), the literature predicts fast, hard, obligatory early commitment instead of staying at a coarse default — holding underspecified there would be psychologically wrong, not just imprecise.

**On the specific ~15-way scheme (WordNet verb supersenses / lexicographer files, or FrameNet, or Levin classes) as THE psychological grain:** no direct behavioral validation was found for any single formal taxonomy. WordNet's 15 verb supersenses (verb.motion, verb.communication, verb.possession, verb.cognition, verb.change, verb.perception, verb.social, verb.body, verb.contact, verb.creation, verb.competition, verb.consumption, verb.emotion, verb.stative, verb.weather) are a real, well-defined, computationally-formalized scheme (Schneider/Abend/Ide/Palmer, STREUSLE), but originated as a lexicographic organizing convenience, not something derived from or validated against comprehension data. FrameNet frames have theoretical grounding (Fillmore) but no controlled reading-time/priming/neural study establishing them specifically (vs. generic schema/script knowledge) as a processing unit was found. **Conclusion: the GRAIN (coarse, ~10-20 categories, roughly shared-frame level) has real behavioral support; the SPECIFIC partition (WordNet's 15 vs. FrameNet vs. Levin) does not.** Report this to the owner as: keep the coarse ~15-way supersense target (it's a defensible, convenient instantiation of an empirically-supported grain), but label the specific partition an engineering choice riding on top of psychologically-motivated coarseness, not itself a pinned finding.

**One unverifiable citation flagged:** "Kako" (as in "conceptual basis of grammatical class") could not be located or confirmed via search this pass — recommend dropping it or supplying a corrected reference rather than using it unverified.

**A separate, useful finding not in the original 5 questions:** lexical-aspect/event-structure type (Vendler's state/activity/accomplishment/achievement) behaves like a **second, independent, and more tightly-checked coarse dimension** — coercion-cost studies ("began the book"-type effects) show a real *immediate* online processing cost when aspectual type is violated, a different signature (cost-on-mismatch) than sense-grain's (no-cost, stays-underspecified) profile. Recommendation: keep sense-identity underspecified per the design above, but check aspectual/event-structure compatibility (state vs. process vs. telic change) immediately and tightly — it looks like a harder constraint than sense selection, not a softer one.

## 4. Construction-meaning mapping completeness (Q4)

The 5 pinned mappings are directionally correct per Goldberg construction grammar, but the caused-motion rule is conflating two Goldberg-distinguished constructions, and several high-frequency mappings are missing. Ranked by value (frequency × detection-cleanliness × current-absence):

**1. LIGHT VERB constructions — HIGH priority, currently entirely absent.** "take a walk / give a laugh / have a look / make a decision." Confirmed productive and common (unlike the rare, literary-register-skewed cognate-object construction below). The light verb (take/give/have/make/do) contributes only an abstract causal/inceptive gloss; **the deverbal complement noun carries the actual event semantics**. Detection signature: light-verb lemma ∈ {take, give, have, make, do} + direct object headed by a deverbal/event noun (has a corresponding verb form: look/laugh/walk/glance/decision/attempt). Rule type is different from the other five — it's a **"defer reading to the complement noun's lexical entry"** rule, not a "this construction forces reading X" rule. This is likely the single best-value addition: common, cleanly detectable, currently unhandled.

**2. DOUBLE-OBJECT dative frame → caused-possession, add as its own rule (currently your caused-motion rule only catches the prepositional-to form).** Goldberg (1995) distinguishes the caused-motion construction (`Subj V Obj Obl-PP` → "X CAUSES Y to MOVE Z") from the **ditransitive construction** (`Subj V Obj1 Obj2`, no preposition → "X CAUSES Y to RECEIVE Z" — caused-POSSESSION, not caused-motion). **Rappaport Hovav & Levin (2008)**, "The English Dative Alternation: The Case for Verb Sensitivity," *Journal of Linguistics* 44:129-167 (fetched directly) confirms the general tendency (double-object → caused-possession; prepositional-to → caused-motion) but is explicit it is **verb-sensitive, not fully systematic**: "give"-class verbs reliable, "throw"/"send"-class variable (both readings possible under either frame). **Pinker (1989)**: broad-range rule (cause-to-move ↔ cause-to-have paraphrase) gated by narrow-range rules keyed to verb subclass, plus a possession constraint (recipient must be a plausible possessor — rules out *"sent Chicago the letter"* in double-object form). **Pin this as a PRIOR with an explicit residual-uncertainty band, not a categorical rule** — consistent with the calibration discipline already in the solver's design.

**3. RESULTATIVE construction — add as its own rule, distinct from caused-motion.** "painted the fence red," "hammered the metal flat." Distinguishing signature from the already-pinned caused-motion rule: **object + xcomp/acomp-attached ADJECTIVE** (state-change) rather than object + oblique **locative PP** (caused-motion). **Goldberg & Jackendoff (2004)**, "The English Resultative as a Family of Constructions," *Language* 80(3):532-568, is explicit there is **no single uniform resultative rule** — it's a family with real item-specific idiosyncrasy requiring individual learning/storage. Pin with humility; don't expect one clean rule to cover the whole family. Currently only the "particle away" rule catches a metaphorical-extension subcase of this territory ("passed away") — the general resultative-AP pattern is unhandled.

**4. WAY-construction — good precision investment, lower recall.** "made her way through the crowd," "drank his way across Europe." Signature: `[SUBJ V POSS "way" OBL(path-PP)]` — possessive determiner + "way" as direct object + a path/oblique PP or particle on the same verb. Forces a motion-along-a-path reading with the verb reinterpreted as means/manner/incidental-activity, plus a durative/progressive aspectual coercion not inherent to the verb. Syntactically close to unambiguous to detect (possessive+"way"+dobj is a near-unique fingerprint) but low raw corpus frequency — worth adding for the cases it catches, don't expect much recall.

**5. Cognate object construction — LOW priority.** "slept a troubled sleep," "died a horrible death." Confirmed real (requires unergative verb + morphologically/semantically cognate postverbal NP, contributes manner/intensity characterization rather than a new argument) but a dedicated corpus-frequency study (Miscelánea journal) plus cross-corroborating results confirm it is **markedly rarer and literary-register-skewed** — likely not worth a dedicated rule unless the corpus is narrative/literary-heavy.

**Corpus-frequency context for prioritization:** argument-structure construction frequency is Zipfian (a handful of constructions — transitive, intransitive, caused-motion, ditransitive — dominate; way/resultative/cognate-object are a long thin tail). One hard number found: ditransitive construction occurs 1,842 times in ICE-GB (Perek, *Argument Structure in Usage-Based Construction Grammar*). This supports the priority order above.

## 5. Honest ceiling / known failure modes (Q5)

Three well-established, multiply-corroborated boundary conditions where syntax + thematic-type disambiguation **provably cannot succeed alone** — treat the existence of a hard residual as high confidence; treat any specific failure-RATE estimate below as a single-study estimate, not a replicated fact:

1. **Novel/live metaphor.** **Bowdle & Gentner (2005)**, "The Career of Metaphor," *Psychological Review* 112(1):193-216 (fetched, Northwestern-hosted PDF). Processing mode SHIFTS as a metaphor conventionalizes: novel metaphors require **structural comparison/analogical alignment** between two conceptual domains — a categorically different computation from frame-matching. Conventionalized metaphors (already lexicalized, e.g. the solver's existing "particle away" rule for "passed away") are processed via **categorization** — retrieved like an ordinary polysemous word sense, and ARE in-scope for frame-matching. The boundary between in-scope and out-of-scope is entrenchment/frequency, not syntax — a genuinely novel metaphorical extension of any pinned construction will not be resolvable by this system, by design, not by bug.

2. **Idiom lookup.** **Swinney & Cutler (1979)**, *J. Verbal Learning and Verbal Behavior* 18:523-534 (Lexical Representation Hypothesis: idioms are long lexical entries retrieved as stored units, recognized faster than matched literal controls) vs. **Cacciari & Tabossi (1988)** (Configuration Hypothesis: compositional processing until enough of the string triggers recognition of the idiom "configuration," which then suppresses the literal reading). **Both theories agree on the load-bearing point**: idiomatic strings require lookup against a **stored non-compositional unit** — neither derives figurative meaning by composing argument-structure-construction with thematic-role fit. Corollary: verb-particle noncompositionality ("give up" ≠ give + up) is reported as a **large, unavoidable failure class**, not a rare edge case — one automatically-extracted-corpus study found roughly 60% compositional / 40% noncompositional among verb-particle combinations (single study, not replicated — treat the 40% as an estimate, not a fact, but treat the existence of a large noncompositional minority as solid).

3. **PP-attachment / world-knowledge ambiguity.** **Hindle & Rooth (1993)**, "Structural Ambiguity and Lexical Relations" — the classic "I saw the man with the telescope" case: identical syntactic structure supports two attachment sites, and resolution requires lexical-semantic plausibility/world-knowledge, not more syntax. Confirmed as an actively-studied, still-open ceiling (recent 2025-era work shows humans override syntactic/frequency-based attachment defaults when world knowledge conflicts) — a well-calibrated syntax+frequency prior is provably insufficient on a measurable subset of cases, independent of how good the prior is.

**Coreference-dependent caused-motion residual (the solver's own example: "throw/send" + "to X").** No dedicated named phenomenon/literature was found treating this as its own subfield — it is standardly the general **implicit-argument / null-complement-anaphora** problem (Fillmore's definite/indefinite null complements; the broader ellipsis/discourse-anaphora literature) intersecting with construction detection. "he threw it to her" and "send it over" have a fully-determined EVENT TYPE from the construction; only the PARTICIPANTS are ungrounded without resolving the pronoun/particle target. **This is not a gap in the construction-mapping rules — it's an architectural seam**: feed the construction-reading output into the existing coreference module rather than trying to extend construction rules to cover it.

## Cheap decisive test

Build a small (~25-30 sentence) held-out eval set with **dual ambiguity by construction**: each item has BOTH a genuinely verb-sense-ambiguous verb AND a genuinely sense-ambiguous argument noun in the same governor-dependent relation (e.g. "left the room" — leave: depart/deposit-sense ambiguity crossed with room: place/scope/artifact-sense ambiguity; "observed the key" — observe: perceive/remark-sense crossed with key: object/island-sense; construct ~10 more pairs by the same recipe, ideally crossing the light-verb, dative, and resultative rules from Section 4 too so the test set exercises more than one recommendation at once). Run the CURRENT pipeline (noun-first-then-verb sequential typing) and the PROPOSED pipeline (joint `(verb_sense, noun_sense)` scoring at the governor edge, per the score() function in Section 2) on the same set, bootstrap a 95% CI on the accuracy delta specifically on the subset where the two pipelines' NOUN-sense assignment actually differs (that's the subset where sequential typing could have propagated an error into the verb-sense decision — the mechanism under test).

## Falsifiable predictions

**Joint governor-edge scoring vs. sequential noun-first-then-verb (Section 2, the primary recommendation):**
- **HARD-PASS:** joint scoring beats sequential typing by a CI-separated margin of ≥15 percentage points on the dual-ambiguity subset where noun-sense assignment differs between pipelines (non-overlapping bootstrap 95% CIs), with no more than a 3pp regression on the easy/unambiguous-noun majority of a general eval set.
- **HARD-FAIL:** margin <5pp or negative on the dual-ambiguity subset, OR a >5pp regression on the easy-noun majority (meaning the added cross-product search space dilutes an already-strong single-sense signal more than it fixes joint-ambiguity cases). On HARD-FAIL: don't revert wholesale — restrict joint scoring to a special-case trigger (both verb AND noun show high sense-entropy under the current sequential scorer), keep sequential typing as the default elsewhere.

**Coarse-grain-with-homonymy-exception (Section 3):**
- **HARD-PASS:** removing forced fine-sense commitment for polysemy-cluster verbs (staying at the ~15-way supersense) does not increase downstream error on tasks that don't need fine sense, AND a held-out set of true-homonym-split verbs shows measurably better accuracy when routed through an early-commitment path instead of the coarse-default path (a CI-separated margin, not just a point estimate).
- **HARD-FAIL:** downstream tasks needing fine sense degrade with no forcing mechanism in place (meaning "stay coarse until task-forced" was implemented as "stay coarse, full stop" — the forcing trigger itself needs to be built, not just the default).

**Light-verb-construction rule (Section 4, item 1):**
- **HARD-PASS:** ≥80% accuracy rerouting reading to the complement noun on a held-out light-verb eval set, with a false-positive rate <10% against genuine full-verb uses of take/give/have/make/do (these lemmas are common as ordinary transitive verbs too — "took the book," and note the Section 4 flag that idiomatic extensions like "gave Mary a headache" are a known confusable case).
- **HARD-FAIL:** false-positive rate ≥25% (the light-verb detector is confusing itself with ordinary transitive uses more than it's correctly deferring) — in that case the deverbal-noun-object signature needs a stricter filter (e.g. require the noun to lack a strong independent concrete-object reading) before shipping.

## Substrate-product implications

This drill does not change the substrate's binding/representation invariants — it's a design correction to a downstream comprehension-pipeline stage (verb-sense/event-frame disambiguation over the dependency parse), not a claim about the representational substrate itself. Product-relevant takeaways:

1. **Sequencing bug, not a missing-mechanism bug.** The wall the solver hit ("typing the noun is itself a WSD problem") is evidence the current pipeline has an ordering assumption (noun-sense resolved before verb-sense scoring) that the literature does not support — the fix is architectural (score jointly at the parse edge) and reuses machinery the solver already has (dependency parse, WordNet sense inventories, construction-match and thematic-fit scorers), not a new component.
2. **Three concrete, ranked, buildable additions** (light-verb defer-to-complement rule; double-object dative→caused-possession prior; resultative-AP→state-change rule) close identified coverage gaps in the construction-mapping layer at low implementation cost, each with a clean dependency-parse detection signature.
3. **A permanent, honest ceiling exists** (novel metaphor, idiom lookup, PP-attachment/world-knowledge cases, coreference-dependent participants) — this bounds the claim the solver should make about this mechanism's coverage. These are not solvable by adding more construction rules; they require a categorically different mechanism (analogical alignment, non-compositional lookup, world-knowledge plausibility scoring, or a downstream coreference module) and should be reported as known-out-of-scope, not silently mis-scored.
4. **The homonymy/polysemy distinction is a genuine missing dimension in the current design** — the solver's WordNet-sense inventories don't currently distinguish "these senses are a polysemy cluster" from "these senses are unrelated homonym splits," but the literature says the correct default behavior (stay coarse vs. commit early) depends on which case applies. This is cheap to add (WordNet sense clusters can be approximately grouped by shared hypernym-chain distance or by the "related_form"/similarity relations already in the lexicon) and meaningfully changes correctness on a subset of verbs.
