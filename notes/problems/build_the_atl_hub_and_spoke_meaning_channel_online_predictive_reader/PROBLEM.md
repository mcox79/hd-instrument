## 1. THE PROBLEM IN PLAIN LANGUAGE
Our reader represents a word's meaning as one fixed list of numbers learned from which words appear near it. In that space, a word's different meanings are blended together (river-bank and money-bank sit almost on top of each other), so when the text uses a rare meaning, the reader can't tell it apart from the common one — this caps "pick the right specific meaning" at about a third. The brain avoids this: it stores meaning as a hub linked to GROUNDED "spokes" (what a thing looks like, how you act on it, how it feels, what it relates to). Those grounded features are independent of the word-neighbour statistics, so they PULL the blended meanings apart. The job: build that hub-and-spoke meaning representation with RICH ENOUGH grounded features (the small 12-feature version was already shown too coarse), fill it out to broad vocabulary by inheriting features down known relations, and let the reader RE-SETTLE each word's meaning from context as it reads (one pass, no training) — and show it finally beats the ceiling the fixed-vector reader cannot.
