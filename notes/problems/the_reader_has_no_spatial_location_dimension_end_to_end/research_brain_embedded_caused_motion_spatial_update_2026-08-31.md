# Research drill — brain-faithful SPACE update from EMBEDDED-CLAUSE motion, CAUSED-MOTION themes, and STATIVE locatives

**Date:** 2026-08-31 · **For:** `the_reader_has_no_spatial_location_dimension_end_to_end`
**Scope:** online literature scan (cite-specific) on the TWO dominant recall walls of the where-is tracker (embedded/reported-clause motion; caused-motion where the tracked person is the theme) plus non-motion locative predication. Complements — does not duplicate — `research_brain_space_update_mapping_2026-08-31.md` (that doc covered the general language→location mapping: Source-Path-Goal, deixis, noisy-channel, categorical/region hierarchy). This one answers: *does a competent reader update the spatial situation model from these constructions, how, and how strongly should we trust each?*
**Calibration:** lit-scan penalty applied (P deflated 0.15–0.25; novel-synthesis capped 0.50). Confidences are the drill's read, hypothesis-pending-VET.

**One-line frame:** BOTH walls are **real recall gaps, not fidelity traps** — the brain DOES update the spatial model from embedded factual events and from caused-motion themes. The organizing principle for Wall 1 is **VERIDICALITY of the embedding predicate, not clause position**: factual embeddings (factive + perception-report) update the primary space model at full confidence; reported/believed embeddings (non-factive) update a *separate belief-world channel* at lowered confidence; counterfactual/modal/conditional embeddings do not update at all. For Wall 2 the principle is **construction-level entailment**: the caused-motion construction semantically entails the THEME (direct object) reaches the goal, so the reader updates the theme, not (only) the agent.

## WALL 1 — MOTION UNDER A MATRIX VERB / REPORTED CLAUSE

Examples the extractor misses (routes only the matrix/root verb): "no one **saw** them **arrive** at the station", "she **said** he had **gone** to town", "I **heard** that they had **reached** the castle", "he **knew** nothing when the train was flagged".

### PINNED-BY-EVIDENCE

**P1 — Readers DO integrate events expressed in subordinate/embedded/complement clauses into the situation model.** The situation model is built from the *propositional content* of the whole sentence, not just the root clause; embedded-clause events are integrated (event-indexing model, Zwaan & Radvansky 1998; the clause-order/at-issueness reading-process literature confirms readers integrate embedded-clause events, with clause structure affecting mainly *early* processing and discourse-level factors like givenness/event-order dominating *later* integration). **Confidence: PINNED that integration happens; MEDIUM on the exact time-course.** So: routing only the matrix verb is a genuine under-read of the reader's own competence — the fix is not a hack, it is fidelity.

**P2 — There is a main-vs-embedded ASYMMETRY, and it is an AT-ISSUENESS / status asymmetry, not an inclusion/exclusion one.** Main-clause content is *at-issue* (the asserted point); certain embedded content (appositive relatives, parentheticals, presupposed clauses) is *not-at-issue* / backgrounded (Simons, Tonhauser, Beaver & Roberts projective-content framework; the at-issueness literature). Not-at-issue does **not** mean not-represented — it means represented with a different discourse status (backgrounded, harder to directly deny/question, but still in the model). **Build consequence: do not drop embedded motion; represent it, but you MAY carry a status/salience tag.** Confidence: PINNED (the distinction), MEDIUM (its quantitative effect on spatial retrieval specifically).

**P3 — The decisive gate on WHETHER the embedded event is entered as REAL is the VERIDICALITY / FACTIVITY of the embedding predicate — this is strongly pinned and has direct neural evidence.**
- **Factive** predicates (*know, realize, see-that, notice, discover, remember, forget, be aware, learn, find out, regret*) **presuppose their complement is TRUE**; non-factive predicates (*think, believe, say, claim, suppose, hope*) do not (Kiparsky & Kiparsky 1970, "Fact"). During active comprehension, "the presuppositions triggered by factive verbs are **encoded and maintained within the comprehender's discourse model**" — i.e. the reader automatically incorporates the complement as true (Kuperberg lab: "What we know about knowing: presuppositions generated by factive verbs influence downstream neural processing", *Cognition* 2018/19). **Direct neural evidence:** downstream words inconsistent with a *factive* complement elicit a larger late posterior positivity / **P600 (500–700 ms)** — the brain treats the complement as an established part of the event model and flags violations of it; **no such effect follows non-factive verbs.** This is as close to a settled result as this drill found. **Confidence: PINNED.**
- **Veridicality ⊋ factivity.** "All factive verbs are veridical, but not all veridical verbs are factive; factives *background* the veridicality as a presupposition" (so the complement's truth **survives negation and modal embedding**). This matters for the exact examples:
  - **"no one saw them arrive"** — *see + naked infinitive* is a **veridical perception report**: the perceived event is entailed to have happened, and crucially **the entailment survives matrix negation** — negation scopes the *seeing*, not the *arriving*. "No one saw them arrive" **still entails they arrived** (Barwise 1981, "Scenes and other situations"; naked-infinitive perception statements are veridical in normal usage). → **UPDATE at full confidence.**
  - **"I heard that they had reached the castle"** — *hear-that* is factive-veridical for the reported event's truth (reliable-source perception) but shades toward report; treat as veridical-update unless the source is explicitly unreliable. → **UPDATE (near-full).**
  - **"he knew nothing when the train was flagged"** — *knew* is factive; the *when*-clause ("the train was flagged") is presupposed true regardless of the matrix. → the flagging happened; **UPDATE the train's location/state.**
  - **"she said he had gone to town"** — *say* is **non-factive**: the complement is **reported, not entailed**. He *may* be in town per her report; the reader does **not** commit it to the primary reality model. → **HOLD in a belief-world / reported channel at lowered confidence** (see P4).
- **Implicative predicates** (Karttunen 1971) sharpen the gate further and are common in narrative: *managed to reach* / *got to* → the reaching **happened**; *failed to reach* / *tried to reach (but…)* → it **did NOT happen**; the entailment **flips with negation**. → gate on the implicative signature, not just the verb. **Confidence: PINNED.**

**P4 — Reported/believed content is represented in a SEPARATE, nested model, not merged into primary reality — and readers keep the nesting SHALLOW.** Readers construct nested belief/report representations (theory-of-mind embedding), but corpus and processing evidence show they do **not** compute deep infinite nestings — real comprehension rarely exceeds ~2 levels of belief nesting, and nested representations are built only when required; the reported situation is "represented separately from the speech structure" (nested-belief / dialogue-nesting literature; Zwaan & Radvansky mental-models). **Build consequence: a non-factive report's location belongs in a distinct channel (a belief-world tagged to the reporter), not the ground-truth SPACE index.** This is exactly your PROBLEM.md's "held as a belief-world location," and it is brain-faithful. **Confidence: PINNED (separate nested representation), MEDIUM (how a where-is query should surface it).**

**Salience caveat (fidelity, not a blocker):** SPACE is the *least spontaneously updated* dimension in normal reading — readers update space reliably only when oriented to it or when it is causally relevant (Zwaan; Speer & Zacks). This is a **salience/effort** fact, **not** a capability limit: the reader *can* and *does* update from embedded factual motion when attending to place. For a where-is instrument we **want** to recover every factual location event, so we should extract them all and let the confidence/salience tag (not omission) encode the asymmetry.

### OPEN / to VET
- Exact quantitative retrieval cost of not-at-issue (embedded) vs at-issue (matrix) location for a *where-is* probe — the projective-content literature predicts a backgrounding penalty but I found no narrative where-is experiment measuring it directly. **P(embedded factual location is retrievable but slower/weaker than matrix) ≈ 0.6 — VET on your gold.**
- Whether a where-is read-out should ever *return* a belief-world location when the primary is unknown (e.g. "where is he?" → "reported to be in town"). Design call; the nested-model evidence supports *offering it as reported*, not as fact.

### BUILD RECOMMENDATION — Wall 1
1. **Stop restricting extraction to the matrix/root verb.** Traverse into complement (`ccomp`/`xcomp`), relative (`acl:relcl`/`acl`), and adverbial (`advcl`) clauses and route their motion verbs. This is the primary recall lever and it is brain-faithful (P1).
2. **Gate the UPDATE on the veridicality class of the embedding predicate — not on clause position.** Maintain three small lexicons + a modal/polarity check:
   - **VERIDICAL → UPDATE primary SPACE at full confidence:** factive verbs (*know, realize, see, notice, discover, remember, forget, learn, be aware, find(out)*), **perception-report** frames (*see/hear/watch/feel + NP + bare-infinitive/participle*: "saw them arrive", "heard them reach"), and **positive implicatives** (*manage/get/happen/come to*). Handle **negation correctly**: negation of a factive/perception matrix does **NOT** cancel the complement ("no one saw them arrive" → arrival TRUE).
   - **NON-VERIDICAL / REPORTED → belief-world channel at LOWERED confidence (or single-channel update with a `reported` flag):** non-factive report/attitude verbs (*say, tell, believe, think, suppose, claim, hope, imagine, be afraid, assume*). Tag the location to the reporter's belief-world; do not let it override a primary fact.
   - **DO NOT UPDATE:** negative implicatives (*fail/neglect to reach*), and complements under conditional/counterfactual/modal/future/interrogative/negated-non-factive embedding ("if he had gone", "would go", "before he could reach", "did he go?", "she didn't think he had left"). These are non-veridical for the event.
3. **Confidence output:** attach a per-event confidence/veridicality tag (veridical=1.0, reported≈0.4, non-veridical=drop) so the where-is read-out can grade and, optionally, surface reported locations as *reported*.
4. **Can-fail test (info-free twin MUST lose):** build a gold with (a) embedded **factive/perception** motion (must be recovered → recall ↑), (b) **reported** non-factive motion (must be held/lowered, not committed), (c) **counterfactual/negative-implicative** motion (must NOT update). Twin A = *route embedded clauses but ignore veridicality* (update everything at full confidence): this must be **worse** on the (b)+(c) subsets (false-positive locations) — proving the veridicality gate carries information, not just the clause-traversal. Twin B = matrix-only (current): must be worse on (a) recall. If the veridicality gate does not beat Twin A on (b)+(c), report that honestly (the gate is not yet earning its complexity).

## WALL 2 — CAUSED-MOTION WHERE THE TRACKED PERSON IS THE THEME (object), not the agent

Examples: "one stop **brought** us into the sitting-room", "the guard **led** him to the cell", "they **carried** her to the couch". The moved entity is the grammatical OBJECT/theme; the extractor currently updates the AGENT's location and misses the theme's relocation.

### PINNED-BY-EVIDENCE

**P5 — The caused-motion construction ENTAILS the theme reaches the goal; a competent reader updates the THEME's location.** Goldberg (1995, *Constructions*) analyzes the caused-motion construction as form `NP V NP PP` with semantics **"X CAUSES Y to MOVE Z"**, where Y (the theme) = the direct object and Z = the goal. When path/goal is present and telic, "motion is strictly entailed… enabling the inference that the object is in motion (physical or abstract) **with a corresponding change of location**" (caused-motion annotation criteria, LREC 2014; construction-grammar analyses). So "led him to the cell" **semantically entails HIM ends at the cell**; "carried her to the couch" → SHE at the couch; "brought us into the sitting-room" → US in the sitting-room. **This is a semantic entailment of the construction, not a defeasible inference — it is at least as firm as self-motion.** **Confidence: PINNED (semantic entailment).**

**P6 — Readers actually track the moved (theme/patient) entity's new location during comprehension.** Object-location updating is empirically attested: moving an entity leaves a working-memory trace of the **new** location (plus an episodic trace of the old); information about a change in an object's location is stored in WM during sentence comprehension and combines with world knowledge (PMC5827356, "Integration of World Knowledge and Temporary Information about Changes in an Object's Environmental Location"; "Actions travel with their objects: dynamic event files", *Psych. Research* 2008). So the reader does not just note that an agent acted — it **relocates the theme**. **Confidence: PINNED that theme relocation is tracked; MEDIUM on whether it is *as automatic/fast* as self-motion (see OPEN).**

**P7 — Agent co-location is a SEPARATE, verb-class-dependent inference from theme relocation.** Some caused-motion verbs are **accompanied-motion** (agent co-moves to the goal): *lead, bring, take, carry, escort, conduct, usher, guide, walk, drive, accompany*. Others are **ballistic/transfer/placement** (agent stays put, only the theme moves): *send, throw, push, roll, place, put, mail, kick*. "The guard led him to the cell" → both the guard and him plausibly end near the cell; "she sent him to town" → only *him* moves. **Build consequence: always update the THEME to the goal; update the AGENT to the goal only for the accompanied-motion subclass.** **Confidence: PINNED (the lexical-aspectual split is standard); the agent co-location default is a design choice, MEDIUM.**

### OPEN / to VET
- Is theme relocation updated **as reliably/automatically as self-motion** in the spatial situation model, or is it slightly weaker because the theme is not the attentional focus/protagonist? Rinck & Bower show accessibility tracks the *protagonist's* focus; a non-protagonist theme may sit at lower activation. **P(theme relocation ≈ self-motion in reliability when goal is explicit) ≈ 0.55 — VET via the distance curve, split by self-motion vs caused-motion.**
- Animacy/agency modulation: the **goal-over-source** memory bias is animacy-modulated (Lakusta & Landau 2012 — see the prior drill's AUDIT UPDATE), **but** the caused-motion *theme-reaches-goal entailment* is a property of the construction, not the memory bias, so it should hold for animate and inanimate themes alike **when the construction is telic (goal PP present)**. Net: animacy modulates *salience/source-recall*, not the *theme→goal* entailment. **Confidence: MEDIUM — VET by testing inanimate-theme caused-motion ("placed the letter on the table") vs animate ("led him to the cell").**

### BUILD RECOMMENDATION — Wall 2
1. **Add a caused-motion construction detector:** pattern `NP-subj(agent) V NP-obj(theme) PP-goal` where V ∈ the caused-motion/placement/accompanied-motion verb set (*lead, bring, take, carry, escort, conduct, usher, guide, drag, haul, walk, drive, send, push, throw, place, put, set, show(into), march, hurry*). **Update the DIRECT OBJECT (theme)'s location to the goal-PP node** — this is the entailed relocation the extractor currently drops.
2. **Agent sub-rule:** for **accompanied-motion** verbs (*lead, bring, take, carry, escort, conduct, usher, guide, walk, drive, accompany*) also update the AGENT to the goal; for **ballistic/transfer/placement** verbs (*send, throw, push, roll, place, put, mail*) update **only** the theme.
3. **Gate identically to self-motion (reuse the adapter's existing gates):** require a **goal PP with a place-typed ground** and telicity. No goal ("led him around") → path-only, do not pin a node. Non-spatial/metaphorical goal ("brought us to tears", "led him to believe") → skip (the place-typing gate already handles this).
4. **Can-fail test (info-free twin MUST lose):** gold with caused-motion sentences where the theme's post-event location is queried. Twin = *update the AGENT (subject) instead of the theme*, or *update neither* (current behavior) — must LOSE on theme where-is recall. Split the distance curve by self-motion vs caused-motion to answer the OPEN reliability question.

## ALSO — NON-MOTION LOCATIVE PREDICATION (stative "hid herself in the nursery", "sat in a corner", "was kept in the workhouse")

**Question:** does the reader set SPACE from a stative locative even when the verb is not a canonical motion/posture verb, and what defines the class?

### PINNED-BY-EVIDENCE
**P8 — Yes: any explicit assertion that a figure IS AT a place sets/updates that figure's SPACE index (event-indexing model — the SPACE index is updated by any signalled location, and persists).** The relevant linguistic object is the **Basic Locative Construction** (Levinson & Wilkins 2006) — the default answer to "where is X?" — which cross-linguistically uses three verb strategies: **neutral/copular** ("is in"), **posture verbs** (*sit, lie, stand, kneel, crouch* — semantically **stative**: "being in a seated posture", not "getting seated"), and **positional/dispositional** verbs. Stative verbs of location/position "merely express the localization of an object", and their **locative phrase has the status of an ARGUMENT, not an adjunct** (stative-verbs-of-location literature; BLC typology). **The defining test for "does this verb's locative PP set the figure's location" = the predication asserts the figure is spatially AT the ground for the event's duration, and the locative PP is a core argument/complement rather than a detachable scene adjunct.** **Confidence: PINNED (the typology and the argument/adjunct diagnostic); MEDIUM (that a where-is reader should treat all of them identically).**

**P9 — The class is broader than posture verbs.** It includes: **existence/position** (*be, remain, stay, live, dwell, reside, lodge, wait, linger*), **concealment** (*hide, conceal oneself, shelter, take refuge, lurk*), **confinement/maintenance** (*be kept, be confined, be imprisoned, be held, be lodged, be shut up, be locked*), and **activity-at-a-place** (*sit/work/sleep/dine/read in X*). All entail the figure is located at the ground for the interval → they **set** (not move) the SPACE index, and it **persists**. Reflexive-locative ("hid **herself** in the nursery") locates the reflexive's referent (= subject) → subject at nursery. **Confidence: PINNED for existence/posture/confinement; MEDIUM for bare activity-locatives (they locate the figure for the interval but are lower-salience scene-setting).**

### OPEN / to VET
- Where to draw the line on **activity-locative adjuncts** ("she sang in the parlour"): these *do* place the figure in the parlour for that interval, but the PP is arguably an adjunct, lower-salience. **Recommendation: include them as SET-at-lowered-confidence** (they are still the reader's best where-is evidence for that span), and VET whether including them helps or hurts precision on the gold. **P(activity-locatives net-help where-is recall) ≈ 0.5 — VET.**

### BUILD RECOMMENDATION — locative predication
1. **Add a stative-locative rule (a SET, not a MOVE event):** `NP-subj {be | posture | existence/position | concealment | confinement-passive | activity} verb + locative-PP(in/at/on/inside/within/by + place-typed ground)` → **SET subj's location to the PP node**; emit a *state/presence* assertion that **persists** (no Source-Path-Goal, no goal-vs-source logic). Resolve reflexive objects to the subject.
2. **Confidence tiering:** existence/posture/concealment/confinement = full; bare activity-locative adjunct = lowered (VET whether to include).
3. **Gate:** place-typed ground (reuse adapter place-typing); exclude non-locative PPs (instrument/manner/temporal) and non-spatial "in" ("in trouble", "in love").
4. **Can-fail test:** gold with stative-locative-only spans (no motion verb) where the figure's location is queried. Twin = motion-verb-only extraction (current) → must LOSE on these spans (they are exactly the spans it drops).

## UNIFIED VERIDICALITY / CONFIDENCE GATE (the through-line across all three)

All three walls collapse to one brain-faithful principle: **enter a location event into the primary SPACE model iff the construction ENTAILS the figure is (or ends up) at the ground; grade confidence by the entailment's strength; route reported/believed content to a nested belief-world channel; drop non-veridical (counterfactual/modal/negative-implicative) content.**

| Source construction | Entailment | Action | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Matrix motion (current) | figure moves to goal | UPDATE primary | 1.0 |
| Embedded under **factive/perception-report** (Wall 1) | complement true (survives negation) | UPDATE primary | 1.0 |
| **Caused-motion theme** (Wall 2) | theme reaches goal (construction entailment) | UPDATE theme (agent iff accompanied-motion) | 1.0 |
| **Stative locative** (P8/P9) | figure AT ground for interval | SET primary (persists) | 1.0 (activity-locative ≈0.6) |
| Positive **implicative** ("managed to reach") | event happened | UPDATE primary | 1.0 |
| Embedded under **non-factive report** ("said he had gone") | reported, not entailed | belief-world channel, tag to reporter | ≈0.4 |
| **Counterfactual/modal/conditional/future/negative-implicative** ("if he had gone", "failed to reach") | not veridical | DROP | 0.0 |

This is one lexicon + one polarity/mood check feeding one gate — small, glass-box, no LLM. It is the same "parse-as-evidence, meaning-by-entailment" spirit the prior drill argued for; here the *entailment* (veridicality/construction) is the prior that decides commitment, complementary to the noisy-channel *likelihood* term.

Refines the prior AUDIT note (animacy modulates the goal *bias/salience*, not the caused-motion *theme→goal entailment*).
- **Stative locative predication SETS the SPACE index** (Basic Locative Construction; posture/positional/existence/confinement verbs with a locative *argument*) — not just canonical motion/posture verbs.

## TLDR (plain English)
A good reader keeps a mental map of where everyone is, and updates it from far more than the main verb of a sentence — but not blindly. **Wall 1:** when a movement is buried in a "reported" clause, whether the reader believes it happened depends on the reporting word. "No one *saw* them arrive" and "he *realized* they had left" — the reader files the arrival/leaving as real (even the "no one saw" version, because the not-seeing doesn't undo the arriving). But "she *said* he had gone to town" is only a claim — the reader keeps it as *her story*, not as a fact, filed under her, at lower trust. And "if he had gone" or "he failed to reach it" don't move anyone at all. So our fix is: read inside those clauses too, and decide whether to trust the move by the reporting word (see/know/notice → trust; say/believe/think → keep as hearsay; if/failed-to → ignore). **Wall 2:** in "the guard led him to the cell", the person who ends up in the cell is *him*, not the guard's grammar-subject — the construction itself means "made him go there", so we should move the object of the sentence, not the doer (and move the doer too only for verbs like *led/brought/carried* where they go along). **Also:** "hid herself in the nursery", "was kept in the workhouse", "sat in a corner" set a person's location even though nobody is moving — any verb that says a person *is at* a place for a while should set the map, not just walking/going verbs. All three come down to one simple rule: put a location on the map when the sentence *really commits* to the person being there; keep hearsay in a side-note; drop pure hypotheticals.

## QUESTIONS
None blocking. One design call for strategy: should a "where is X?" read-out ever *return* a hearsay/belief-world location (e.g. "reported to be in town") when the primary location is unknown? The nested-model evidence supports offering it *labelled as reported* rather than as fact — but that is a read-out UX choice, cheap to defer.
