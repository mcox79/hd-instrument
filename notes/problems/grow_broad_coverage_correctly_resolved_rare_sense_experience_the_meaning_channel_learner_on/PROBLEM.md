## 1. THE PROBLEM IN PLAIN LANGUAGE
The reader is good at common word meanings but weak at RARE ones (about a third right), and we now know exactly why. It isn't a bad meaning-detector, a bad readout, or missing facts — the brain-faithful pipeline was built end-to-end and it climbs correctly, then stalls at one stage: learning a rare meaning from experience. Rare meanings barely appear (once or twice in a whole corpus), so there's almost nothing to learn from — and if the reader guesses which rare meaning it's seeing and learns from the guess, it makes things worse (it wrongly glues a common meaning's neighbours onto the rare one). Even handing it perfect answers doesn't help the rare cases (too few examples). The only honest way forward that doesn't use a big pretrained model: let the reader read a LOT, resolve the easy/concrete cases first, and slowly build up correct experience of rare meanings from there — carefully, so it never poisons what it already knows. The job: build that safe, growing rare-meaning experience and show the rare-meaning score rises as coverage grows, without hurting the common meanings.
