## 1. THE PROBLEM IN PLAIN LANGUAGE
Many words have more than one meaning, and the rare meaning is the hard case. A large, careful effort just proved exactly where our reader is stuck. It CAN reliably notice when the common meaning does not fit — that "surprise" detector now works well (it is the brain's N400 prediction-error signal, and its brain-faithful directional form spots the rare-meaning cases about seven times in ten). What it CANNOT do is, once it knows the common meaning is wrong, pick the SPECIFIC right rare meaning — it gets that only about a third of the time. And that final choice cannot be improved by reading the answer off the word-web more cleverly (that was tested and did not help); it needs the reader to actually UNDERSTAND the situation the sentence describes and let that understanding say which meaning is coming. The brain does this with a running, generative model of the situation — it knows, from world knowledge, what kind of thing is being talked about, so it predicts the specific expected meaning of the next word and is surprised when reality differs. We have the surprise detector and the meaning-selection circuit; what we lack is the generative understanding that supplies the specific prediction. The job is to BUILD that: a glass-box situation model rich enough to predict which specific rare meaning is expected, and show it raises that one-in-three number and, through it, wins overall.

## 2. WHY THIS ONE — the meaning gains live here, and the parent proved it is the ONLY place left

## 3. HOW THE BRAIN DOES THIS (the opening move)
PINNED: sense selection is TOP-DOWN prediction from a hierarchical GENERATIVE model of the SITUATION (world knowledge, events/entities/scripts), not bottom-up co-occurrence — predictive coding (Rao & Ballard 1999; Friston 2010): higher levels predict lower-level activity, the residual is prediction ERROR, and the N400 is the SEMANTIC prediction error (Kutas & Federmeier 2011; Rabovsky, Hansen & McClelland 2018). The generative situation model activates UNMENTIONED concepts by schema/script knowledge (Schank-Abelson scripts; Chambers-Jurafsky narrative chains; FrameNet role expectations; McClelland's situation-model semantics), which is precisely what supplies a sense-SPECIFIC top-down prediction a local graph cannot. The apparent circularity (need comprehension to disambiguate, disambiguation to comprehend) DISSOLVES by BOOTSTRAPPING: senses and comprehension co-develop through cross-situational reading (Yu & Smith 2007), carved to the right granularity by usage (the ultrametric re-carving).
