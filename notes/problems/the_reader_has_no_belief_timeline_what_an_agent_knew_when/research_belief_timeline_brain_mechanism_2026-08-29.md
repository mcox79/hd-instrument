# Research drill — brain mechanism for a per-agent belief timeline ("what did A know when")

Date: 2026-08-29 · Author: research sub-agent (Opus) · Type: ONLINE literature scan
Calibration note: this is a lit-scan. P-estimates deflated 0.15–0.25; novel-synthesis capped at 0.50. Where the literature is genuinely mixed I say so and hand you a can-fail discriminator rather than a verdict.

## One-paragraph orientation
Your design is **mostly right at the computational (Marr level-1) level and wrong in KIND at the storage/algorithmic level.** The brain does NOT keep an equally-accessible dense per-time belief register that it reads at T. It keeps a richly-stored **CURRENT** per-agent belief (situation-model updating) plus **sparse, time-tagged observation events**, and it **RECONSTRUCTS** any *past* belief on demand — and that reconstruction is **systematically corrupted by current knowledge** (hindsight bias / curse of knowledge). Your sample-and-hold step function is the correct *idealized readout*; it is not how the substrate is stored. The single most load-bearing correction: **the agent-belief store must be architecturally DECOUPLED from the world/reality store** — that decoupling (not inertia) is where the brain's robustness lives, and it is also where the brain's failures come from.

## Q1 — STORED TRAJECTORY vs ON-DEMAND SIMULATION (the biggest fork)

**VERDICT: needs-correction. The brain is (c) hybrid, tilted toward reconstruction for PAST queries. A dense stored belief-timeline read at T is NOT brain-faithful; store sparse update-events + current state, reconstruct the past.**

What the evidence actually says:
- **Situation-model / event-indexing (Zwaan & Radvansky 1998; Radvansky & Zacks 2011).** Readers maintain a *current* situation model, updated incrementally at event boundaries along ~5 indices (time, space, entities, causation, intentionality/protagonist goals). What is *maintained* is the CURRENT state, not a full trajectory. fMRI: the **posterior-medial network** (PMN: hippocampus, angular gyrus, posterior cingulate, retrosplenial, mPFC) carries this updating for *all* dimensions (recent naturalistic-reading fMRI coding cause/character/goal/object/space/time — posterior-medial areas show the most robust updating).
- **The "here-and-now" dominance (Anderson, Garrod & Sanford 1983; Glenberg et al. 1987; Rinck & Bower).** The *current* protagonist state is most accessible; **prior states are demoted/less accessible** and accessibility tracks the protagonist's *current* situation, not recency of mention. So there is no equally-readable past register — the brain actively down-weights superseded states.
- **Memory-based / resonance processing (O'Brien, Rizzella, Albrecht & Halleran 1998; Myers & O'Brien).** Backgrounded/outdated/false info is **not held active on purpose**; it is *passively reactivated by resonance* when it shares features with current working-memory contents. Past belief states are cue-reconstructed, not indexed-and-read.
- **Constructive episodic simulation / scene construction (Schacter & Addis 2007; Hassabis & Maguire 2007; Addis et al. 2007).** Remembering a past episode (hence "what A knew then") is *constructive* — reassembled from elements via hippocampal pattern-completion, not replayed from a stored tape.
- **The clincher against a clean stored register (Fischhoff 1975; Birch & Bloom 2007).** If past belief were a stored value read at T, later knowledge could not corrupt it. It systematically does (see Q5). That is direct evidence *for* reconstruction and *against* a read-only trajectory.

**What to BUILD/CHANGE:** store the belief timeline as **sparse, time-tagged observation/update EVENTS per (agent, fact)** + a maintained CURRENT value; answer "what did A know at T" by **retrieving the most-recent update-edge before T** (a query, not a table read). Your dense piecewise-constant step function is the *ideal readout of* that sparse event store, not the store. This is cheaper, it is what enables the graded/confidence layer, and it naturally predicts the human failure mode.

## Q2 — PERSISTENCE: sample-and-hold vs decay vs reality-overwrite

**VERDICT: PINNED at the computational level (default-persistence/temporal inertia is the right model) BUT needs-correction — attributed belief is NOT held as cleanly as a physical state; it leaks toward reality and its ACCESS decays.**

- **Default-persistence is the right level-1 model, and it is the *same* principle physical states use.** Dowty's (1986) **temporal inertia** (states persist by default across the narrative "now" unless an event changes them) is the linguistic form; the AI form is McCarthy & Hayes' **common-sense law of inertia** / the **frame problem** (Dean's "rule of persistence"; Shanahan's *Solving the Frame Problem*; the Yale-shooting cautionary case). Discourse semantics carries this via Reichenbach's **reference time R** advanced across sentences (Kamp DRT; Partee). So invoking Dowty + frame-problem inertia for the belief register is defensible and **PINNED**.
- **But attributed belief differs in KIND from location/possession in two ways:**
  1. **Reality-intrusion / egocentric leak (curse of knowledge; Birch & Bloom 2007; Keysar et al. 2003; Samson's altercentric intrusions).** The default pressure is for the comprehender's *own current knowledge* to overwrite the attributed belief. A physical location is never "overwritten" by anything; a stale belief is under constant pressure to be overwritten by reality. False belief is *effortfully maintained against* that leak — which is exactly why false-belief is later-developing than knowledge-access and is inhibition-dependent, not representation-limited.
  2. **Access decays, value does not.** Time-since-last-update degrades *retrievability/confidence*, not the stored content (ordinary interference/decay). This matches your rep-B-as-confidence intuition.

**What to BUILD/CHANGE:** keep sample-and-hold for the *value*, but (a) add an explicit **anti-intrusion decoupling** so a world/reality update can never silently rewrite an agent-belief slot (see Q5, the #1 directive), and (b) attach a **confidence that decays with time-since-last-update** (recency), leaving the value constant. Do NOT model belief as decaying toward reality *by default* — the brain does that as a *bug*, and you almost certainly want the clean version (this is a place you can legitimately beat the brain; see Q5).

## Q3 — TEMPORAL BINDING: discrete event-model (rep A) vs graded temporal-context (rep B)

**VERDICT: PINNED. Your split is well-matched to the brain, and your expectation (discrete adequate for the value; graded = confidence/recency, not accuracy) is consistent with the evidence — with one refinement: rep B is not strictly dominated by rep A.**

- **The belief VALUE and its change-points are DISCRETE and event-based.** Event segmentation (Zacks, Speer, Swallow & Reynolds 2007; Kurby & Zacks 2008) and the event-indexing model say updating happens at **event boundaries**, not continuously. So rep A (discrete "sample-and-hold" intervals) is the accuracy-bearing representation for *what* is believed and *when it changed*.
- **"When did A last witness it / how recently" is GRADED.** Howard & Kahana's **Temporal Context Model** (2002; Sederberg et al. 2008) + hippocampal **time cells** (MacDonald et al. 2011; Eichenbaum 2014) implement a slowly-drifting context vector; temporal localization is *approximate*, with a forward-asymmetric contiguity gradient. This is exactly a lossy, graded recency/confidence signal — rep B.
- **Refinement:** rep B carries information rep A lacks — **order-uncertainty near boundaries** ("was A's observation before or after the theft?") and **staleness magnitude**. Treat B as the recency/confidence/**order-uncertainty channel**, not merely a lossy copy of A. Your FHRR temporal-context superposition is a good implementation of the drifting-context idea.

**What to BUILD/CHANGE:** (i) **derive the discretization grid from event boundaries** (align belief-update change-points to segmented events, per Zacks) rather than per-token; (ii) keep rep A as the value/change-point truth layer and rep B as the graded recency/confidence/order-uncertainty layer; (iii) when rep A gives a crisp interval but two events are near-simultaneous, let rep B express the order *uncertainty* rather than forcing a false crisp order.

## Q3b — Is it a COMPOSITION of two systems, or one integrated event-model?

**VERDICT: PINNED-faithful as a composition — with the load-bearing caveat that the two systems must be BOUND at event boundaries.**

- **Neural dissociation supports composition.** The **mentalizing network** (bilateral TPJ, mPFC, precuneus; Saxe & Kanwisher 2003; Schurz et al. 2014 meta-analysis "Fractionating theory of mind") is dissociable from the **episodic/temporal MTL–PMN system** (hippocampus + posterior-medial network; Howard/Eichenbaum). RTPJ carries belief *content*; hippocampus carries **event-specific temporal binding**; mPFC generalizes across schema/event-type. Impaired **RTPJ–hippocampus connectivity** degrades other-mind representation (schizophrenia work). So belief-attribution and event-ordering are genuinely separable systems — validating your compositional design.
- **BUT the event-indexing account treats the protagonist/intentionality dimension as ONE index of a SINGLE integrated event model**, and Apperly & Butterfill's efficient system tracks belief-like states *inside* fast processing. Reconciliation: **separable neural systems integrated/bound via the event model (PMN/hippocampus as the binding hub).** The hippocampus binds *who-believed-what* to *when* at event boundaries.

**What to BUILD/CHANGE:** build the composition (ToM system × temporal-order system) — but make the **BINDING explicit**: bind `belief_content ⊗ temporal_context` **at event boundaries**. This is precisely the job of your FHRR bind, and it is where the two systems become one queryable timeline. Do not leave belief and time as two unbound registers; the brain's integration site is the bind.

## Q4 — DRAMATIC IRONY / DECEPTION / STALE BELIEF; parallel states; capacity

**VERDICT: PINNED. The brain maintains PARALLEL per-agent knowledge states and actively tracks the reader–character GAP; capacity is limited, and the limit bites on RECURSION, not on first-order parallelism.**

- **Parallel states are actively maintained.** Dramatic-irony work (Bruckert/Levine-style eye-tracking; "The audience who knew too much," *Frontiers in Psychology* 2023; "**Viewers Actively Extract and Maintain** Spontaneous Theory-of-Mind Representations in Dramatic Irony Scenes," 2025) shows viewers hold the character's *outdated/false* knowledge state **in parallel with** their own superior knowledge, and the belief-discrepancy shapes **real-time attention and prediction**. This directly supports (a) parallel per-agent tracks and (b) the stale character belief being *actively held*, not overwritten.
- **The decision-relevant quantity is the GAP.** Dramatic irony = reader-knows-p ∧ character-believes-¬p; suspense/deception are computed over `belief_A vs reality` and `belief_A vs belief_B` — the substrate should compute the *difference*, not just per-agent states.
- **Capacity:** first-order per-agent beliefs — several can be tracked, organized around protagonists (character-indexed situation models; Cowan's ~4-item WM bound as a soft ceiling). **Recursion is the hard limit:** humans degrade sharply beyond ~2nd order and only reach ~4th–5th with effort (Kinderman, Dunbar & Bentall 1998; Stiller & Dunbar 2007; mirrored by the Hi-ToM LLM benchmark). Relational-complexity theory (Halford) explains why: each embedding adds re-entrant binding load.

**What to BUILD/CHANGE:** represent an explicit **reader/omniscient track** alongside per-agent tracks and make the **gap** a first-class query; expect and *test* graceful degradation with number of agents and (especially) recursion depth.

## Q5 — BOUNDS / WALLS (a rigorous, brain-grounded negative = a full pass)

**Wall 1 (biggest): past-belief reconstruction is systematically biased by current knowledge — HINDSIGHT BIAS / CURSE OF KNOWLEDGE.**
- Finding: Fischhoff (1975) creeping determinism; Birch & Bloom (2007) *Psych Science* — adults' knowledge of the true outcome contaminates their judgment of what an agent believed *before* they knew it (Vicki/violin: bias scales with perceived plausibility). Mechanism model: **SARA — Selective Activation and Reconstructive Anchoring** (Pohl, Erdfelder): current knowledge acts as an *anchor* on reactivated memories.
- **Why the brain succeeds when it does:** it maintains a **decoupled** agent-belief file (TPJ/mentalizing) and **inhibits** the reality intrusion (executive/inhibitory control — the reason 3–4-yr-olds fail is inhibitory immaturity, not representational lack). **Build across the gap:** keep the agent-belief store **strictly decoupled** from the world store; a world-state update may write an agent slot **only** via an explicit observed/communicated edge. A software store has no leak by default → **you can legitimately beat the brain here** (no curse of knowledge). *This is your #1 build directive and also your best can-fail control:* a correct reader must NOT let a later world-change contaminate its answer to "what did A believe at T." If it does, you have a leak = bug; if a brain-faithful reconstruction is your goal instead, you would deliberately *add* the anchor. Decide which, and test for it.

**Wall 2: higher-order/recursive ToM is capacity-limited (~2 orders robust).** Stiller & Dunbar 2007; Kinderman et al. 1998; Hi-ToM. Why: WM/relational-complexity (Halford). Build-across: nest beliefs as explicit nested belief files keyed by `(holder, about=<agent,fact>)`; a software store can exceed the human cap — **OUR-INVENTION-acceptable to beat the brain**, but validate that nesting doesn't blow up combinatorially.

**Wall 3: the identity/intensionality signature limit (Apperly & Butterfill 2009; Butterfill & Apperly 2013 "minimal ToM").** The efficient system CANNOT track beliefs that hinge on mistaken identity (Lois Lane believes Superman flies but not that Clark does — same referent). **If your belief facts are keyed by world REFERENT, you reproduce this exact failure.** Build: key beliefs by the agent's **mode-of-presentation/guise**, not referent alone, wherever intensional contexts matter. (You can beat the human efficient system here too, but only if you key intensionally.)

**Wall 4 (a correction to your "observed events only" rule): belief also updates on INFERENCE and COMMUNICATION/TESTIMONY.** "Seeing leads to knowing" (Pratt & Bryant 1990) is a *knowledge-access* competence — necessary but not sufficient. Deception specifically requires that an agent can *cause* a false belief in another via a (possibly false) assertion. Build: add update-edge TYPES — `observed(world-state)`, `inferred(from premises)`, `communicated(asserted content, which may be false)`. Deception = assert p while believing ¬p; testimony = A tells B p ⇒ B believes p ∧ A believes B-believes-p.

**Also correct — factive vs defeasible:** distinguish **KNOWS(A,p)** (factive, from observation, true) from **BELIEVES(A,p)** (defeasible, persists, can be false). *Stale/false belief is exactly an old KNOWS persisting as a now-false BELIEVES after an unobserved world change.* Model the transition explicitly: observe at t1 → world changes unobserved at t2 → at t3 the register still holds p (now false). That transition IS your use-case; make it a named edge.

## RANKED concrete design changes (most load-bearing first)

1. **DECOUPLE the agent-belief store from the world/reality store.** A world update may write an agent slot **only** through an explicit observed/communicated edge. This defeats curse-of-knowledge, is the brain's robustness mechanism, and is your #1 can-fail control. (Q2, Q5-Wall1)
2. **Store sparse time-tagged update EVENTS per (agent, fact) + current value; answer "knew at T" by retrieving the most-recent edge before T.** The dense step function is the *readout*, not the storage. (Q1)
3. **Align belief change-points to EVENT BOUNDARIES (event segmentation), not per-token.** (Q3)
4. **Add update-edge TYPES: observed / inferred / communicated(may-be-false).** Enables deception + testimony; fixes "observation-only." (Q5-Wall4)
5. **Distinguish factive KNOWS from defeasible BELIEVES; model stale belief as old-KNOWS-persisting-as-now-false-BELIEVES.** (Q5)
6. **Keep rep A as value/change-point truth; keep rep B (FHRR temporal-context) as recency/confidence/order-uncertainty AND as the belief⊗when BINDING at event boundaries — not a lossy copy.** Confidence decays with time-since-update; value does not. (Q3, Q3b)
7. **Represent an explicit reader/omniscient track; make the GAP (belief_A vs reality, belief_A vs belief_B) a first-class query.** (Q4)
8. **Key beliefs by mode-of-presentation/guise where identity matters, not referent alone** (avoid the minimal-ToM identity signature-limit). (Q5-Wall3)
9. **Nested beliefs as explicit nested files; you may exceed the human ~2-order recursion cap (OUR-INVENTION-acceptable) but validate against combinatorial blowup.** (Q5-Wall2)
10. **Optionally add a controllable curse-of-knowledge INTRUSION term** — as a discriminator (a clean reader shows none; a leak reveals a bug) and/or to *model* human-like errors when that is the goal. (Q5-Wall1)

## Where I am NOT confident (VET before trusting)
- The store-vs-reconstruct fork (Q1) is genuinely mixed in the literature; my "current-state-stored + past-reconstructed" synthesis is a defensible reading (P≈0.45 deflated), not settled. **Discriminator:** run the hindsight-bias probe — does your reader's answer to "what did A believe at T" shift when you change *only* a later, unobserved world event? A stored-clean substrate: no shift. A brain-faithful reconstruction: shift. Pick the target and test.
- Exact capacity numbers for parallel *first-order* character-belief tracks are under-reported for reading specifically (organized-around-protagonists is well-established; a hard N is not). Treat "several, soft ceiling ~4" as a hypothesis pending VET.

## TLDR (plain language)
The brain does not keep a neat little timeline of "what each character knew at every moment" and look up the answer. It keeps what each character believes *right now*, plus a few time-stamped memories of when they last saw things, and it *rebuilds* the past on demand — and that rebuild gets contaminated by what the reader now knows (the "I knew it all along" bias). Your sample-and-hold picture is the right *description* of how a belief behaves (it stays put until the character sees something change), but you should *store* it as a handful of "saw-it" events rather than a full frame-by-frame tape. The most important fix: keep each character's beliefs in a separate box from the real world, so real-world facts can only get in when the character actually observes or is told them — that one rule is what lets people (and will let your reader) hold a character's stale, wrong belief without it being silently corrected. Also: beliefs change not just from seeing, but from being *told* (which is how lies and deception work), so add that.

## INFERENTIAL BELIEF DRILL (2026-08-30)
Author: SOLVER (belief-timeline problem) · Type: ONLINE literature scan · Calibration: lit-scan, P deflated 0.15–0.25, novel-synthesis capped 0.50.
**Scope:** the ONE update route not yet built — does the brain attribute INFERENTIAL belief (A concludes Q by reasoning from premises A observed, without perceiving/being told Q), by what mechanism, and is it part of the first-order belief timeline or a distinct epistemic-reasoning capability (the next problem)?

**Orientation (one paragraph):** Yes — inference is a recognized, dissociable SOURCE OF KNOWLEDGE in ToM, and *attributing* it to another agent is a real, later-developing, neurally-distinct competence, NOT a free by-product of "seeing leads to knowing." The brain does something my observation+testimony model cannot: it credits an agent with a conclusion the agent **never perceived and was never told**, derived from premises the agent holds — and (the fidelity crux) it must BOUND that credit to the inferences *that agent* could make from *that agent's* evidence, a bounding humans do imperfectly (curse of knowledge for inference). The route decomposes into two separable pieces — an **attribution edge** (belongs in my timeline) and a **derivation engine** (its own next problem).

### Q1 — Inference as a source of knowledge; developmental dissociation
- **Sodian & Wimmer 1987** (*Child Development* 58:424–433, "Children's Understanding of Inference as a Source of Knowledge"): the canonical result. A ball is drawn unseen from an all-red box. Both 4- and 6-yr-olds *use* inference themselves (they know the drawn ball is red). But asked whether **another person** who knows the box contents *knows* the ball's colour, **only 6-yr-olds attribute the inferential knowledge; 4-yr-olds deny it.** So USING inference as a source of one's OWN knowledge precedes ATTRIBUTING inferential knowledge to others by ~2 years.
- **The dissociation from perception:** perception-as-source ("seeing leads to knowing," Pratt & Bryant 1990) is grasped ~3–4; inference-as-source-for-others ~6 — LATER. Extra computation = representing that the agent will *derive* a conclusion it never perceived. Follow-ons: "When do children begin to understand logical inference as a source of knowledge?" (*Cognitive Development* 1994); Miller/Hardin-style certainty-of-deduction-vs-perception-vs-guessing work; Ruffman replications.
- **Implicit competence is early, though:** **Ting, He & Baillargeon 2021** (*JECP* 208:105126): 5-month-olds expect an agent to INFER that a wide toy hidden in her absence is in the wide box (general knowledge: wide things fit only wide containers), with violation-of-expectation on the narrow-box choice. So an IMPLICIT system attributes general-knowledge inference to agents in infancy; the LATE-developing part is the EXPLICIT, reflective, verbally-reportable version — the same implicit/explicit gap seen for false belief (Apperly & Butterfill two-systems).
- **VERDICT Q1: PINNED.** Inference is a genuine, dissociable knowledge-source in ToM; attributing it is later-developing than perception-attribution because it adds a derivation step. This means the inference route is NOT free once observation-attribution exists — it is a real added capability.

### Q2 — Mechanism (simulation / stored rule / comprehension-inference) + neural substrate
- **Simulation vs theory (Ruffman 1996**, *Mind & Language* 11:388–414): children readily "input" the correct premises to a doll yet still predict the doll holds a FALSE belief when it did not look — a coherence/**theory**-driven pattern, read as evidence *against* clean forward-simulation (contested; simulationists reinterpret). Takeaway: developmental mechanism is theory/rule-like, applied to the agent's evidence, not transparent perspective-merge.
- **Comprehension-inference machinery (the reader's own engine):** McKoon & Ratcliff 1992 (*Psych Review* 99:440–466) **minimalist** — only locally-coherence-required or fast-from-general-knowledge inferences are automatic; Graesser, Singer & Trabasso 1994 **constructionist/search-after-meaning** — readers generate causal/goal inferences to explain WHY; Garnham/Garrod "false dichotomy." Forward causal-antecedent + character-goal inferences are routinely generated (McKoon & Ratcliff 1986). Attributing an inference to a character = the reader generates a **bridging/elaborative** inference, then must BIND it to the character and BOUND it by the character's evidence.
- **Neural substrate = a COMPOSITION:** mentalizing network (TPJ + mPFC + precuneus; Saxe, Schurz et al. 2014) binds "whose belief" (TPJ = transient belief content; mPFC = reasoning-based attribution using social/semantic knowledge) **plus a distinct fronto-parietal reasoning substrate** for the derivation itself ("Distinct neural substrates for deductive and mathematical processing"; deductive reasoning recruits rostrolateral/inferior-frontal + parietal, separable from mentalizing). So inferential-belief-attribution = derive-the-conclusion (reasoning system) ⊗ bind-to-agent (ToM system) — exactly parallel to the ToM×temporal-order composition in Q3b above.
- **VERDICT Q2: PINNED as a composition** (derivation step + ToM-attribution/binding step). Pure stored-rule (b) under-covers; pure simulation (a) is contested. The exact simulation-vs-theory implementation is **OUR-INVENTION-acceptable** — glass-box, I recommend explicit bounded derivation + bind (the theory/rule-over-the-agent's-evidence reading), which is defensible and testable.

### Q3 — BOUNDEDNESS (the fidelity crux) + failure mode
- The reader must attribute to A only inferences A could make from what A OBSERVED — the inference analog of observation-gating / curse-of-knowledge decoupling (Wall 1 above).
- **Readers DO bound, but imperfectly.** "Bias in perspective-taking during reading: Adjusting the knowledge of characters" (*J. Pragmatics* 2011) + the reading curse-of-knowledge line: readers over-attribute their own privileged knowledge to characters via **anchoring-on-own-knowledge + insufficient adjustment / failure to inhibit** (same mechanism as Birch & Bloom 2007; Keysar et al. 2003). The characteristic failure is **OVER-attribution**: crediting the character with an inference only the reader (with extra info) could make.
- The brain's success mechanism = the decoupled agent-belief file (mentalizing) + executive inhibition of reality/own-knowledge intrusion — the same decoupling that is your #1 directive from the prior drill, now applied to *inferences* not just *facts*.
- **VERDICT Q3: PINNED.** Boundedness is the load-bearing constraint and the source of the human failure mode; a software store with strict evidence-gating can BEAT the brain (no curse of knowledge). This IS the can-fail discriminator (see below).

### Q4 — Inference-based deception
- **Epistemic vigilance (Sperber et al. 2010):** comprehenders track not just source honesty but the source's **intention to induce a belief**. Deception splits into assertion-based (state false p) and **inference-based** (supply true-but-misleading evidence E so the target infers a false Q — implicature/insinuation). Tracking inference-based deception REQUIRES representing: B infers Q from E, PLUS A intends B to infer Q while A believes ¬Q.
- **VERDICT Q4: PINNED, and load-bearing for a feature you already have.** Your deception route is currently assertion-only; a large class of real deception is inference-based and is *unrepresentable* without the inference edge. This is an independent argument that the inference route is needed now, not optional.

### THE VERDICT: (a) extension vs (b) separate problem — RECOMMENDATION
**Split at a clean seam — build the ROUTE now as (a); scope the DERIVATION ENGINE as (b).**
- **(a) — build NOW, in the timeline:** add an `inferred(from=P1..Pn, rule=R)` update-edge TYPE alongside `observed`/`communicated`. Semantics: *A believes Q at T iff A holds premises P1..Pn by T (via observed/communicated edges) AND a schema R: P1..Pn ⊢ Q fires, with the conclusion-edge time-stamped at the moment the LAST premise entered A's evidence*, gated strictly by A's evidence set. This is bounded modus-ponens over the agent's observed belief set — cheap, glass-box, composes with the existing edges/binding, and is REQUIRED by inference-based deception (Q4). Restrict R at first cut to a SMALL CLOSED set of brain-plausible, bounded schemas: disjunctive/exclusion (all-red-box ⊢ drawn-ball-red = the Sodian & Wimmer schema), transitive-spatial (X in box ∧ box in room ⊢ X in room), simple modus ponens over an observed conditional. OUR-INVENTION-acceptable, can-fail-testable.
- **(b) — its OWN next problem:** the general DERIVATION ENGINE (which rules R exist, which Q follows from arbitrary premises) is the whole of reasoning, sits on a NEURALLY DISTINCT fronto-parietal substrate (Q2), and is DEVELOPMENTALLY dissociated/later (Q1). Do NOT try to build a general inferential ToM inside the belief timeline. The timeline owns *binding a derived conclusion to an agent, gated by that agent's evidence*; the reasoning organ owns *producing the conclusion*. The seam = the schema set R.
- **CAN-FAIL TEST DESIGN (mirrors Sodian & Wimmer's partial-premise manipulation — brain-grounded validation):**
  1. **Positive (must infer):** agent observed ALL premises of a closed schema before T → reader attributes Q at T. **Floor that never infers UNDER-attributes** (fails positive).
  2. **Boundedness (must NOT infer):** agent observed only SOME premises (one missing) → reader must NOT attribute Q. **Omniscient floor that credits the reader's own inference OVER-attributes** (fails negative) — this is the curse-of-knowledge-for-inference control, the discriminator that matters most.
  3. **Temporal gating:** agent observes the last premise at t_k → the Q-belief edge appears at t_k, never earlier.
  4. **Inference-deception / stale inferred belief:** A gives B true evidence E → B infers Q; later an unobserved-by-B fact makes Q false → reader tracks B still-believes-Q (stale inferred belief) while reader knows ¬Q (a GAP query, reusing the Q4-above gap machinery).
  Gate: CI-separated margin over BOTH floors (never-infer and omniscient-infer) on the same items, per the measurement bar.

**TLDR (plain language):** People really do credit a story character with a conclusion the character was never shown and never told, as long as the character saw the clues to work it out — and the hard part is *not* over-crediting them with clues only the reader saw (the "I knew it, so surely they do" trap). This is a real added ability that shows up later in children (~age 6, two years after "if you saw it you know it") and uses a separate reasoning part of the brain on top of the mind-reading part. So: build the small piece that *attaches a worked-out conclusion to a character and only if that character had the clues* into the belief timeline now (a lie can work by feeding true-but-misleading clues, so you need this anyway) — but treat *general reasoning* (figuring out what follows from anything) as its own next project.

**QUESTIONS:** None.

**NEXT STEPS:** Implement the `inferred` edge with the 3-schema closed set + evidence-gating; run test #2 (partial-premise boundedness) FIRST as the can-fail control; file the general derivation engine as a separate brief.

## UNCERTAIN / DISTRIBUTIONAL BELIEF DRILL (2026-08-30)
Author: SOLVER (belief-timeline problem) · Type: ONLINE literature scan · Calibration: lit-scan, P deflated 0.15–0.25, novel-synthesis capped 0.50.
**Scope:** I replaced crisp point-beliefs with a PARTIAL belief = a SET of possible object-locations the agent considers (FHRR superposition, read via a CA3-style `cleanup_set`), narrowing over time by set-EXCLUSION; plus a separate scalar decaying-confidence (recency); plus an occlusion-aware observation ledger. Does the brain attribute a graded POSTERIOR (not just hold one)? Is exclusion the right update? Is the occlusion belief a flat set or a structured region? Is confidence one thing with the distribution or two? What is the single next fidelity step?

**Orientation (one paragraph):** The equal-weight SET is a REAL cognitive stage, not an error — it is the brain's *minimal/early* form of representing alternatives (possibility-BEFORE-probability), and it is also the exact-right limit for HARD logical evidence (exclusion = Bayesian update with 0/1 likelihoods). But it is the FLOOR. The mature adult brain attributes a GRADED posterior over locations (Baker et al. 2017 BToM; people attribute probabilities to *others*, not just hold them), updates it by precision-weighted evidence (predictive coding), and — critically for your occlusion case — attributes a STRUCTURED, peaked prior (object-permanence + motion-inertia), never a flat set. Your three currently-separate pieces (the set, the recency scalar, the occlusion ledger) are three views of ONE object: a weighted spatial posterior whose precision decays with recency. The single frontier step unifies them: **give the set graded weights (a real posterior).** Everything else (precision-weighted update, structured occlusion priors, confidence-from-entropy) is an operation that only becomes expressible once weights exist.

### Q1 — Is attributed belief a distribution/posterior, and do observers attribute graded uncertainty to OTHERS? Is the equal-weight SET faithful?
- **YES — the mature form is a distribution attributed to others. Baker, Jara-Ettinger, Saxe & Tenenbaum 2017** (*Nature Human Behaviour* 1:0064, "Rational quantitative attribution of beliefs, desires and percepts"): BToM represents the agent's belief as a **probability distribution over world states (a POMDP belief state)**, and human *quantitative* judgments of what another agent believes track that graded posterior — people attribute graded uncertainty to others, not merely carry it themselves. **Ying, Zhi-Xuan et al. 2025** (*TACL*, "Understanding Epistemic Language with a Language-augmented Bayesian Theory of Mind"): "might / probably / I think / knows" are evaluated against a graded POMDP posterior and match human judgments — direct evidence the attributed object is graded. **Gerchen 2025** (bioRxiv 2025.07.14.664652, "Neural Convergence of Graded and Binary Belief Uncertainty"): graded-probabilistic and binary-categorical belief attributions **converge on a shared mentalizing substrate** — the brain runs both, not one or the other.
- **BUT the equal-weight SET is a genuine, brain-faithful FIRST CUT, not a mistake.** Possibility precedes probability developmentally: **Leahy & Carey 2020** (*TiCS*, "The Acquisition of Modal Concepts") distinguish **minimal** representations (an unweighted set of alternatives, no modal/probabilistic status) from **modal** representations (weighted possibilities); **Redshaw & Suddendorf 2016** (forked-tube) + **Leahy et al. 2022** (*PNAS*, "Minimal representations of possibility at age 3") show children represent *a set of mutually-exclusive possibilities* before they can weight them (~age 4). So your equal-weight set = the minimal/implicit stage; graded weights = the mature adult upgrade.
- **VERDICT Q1: PINNED-faithful as the FLOOR (minimal-modal stage + hard-evidence limit); needs-correction toward graded weights for the mature system.** The set is right; equal-weight is the frontier gap.
- **What to build/change:** add optional **per-element weights** to the FHRR superposition (weighted-superposition coefficients per bound location) → the set becomes a real posterior; `cleanup_set` returns ranked similarity scores as weights instead of a thresholded membership set.

### Q2 — Update mechanism: exclusion vs evidence-accumulation vs predictive-coding; when collapse to a point?
- **Set-EXCLUSION is a SPECIAL CASE of Bayesian update — the hard-likelihood (0/1) limit.** "The ball is NOT in the red box" = likelihood 0 for red-box → mass zeroed. That is genuinely how the brain handles *definite/logical* evidence (disjunctive elimination — the Sodian & Wimmer all-red-box schema; infants also narrow by exclusion). So exclusion is faithful for CATEGORICAL evidence and INCOMPLETE for graded evidence, where the brain *re-weights* rather than deletes.
- **The general mechanism is Bayesian belief-update, implemented two compatible ways.** (i) Process level: **sequential-sampling / drift-diffusion** integrate the log-likelihood-ratio and are provably equivalent to normative Bayesian updating (Gold & Shadlen; "Dynamics of decision-making," PMC3798759). (ii) Cortical level: **predictive coding** (Rao & Ballard 1999; Friston 2005) — belief updates by **precision-weighted prediction error**, where a *precision ratio* (prior precision vs observation precision) scales how far each observation moves the posterior. You already have a `predictive_coding` organ — this is its job.
- **Collapse to a point is EMERGENT, not a separate rule:** the posterior collapses when it becomes sufficiently peaked (one weight dominates / entropy → 0 / a commitment boundary is crossed, as in the DDM bound). Do not hard-switch set→point; let precision drive it.
- **VERDICT Q2: exclusion = PINNED-as-special-case (hard-likelihood limit), OUR-INVENTION-acceptable to keep; needs-correction to add SOFT precision-weighted updates.** Collapse = emergent from entropy/precision, not a rule.
- **What to build/change:** route graded belief updates through the `predictive_coding` organ as **precision-weighted** re-weighting; keep set-exclusion as the 0/1-likelihood limit; trigger point-collapse when posterior entropy < threshold.

### Q3 — Occlusion / "somewhere in the region": flat set, peaked prior, or unknown?
- **NOT a flat set — a STRUCTURED, peaked region.** Object permanence (**Baillargeon, Spelke & Wasserman 1985**; **Baillargeon 1987**) → a hidden object persists **at its last-seen location** = a prior peaked at last-location. If it was MOVING, infants **extrapolate the trajectory by inertia** (**Spelke & von Hofsten 2001**, "Predictive Reaching for Occluded Objects"; **von Hofsten et al. 1998**) → prior peaked at the *extrapolated* emergence point (head-tracking anticipates it). Occlusion specifically *degrades* this (**Hespos & von Hofsten 2009**, "Occlusion is Hard" — reaching drops under occlusion even as tracking predicts emergence), so the region prior is real but noisier. And infants represent the location **probabilistically and graded**, modulated by probability info, not flat (**12/24-mo search under informational uncertainty**, *Frontiers Psych* 2020).
- **VERDICT Q3: needs-correction — my equal-weight set over the region is UNDER-structured.** The brain attributes a region bounded by the occluder BUT peaked by (a) last-seen location and (b) motion/trajectory extrapolation. Uniform-over-region is only the no-motion-info limit.
- **What to build/change:** seed the occlusion belief with **structured weights** — highest prior at last-seen cell; if moving, up-weight the trajectory-extrapolated cell; occluder region bounds the support. (This is again *graded weights* → converges with Q1.)

### Q4 — Confidence (scalar recency) vs the distribution: one quantity or two?
- **Two RELATED-but-DISTINCT quantities — do not fully merge them. Pouget, Drugowitsch & Kepecs 2016** (*Nature Neuroscience* 19:366–374, "Confidence and certainty: distinct probabilistic quantities for different goals"): **confidence = the probability a specific decision/proposition is correct** (a scalar readout for action), while the full posterior / "certainty" is the richer distributional object; the brain keeps *multiple* uncertainties for different goals. The **Bayesian-confidence hypothesis** (confidence ≈ posterior prob-correct; **Meyniel, Sigman & Mainen 2015**, *Neuron*) is the leading account but is **challenged** — confidence is NOT a lossless readout of the posterior (violations with >2 alternatives, **Li & Ma 2020**; *PNAS* 2024 "Challenging the Bayesian confidence hypothesis").
- **The decisive point for your design:** your **recency/decay scalar is a MEMORY-STRENGTH / access signal**, a *source* of uncertainty that should feed the posterior's PRECISION — it is NOT a parallel, independent confidence channel, and it is NOT identical to the posterior's entropy over locations either. Clean relationship: **recency modulates precision** (stale memory → lower precision → flatter/wider posterior → higher entropy → lower confidence); overall confidence is then a *readout of posterior entropy/peakedness that already includes the recency contribution.*
- **VERDICT Q4: needs-correction toward PARTIAL unification.** Not one thing (Pouget: distinct quantities), not two disconnected things (your current split). Recency = a precision INPUT to the one posterior; confidence = entropy readout OF that posterior.
- **What to build/change:** make the recency scalar an input to posterior precision (flatten the weights as recency decays) and DERIVE confidence from posterior entropy — retire the free-floating independent confidence scalar.

### Q5 — THE FRONTIER: (a) graded weights, (b) precision-weighted update, (c) structured occlusion priors, (d) other. This problem or next brief? + can-fail test.
- **The single highest-value step is (a): give the set GRADED WEIGHTS (a real posterior over locations).** Note the convergence: Q1 (graded attribution), Q3 (structured priors = spatially-structured weights), and Q4 (precision = weight concentration) ALL reduce to the same primitive. (b) precision-weighted update and (c) structured occlusion priors are **operations on a weighted representation** — they are *unlocked by* (a) and are secondary; (a) is the enabling substrate change. It is FHRR-native (weighted superposition) and every existing edge (observed / communicated / inferred) just writes weights instead of set-membership.
- **THIS problem, not the next brief.** (a) is a representation upgrade to the existing belief-timeline slot (set → weighted set); it composes with the existing binding + edge types. What IS a distinct next brief is the **probabilistic/Bayesian ToM INFERENCE ENGINE** — inferring the posterior from an agent's *actions* by inverse planning / POMDP inversion (Baker 2017). That parallels the prior drill's seam exactly: the timeline owns *the weighted belief slot + binding + evidence-gating*; a separate "probabilistic ToM" organ owns *inferring the posterior from behavior*. Build (a) here; file inverse-planning as the next brief.
- **CAN-FAIL TEST DESIGN (graded weights):**
  1. **Graded ranking (THE discriminator):** agent gets partial evidence making location X twice as likely as Y (neither excluded) → reader's posterior must rank weight(X) > weight(Y). **FLOOR = the current equal-weight set is STRUCTURALLY INCAPABLE of expressing this → fails.** A win here is a real capability gain, not a tuning gain.
  2. **Recency→precision (Q4):** two agents, identical last-seen location, one observed recently, one long ago → the stale agent's posterior must be FLATTER (higher entropy) at the SAME peak. **Floor = value + constant separate confidence fails** (peak identical, no entropy difference).
  3. **Occlusion structure (Q3):** object moves into a region → posterior peaked at the trajectory-extrapolated cell. **Floor = flat set over region fails.**
  4. **Collapse:** accumulate evidence until one weight crosses threshold → posterior collapses to a point (certainty); verify NO premature collapse under weak evidence.
  - **Gate:** CI-separated margin over BOTH floors (equal-weight-set AND value+scalar-confidence) on the SAME items, per the measurement bar. Test #1 is the load-bearing can-fail: the equal-weight floor *cannot* pass it, so it cleanly proves graded weights add capability; a floor that can't rank = under-expresses.

**TLDR (plain language):** People really do credit a story character with a *hunch* — "she thinks it's probably in the kitchen, maybe the hall" — not just a flat list of possible spots, and they attribute those odds to the character, not only feel them themselves. My current design gives the character a plain list where every spot counts equally; that is how a very young child (and the brain's quick-and-rough system) starts, and it is exactly right when a clue flatly rules a spot out — but the grown-up brain carries *weights*. Two things I keep separate are really one: how sure the character is, and how long ago they looked, should be the same knob — an older memory just makes the guess fuzzier. When an object slips behind something, the brain doesn't think "could be anywhere back there" — it bets on where it last was and where it was heading. So the one next step is: let each possible spot carry a *weight* (a real hunch), fed by how fresh the memory is and which way the object was moving. That upgrade lives in *this* project; the harder job of *figuring out a character's hunch from watching what they do* is its own next project.
