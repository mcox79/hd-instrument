## 1. WHAT WAS BUILT, IN PLAIN LANGUAGE

Working out which meaning of a verb is meant costs about three fifths of the time it takes the reader to read
a page. It does that by letting activation spread through its dictionary until it settles — an expensive
operation — and then weighing the result against how common each meaning of that verb is.

**Two cheap facts, either of which often settles the matter before the spreading starts.**

**The first is how common the meanings are.** On nine occasions in ten the commonest meaning is so far ahead
that nothing the sentence could contribute would change the answer. People behave the same way: when a word
has one clearly dominant meaning, readers do not recruit the sentence's context to choose it and their eyes
show no sign of the extra work; when two meanings are about equally common, they do. That is a measured
effect in reading research, not an analogy. So the reader now looks first at how far ahead the commonest
meaning already is — which costs nothing — and only spreads activation when the top two are close. The head
start it requires was fixed on twelve pages and then applied unchanged to twelve different pages it had never
seen.

**The second is who the thing happening to.** The reader is using all this to decide whether an event helped
or harmed someone. On **863 of 932** occasions the thing being acted on is not a person or an animal — it is
a door, a number, a paragraph — and the answer is "nobody was helped or harmed" from a single lookup. Yet the
expensive spreading ran anyway, on **495 of the 535** occasions it ran at all. A rock has no welfare; the
brain does not work out how a rock feels about being moved. So the reader now checks who is being acted on
before it works out what was done.

**Together these two checks remove nineteen out of every twenty spreads, and the reading is exactly the same
— byte for byte, on all twenty-four pages tested**: the same sentences, entities, events, who-did-what-to-whom,
and the same helped/harmed judgement on every one of 1,106 decisions. **Reading a page is about four times
faster.** A version that skips exactly as many spreads but picks them at random changes the meanings the
reader settles on every single time it is run, so the checks are doing real work and are not merely lucky.

**Nothing about the spreading itself changed** — not how far activation travels, not how long it settles for,
not the dictionary it travels through, not how its answer is weighed. Only *when* it is asked.

## 3. THE MECHANISM, AND WHY EACH PIECE IS THE BRAIN'S

**LEVER 1 — PINNED: reordered access / the subordinate-bias effect.** For a BALANCED ambiguous word (two
senses of similar frequency) a disambiguating prior context measurably changes the reading of it; for a BIASED
one it does not, unless the context is strong enough to overcome the dominance (Duffy, Morris & Rayner 1988,
*Lexical ambiguity and fixation times in reading*; Rayner & Frazier 1989; Binder & Rayner 1998; Duffy, Kambe
& Rayner 2001 for the review). **Context is RECRUITED where the resting levels are close and not where one
already dominates.**

**And the barrier is a difference of base-level activations, which is also pinned.** In ACT-R the probability
of retrieving the runner-up rather than the dominant chunk is a softmax over base-level activations with noise
`s`, so the log-odds of the competitor is exactly `A_1 − A_2` (Anderson & Schooler 1991). Our resting level is
a distribution over senses from presentation counts, so `log P(top-1) − log P(top-2)` **is** that activation
difference, in nats. `affect_lexicon.resting_level`'s own docstring already names ACT-R base-level activation.

**LEVER 3 — PINNED: organs take data in order, and welfare is a property of an experiencer.** The harm/help
read is a judgement about a *patient's welfare*; an inanimate patient has none, which is why
`harm_help_arithmetic` answers NA on its first line. Running the sense read first is an ordering defect, not a
modelling choice: the cheap decisive cue must gate the expensive one. This is the same brain principle as
lever 1 one rung up — *recruit the expensive machinery only where it can change the answer* — and the owner's
own standing rule (2026-09-13).

**OUR-INVENTION, swept, never adopted — the thresholds.** tau is a criterion, and criteria are not read off a
table: §6 replaces the constant with an ONLINE criterion the reader sets from its own experience.

**What the patch does** (`walk_gate_patch.diff`, two files, `git apply --check` clean):

* `hdlab/grounded_semantic_graph.py` — factors the resting level's normalisation out of `_blend_pick` into
  `_norm_prior` so there is **exactly one implementation** of it; adds `frequency_barrier(prior, alpha)`, which
  calls that same function; adds the swept constant `WALK_GATE_TAU`; and makes `select_sense_blended` compute
  the prior **before** requesting the walk, skip the request when the barrier has already decided, and hand
  `_blend_pick` its **own existing `ppr is None` branch**. There is no second argmax anywhere in the patch.
* `hdlab/force_dynamics_valence.py` — the same gate on the distribution path, on **its** resting level
  (`affect_lexicon.resting_level`, called, never re-derived) with **its own** threshold; and `ANIMACY_FIRST`,
  which puts both sense reads behind one `sense_can_matter` guard and **stands down in the one branch
  (`SENSE_ABSTAIN`) that consults the sense before animacy.**

**A structural safety property I did not design and then found, now a self-test:** `_sense_prior` falls back
to `1/(1+rank)` when a lemma has **no SemCor counts at all**. That fallback's barrier is **0.6061 nats for
every candidate count**, below tau — so **the gate can never fire on a lemma whose frequency prior is a rank
guess rather than evidence.**

## 6. THE QUALITY PUSH — THE CRITERION IS LEARNED ONLINE, AND IT BEATS THE SWEPT CONSTANT

**Lever A — the criterion is LEARNED ONLINE, so nothing is frozen.** A swept constant measures an equilibrium;
it is not a mechanism. Criterion setting from experienced outcomes is the brain's form (signal-detection
criterion setting, Green & Swets 1966; the same accumulate-to-criterion shape as ACT-R retrieval and the
drift-diffusion boundary). `WalkGateCriterion` starts with the gate **OFF**, observes every walk it lets
through, and **raises** its criterion whenever it is surprised — so it can only ever become MORE cautious:

    tau_t = max(tau_floor, margin + max{ evidence seen so far })

**Lever B — and it is the better observable: the blend's own algebra makes the gate an IDENTITY.** The blend
picks `j` over the prior's winner `i*` iff `lam·(log pp_j − log pp_i*) > log pf_i* − log pf_j`, and for every
`j ≠ i*` the right-hand side is **at least the barrier B** (B is the smallest such gap, by definition). So

> **a flip REQUIRES `A := lam · max_j (log pp_j − log pp_i*)  >  B`.**

`A` is the entire force the walk can exert on that call and it is measurable on **every** walk that runs — not
only on the ~11% that flip — so the criterion is estimated from all of them, and **gating at `tau ≥ sup A` is
lossless by construction rather than by fit.** The derivation is checked against the observed outcome on every
recorded call: **844 of 844 agree, 0 disagree, and 0 flips occurred with `A ≤ B`.**

**Replayed in reading order over the 12 TEST documents, causally (a walk the criterion skips is one whose
outcome it then does not observe):**

| operating point | how tau is set | tau | walks skipped | overturns lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| the shipped constant | swept on TRAIN, frozen | 1.25 | 263 of 844 (31.2%) | **0 of 91** |
| **online, `outcome` mode** | **learned from being surprised, no TRAIN split at all** | **0.886** | **408 of 844 (48.3%)** | **0 of 91** |
| online, `advantage` mode | learned from every walk; lossless by the algebra | 2.547 | 91 of 844 (10.8%) | **0 of 91** |
| (the provable static bound) | `sup A` over the population | 2.297 | 99 of 844 (11.7%) | 0 by construction |

**The plastic mechanism beats the frozen constant by 17 points of skipped share and needs no held-out fitting
at all.** Its honest risk, stated: `outcome` mode is a running **max** with a margin, so it is exposed to
ORDER — it happened to meet its highest-barrier overturn (0.6363) inside the first document and raise itself
above it. `advantage` mode is not exposed that way (it observes on every walk and reaches `sup A` by document
4) but is far more conservative. **The constant ships because it is the operating point both splits certify;
the criterion is built, measured and ready as the plastic replacement.**

## 9. KEY REALIZATIONS

1. **The invocation share is not the saving, and only the join tells them apart.** Once another optimisation
   is already in place, "the gate skips 31.2% of walks" and "the gate removes 31.2% of the cost" are different
   claims: 121 of 263 skipped requests are **moved** to the sibling caller, not removed. The only way to see
   it is to record the memo *keys* and ask who asks them again.
2. **A "no change" result is only evidence if you count the chances it had to change.** Arm C's zero is worth
   something because the same run reports 241 consumer calls that lost their graded posterior; without that
   denominator it is indistinguishable from a gate that never fired.
3. **My own explanation of the downstream loss was wrong, and measuring it is what found the biggest lever.**
   I predicted the posterior-blind result-state rung pre-empted the distribution; it pre-empts 1 of 535. The
   real answer was one rung up and four times larger: 92.6% of the judgements are answered by animacy before
   meaning is consulted. **The hypothesis that gets measured is worth more than the hypothesis that is right.**
4. **An argmax statistic about a quantity nobody reads as an argmax is not a signal-loss measurement.** "The
   posterior's argmax moves on 42% of calls" is the headline of the sibling brief; the consumer reads the
   *expectation*, whose median move is 0.0018 against a 0.10 threshold.
5. **A guard that fires because the effect is large is a broken guard.** The 3× cross-arm discard rule
   rejected 4 of 4 documents precisely because the gate is more than 3× faster. Fixing it in the open, with
   the old number printed beside the new one, is the only honest way to change a guard mid-result.
6. **Fit the threshold honestly and take the loss** — TRAIN chose 1.25 where the TEST sizing suggested 1.0,
   giving up 16.7 points — **and then let the plastic version win it back**: the online criterion settles at
   0.886 and skips 48.3% with no held-out fitting at all.
7. **The blend's own algebra turns the threshold into an identity.** A flip requires the contextual advantage
   to exceed the barrier; that advantage is measurable on every walk, so `sup A` is a provably-lossless tau
   and the criterion has something to estimate from **all** the walks rather than the 11% that flip.
8. **Prove the reuse duplication numerically instead of arguing it.** "This is the same computation as
   `graded_competition`'s margin" is prose until it is `max |diff| = 0.00e+00 over 500 random priors`; then
   the consolidation is mechanical rather than a debate.

## 10. ALTERNATE PATHS (brain structure + computation, the math, what it would take, why not now)

**A. ONE ACCESS, TWO READOUTS.** *Structure:* anterior-temporal hub access, read out by the task (Patterson,
Nestor & Rogers 2007; Swets et al. 2008 for readout-as-collapse). *Math:* compute the sense posterior
`q ∝ P_freq · PPR^lam` once per (verb, sentence); `context_sense_sign` takes `argmax q`, `harm_help_arithmetic`

`affect_lexicon.sense_rows`) and two temperatures (0.5 vs 4.0 — the second is a readout precision and can stay
per-readout; the first is a real decision about which inventory the reader commits to). *Why not now:* it
changes WHAT is computed, which this brief forbids, and needs its own identity gate. *Its size is measured
here:* it converts the 121 moved requests into removed ones.

**B. DELEGATE THE BARRIER TO `graded_competition` AND FOLD BOTH GATES INTO `semantic_control`.** *Math:*

on `semantic_control`, repointing `_blend_pick`. *Why not now:* three `hdlab/` files, one owned by pri 143's
in-flight consolidation.

**C. A PROVABLY-LOSSLESS TAU FROM `sup A`.** *Math:* §6. *Why not as the shipped default:* far more
conservative (11.7% vs 31.2%); the right shipping form is the online criterion in advantage mode, which
converges to it. Reported, not adopted.

**D. Fewer iterations / a sparser graph / a smaller seed.** Forbidden and rightly — they change answers. The
honest version of that idea is these gates.
