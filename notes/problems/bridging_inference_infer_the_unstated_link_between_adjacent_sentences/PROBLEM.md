## 1. THE PROBLEM IN PLAIN LANGUAGE
When you read "John was murdered. The knife was found nearby," nobody tells you the knife is the murder weapon -- you fill that in without noticing. Same with "Mary poured water on the fire. It went out" (the pouring is why it went out) and "We checked the picnic supplies. The beer was warm" (the beer is one of the supplies). Understanding a text IS this quiet gap-filling: a reader silently infers the connection between one sentence and the next so the story hangs together. Our reader does the first half well -- it works out what each sentence says -- but it never fills the gap, so what it ends up with is a pile of separate facts instead of a connected story. The job is to build the small, honest step a human does automatically: look at two neighbouring sentences, consider the few kinds of link that would make them cohere (one thing caused another, this object was the tool, this thing is a part of that group), and pick the one the meaning of the words actually supports. Do it the way the brain does -- by judging which link the words are most related to -- not by guessing and not by a black box.

## 2. WHY THIS ONE
Two payoffs. **(a) It is the core comprehension inference.** The representation tier is now built out -- the reader tracks who/what/when/where/why plus goals, affect, belief and state -- but it does not yet REASON over that representation. Bridge SELECTION is inherently a semantic-relatedness judgment (which instrument / cause / whole is plausible), so it is the natural, load-bearing first consumer that finally exercises the meaning store live.

## 3. HOW THE BRAIN DOES THIS (the opening move -- name the structure, replicate the operation)
- **PINNED (replicate the operation):** **Kintsch (1988) CONSTRUCTION-INTEGRATION** -- comprehension CONSTRUCTS a weakly-connected network of the stated propositions plus a small set of likely inferences, then INTEGRATES by spreading activation until it settles into a coherent situation model; the bridging inferences are exactly the links that survive integration. **Clark & Haviland GIVEN-NEW / bridging** -- the reader connects NEW information to GIVEN by inferring the implicit relation (the "bridging assumption"; Haviland-Clark measured the +181ms bridging cost). **McKoon & Ratcliff (1992) minimalist-vs-constructionist** -- readers reliably draw the LOCAL-coherence bridges (causal / referential) needed for connectedness, and NOT unconstrained elaboration; so build the constrained, coherence-required set, not open-ended world-knowledge inference.
- **Substrate (where + from what):** the anterior temporal SEMANTIC HUB (ATL) supplies the relatedness that says which instrument/cause/part is plausible; mPFC integrates it into the running situation model. The relation is inferred from SEMANTIC RELATEDNESS in the associative store -- which is precisely why this operation pulls the meaning channel live.
- **THE OPERATION to build:** over adjacent situation-model events/entities, (1) PROPOSE the small closed set of coherence-required bridge candidates (causal A->B; instrument entity->event; referential part-of / set-membership), (2) SCORE each by semantic relatedness in the meaning store conditioned on the situation model, (3) SELECT the best-supported one (with an honest abstain when none clears). Glass-box, hand-auditable at every step.
