## HEADLINE

**Glass-box real-prose reading is achievable, and the human parser is a real existence proof of a SMALL, non-LLM toolkit — but the ranking the premise proposes is WRONG, and we have direct empirical evidence for the correction.** A real cell run TODAY (`research_classical_openie_glassbox_parsing_2026-07-17.md`) hand-built a glass-box SVO/passive/coordination/relative-clause grammar and HARD_FAILed on real general-register prose: 58.6% of sentences fell into an unhandled "other" bucket and precision collapsed to ~0.18 via spurious firing on constructions the grammar didn't recognize. This failure was **not** caused by working-memory overload, not by insufficient foundation/world-knowledge, and not primarily by a lexicon gap — it was caused by **insufficient construction/grammar coverage** (missing copular, adjunct, and other Quirk clause types that a hand-built toy grammar never enumerated). Swapping toward the published classical clause-typology toolchain (ClausIE-class, on a real dependency parse) is expected to lift precision toward the 0.40-0.57 / recall 0.30-0.50 classical frontier — a direct, already-measured, already-diagnosed fix. **This is the single most decisive fact in this whole synthesis: the dominant barrier, empirically, is core-syntax/construction coverage, not foundation-size and not working-memory.**

The five-component ranking this drill produces, from most to least load-bearing for ACHIEVING a working glass-box parse of real prose (justification in section (d) below): **(1) core-syntax/construction coverage, (2) lexicon quality/disambiguation, (3) prediction/probabilistic-disambiguation, (4) working-memory/incremental-parse capacity, (5) foundation/world-knowledge size.** Foundation-size and working-memory — the two components the premise names — rank **last**, not first: you can correctly parse "the cat sat on the mat" with zero world knowledge and no working-memory pressure at all; what breaks glass-box reading today is not having enough GRAMMAR RULES and not having a principled way to attach WORD-FORMS TO MEANINGS, both of which are solved-in-the-literature-but-unbuilt problems, not open research questions. Foundation-size and WM matter enormously for what the parse ultimately MEANS (semantic depth, disambiguation, long-document/conversation capacity) — but they are not why parsing itself is failing right now.

This does not contradict the human-parser existence proof — it sharpens it. The consensus cognitive-science architecture of reading (Perfetti's Reading Systems Framework, built on the **Lexical Quality Hypothesis**) independently names the LEXICON, not syntax or general reasoning capacity, as comprehension's deepest single bottleneck in humans — and the poor-comprehenders literature (Cain & Oakhill) independently implicates **inference-making** ability, not raw working-memory or knowledge-store SIZE, as the sharpest discriminator of comprehension failure with intact decoding. Two independent human literatures and one direct substrate measurement all point away from "just foundation-size and/or working memory."

P_deflated (novel-synthesis ranking claim, capped per lit-scan calibration discipline): **0.45** for the ranking ORDER as stated; individual component P estimates below range 0.30-0.55, see calibration section.

## (a) The human parser's component decomposition — load-bearing vs. supporting

The brain is a real existence proof: bounded capacity, no LLM, glass-box-ISH (inspectable via ERP/eye-tracking/lesion evidence even if not fully transparent), reads real prose. Its actual load-bearing components, synthesized across this week's WM/lexicon/parsing lit-scans plus this cycle's two new lanes:

1. **Lexical access — the LOAD-BEARING HUB, not a peripheral input.** Perfetti's Reading Systems Framework (Perfetti & Hart 2002; Perfetti 2007, *Scientific Studies of Reading* 11:357-383) makes the lexicon the connection hub between low-level word recognition and higher comprehension (situation model, inference, discourse integration), formalized as the **Lexical Quality Hypothesis**: comprehension skill is gated more by the PRECISION, REDUNDANCY (form-meaning coherence), and FLEXIBILITY of individual word representations than by syntax or general reasoning capacity per se. This has real empirical support — differential lexical predictors (vocabulary depth, orthographic-phonological consistency) explain substantial comprehension variance even after decoding is controlled (PMC 2017, 4th-grade cohort), and vocabulary shows direct, indirect, AND reciprocal causal paths to comprehension across development (PMC 2014). At the word-recognition level itself, the consensus computational architecture is **interactive/connectionist**, not a serial pipeline: Seidenberg & McClelland's (1989) "triangle model" has orthography-phonology-semantics as parallel, mutually-constraining pathways, learned rather than hand-specified.
2. **Working memory — a nested, NOT single-number, capacity system** (full detail in the WM note): a small ~4-chunk attentional focus (Cowan 2001) nested inside a much larger activated-LTM pool, escaping its own limits via hierarchical chunking (Miller 1956; Gobet & Simon's template theory) and Ericsson & Kintsch's practiced long-term-working-memory retrieval structures, bounded in recursive depth at ~2-3 levels (Miller & Chomsky 1963; Christiansen & Chater's "Now-or-Never" chunk-and-pass account — the brain does NOT implement a true symbolic stack).
3. **Syntactic parsing — an incremental, resource-bounded, DEFAULT-HEURISTIC-DRIVEN commitment process, not a full-grammar-first analyzer.** Frazier's Minimal Attachment/Late Closure defaults commit early; Lewis & Vasishth's (2005) content-addressable cue-based retrieval (not a dedicated stack) resolves long-distance dependencies subject to activation decay and similarity interference; MacWhinney's Competition Model / Dowty's proto-role entailment-counting assigns thematic roles via a small, validity-weighted cue vocabulary (word order, animacy, case marking). Critically — and this is the load-bearing correction from today's HARD_FAIL analysis — **Ferreira's "good-enough" parsing program shows the human parser does NOT gracefully abstain on noncanonical constructions; it commits to a confident, often-WRONG shallow interpretation** (the NVN "first-noun-is-agent" heuristic misfiring on passives; Christianson et al. 2001's persistent garden-path misparse residue; Qian, Garnsey & Christianson 2018's finding that PLAUSIBILITY, not grammaticality, predicts what a stalled parse lands on). This is directly, brain-consistent with our own substrate's spurious-firing failure mode — the brain's baseline here is honestly mediocre, a case for nailing (and then exceeding) the brain-faithful baseline, not copying it uncritically.
4. **Prediction/expectation — a real, contested-strength signal, not settled as constitutive.** N400 amplitude tracks contextual predictability robustly (Kutas & Hillyard 1980; Kutas & Federmeier reviews) and Hale's (2001)/Levy's (2008) surprisal theory gives a computable expectation signal from a small structured grammar (a PCFG, not a giant corpus LM). BUT the strongest form of the prediction claim — genuine probabilistic PRE-ACTIVATION ahead of the input word (DeLong, Urbach & Kutas 2005, *Nat. Neurosci.*) — **failed to replicate** in a large multi-lab direct replication (Nieuwland et al. 2018, *eLife*), and the field's current position (Kuperberg & Jaeger 2016; *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 2023) is that whether prediction is CONSTITUTIVE (necessary for comprehension) or merely FACILITATIVE remains genuinely open and methodologically hard to dissociate from ordinary integration cost. No evidence was found that prediction-failure ALONE (independent of lexical/WM deficits) causes comprehension breakdown.
5. **World/background knowledge — necessary for the SITUATION MODEL (Kintsch's Construction-Integration model), not for the parse itself.** Discourse-level integration (protagonist/time/space/causality tracking, Zwaan & Radvansky's event-indexing model) and inference-generation both draw on world knowledge, but this operates AFTER a syntactic parse has assigned local structure — knowledge does not typically substitute for missing grammar coverage at the sentence level.

**The closest real "lesion" evidence — poor comprehenders with intact decoding (Nation, Snowling, Cain & Oakhill)** — directly tests which single component's absence breaks reading comprehension in humans. The answer is contested between two research traditions, but NEITHER names raw working-memory capacity nor knowledge-STORE SIZE as the primary driver: (a) Cain & Oakhill's explicit thesis is **inference-making deficit** — poor comprehenders generate fewer inferences, and this deficit **persists after controlling for background knowledge** (PMC 2018 meta-analysis) — ruling out "it's just a knowledge gap"; (b) Perfetti's explicit thesis is **lexical quality/vocabulary depth**. Verbal working memory co-occurs as a correlated deficit but is treated in this literature as likely partly downstream/shared-resource, not the root cause. **This is directly informative for the premise's hypothesis: the best human natural experiment for "what breaks reading" does not point at working-memory capacity or knowledge-store size as the primary lesion site.**

## (b) Glass-box, non-LLM systems that read real prose — accuracy, requirements, and the inspectability line

Drawing the glass-box line explicitly: **fully inspectable** = every decision traces to a hand-specified rule, a linear/log-linear feature weight, or a symbolic pattern match over an auditable feature vector. **NOT glass-box** = any component whose decision surface is a learned dense neural embedding/transformer with no per-decision provenance. One accepted, literature-supported carve-out exists at exactly one place: a structure-only dependency/POS parser with a CLOSED output space (fixed tag/arc-label vocabulary, applied only to tokens already in the input) cannot by construction emit a new fact or entity — so a narrow neural parser at this one stage does not compromise fact-level provenance even though its internal weights are not inspectable (already established in `research_glass_box_reading_robust_parsing_ceiling_2026-07-16.md`).

**CCG's specific relevance to grounding**: because CCG categories are built from the same combinatory rules that drive semantic composition, a CCG derivation yields a compositional logical form (lambda calculus) essentially for free alongside the syntax tree — the property most directly relevant to fact-grounding, since the output is a directly-executable semantic representation, not a bare tree needing a separate semantic-role layer bolted on. This is a genuinely different and arguably stronger glass-box property than ClausIE's clause-typology (which yields triples, not a full compositional logical form) — worth flagging as an under-explored upgrade path, though CCG's out-of-domain degradation (10-19 points) is materially larger than the in-domain neural-vs-classical gap and would need domain-tuned lexical categories to be viable on real general-register prose.

**Grammar induction is honestly NOT a lever here**: the F1 gap to supervised parsing is double-digit and induced trees don't reliably correspond to standard constituent/semantic categories — it is a low-resource/no-treebank tool, and treebank-supervised parsing (or the narrow-neural closed-output-space carve-out) is available, so grammar induction should be deprioritized rather than adopted.

## (c) The minimal achievable glass-box architecture

Synthesizing the concrete, already-scoped build recommendations across all five source notes into one architecture:

1. **Parse stage, curriculum-rung-gated (per `research_glass_box_reading_robust_parsing_ceiling_2026-07-16.md` + `research_classical_openie_glassbox_parsing_2026-07-17.md`):** a hand-written closed-grammar rule set for the earliest, most restricted curriculum rungs (SVO-only, no passive, no subordinate clauses); a real dependency parse (classical statistical — MaltParser/Charniak/Collins-class, or the accepted narrow-neural closed-output-space carve-out) feeding a **ClausIE-style clause-type classifier** (full Quirk taxonomy: SV/SVA/SVC/SVO/SVOA/SVOC/SVOO) once grammar variety exceeds the hand-written rule set's coverage. A **CALM-style rule-based coordination pre-splitting pass** (precision-positive, not a trade-off) runs before extraction. **RELNOUN**-style hand rules cover nominalizations; **Hearst patterns** run as a separate, narrow taxonomic-fact extractor. **Open, not closed, relation vocabulary** — extract the lexicalized predicate itself rather than mapping into a small fixed relation set (per the acquisition-literature finding that verb/relation vocabulary growth is general-purpose and scale-agnostic with no closed ceiling at any human developmental stage).
2. **Precision gate — a strict abstain-on-partial-match rule**, requiring ALL syntactic slots of a candidate construction pattern to be satisfied before emitting a triple. This is explicitly flagged as substrate-native, NOT brain-copied (the biology commits to confident wrong parses rather than abstaining) — a deliberate exploitation of glass-box exactness a biological system can only crudely approximate via graded RT/regression/P600 signals.
3. **Coreference — a first-class, non-optional pipeline stage from curriculum Rung 3+** (rule-based: Hobbs' algorithm or a centering-theory resolver). This is the one failure mode that does NOT shrink with prose simplification (simplification substitutes pronouns for long NPs) and is unaddressed by every classical IE technique reviewed.
4. **Lexicon — a literal, inspectable lookup table**, `word_form -> ranked (foundation_concept_id, weight, exemplar_count)`, updated by four converging child-language mechanisms combined glass-box: cross-situational co-occurrence tracking, mutual exclusivity, syntactic-role-restricted candidate narrowing, and single-exposure fast-mapping-with-later-confirmation. This attaches to the SAME role-filler binding scaffold already proven for syntax (Smolensky TPR / Plate HRR / Eliasmith SPA), making word-phasors literal pointers into foundation-concept-phasors rather than random vectors — the structure-content unification.
5. **Working memory — hierarchical chunking**, encoding each clause as its own bundle and binding it as a single filler into an outer sentence-level bundle via a fixed cross-dimensionality projection (the EC-analog interface), targeting the SAME ~2-3 nesting-depth ceiling and F=3-4 joint-decode ceiling the brain itself hits — not fighting past it via more cleanup iterations. For document/conversation length beyond this, exact external paging (a substrate-native affordance the brain lacks) converts the problem from a capacity wall into a retrieval-precision engineering problem.
6. **Foundation — a two-tier CORE (universal: Wikidata + ConceptNet + ATOMIC + WordNet + Wiktionary + YAGO4 + BabelNet) plus an OPEN MODULE REGISTRY** (PubChem, GeoNames, UMLS, etc.) admitted via a generic anchoring + non-redundant-content + declared-provenance test, feeding the SAME role-filler binding scaffold as both the reasoning architecture's additive_map fillers and the language scaffold's fillers.
7. **Prediction — a small, structured-grammar-derived surprisal signal (Hale/Levy-style, computed from the SAME small PCFG/clause-grammar already being built for parsing, not a giant corpus LM)**, used to calibrate reanalysis cost and rank ambiguous parses by plausibility — matching the human parser's own mechanism, with the explicit caveat that this buys ROBUSTNESS/quality at the margin, not a hard requirement for basic parse achievability (see ranking below).

Every stage above is either already fully scoped with falsifiable HARD-PASS/HARD-FAIL predictions in a prior note (parse stage, coreference, WM, lexicon, foundation) or laid out concretely here for the first time (prediction, CCG as an alternative/upgrade path).

## (d) RANKED barriers — the core deliverable

Ranking the five candidate barriers named in the premise, from MOST to LEAST load-bearing for ACHIEVING a working glass-box parse of real prose (not for what the parse ultimately grounds to or scales to — that is a separate, later question):

**#1 — Core-syntax/construction coverage.** Directly, empirically confirmed as the dominant bottleneck TODAY: a real cell's HARD_FAIL (58.6% unhandled sentences, precision collapsed to 0.18) traced entirely to insufficient construction/grammar coverage in a hand-built toy grammar, not to WM overload, lexicon gaps, or foundation-size. The fix (adopt the published ClausIE-class classical toolchain) is a KNOWN, already-benchmarked, already-diagnosed engineering move — this is the highest-confidence item in the entire ranking because it is the only one with a direct same-week empirical measurement behind it, not just literature extrapolation.

**#2 — Lexicon quality (disambiguation/meaning-attachment, not raw vocabulary size).** Two independent, converging lines: (a) Perfetti's Lexical Quality Hypothesis names the lexicon, not syntax or general reasoning, as the deepest single human comprehension bottleneck, with direct empirical support (vocabulary depth predicts comprehension variance beyond decoding); (b) our own substrate-specific gap — no published account exists anywhere in the literature for the actual LEARNING RULE that attaches a word-form's pointer to its target meaning (flagged as thin/unverified even within Eliasmith's own SPA program) — is genuine, unsolved novel-synthesis territory, unlike #1 which is solved-but-unbuilt. Ranked just below #1 because the BINDING algebra itself (role-filler composition once real fillers replace random ones) is close to a mechanical consequence of already-proven machinery (P=0.42, `research_word_grounding_lexicon...`), while the learning-rule component is capped lower (P=0.35) — the mechanism to build is clear even though it's unprecedented, keeping this from displacing #1's directly-measured status.

**#3 — Prediction/probabilistic disambiguation.** Real and useful (surprisal-based reanalysis-cost calibration, thematic-role competition), but the literature's own contestedness is the reason this ranks third, not first or second: the strongest form of the human prediction claim (genuine pre-activation) FAILED a large direct replication (Nieuwland et al. 2018), and no study shows prediction-failure ALONE (independent of lexical/WM deficits) causes comprehension breakdown. Prediction functions as a QUALITY/robustness lever for resolving genuine local ambiguity (PP-attachment, word-sense competition) — valuable, but not shown to be a hard precondition for basic parse achievability the way construction coverage and lexical attachment are.

**#4 — Working-memory/incremental-parse capacity.** Real, measured (N/16-32 crosstalk cliff, F=3-4 resonator sweet spot, ~2-3 nesting-depth wall, brain-matched) — but NOT shown to be the binding constraint at the curriculum stages where reading is currently failing. Today's HARD_FAIL occurred on ordinary-length real-prose sentences, well within any plausible WM budget; the WM ceiling becomes binding only for genuinely long/deeply-nested sentences or multi-turn conversation length, which is a LATER-stage concern once basic construction coverage and lexical attachment are solved. Ranking WM this low directly refutes the premise's "just... working memory" framing for the CURRENT problem, without denying WM matters for a later stage.

**#5 — Foundation/world-knowledge size.** Least load-bearing for PARSE ACHIEVABILITY specifically — you can correctly parse "the cat sat on the mat" with zero world knowledge, and the actual HARD_FAIL today was a grammar-coverage failure on sentences whose CONTENT was not particularly knowledge-dependent. Foundation size and quality (per the two-tier CORE+MODULE spec) matter enormously for what a successful parse ultimately GROUNDS TO — semantic disambiguation via selectional restrictions, inference over the situation model, answering "does this fact make sense" — and the poor-comprehenders literature's own finding that inference deficits persist even after controlling for background knowledge independently supports demoting raw knowledge-store SIZE below inference-quality and lexical-quality as a root cause. This directly refutes the second half of the premise's named hypothesis.

**Direct verdict on the premise**: NO, it is not "just foundation-size and/or working memory" — both rank last of five. The dominant, empirically-confirmed barrier is core-syntax/construction coverage, with lexicon-quality (specifically the word-meaning ATTACHMENT mechanism, not raw size) a close second. This is a real, literature-and-measurement-backed correction, not a hedge.
