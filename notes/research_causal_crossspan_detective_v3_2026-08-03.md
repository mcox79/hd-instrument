## HEADLINE

Mined public-domain Sherlock Holmes short-story collections (Doyle, *The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes* / *The Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes*, Project Gutenberg #1661 / #834) for the `multi_candidate_causal_attribution` schema. Built **10 genuinely-discriminating, fully-independent** cross-span items (`crossspan_det_001..010`), one per distinct story, each with a machine-verified substring-guard PASS, a MEASURED recency-baseline defeat (10/10, not eyeballed), and a MEASURED surface-valence non-separation check (a naive higher-violence-word-count-wins rule would only "work" on 3/10 items, with mixed sign across the set — no reliable surface tell). This roughly doubles the existing `multi_candidate_causal_attribution` pool: prior pool was v1 (n=4, McGuffey/general-narrative) + v2 draft (n=4, Tom Sawyer / Anne of Green Gables, itself with internal span-reuse caveats) = n=8; this v3 detective-fiction set adds **10 more, zero span reuse anywhere in the new set**.

I mined and evaluated candidate scenes from 9 stories across both collections (Boscombe Valley Mystery, Blue Carbuncle, Beryl Coronet, Speckled Band, Man with the Twisted Lip, Silver Blaze, Crooked Man, Naval Treaty, Resident Patient, Noble Bachelor -- 10 stories, 10 items, 1 each) and explicitly rejected several strong-looking candidates as not clean fits for the schema (see "Stories considered and rejected" below) rather than force weak items. ## Source and method

- **Sources fetched fresh this session** (not previously on disk): `data/corpora/sherlock_holmes/raw/adventures_1661.txt` (Project Gutenberg eBook #1661, `https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1661`), `data/corpora/sherlock_holmes/raw/memoirs_834.txt` (Project Gutenberg eBook #834, `https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/834`) — downloaded verbatim via `curl` directly from gutenberg.org cache, confirmed by header inspection (`Title:`, `Author: Arthur Conan Doyle`, `*** START/END OF THE PROJECT GUTENBERG EBOOK ***` markers intact).
- Cleaned with `data/corpora/sherlock_holmes/clean_sherlock.py` (same PG-boilerplate-strip convention as the existing `data/corpora/clean_gutenberg_multi_v1.py` used for `tom_sawyer`/`little_women`/`wizard_of_oz`/`alice_in_wonderland`) into `data/corpora/sherlock_holmes/cleaned/adventures.clean.txt` (11,857 lines, 105,125 words) and `.../memoirs.clean.txt` (11,319 lines, 96,256 words). Pure stdlib, no network at clean time.
- Item mining: manual literary recall of known whodunit-with-misdirection scenes across both collections, `Grep`/`Read` to locate exact line numbers in the cleaned files, hand-transcription of the `{line_range, text}` spans (same schema as `gold_grounded_causal_crossspan_v2_DRAFT.jsonl`), then a purpose-built, **committed-to-repo** verification script: `data/eval_gold_mention_role_mcguffey_v1/build_detective_v3.py`. Unlike the prior growth pass, this script is not ad hoc/scratchpad-only — it is the actual generator: it loads both cleaned corpus files, checks every one of the 30 spans (10 items x 3 span fields) is a literal (whitespace/quote-normalized) substring of the source file within a small line-window tolerance, computes the recency and surface-valence stats below, and only writes an item to the output JSONL if all 3 of its spans pass the guard. Re-run command: `.venv/Scripts/python.exe data/eval_gold_mention_role_mcguffey_v1/build_detective_v3.py`.
- No paraphrase, no synthesized narrative text, no LLM-authored content. Every `text` field is a Doyle sentence/passage typed out from the source and then mechanically re-verified against the on-disk file.
- During construction, the substring-guard caught and forced correction of **3 line-number transcription errors** (Silver Blaze's true-blocker/distractor/query spans, and Crooked Man's true-blocker/distractor spans, were initially mis-numbered by several thousand lines due to copy-paste confusion between the two source files during manual mining) and **1 recency-defeat failure caught by the measured baseline check** (Silver Blaze) plus **1 more caught the same way** (Crooked Man) — both had a query point positioned such that the naive line-distance recency baseline actually landed on the TRUE blocker, not the distractor, which would have made those items invalid per the "recency must be wrong" requirement. Both were fixed by relocating `query_span` to a point in the text that sits nearer the distractor and further from the (much-later) reveal — this is a legitimate choice of where in the story to "ask the question," not a change to any evidentiary content. This is reported because it demonstrates the guard did real work, not just a formality.

## Baseline-defeat stats (MEASURED, computed by `build_detective_v3.py`, not eyeballed)

- **Recency-wrong rate: 10/10 (100%)**. Operational definition: for each item, the "recency baseline" predicts whichever of {true_blocker, distractor} has a span line-number numerically closer to the query_span's line (a "most recently/saliently mentioned agent wins" heuristic). Computed line-gaps (`query_to_true`, `query_to_distractor`) are recorded per item in the output JSONL's `measured_recency_gap_lines` field. In every item the distractor is closer to the query point than the true blocker is — by a wide margin in most cases (e.g. item 001: distractor 19 lines away vs. true blocker 874 lines away; item 008: 5 vs. 766). Two items (Silver Blaze, Crooked Man) initially failed this check with the first-chosen query point and were fixed by relocating the query point (documented above) — this is disclosed, not hidden.
- **Surface-valence non-separation: measured with a 34-word hand-built violence/negative-affect lexicon** (blood, villain, thief, murder, kill, dead, stole, guilty, crime, wicked, scream, fear, weapon, knife, stab, suicide, hanged, etc.), scored as raw case-insensitive whole-word hits over `true_blocker_span.text` and `distractor_span.text` separately (`surface_valence_score` field per item). A naive "the span with MORE violence-words is the true culprit" rule would only correctly separate true-from-distractor on 3/10 items; the sign of `(distractor_score - true_score)` is mixed across the set: [+3, -1, +2, -1, 0, -4, 0, 0, +1, 0] for items 001-010 respectively — sometimes the distractor is more violently worded (e.g. item 001: "found his father dead... blood" vs. Turner's confession scoring 0), sometimes the true blocker is (e.g. item 006: Silver Blaze's kick/gash passage scores 4 vs. Simpson's arrest passage scoring 0), and 4/10 items are tied at equal or near-equal valence. This is a genuine, measured non-separation — not asserted from an eyeball pass like the prior v2 growth note's qualitative claim.
- **True-blocker position: NOT varied — always textually last.** In all 10 items the true_blocker_span's line number is the largest of the three spans (query always precedes it, distractor always precedes it). This is an **honest structural finding specific to the whodunit genre**, not a mining artifact to paper over: classic detective-fiction structure puts the reveal at the story's climax by convention, so "true cause revealed after the query, near the end" is close to definitional for this genre. Item 003 (Beryl Coronet) and 004 (Speckled Band) have distractor appearing AFTER the query but still before the true blocker, so distractor-position does vary relative to query even though true-blocker-position does not vary relative to either. Flagging this explicitly per the task's "vary true-blocker POSITION" requirement — I did not force a counterexample by contorting a story's structure, since doing so would mean writing content that isn't actually how the story reads.
- **Span independence: 30/30 unique.** Programmatically verified (`set()` over all 30 span texts, 10 items x 3 fields) — zero duplicate text across the entire set, unlike the prior v2 growth note's items 006/007 (shared true_blocker_span) and 008/009 (shared distractor_span). Achieved for free here because every item is sourced from a different story.

## Stories considered and rejected (honest negative findings)

- **A Case of Identity**: Hosmer Angel's disappearance turns out to be the stepfather Windibank in disguise — a real disguise-reveal mystery, but there is no second, distinct, textually-accused WRONG agent (no one else is blamed for anything); the story is single-suspect-in-disguise, not a 2-candidate selection problem. Does not fit `>=2 competing agent-caused candidates`.
- **The Musgrave Ritual**: Butler Brunton and housemaid Rachel Howells both vanish; Howells's jealous-revenge role in trapping Brunton in the vault is the true resolution, but I could not find a clean textual passage where a THIRD party or a specific alternate theory of Brunton's fate is explicitly stated as a wrong accusation (the text stays genuinely ambiguous "what could have become of him" rather than naming a false culprit) — including it would have required writing an inferred distractor claim not actually present in the source text, which would cross into synthesis. Skipped rather than forced.
- **The Red-Headed League, The Five Orange Pips, The Adventure of the Engineer's Thumb, The Adventure of the Copper Beeches, The Reigate Squires, The "Gloria Scott", The Yellow Face, The Stockbroker's Clerk, The Greek Interpreter, The Final Problem**: scanned by recall; none present a clean "salient wrongly-accused agent + hidden true agent, both textually confirmed" structure — either there is no false accusation at all (a mystery of method, not blame), or the eventual culprit is obvious/named from early on with no real distractor, or (Reigate Squires) the two suspects ARE both guilty together, with no innocent party to falsely blame.
